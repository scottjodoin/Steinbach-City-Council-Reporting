Taxes and spending · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

How did Steinbach City Council set taxes and spending from December 2015 to November 2018, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • A long tax freeze, admitted to have limits. The 2016 budget cut the rate to 13.23 mills so the reassessment brought in no extra money. The mayor said four years without an increase had come “sometimes to a fault.”
  • Policing ended the freeze. When Steinbach passed 15,000 people, the city’s share of RCMP costs rose from 70% to 90%. The rate rose 0.7 mill in 2017 and 0.16 mill in 2018.
  • Big recreation projects split council. The multiplex was dropped as unaffordable (2016); the $24 million arts centre died when its funding deadline passed (2017).
  • The golf club land deal became a budget fight. Council bought 40 acres for $500,000 in December 2017, about 4–3; three members then voted against the 2018 budget because its increase matched the first payment.
  • Short debt and reserves throughout. Every loan was on a five-year term, and surpluses went into reserves.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr. This record starts in December 2015, the first meeting with a transcript.

What happened

2016: a “net zero” budget and a costly freeze

2016 was a reassessment year, when the province revalues every property. Council cut the city’s mill rate by just over 1 mill, to 13.23, so that the city would collect no more money because of higher values:

“We are not increasing taxes this year. It is a reassessment year, and so we are adjusting the mill rate to make this a net zero increase. And what that means Is that we will not take in any extra money as a result of the reassessment.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving the 2016 financial plan · Feb 16, 2016 · 0:16:35

The same night, Councillor Earl Funk moved that council ask the Hanover School Division to freeze school taxes too, which he said were 55% of the tax bill. Mayor Goertzen acknowledged what the long freeze cost:

“Council, I think for the last 4 years we've had no tax increases brought forward by the city administration and by this council. We've worked very hard to stay within that and sometimes to a fault. We do limit what we can do then because of that.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Feb 16, 2016 · 0:22:08

With no room in the budget, a $200,000 overrun on Old Tom Road was paid from surplus or reserves rather than cutting the project, and residents’ request for a $700,000–800,000 sidewalk on the road was left out. Development fees were renewed with 2.5% yearly increases to 2019, and water and sewer rates went to the Public Utilities Board for about 3% a year.

Recreation was where council divided. In April 2016 Councillor Susan Penner moved to drop the multiplex as unaffordable on a city tax base of about $13 million a year, while keeping 1.241 mills a year flowing into the recreation reserve; the mayor opposed it, saying governments were “ready to pour millions” into such projects. It carried 5–2, with the mayor and Councillor John Fehr against, according to SteinbachOnline’s report (the video ends before the vote). In December, when the performing arts centre’s estimate reached $24.3 million, council reaffirmed its $7.5 million share with a June 30, 2017 deadline, 4–3, with Councillors Zwaagstra, Susan Penner and Funk against:

“We have never had a vote on a $24.3 million performing arts centre. That has never come to council as a vote.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Dec 20, 2016 · 0:44:53

2017: policing ends the freeze, the arts centre ends

The 2017 budget raised the mill rate 0.7 mill to 13.93. Staff tied all of it to policing:

“All of the additional revenue generated by the mill rate increase will be used to fund the additional policing costs as a result of the increased funding requirement now that we have reached or surpassed a population of 15,000.”

Brian Hrehirchuk, Director of Finance · Feb 21, 2017 · 0:00:49

Councillors Zwaagstra and Funk voted against the budget because the arts centre, and the $5 million council planned to borrow for it, was its largest item. The arts centre did not go ahead: its outside funding had not all arrived by the June 30 deadline. An $11 million water supply project did go ahead, because $5.6 million came from other governments; when bids came in high, council trimmed the scope and used utility surplus rather than raise rates. A citizen task force recommended higher council pay, and council adopted it.

In December 2017 council bought 40 acres from the Steinbach Fly-In Golf Club for $500,000, paid over three years, and leased it back for $1 a year. No vote count was announced; by members’ stated positions it carried about 4–3:

“This is not a land deal. This is being dressed up as that. It is a bailout that is dressed up as a land deal.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Dec 5, 2017 · 1:09:41

“we as a city can acquire 40 acres at a reasonable cost and have an investment in the long-term strategy”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Dec 5, 2017 · 1:21:30

2018: a 4–3 budget

The 2018 budget raised the rate 0.16 mill (about 1.1%) to 14.09. It passed 4–3, with Councillors Zwaagstra, Susan Penner and Funk against. Their argument was that the increase equalled the first golf payment:

“If you subtract $167,000 from this budget, you would not have the 0.16 mill rate increase. It would disappear.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:30:22

“If we compare this to the same request made by the Pat Porter Centre, on one hand we had an organization, our seniors, that followed the right process, made public requests, and to date have merely received a pat on the back.”

Councillor Earl Funk · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:24:42

The majority said any line of the budget could be blamed for the increase, and that the budget was a compromise. Councillor Siemens named what had been left out:

“There are items that are not in this budget, like additional grant funds for seniors or youth programs that play a vital role in the quality of Steinbach.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:38:13

“Our job as municipal officials is to find compromise, and that's what this budget does.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:39:53

The rest of 2018 followed the usual pattern: almost $2 million of surplus, much of it development fees, went into reserves; council tabled, then approved, a $65,000 match to repair the city-owned Arts Centre; and it bought three more downtown lots for $647,400, with Councillor John Fehr voting alone against each as overspending.
