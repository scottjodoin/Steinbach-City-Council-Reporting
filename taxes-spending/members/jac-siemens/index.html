Taxes and spending · Council member

Jac Siemens: taxes, budgets and spending

What did Jac Siemens say and do about Steinbach's taxes, budgets, debt and spending between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Jac Siemens supported every financial plan in this record and often explained them in practical terms: the 2018 increase covered policing costs council had agreed to pay with new tax dollars, and the 2024 increase came partly because earlier budgets had already counted on supplementary taxes from new construction, leaving no 'wiggle room.'

His votes against were about who pays. He opposed charging the Clearspring Greens neighbourhood most of a pathway's cost ('the first time that we're asking residences from any area to pay the majority'), was the only vote against keeping a road levy on a disability-services charity (2020), and in 2016 opposed buying downtown land with no plan in place. In 2025 he moved to end the $65,000-a-year economic development grant for lack of results.

“But this is the first time that we're asking residences from any area to pay the majority of the cost of a pathway to connect them to our community.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Voting against the Clearspring Greens pathway levy · Jan 7, 2020 · 1:39:10

Term by term

2014–2018

Stated he would vote against the 2016 letter asking the school division to freeze taxes (he wanted to meet first) and against a 2016 downtown land purchase made without a plan. Tried to delay the over-budget Old Tom Road tender to find savings. Seconded the golf land purchase in 2017 and moved the 2018 budget, defending its increase as the policing cost council had agreed to fund, while naming what was left out: grants for seniors and youth, pathways, a recreation facility.

Took part in 22 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Voted for every budget, but was the most frequent dissenter on cost-sharing: he moved to cut the Clearspring Greens pathway's scope, voted against the levy (5–2) and alone against moving the lighting cost to the city (6–1, no reason given on the record), and alone against keeping the Acres Drive levy on a charity (5–1). He also voted to delay the 2019 purchasing by-law for more study. Called the event centre a once-in-20-years decision while noting its cost to taxpayers.

Took part in 35 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Explained the 2024 pressures in detail: budgeted supplementary taxes, higher RCMP cost shares and renewal projects pushed into later years. Said he liked staff's 7.5% development-fee increase but voted for 5% with a 2027 review. Moved debt issues and the PTH 12/Loewen contract. Moved the end of the economic development office grant in 2025. Did not run in 2026.

Took part in 37 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A steady supporter of council's budgets whose dissents clustered on one question: whether a particular group of residents should carry a cost that benefits the city. His departure in 2026 removes the member who most often voted that way.
