Taxes and spending · Council member

Chris Goertzen: taxes, budgets and spending

What did Chris Goertzen say and do about Steinbach's taxes, budgets, debt and spending between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

In short

Chris Goertzen chaired council through four years with no city tax increase, then two small increases (2017 and 2018) that staff tied to the jump in RCMP costs once Steinbach passed 15,000 people. He was candid that holding the line had a cost: 'We do limit what we can do then because of that.'

He championed the performing arts centre until its outside funding deadline passed in 2017, opposed dropping the multiplex master plan in 2016, and supported buying 40 acres from the golf club for $500,000. Those choices produced the sharpest money divisions in this record, including the 4–3 vote on the 2018 budget. He defended both budgets as compromise.

“Council, I think for the last 4 years we've had no tax increases brought forward by the city administration and by this council. We've worked very hard to stay within that and sometimes to a fault. We do limit what we can do then because of that.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · At the 2016 budget, after four years without a city tax increase · Feb 16, 2016 · 0:22:08

Term by term

2014–2018

Chaired the 'net zero' 2016 budget and the 2017 and 2018 increases for policing. Opposed the 2016 motion to drop the multiplex (federal money was 'ready to pour'), worked for the arts centre despite not having voted for its $7.5 million cap, and supported the golf land deal as an investment in long-term land. Kept the grants policy 'focused,' which meant the seniors' centre's request for a grant in lieu of taxes was not funded. Stressed partner money from other governments, including the $5.6 million that made the water supply project possible.

Took part in 22 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

He left office in 2018 after a term in which council's majority followed his lead on the big spending choices, but by narrowing margins: the arts centre and the golf deal both divided council, and three of the six councillors who voted against him on money went on to form the core of the next council.
