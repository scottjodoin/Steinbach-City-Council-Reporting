Taxes and spending · Council member

Damian Penner: taxes, budgets and spending

What did Damian Penner say and do about Steinbach's taxes, budgets, debt and spending between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Damian Penner has been one of council's firmest voices for cash and reserves over debt. He has praised low borrowing rates, the five-year payoff ('the envy of many cities across Canada') and putting money back into reserves, and he moved the 2026 budget as 'a zero tax increase budget.'

His clearest dissent came in 2023, when he and Jake Hiebert voted three times against holding development fees to 5% a year. Existing residents on fixed incomes, he said, pay for growth either up front through fees 'or you're going to be paying it through the mill rate,' and the mill rate had been subsidizing growth since 2012.

“A lot has been said about affordability, and the reality is if you're a citizen of Steinbach, you're either paying for it upfront on the development fees or you're going to be paying it through the mill rate.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Voting against 5% development fees · Oct 17, 2023 · 0:14:48

Term by term

2018–2022

Voted for council laptops (defeated 2–5) in his first month. Pushed the Aquatic Centre toward its 66% cost-recovery target, brought a one-time library technology grant, and moved the Clearspring Greens levy before saying the revised cost split was 'the decision that I wish we would have made in the first place.' Explained the budget process in a 2021 opening and praised borrowing at 1.53%.

Took part in 27 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Voted against all three readings of the 5% development-fee by-law, arguing for staff's 7.5%. Championed reserves and cash over debt, called reserves 'uncomfortable' in 2025 and backed putting $5 million back, defended short debt, and moved the 2026 budget. Moved the $400,000 loan to the event centre operator, stressing 'this is a loan… and not a grant.' Re-elected in 2026.

Took part in 30 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistent on reserves and short debt from his first term; on growth costs he moved from defending the neighbourhood-pays Clearspring plan (2020) to arguing that existing taxpayers subsidize new development (2023). He returns on the new council as one of its most experienced members on budgets.
