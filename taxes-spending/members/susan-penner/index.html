Susan Penner: taxes, budgets and spending

What did Susan Penner say and do about Steinbach's taxes, budgets, debt and spending between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

In short

Susan Penner has questioned spending more openly than most of her colleagues while voting for every budget since 2019. In 2016 she argued that Steinbach taxpayers carry the cost of regional facilities used by people from outside the city. In 2018 she voted against the budget over the golf land deal. In 2019 she praised council for not using a debt-payment spike as an excuse to raise taxes.

She led the 2021 redesign of the Clearspring Greens pathway levy after 78 objections, capping each lot at $1,610. In 2025 she called the budget 'a very significant tax increase' while seconding it, pushed to show the event centre subsidy openly in the grants line, and called the Chamber of Commerce's growing share of the business tax 'way out of line.' After the 2024 and 2025 floods she pressed for drainage spending 'now,' and in 2026 she questioned the provincial rules driving the wastewater plant's cost.

“And I think it would be easy for council to have a good legitimate excuse or reason to raise taxes this year. We could easily have defended saying, look, our debt payments are going up significantly this year, we need to raise taxes.”

Councillor Susan Penner · On the 2019 budget, which held the rate despite a spike in debt payments · Mar 19, 2019 · 0:23:40

Term by term

2014–2018

Moved the April 2016 motion to drop the multiplex as unaffordable (carried 5–2) and voted against reaffirming the $7.5 million arts-centre share when its estimate reached $24.3 million (4–3). Said in 2016 that 'growth costs money' and that Steinbach residents cover the aquatic centre's deficit though most users live elsewhere. Several other 2015–2017 remarks could be hers or Cari Penner's and are not attributed. Voted against the golf land deal (inferred from the debate) and the 2018 budget, saying it compromised the grants process, and moved the Arts Centre matching grant, calling for a more flexible grants policy.

Took part in 11 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Moved the 2019 budget; favoured user fees as fair ('people using the services are paying for them'); said the school-tax system makes property taxes look higher than the city's share; asked council to review the Chamber of Commerce's business-tax grant (no review followed). Rebuilt the Clearspring Greens levy as a 50/50 split capped at $1,610 a lot, which cut objections from 78 to 6. Backed the event centre borrowing, stressing the five-year payoff.

Took part in 34 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Supported proactive infrastructure renewal and the five-year rule, and said she would not have supported 7.5% development fees. In 2025 she was the most questioning voice at the budget hearing (the increase, the event centre subsidy, the Chamber's $225,000), pressed for urgent flood spending, and in May 2026 asked whether the environmental requirements behind a wastewater project 'skyrocketed to over $200 million' produce measurable benefits. Became Deputy Mayor in November 2026.

Took part in 32 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From a dissenter in 2018 to a member of the unanimous majority who still names the lines she would change. As Deputy Mayor on the new council, and the longest-serving councillor, she is likely to be central to the 2027 budget and the wastewater financing.
