Council members on taxes and spending

How did each council member vote and speak on Steinbach’s taxes, budgets, debt and spending between December 2015 and November 2026?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. Since 2019 every budget has passed unanimously, so the differences between members show up in who explained the budget, what they questioned, and a small number of split votes.

  • 2017–2018 was the divided period. Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra and Susan Penner voted against the 2018 budget, 4–3, over the $500,000 golf club land deal. Funk and Zwaagstra had also voted against the 2017 budget over the arts centre.
  • Since then the splits are about who pays. Jac Siemens and Jake Hiebert voted against charging the Clearspring Greens neighbourhood most of a pathway’s cost (2020); Damian Penner and Jake Hiebert voted against keeping development fees to 5% a year (2023).
  • Michael Zwaagstra explained the budget most often for a decade; Susan Penner questioned individual lines most openly. Both Zwaagstra and Siemens left council in 2026.

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

As a councillor he voted against two budgets over how money was spent; as mayor he has chaired every budget since 2019, all of them unanimous.

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Chaired years of tax freezes and admitted they came 'sometimes to a fault'; pushed for the arts centre and the golf land deal, which split his council 4–3.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Council's main voice on money for a decade: moved most budgets and debt by-laws, defended the five-year rule, and led the opposition to the golf deal and the arts centre.

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Backed every budget, but was the member most likely to vote against charging a cost to one group of residents.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

Voted against the 2018 budget over the golf deal, redesigned the Clearspring Greens levy, and became the council member most willing to question spending lines.

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

A strong defender of reserves and short debt, and one of two votes against keeping development fees low in 2023.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Voted with the majority on every money decision in this record; said the new arena was a main reason he ran.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Joined Jac Siemens against the Clearspring Greens levy, and Damian Penner against keeping development fees low.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

Moved the golf land purchase and defended it; alone voted against three downtown land purchases in 2018 as overspending.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Supported the 2018 budget and the golf land deal; praised low debt payments and healthy reserves.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; their first budget is the 2027 financial plan. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair, and set school budgets; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.
