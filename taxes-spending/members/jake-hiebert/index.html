Taxes and spending · Council member

Jake Hiebert: taxes, budgets and spending

What did Jake Hiebert say and do about Steinbach's taxes, budgets, debt and spending between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Jake Hiebert's recorded dissents on money are about who pays. In January 2020 he seconded Jac Siemens' attempt to cut the Clearspring Greens pathway's scope and voted against charging the neighbourhood most of its cost. In 2023 he voted three times against holding development fees to 5% a year: 'Because the development fee does not pay for the complete cost, the city pays the difference… I think this is unfair.'

Otherwise he has supported council's budgets, seconding both the 2023 and 2024 plans and calling the 2024 increase 'what we require to make ends meet,' and he backed the event centre as a good deal for taxpayers. He moved the defeated 2018 motion to buy council members laptops.

“Because the development fee does not pay for the complete cost, the city pays the difference. Where does the city get their money? From the taxpayer. I think this is unfair.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert · Voting against 5% development fees · Nov 7, 2023 · 0:09:31

Term by term

2018–2022

Moved council laptops (defeated 2–5) and the motion to delay the 2019 purchasing by-law over the $350,000 limit staff could spend without council (defeated 3–4). Seconded the Clearspring scope cut and voted against the levy, then supported the compromise. Seconded the added RCMP officer.

Took part in 23 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Voted against all three readings of the 5% development-fee by-law. Seconded the 2023 and 2024 financial plans, backed the event centre change order ('the taxpayers are getting a $69+ million building for $30+ million'), and explained the Municipal Act rules for the 2026 operator loan. Re-elected in 2026.

Took part in 28 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A supporter of council's budgets whose dissents, like Jac Siemens' and Damian Penner's, were about fairness between residents and developers or neighbourhoods. He returns on the new council.
