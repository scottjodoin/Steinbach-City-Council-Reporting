Taxes and spending · Council member

John Fehr: taxes, budgets and spending

What did John Fehr say and do about Steinbach's taxes, budgets, debt and spending between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

In short

John Fehr supported the old council's major spending choices: he moved the 2016 City Hall addition after years of deferral, moved the arts-centre reaffirmation in December 2016 (carried 4–3), moved the golf club land purchase in 2017 ('it will cost the taxpayers no extra money'), and backed the 2017 and 2018 budgets, blaming the increases on RCMP costs.

He was also cost-conscious in other ways: he resisted a dog-licence fee increase, asked whether the province should pay for drainage through the golf course, and in 2018 was the only vote against three downtown land purchases, saying the city had spent 'a fair bit more than our budget allowed.'

“I believe that we've spent a fair bit more than our budget allowed in the last 3-plus years, and so I would rather see that money go somewhere else.”

Councillor John Fehr · Voting alone against a downtown land purchase · Oct 16, 2018 · 0:31:25

Term by term

2014–2018

Voted against dropping the multiplex (2016, with the mayor; carried 5–2) and moved the arts-centre reaffirmation with a June 2017 deadline. Moved the City Hall addition and supported covering the Old Tom Road overrun ('if we're going to delay any part of this, it will only cost us more money'). Moved the golf land purchase and defended doing land deals in closed meetings. Backed both increase budgets. Moved the water supply tender with a reduced scope rather than raise taxes. Moved to table the Arts Centre's $65,000 match, preferring 'a concrete plan.' Sole vote against the 2018 downtown land purchases.

Took part in 19 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

A reliable vote for the majority's big projects whose late-term dissents on downtown land spending showed a narrower view of what the city should buy.
