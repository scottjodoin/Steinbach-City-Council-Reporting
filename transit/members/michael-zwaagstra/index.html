Michael Zwaagstra: transit and accessible transportation

What did Michael Zwaagstra say and do about transit and accessible transportation in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Michael Zwaagstra backed accessible transit run by a community partner: he moved the December 2021 agreement that handed Handi-Transit to Serving Seniors Inc., calling it 'a natural fit.' He also seconded routine Handi-Transit reports and moved the third reading of the 2018 Official Community Plan.

On everything beyond that he was the most consistent sceptic. In 2018 he questioned Steinbach Taxi's request for regulation as 'a request to limit competition,' and SteinbachOnline reported that he moved the May 2018 decision not to create a by-law. In February 2024, at an unrecorded session, he said a fixed bus loop 'would be very expensive' with a risk of 'empty buses,' and that the city should not set up a system that competes with taxis (SteinbachOnline). In 2025 he said most people in Steinbach drive, 'particularly in the winter months,' and that council would not impede that.

“It sounds like this is a request to limit competition. Is that not the case?”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Questioning Steinbach Taxi's request for a by-law · Feb 20, 2018 · 0:51:00

Questioned Steinbach Taxi's 2018 request ('it's not often we have a business requesting that we regulate them') and, per SteinbachOnline, moved the May 2018 decision not to make a taxi by-law. Moved third reading of the 2018 Official Community Plan (routine).

2018–2022

Moved the December 2021 agreement handing Handi-Transit to Serving Seniors Inc., 'a natural fit'; seconded the Handi-Transit financial reports in 2019 and 2021 (routine).

Took part in 1 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Per SteinbachOnline (February 2024), warned of 'empty buses' and of competing with taxis. Supported the 2025 Active Transportation Plan in principle while insisting it not impede vehicles, and said in a parking debate that most people, even on Main Street, get around by car. Seconded the 2025 plan's first and third readings (routine).

Took part in 3 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistent: a partner-run accessible service yes, regulation or a city transit system no. He did not run in 2026.
