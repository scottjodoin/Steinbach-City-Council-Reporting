Transit · Council members

Council members on transit

How did each council member speak and vote on transit and accessible transportation in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. No vote on transit ever divided council, and no member moved a motion to study or fund general transit. The differences show up in what members said, much of it at unrecorded sessions reported by SteinbachOnline.

  • Accessible transit had everyone’s support. John Fehr moved the 2018 grant application for a new van. Michael Zwaagstra moved, and Jac Siemens seconded, the 2021 handover to Serving Seniors Inc., which Mayor Earl Funk praised.
  • The car and the market. Michael Zwaagstra called a taxi by-law “a request to limit competition” (2018) and warned of “empty buses” (2024). Mayor Earl Funk called transit “very expensive” (2023) and Steinbach “the automobile city” (2025).
  • Openings without a motion. Jac Siemens said planning should move people, not only vehicles (2025). Damian Penner said council had “an obligation to start looking at exploring” alternatives, and Susan Penner asked for a cost and funding analysis (both 2024, per news).
  • Little or no record. Bill and Jake Hiebert had only routine roles. Ron Falk has no record on this topic; Greg Carlos rode Winkler’s on-demand van as a candidate (CBC, July 2026).

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

Backed handing Handi-Transit to Serving Seniors at steady cost; as mayor called transit 'very expensive' and Steinbach 'the automobile city,' and told a ride-share founder the city partners only with accessible transit.

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Chaired the 2018 hearing on Steinbach Taxi's request and referred it to administration; his council then decided against a taxi by-law.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Moved the 2021 Serving Seniors handover, but was council's firmest voice for the market and the car: taxi rules would 'limit competition' (2018), a bus loop risks 'empty buses' (2024), and planning must not impede drivers (2025).

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Seconded the 2021 Serving Seniors handover and became council's clearest voice for planning that moves people, not only vehicles.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

Agreed in 2024 that a bus loop would not work and asked for 'the cost analysis, how this would be funded, what grants are available' (news); otherwise routine votes.

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Said in 2024 council has 'an obligation to start looking at exploring' alternatives (news); seconded the Active Transportation Plan; in 2026 a Councillor Penner, most likely him, praised private innovation over public spending.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Only routine roles: moved a Handi-Transit financial report (2019) and seconded a reading of the 2025 Official Community Plan.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Only routine roles: moved a Handi-Transit financial report (2021) and second reading of the 2025 Official Community Plan.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

Moved the 2018 grant application for a new Handi-Transit van, calling it 'a very, very important part of the community'; on taxis, said the province gave too little time and more research was needed.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Seconded the 2018 Handi-Transit van grant application; per SteinbachOnline, said less regulation was best for competition between ride services and taxis.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; no record on this topic yet. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.
