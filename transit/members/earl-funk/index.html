Earl Funk: transit and accessible transportation

What did Earl Funk say and do about transit and accessible transportation in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

In short

As a councillor, Earl Funk's part in this record was routine: he moved the city's annual Handi-Transit financial reports at meetings in 2017 and 2018. As mayor since November 2018 he chaired the December 2021 vote that handed the service to Serving Seniors Inc. (the Pat Porter Active Living Centre), which he praised as a partnership that serves the people who use it. Each year he tables the provincial accessible-transit grant, which the city passes on to Pat Porter.

On general public transit he has been the most quoted sceptic. In June 2023 he told SteinbachOnline the city was doing its due diligence, but 'every time we talk, there is one common theme, it is very expensive.' At the 2025 Official Community Plan hearing he said 'we are the automobile city.' Reporting from the Manitoba municipalities convention, he described transit as a cost for the bigger cities and backed the push to restore 50/50 provincial operating funding. When the founder of KodaGo, a local ride-share app, asked for a partnership in August 2026, he said the city does not do that at this point and invited a letter. He and council did not take up an invitation to ride Winkler's on-demand van in July 2026, CBC reported.

“But we also realize that we are the automobile city and we do have to make sure that our vehicles still have a place on the road.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Answering a resident at the Official Community Plan hearing · Apr 1, 2025 · 0:46:15

2022–2026

Told SteinbachOnline in 2023 that transit 'is very expensive' and told CBC a study suggested Steinbach would need 20,000 to 25,000 residents. Called Steinbach 'the automobile city' at the 2025 plan hearing, backed the cities' call to restore 50/50 provincial transit funding, and told KodaGo in 2026 the city's only transport partnership is accessible transit.

Took part in 4 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Steady across eleven years: supportive of accessible transit as a partnership at a stable cost, sceptical of general transit on cost and car-dependence grounds, and welcoming of private services without a city role. He leads the new council, which inherits the same question.
