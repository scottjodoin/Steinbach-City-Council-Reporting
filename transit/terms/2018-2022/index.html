Transit · By term

2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk’s first council

What did Steinbach City Council say and do about transit and accessible transportation from November 2018 to October 2022?

In short

  • A quiet term. The council took office a week after Greyhound left Manitoba. General transit did not come up at a recorded meeting in these four years.
  • Handi-Transit changed hands. In December 2021 council handed the service to Serving Seniors Inc., which runs the Pat Porter Active Living Centre, under a grant agreement. Its condition was no significant change in cost.
  • Praised as a partnership. Councillors Zwaagstra and Siemens and the mayor called it a natural fit for a seniors’ organization. The vote was unanimous; the city’s grant in 2022 was $102,921.49.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2018–2021: routine items

Council kept accepting Handi-Transit’s yearly financial reports (moved by Councillor Bill Hiebert in 2019 and Councillor Jake Hiebert in 2021) and received the province’s grants, mostly $15,000 at a time. No one raised a bus to Winnipeg or local transit at a recorded meeting.

2021: Handi-Transit goes to Serving Seniors

After a closed study session, administration negotiated the Steinbach Accessible Transportation Services Agreement. The city grants money to Serving Seniors Inc., which runs the service from February 2022, keeping basic service levels with room to expand. Staff explained council’s condition: proceed only if it could be done without a significant change in the cost of the service.

“having this service delivered by a local nonprofit that is focused on serving the seniors population would be a long-term benefit to the service because of their connection to and awareness of the needs of seniors.”

Adam Thiessen, Corporate Services Manager · Dec 7, 2021 · 0:48:03

“I think this is a great opportunity here that that to have the service provided by an organization like Serving Seniors that already specializes in providing a variety of services to seniors in our community, and this becomes one of the services that they provide. It's a natural fit.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving the agreement · Dec 7, 2021 · 0:50:06

“I think it's a good partnership as well. It's a local nonprofit that serves seniors, as per their name…”

Councillor Jac Siemens, seconding · Dec 7, 2021 · 0:51:21

“I think this is a great example again of partnerships This is a city partnership with an organization in our community to provide great service for people that are actually using their service.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Dec 7, 2021 · 0:51:55

The agreement passed unanimously (R21-253). The service, renamed ACCESSible Transit, gave 3,853 rides in 2022, SteinbachOnline reported. The city still owns the vans; the agreement’s hours and eligibility terms are in the council package, which we did not review.

Where members differed

Every recorded vote on transit in this term was unanimous.

No recorded split votes on this topic in this period.
