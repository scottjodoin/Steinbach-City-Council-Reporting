Transit · By term

The story by term

How did Steinbach City Council handle transit and accessible transportation from one council to the next between December 2015 and November 2026, and what does the new council inherit?

In short

Two things stayed the same across all three councils. The city kept funding a door-to-door service for seniors and people with disabilities, first running it itself and then, from 2022, through a seniors’ organization at about the same cost. And it never took the next step toward transit for everyone else. The province advised planning for transit in 2018; the answer was “within the future.” When a community coalition brought survey results (2024) and an on-demand plan (2025), and a local ride-share founder asked for a partnership (2026), council listened, voiced support or invited a letter, and made no decision. Every recorded vote on the topic was unanimous.

2014–2018

Mayor Goertzen’s council

Handi-Transit run by the city; a van grant refused by the province; a taxi by-law declined; the province’s advice to plan for transit.

2022Pat Porter runs the service, renamed ACCESSible Transit, from February: 3,853 rides in the year. City grant $102,921.49.
2023The mayor tells SteinbachOnline the city is doing its due diligence on transit but “it is very expensive” (June). ACCESSible Transit gives 5,477 rides.
2024The South East Equity Coalition presents its transportation survey at an unrecorded session; members reject a bus loop and ask for costs (February). A developer at a hearing hopes for transit “one day soon” (March). Council accepts the new Official Community Plan’s recommendations (December).
2025The new plan adds that the city “could consider” public transportation; the mayor says “we are the automobile city” (April; final reading September). The mayor backs the cities’ call to restore 50/50 provincial transit funding (April). The first Active Transportation Plan covers walking and cycling (May). The coalition’s LinK Transit on-demand plan gets verbal support only (May).
2026City grant to ACCESSible Transit $105,000; the province pays about $30,000 (April). Staff list public transportation as an eligible use of a $200,000 provincial safety grant, but recommend a truck and cameras (July). Council tells CBC it needs “a viable plan”; the mayor and council skip a ride on Winkler’s van (July). KodaGo asks for a partnership; the mayor invites a letter (August).

2026–2030: the new council

The council elected on October 28, 2026 is the first change in membership since 2018. Michael Zwaagstra, council’s firmest voice for the car and the market on this topic, and Jac Siemens, who wanted planning that moves people and not only vehicles, did not run. Ron Falk, a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years (14 as chair), and Greg Carlos, a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association in his first elected office, are new. As a candidate, Greg Carlos rode Winkler’s on-demand van with coalition members on July 14, 2026, CBC reported. Susan Penner, who asked in 2024 for a cost and funding analysis, is Deputy Mayor.

What the new council inherits:

  • Two requests without an answer. The South East Equity Coalition’s LinK Transit plan (four on-demand vans run by Pat Porter, asking the city for $6,000 in seed money, about $96,000 a year and help with grants) and KodaGo’s request for a partnership on medical and airport trips.
  • A plan policy that only says “could consider.” No transit study has been commissioned since the province’s 2018 advice, and the federal planning grant that peers used closed in March 2026.
  • ACCESSible Transit’s agreement and grant, $105,000 in 2026, with rides up about 40% from 2022 to 2023.
  • The $200,000 provincial safety grant, where public transportation was an eligible use; its final use has not been made public.

The new council’s inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026 had no transit items.
