Transit · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

What did Steinbach City Council say and do about transit and accessible transportation from December 2015 to October 2018?

In short

  • Handi-Transit was the city’s own service. Council accepted its financial reports and provincial grants each year without debate.
  • A new van, refused. Council applied in February 2018 for half the cost of a new Handi-Transit van; the province said no in August.
  • Taxis left unregulated. When the province handed taxi regulation to cities, Steinbach Taxi asked for a by-law. Councillor Zwaagstra called it “a request to limit competition,” and in May council decided against a by-law (SteinbachOnline).
  • Advice to plan for transit, deferred. In July 2018 the province advised the city to “begin planning for a transit system now.” Staff said a transportation plan would come “within the future.” Three months later Greyhound left Manitoba.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr.

What happened

2016–2017: a routine service

The city ran Handi-Transit for residents with mobility disabilities and seniors. Each year council accepted the audited financial report the province requires for cost-sharing, and received the provincial grant as information. None of these items was debated.

2018: a van, taxis and a plan

In February council applied to the province’s Small Communities Transportation Fund for half the cost of a new van, up to $50,000, with the city to pay the rest in 2019:

“It's just the handy van is a very, very important part of the community, and I think it's important that we keep equipment in very good condition, so I think it's a good idea to update it.”

Councillor John Fehr, moving the application · Feb 20, 2018 · 0:32:36

The province turned it down in August. Whether the city bought the van on its own is not in the minutes.

The same night, Steinbach Taxi’s president and a consultant asked council for a by-law under the province’s new vehicles-for-hire law, which since February 28 left taxi rules to each city. They wanted the company’s 15 licences recognized and illegal ride groups watched, and offered to expand handi-transit service:

“You know, in the case of the City of Steinbach, which does not have public transport, the taxi service provides an important service.”

S.M., consultant for Steinbach Taxi · Feb 20, 2018 · 0:49:53

“It sounds like this is a request to limit competition. Is that not the case?”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Feb 20, 2018 · 0:51:00

“And the province lately, it seems that they give us very little time to react.”

Councillor John Fehr · Feb 20, 2018 · 0:55:22

“What we will do, of course, as council, we'll ask administration to give us a small report.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Feb 20, 2018 · 1:00:56

In May, SteinbachOnline reported, council adopted administration’s recommendation not to make a by-law, on Councillor Zwaagstra’s motion and unanimously, at a committee meeting that was not recorded. He said a by-law would only “limit competition”; Deputy Mayor Cari Penner said less regulation was best for competition between ride services and taxis.

In July, at the hearing on the new Official Community Plan, which expected 25,000 people within ten years, the province’s planners gave this advice:

“Community and Regional Planning recommends that Steinbach begin planning for a transit system now and incorporate design considerations for a transit system.”

S.W., regional manager, Province of Manitoba Community and Regional Planning · Jul 17, 2018 · 0:45:45

“In regards to transportation, we note that their comments are definitely something we should be looking into, and within the future, we do want to take on a transportation plan. But that's not something we would be doing immediately.”

Lacey Gaudet, City Planner · Jul 17, 2018 · 0:56:06

The plan passed third reading unanimously on October 16, 2018, with no transit policy that we found. Greyhound’s Winnipeg–Steinbach route ended with its last day in Manitoba on October 31, 2018; no scheduled bus has replaced it.

Where members differed

Every recorded vote on transit in this term was unanimous. The differences were in what members said about taxis.

No recorded split votes on this topic in this period.
