Transit · By term

2022–2026: Mayor Earl Funk’s second council

What did Steinbach City Council say and do about transit and accessible transportation from November 2022 to October 2026?

In short

  • Transit became a public question. A community coalition surveyed residents (2024) and drafted an on-demand van plan (2025). A developer hoped for transit “one day soon,” and a local ride-share founder asked for a partnership (2026).
  • The key conversations were off the record. Members met the coalition at unrecorded sessions. Per SteinbachOnline, they rejected a bus loop, asked for costs and gave the plan verbal support, but made no decision.
  • Plans, but not a transit plan. The 2025 Official Community Plan says the city “could consider” public transportation, and the first Active Transportation Plan covers walking and cycling. No study was commissioned.
  • “The automobile city.” The mayor and Councillor Zwaagstra stressed that most people drive; Councillor Siemens said planning should move people, not only vehicles.
  • Accessible transit held steady at a city grant of $105,000 (2026) plus about $30,000 from the province.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2023–2024: “very expensive,” and a coalition’s survey

In June 2023 the mayor told SteinbachOnline the city was doing its due diligence on transit: “Every time we talk, there is one common theme, it is very expensive.” He told CBC a study suggested Steinbach would need 20,000 to 25,000 residents for transit to work; that study was not identified.

In February 2024 the South East Equity Coalition presented a survey of 751 people, mostly clients of its member agencies: 32% walk, 23% get rides from others and 12% take taxis. The session was not recorded. SteinbachOnline reported that Councillor Zwaagstra said a fixed loop “would be very expensive and you run the risk of having empty buses,” and that the city should not compete with taxis. Councillor Susan Penner wanted “the cost analysis, how this would be funded, what grants are available.” Councillor Damian Penner said exploring alternatives was “something that we have an obligation to start looking at exploring.”

“This also contributes to the feasibility, attainability, efficiency, and sustainability of public transit, which we hope to see one day soon.”

L.W., Kingdom Home Building, at an infill-housing hearing · Mar 19, 2024 · 0:20:54

2025: “could consider,” and “the automobile city”

“we added a policy statement outlining the ways that the city could consider the future development of a public transportation system.”

City’s planning consultant (WSP), presenting the Official Community Plan · Apr 1, 2025 · 0:21:06

“Because also, how will seniors get around if they can't use their automobiles?”

A.B., resident · Apr 1, 2025 · 0:41:36

“But we also realize that we are the automobile city and we do have to make sure that our vehicles still have a place on the road.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Apr 1, 2025 · 0:46:15

Two weeks later the mayor reported that the cities in the Manitoba municipalities association would press the province to restore 50/50 operating funding for transit, which he described as a cost for “a couple of the bigger cities.” In May council accepted the first Active Transportation Plan:

“Our planning has always been in our community, like many communities, is how do we move the vehicles? It's not how do we move people outside of vehicles.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, moving the plan · May 6, 2025 · 0:58:35

“The reality is that most people in Steinbach do drive vehicles, particularly in the winter months, and we're not going to do anything to impede that.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · May 6, 2025 · 1:03:48

A week later, at another unrecorded session, the coalition presented “LinK Transit”: four on-demand vans run by the Pat Porter Active Living Centre, about $957,000 to start including vehicles and storage. It asked the city for an endorsement, $6,000 in seed money, about $96,000 a year toward operations and help applying for grants. The city gave verbal support only, SteinbachOnline reported, and a later budget request for vans was not advanced.

2026: money passed through, and a letter invited

“Yep, that's correct. Yeah, they pay us in 2 installments every year for a total of $30,000.”

Adam Thiessen, Senior Manager, Corporate Services, on the provincial grant passed to Pat Porter · Apr 21, 2026 · 1:01:05

In July staff presented an unexpected $200,000 provincial safety grant and listed public transportation among the eligible uses, but recommended a second community safety officer truck ($65,000) and security cameras ($135,000); council deferred it. The same month council told CBC it needs “a viable plan” before acting. The mayor and council were invited to ride Winkler’s on-demand van and did not attend.

In August the founder of KodaGo, a ride-share app built in Steinbach, told council why he started it and asked for a partnership on medical appointments and airport trips:

“Okay, can I get a bus to Steinbach? No bus to Steinbach. Okay, can I get— what about Uber? Yeah, well, you can get to Steinbach, but you can't get back.”

J.O., KodaGo founder · Aug 4, 2026 · 1:02:18

“right now we don't have that partnership with any local transportation agency. businesses other than our, I guess, our Handy Transit, accessible transit, which is subsidized by the province”

Mayor Earl Funk · Aug 4, 2026 · 1:14:12

A Councillor Penner, most likely Damian Penner, praised the app as private innovation that “is often far superior to public spent dollars,” while adding that the city may look at public transit in the future. The mayor invited a letter. No letter, motion or partnership appears in the record through November 3, 2026.

Where members differed

Every recorded vote on transit in this term was unanimous. The differences were in what members said: the mayor and Councillor Zwaagstra stressed cars, cost and private business; Councillor Siemens pointed to moving people; Councillors Susan and Damian Penner asked for costs and for exploring alternatives.

No recorded split votes on this topic in this period.
