Every decision and debate on transit

What has Steinbach City Council decided, debated and promised about transit and accessible transportation since December 2015, and how did each item end?

In short

Each row is one decision, debate, promise or request on the topic, from the minutes and the meeting videos. Filter by term, year, theme, council member or result. “No public result” means we found no public decision or follow-up; some work happens in closed study sessions and committees.

YearDecision or debateResult
2016Provincial Mobility Disadvantaged Transportation grants received every year (2016-2026)Every year council receives, as information, letters and payments from the Province of Manitoba's Mobility Disadvantaged Transportation Program (MDTP), which cost-shares the city's handi-transit / ACCESSible Transit service. Since the service was handed to the Pat Porter Active Living Centre in 2022, the city forwards the money to the centre.Done Routine: received as information at roughly two meetings a year, never debated. In April 2026 staff confirmed the province pays about $30,000 a year in two instalments, passed through to Pat Porter.vote: received as information
2017–21Annual Handi-Transit financial reports accepted (2017-2021)While the city ran Handi-Transit itself, council accepted an audited Handi-Transit financial report each year (required for provincial cost-sharing). The motions passed without discussion.Done Accepted unanimously each time: 2017-03-07 (moved by Councillor Earl Funk, per transcript); R18-103 (May 1, 2018); R19-114 (May 7, 2019); R21-162 (Jul 20, 2021). No report found for the 2019 year in the minutes searched. After 2022 the service is a grant to Serving Seniors Inc.R18-103; R19-114; R21-162 · vote: unanimous
2018City applies for a provincial grant to replace a Handi-Transit van; province says noCouncil asked the province's Small Communities Transportation Fund for 50% of the cost of a new Handi Van (up to $50,000), with the city funding the rest in 2019. In August 2018 the province told the city it would not receive funding for the van (or for the sidewalk program applied for the same night).Partly Application approved unanimously (R18-039); the province declined it (correspondence received Aug 7, 2018). Whether the city bought the van on its own was not found in the minutes.R18-039 · vote: unanimous
2018Steinbach Taxi asks council to regulate taxis under the new Vehicles for Hire Act; council decides against a by-lawAfter the province's Bill 30 (Vehicles for Hire Act) handed taxi regulation to municipalities on Feb 28, 2018, Steinbach Taxi's president (P.J.) and a consultant (S.M.) asked council for a by-law that would recognise its 15 provincially issued licences, watch for illegal ride-sharing, and partner with the company; they also offered to expand handi-transit services. Councillor Zwaagstra asked whether this was a request to limit competition; Councillor Fehr said the province gave too little time and more research was needed. Mayor Goertzen asked administration for a report.Declined Mayor Goertzen referred the request to administration. Per SteinbachOnline (May 2018), council then adopted administration's recommendation not to create a vehicle-for-hire by-law, on a motion by Councillor Zwaagstra, unanimously, at a committee meeting that is not in the regular minutes or recordings; Zwaagstra said a by-law would only limit competition. No by-law has been made since. Ride services are licensed as ordinary businesses, e.g. 'Tuk Tuk Taxi - Ride Service' business licence approved Nov 18, 2025 (R25-226, S. Penner / B. Hiebert).vote: unanimous (committee, per news)
2018Province advises Steinbach to "begin planning for a transit system now" in its 2018 Official Community PlanAt the July 17, 2018 public hearing on the new Official Community Plan (By-law 2099), the province's Community and Regional Planning office (regional manager S.W.) recommended that Steinbach start planning for transit and make future development transit-supportive, noting the OCP expected 25,000 people in 10 years. City Planner Lacey Gaudet replied that a transportation plan was wanted 'within the future' but not immediately.Partly The 2018 OCP was adopted (third reading R18-242, Oct 16, 2018) without a found transit policy. The next OCP (By-law 2244, 2025) added a policy (4.2.2.13) on future consideration/studies of the feasibility of public transportation, and the city's first Active Transportation Plan was accepted May 6, 2025. No transit plan or study has been commissioned.R18-242 (OCP third reading) · vote: unanimous
2021Handi-Transit handed to Serving Seniors Inc. (Pat Porter Active Living Centre) under a grant agreementAfter a study session, administration negotiated the 'Steinbach Accessible Transportation Services Agreement': the city grants funding to Serving Seniors Inc., which runs the service (now ACCESSible Transit) from early 2022, keeping basic service levels with room to expand. Council's condition was no significant change in cost. Councillors Zwaagstra and Siemens and Mayor Funk praised it as a 'natural fit' and a true partnership.Done Approved unanimously (R21-253). Serving Seniors / Pat Porter has run the service since 2022; the city's grant was $102,921.49 in 2022 (2022 grant summary, R23-039) and $105,000 in the 2026 financial plan.R21-253 · vote: unanimous
2023Mayor says the city is doing "due diligence" on transit but "it is very expensive" (news)In June 2023 Mayor Funk told SteinbachOnline the city was doing its due diligence on public transit, adding: 'Every time we talk, there is one common theme, it is very expensive.' This was not at a recorded council meeting.No public result No study, report or budget item on general transit followed at any recorded meeting.
2024South East Equity Coalition presents transit survey; council rejects a bus loop and asks for costs (unrecorded session)On Feb 13, 2024, outside a recorded regular meeting, the South East Equity Coalition presented survey results on transportation needs. Per SteinbachOnline, Councillor Zwaagstra said a fixed loop 'would be very expensive and you run the risk of having empty buses' and that the city should not compete with taxis; Councillor Susan Penner agreed a bus loop would not work and wanted 'the cost analysis, how this would be funded, what grants are available'; Councillor Damian Penner said exploring alternatives was 'something that we have an obligation to start looking at exploring'.No public result No motion, study or budget item followed; the coalition went on to draft its own on-demand plan (May 2025).
2024At an infill-housing hearing, the applicant and a resident link density to future public transitAt the Mar 19, 2024 public hearing on a Kingdom Home Building infill project near the hospital, the applicant's representative (L.W.) said infill supports the feasibility of public transit, 'which we hope to see one day soon'; a resident (H.W.) argued density increases access to public transportation and reduces traffic. Council did not discuss transit.No public result Transit was raised only as a supporting argument; no council response on transit.vote: not applicable
2024–25New Official Community Plan adds a policy that the city "could consider" public transportationThe 2025 Official Community Plan (By-law 2244) added policy 4.2.2.13 on future consideration/studies of the feasibility of public transportation; the province welcomed the 'mention of public transportation'. At the Apr 1, 2025 hearing a resident (A.B.) asked how seniors would get around without cars; Mayor Funk replied that active transportation was welcome but 'we are the automobile city'. Councillor Siemens listed 'just starting to understand what public transportation looks like' among the plan's themes.Partly Adopted unanimously: recommendations accepted Dec 3, 2024 (R24-234), first and second reading Apr 1, 2025 (R25-069, R25-070), third reading Sep 16, 2025 (R25-184). The policy is permissive ('could consider'); no feasibility study has been commissioned.R24-234; R25-069; R25-070; R25-184 · vote: unanimous
2025Mayor reports Cities Caucus push to restore 50/50 provincial transit operating fundingReporting from the Association of Manitoba Municipalities convention, Mayor Funk said public transit is 'very dear to a couple of the bigger cities' (Brandon, Winnipeg) and that the urban centres would advocate a return to 50/50 provincial operating funding because 'the cost is killing the communities that are providing transit'.No public result Advocacy item; no council motion. Steinbach itself runs no general transit that would benefit.vote: not applicable
2025First Active Transportation Plan accepted; councillors split on moving people beyond carsCouncil accepted the city's first consultant-prepared Active Transportation Plan (walking, cycling, pathways; about $11 million in total over the long term). Councillor Siemens said the city's planning had always been about moving vehicles, not people; Councillor Zwaagstra said active transportation must not hinder vehicle traffic. In a later parking debate (Nov 18, 2025) Zwaagstra said most Steinbach residents, even on Main Street, have cars. The plan does not cover public transit.Done Accepted unanimously (R25-096) as a guide; not a transit plan.R25-096 · vote: unanimous
2025Coalition's "LinK Transit" on-demand van plan gets verbal support only (news)On May 13, 2025 (not a recorded regular meeting) the South East Equity Coalition presented 'LinK Transit', an on-demand service with four vans run by the Pat Porter Active Living Centre, asking for $6,000 in seed money, an endorsement and help with grants. Per SteinbachOnline the city gave verbal support only; a later budget request for vans was not advanced, and in July 2026 council told CBC it needs 'a viable plan' before acting. The mayor and council were invited to ride Winkler's on-demand van and did not attend.No public result No motion, endorsement, seed funding or grant application found at any recorded meeting through Nov 3, 2026.
2026Public transportation listed as an eligible use of a $200,000 provincial safety grant; staff recommend cameras and a CSO truckPresenting an unexpected $200,000 provincial public safety grant, staff listed 'public transportation' among Community Safety and Well-Being plan priorities council could support, but recommended $65,000 for a second CSO truck and $135,000 for security cameras. Council deferred the item for more discussion.No public result Deferred to a later committee/special meeting (Aug 11 discussed); no transit allocation proposed; final decision not found in minutes searched.vote: deferred
2026KodaGo, a Steinbach-built ride-share app, asks the city to partner; mayor invites a letterKodaGo's founder (J.O.) described starting the app because there was no bus or return Uber to Steinbach; with 246 users it serves southeast Manitoba and asked for a city partnership focused on medical appointments and airport trips. A Councillor Penner praised private innovation as 'often far superior to public spent dollars' while leaving public transit open for the future; Mayor Funk said a partnership would go beyond a letter of support, that the city partners only with its accessible transit service, and asked for a letter.No public result No letter, motion or partnership appears in transcripts or minutes through Nov 3, 2026.vote: no motion; letter invited
