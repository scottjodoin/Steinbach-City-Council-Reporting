Steinbach City Council · Dec 2015 – Nov 2026 · Topic: Transit

Can Steinbach have public transit?

Could a city of about 20,000 like Steinbach realistically run public transit? What has Steinbach City Council done about transit and accessible transportation since December 2015, what reasons has it given for not going further, how did each council member act, and how do similar cities manage it?

Written for residents. Start with the summary, then choose what to read. Last updated Nov 9, 2026.

Executive summary

Steinbach has funded a door-to-door service for seniors and people with disabilities throughout these eleven years, but it has never studied, budgeted for or voted on transit for everyone else.

  • Cities this size do run transit, and some have tried and stopped. Winkler (13,745 people in 2021) has run a one-van on-demand service since November 2024. It costs about $365,000 a year, carries about 900 trips a month, and began as a pilot paid mostly by the Federation of Canadian Municipalities. Selkirk (10,504) has run a loop bus since 2011. Portage la Prairie studied transit and found every option “not financially feasible” (2022), and Thompson suspended its buses in 2024.
  • Steinbach’s one service is ACCESSible Transit. In December 2021 council handed the city’s Handi-Transit to Serving Seniors Inc. (the Pat Porter Active Living Centre), on condition there be no significant change in cost. The city’s grant was $105,000 in 2026, and the province adds about $30,000 a year. Rides grew from 3,853 in 2022 to 5,477 in 2023.
  • Advice to plan was put off. In 2018 the province advised Steinbach to “begin planning for a transit system now”; city staff answered that a transportation plan would come “within the future.” The 2025 Official Community Plan says only that the city “could consider” transit. No city transit study was commissioned in eleven years, and there has been no intercity bus since Greyhound left in 2018.
  • Council’s reasons are cost and “empty buses,” Steinbach as “the automobile city” (0.2% of workers commuted by transit in 2021), and a preference for private businesses. Council declined a taxi by-law in 2018 as something that would “limit competition,” and in 2026 invited a local ride-share app to write a letter. It also wants “a viable plan” first, but has not commissioned one.
  • No vote ever divided council, so the differences between members show up in what they said. Jac Siemens wanted planning that moves people, not only vehicles. Michael Zwaagstra spoke most for the car and the market. Damian Penner said council had “an obligation to start looking.” Susan Penner asked for a cost analysis. On the new council, Greg Carlos rode Winkler’s van as a candidate.

The story in three councils

Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council kept Handi-Transit running, left taxis unregulated and heard the province’s advice to plan for transit. Mayor Earl Funk’s first council handed Handi-Transit to a seniors’ organization at the same cost. His second heard a community coalition’s on-demand plan and a ride-share founder’s request, and wrote transit into the city’s plan only as something it “could consider.”

About this report

Quotes are verbatim from automatic transcripts and link to the meeting videos. Council members, the mayor and staff are named; residents and delegations are identified by initials. Several of council’s most important transit discussions happened at unrecorded sessions and are quoted from news reports, marked as such. The analysis and plain-language summaries are ours.

Methods, coverage and what we couldn’t confirm
  • Transcripts. Meeting quotes come from automatic transcripts of the official videos and are left as transcribed, including repeated words and false starts. The one exception is the city’s name, which the transcription often misspells and which quotes show correctly as Steinbach. Ellipses mark trimmed text; no sentence breaks were added. Each quote links to the start of the speaker’s turn in the video (one, from the mayor’s April 15, 2025 report, links to the moment the remark begins, since his turn starts at the opening of the video); check there before quoting elsewhere.
  • Speakers and names. Every quote shown under a name is from a speaker identified with high confidence. Speakers were matched to names with AI assistance, from how they were addressed in each meeting and from the minutes. Council members, the mayor and city staff are named in full; the city’s planning consultant is shown by firm (WSP). Residents, delegations and other members of the public, including a provincial planner who presented at a hearing, are identified only by initials. Organizations and businesses are named. One exception is described, not quoted: on August 4, 2026 the remarks praising “private innovation” were made by a Councillor Penner, most likely Damian Penner, but the recording and the minutes disagree on which one. Where we trim a remark, leave out a personal passage, or identify a speaker by initials, the full remarks can be watched at the linked moment in the official meeting video.
  • Unrecorded sessions. Council discussed transit with the South East Equity Coalition on February 13, 2024 and May 13, 2025, outside recorded regular meetings, and decided against a taxi by-law at a committee meeting in May 2018. What members said there comes from SteinbachOnline and CBC, not video, and is not shown as a block quote.
  • Coverage. Every council meeting from December 15, 2015 to November 3, 2026 with a recording or minutes was searched: transcripts of 243 meeting videos and minutes from January 2018 on (the city’s older minutes are not online). Not covered: the first 2015 videos (not yet transcribed) and four videos that failed to download (April 4 and October 17, 2017; September 15, 2020; July 6, 2021), where the minutes were used if they exist.
  • What counts. 15 items: Handi-Transit and ACCESSible Transit (agreements, grants, vehicles, reports), taxis and ride-hailing, proposals for public or on-demand transit, transit in the city’s plans, and transit funding. Classifying how an item ended (done, partly, no public result, declined) and tagging council’s reasons is our judgment; the reasons are recorded for each item in the data.
  • Figures. The 2026 grant of $105,000 is from the 2026 Financial Plan presentation; the 2022 grant ($102,921.49) is from the minutes. Provincial grants are from the minutes and staff’s statement on April 21, 2026. Ridership is from SteinbachOnline (the 2023 figure from a search summary of its article). Census figures are from Statistics Canada’s 2021 profile, taken during the pandemic. Peer figures are on How Steinbach compares, with their years.
  • What we couldn’t confirm. ACCESSible Transit’s current hours and fares, and its ridership for 2024 and 2025; city grants before 2022; whether the city replaced the Handi-Transit van in 2019 after the province declined to help; the study the mayor cited in 2023 saying Steinbach would need 20,000 to 25,000 residents; how the $200,000 provincial safety grant was finally spent; and whether TappCar or Uber operate in Steinbach today.
  • Corrections to our October 2026 page. The coalition’s “about $96,000 a year” was its request to the city, not the plan’s operating cost, so we removed our comparison with Winkler’s cost. The federal transit planning grant we pointed to (up to $50,000) required projects to finish by March 31, 2026 and is effectively closed. Winkler’s population is its 2021 census figure, 13,745. We removed a Cochrane cost whose source was a 2018 report, and Innisfil, Cobourg and Brandon-pilot figures we could not re-check. The 2018 taxi by-law decision was added from SteinbachOnline.
  • Data. The data downloads list every item, every council member’s part in it, and every quote with its timestamp and link. Quotes in the downloads come only from council members and staff identified with high confidence. Every quote was checked word for word against its transcript.
  • Council change. The council elected in October 2026 held its inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026. Quotes are from members serving at the time, some of whom are no longer on council. Election results are from SteinbachOnline (unofficial counts).
