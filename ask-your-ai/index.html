Research it yourself

Ask your own AI about council

You don't have to rely on our reports. Here are two ways to put your own questions about Steinbach City Council to an AI assistant, with instructions that keep the answers fair and backed by evidence you can check.

Last updated Nov 14, 2026.

Which option?

  • Option 1: Ask a chat assistant. Quick and easy. Paste a prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot. Best for questions our reports already cover.
  • Option 2: Run an AI agent on the full transcripts. Takes a little setup. Your AI reads every meeting from November 2022 on, line by line. Best for questions our reports don't cover.

Option 1: Ask a chat assistant

  1. Open an AI chat assistant that can browse the web (most can; some need web search turned on).
  2. Copy the prompt below, paste it in, and replace the last line with your question.
  3. Open the video links in the answer for anything that matters to you.
I have a question about Steinbach City Council (Manitoba). Please research it using these sources:
- https://steinbachcitycouncilreporting.ca/llms.txt (a guide to every report on the site)
- https://steinbachcitycouncilreporting.ca/ (the reports themselves)
- https://github.com/scottjodoin/Steinbach-City-Council-Transcripts (full meeting transcripts, if the reports don't cover my question)

How to answer:
1. Be neutral. Describe what council members said and did without guessing at motives or taking sides. Where people disagreed, give each side's strongest points in their own words.
2. Back every point with specific evidence: who said it, the meeting date, a short exact quote, and the link to that moment in the video.
3. For votes and decisions, rely on the city's minutes as cited by the site, not on your own reading of a transcript.
4. Keep three things clearly apart: what the record shows, what the site's analysis says, and your own conclusions.
5. If you can't open these sources, or they don't answer my question, say so plainly. Don't fill gaps from memory or guesswork, and tell me what you could not confirm.
6. Residents are not named in these sources. Don't try to identify them.

My question: [type your question here]

Example questions: “What reasons has council given for not adding more sidewalks in new neighbourhoods?” · “What has council said about a Main Street bypass since 2015?” · “What did each council member say about the 2025 budget?”

Option 2: Run an AI agent on the full transcripts

The speaker-labelled transcripts of every council meeting from November 2022 to November 2026 are free to download, along with the speaker maps that show who each voice is and why. An AI coding agent (one that can read files and run small programs on your computer, such as Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor or GitHub Copilot in agent mode) can search all of them to answer your question.

  1. Download the transcripts. Go to the transcripts on GitHub, press Code, then Download ZIP, and unzip it. (If you use git: git clone https://github.com/scottjodoin/Steinbach-City-Council-Transcripts.git.)
  2. Open the folder in your coding agent.
  3. Paste the ground rules below, then your question.
You are helping me research Steinbach City Council meetings using the transcripts in this folder.
Read README.md, ACCURACY.md and AGENT_GUIDE.md first. Then follow these rules:

1. Be neutral. Report what was said and decided. Don't guess at motives, don't take sides, and when people disagreed, give each side's strongest points in their own words.
2. Back every point with specific evidence: the exact quote, the speaker as labelled, the meeting date, and the line's video_url so I can watch it.
3. Only say a named person said something when the line's confidence is "high". For "medium", say "probably". Never attribute "low" or unidentified lines to anyone.
4. Votes, motions and decisions: check them in the city's minutes (minutes_url in meetings.csv where filled in, otherwise steinbach.ca). Transcripts do not record votes reliably.
5. Residents appear as "Resident 1", "Resident 2" and so on. Never try to work out who they are.
6. Say what you could not find or confirm. Not finding something is not proof it was never said: earlier years are not in this folder yet, and machine transcription misses some words.
7. Don't use line_type, line counts or speaking time to rank or compare people.
8. Keep quotes and your own summary clearly separate.

My question: [type your question here]

The folder's agent guide describes the files, every data field, and a short Python example.

Things to keep in mind

  • AI assistants make mistakes. They can misread a source, mix up two meetings or invent a quote. The video links are there so you can check, and the video and the city's minutes are the official record.
  • The transcripts are machine-made. See Data accuracy for how accurate the words and the speaker names are.
  • Your questions go to your AI provider, not to us. Their privacy terms apply.
  • Found something wrong in our reports or transcripts? Email steinbachcitycouncilreporting@gmail.com.
