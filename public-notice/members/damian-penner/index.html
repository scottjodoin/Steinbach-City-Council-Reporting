Damian Penner: public hearings and residents' objections

What did Damian Penner say and do when residents objected at public hearings on rezonings, variances and plan changes between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Damian Penner usually voted with the majority on contested hearings, but he says openly how neighbours' turnout affects him. He voted against the 32-unit Home Street rezoning in 2024 'with the neighbours coming out', cited neighbours' objections when denying a truck yard's barbed-wire variance and a side yard on Coral Crescent, and cited their absence when approving others. At Third Street in 2023 he argued that due diligence belongs before the hearing and that keeping residents waiting two months would be unfair.

He also decides on principle when it cuts the other way: he backed the Home Street fourth storey because an underground level should not count as a storey, moved the 2020 Clearspring Greens levy while pointing objectors to the formal routes to stop it, and moved 48 units on McKenzie Avenue in 2026 as a balancing act whose benefit to the city 'always is large'. He voted to deny 330 McKenzie Avenue.

“It's a balancing act that we have on council. And it's a balancing act that we should always have on council of the concerns of the citizens and the benefit of the city. With this, I feel like the benefit to the city always is large.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Moving to approve 48 units at 492–506 McKenzie Avenue over a petition · Apr 7, 2026 · 1:18:37

Term by term

2018–2022

Moved the Clearspring Greens local improvement (2020) and told objectors they could still stop it through a Municipal Board hearing or a two-thirds objection. Voted for 44 First Street. Seconded the 2021 Coral Crescent denial, citing the neighbour's letter, and moved to refuse first reading of the industrial-park school rezoning, saying it was a planning question rather than property rights. Moved to table the Murano Cove variance for a fire-safety report (2022), and voted for the 6 First Street plan change while noting 'considerable objections' on traffic.

Took part in 16 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Argued against adjourning Third Street for the developer (2023). Voted against Home Street because the neighbours came out (2024), but backed its fourth storey on principle (2025). Cited objections in denying the truck yard's barbed-wire variance and their absence in approving 600 Main Street. Moved the 492–506 McKenzie Avenue approval (2026) and voted to deny 330 McKenzie Avenue.

Took part in 18 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A member who names turnout as a factor and has twice voted with large neighbourhood opposition, while holding to principles (planning over property rights, consistent definitions, housing need) that sometimes outweigh it.
