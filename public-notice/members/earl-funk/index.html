Public notice and having a say · Council member

Earl Funk: public hearings and residents' objections

What did Earl Funk say and do when residents objected at public hearings on rezonings, variances and plan changes between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

In short

As a councillor, Earl Funk sided with objecting neighbours on the largest fight of 2018: he voted to deny 44 townhouses on Maplewood Street, saying council needed to defend the residents who were there, and voted against closing part of a public reserve that two Harmony Lane neighbours had bought next to. He also opposed the 2017 golf course land deal, which was decided without a chance for public comment.

As mayor since November 2018 he has chaired nearly every public hearing, asking three times for comments, questions or objections and letting residents finish even after an applicant withdrew. He has voted with objectors on several heavily opposed infill projects (205 McKenzie Avenue, Third Street, Hanover Street, 330 McKenzie Avenue) and against them on others, casting the deciding vote for 32 units on Home Street. He says a hearing is where 'your voice matters' but not a vote count, keeps speakers to the question before council, and conceded in 2025 that the city 'could try and do a better job' of telling residents about its new plan.

“We basically vote on it by a case-by-case basis, and that's why we have the public hearing so that we can hear from the public and we can hear from you, the community and the neighborhood. And if there's objection, this is the time to talk about it. So this is where your voice matters, and that's why we're having this.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Explaining public hearings to a resident at the 189 Hanover Street hearing, which council then denied · Mar 18, 2025 · 0:23:23

Term by term

2014–2018

Moved second reading of the 24 Ellis Avenue six-plex with the fence and height conditions neighbours wanted (2017), and reassured neighbours of a seniors' building that seniors are quiet neighbours (2016). Voted against the 2017 golf course land deal and asked when closed matters could be made public. In 2018 he voted to deny the Maplewood Street townhouses after 49 letters ('we need to defend them'), backed a front-yard variance on First Street that the majority refused, and voted against closing the Harmony Lane public reserve, which passed 4–3.

Took part in 11 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Elected mayor in 2018. He chaired every hearing, thanked neighbours for coming ('without the neighborhood, we don't know what everyone's thinking'), credited developers who consulted the block (Maplewood 2020) and voted with the majority on every contested planning hearing in 2019–2021. He argued the province's Bill 48 would take power away from neighbourhoods. He kept speakers to the narrow question: he told a business owner raising access at a 2021 setback hearing he did not want to 'waste any more council time', and told a resident at a 2021 levy hearing it was 'about the debt issue' and not the design. He held third readings when objections might reach the Municipal Board, and approved the Clearspring Greens reset after 78 owners objected.

Took part in 16 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Voted against the 205 McKenzie Avenue fourplex at his first meeting of the term, and with objectors on Third Street (2023), the Tower Avenue truck yard (2024), 189 Hanover Street (2025) and 330 McKenzie Avenue (2026), judging whether a block was 'ready to transition'. He cast the deciding vote for 32 units on Home Street, saying people outside the area have experience worth hearing, and voted for its fourth storey in 2025. He said a majority against 'doesn't mean we won't vote for it', admitted the city could communicate its new plan better, turned flooded residents to the delegation route in 2025, and kept the 2026 Stone Bridge hearing to height and parking when residents raised sewer backups.

Took part in 18 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From a councillor who voted to 'defend' objecting residents to a mayor who hears everyone and decides case by case: with neighbours against mid-block infill on established streets, and for density downtown, near the hospital and for seniors.
