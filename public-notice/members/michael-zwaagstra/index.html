Michael Zwaagstra: public hearings and residents' objections

What did Michael Zwaagstra say and do when residents objected at public hearings on rezonings, variances and plan changes between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Michael Zwaagstra was council's most active voice at public hearings for a decade. He moved most of the denials that followed large neighbourhood opposition (Maplewood Street 2018, Hanover Street 2019, Ellice Avenue 2020, Third Street 2023, the Tower Avenue truck yard 2024) and said plainly that hearings should make a difference, that developers should consult before coming to council, and that the people council most wants to hear are those who live nearby. In 2016–2017 he turned neighbours' specific requests into conditions (fences, access, a height cap), and in 2017 he objected that the public had no chance to comment on the golf course land deal.

He also moved most approvals over objections, citing staff advice, the community plan, housing need and the rule of law of a properly noticed hearing, and argued that a small turnout may not represent a neighbourhood. He welcomed the 2019 provincial change that stopped one or two objectors from sending a rezoning to the Municipal Board, while opposing developer appeals in 2020 as taking decisions away from neighbourhoods. In his last term he set the bar at compliance: projects needing no variances should be approved, and being heard is not the same as winning.

“public hearings do matter. It actually does make a difference when people come to these hearings.… I think the message is also very clear that if you're looking to develop in an established neighbourhood, it would be wise to consult with neighbours, with the neighbourhood prior to coming to a public hearing.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Moving to deny the 32-unit Third Street rezoning after 13 neighbours objected · Aug 1, 2023 · 1:08:42

Term by term

2014–2018

Added objectors' requests as conditions: a fence and Woodhaven access for a seniors' building (2016) and a fence and 1.5-storey cap at 24 Ellis Avenue (2017). Questioned rezoning Bridge Park with no plan but moved it anyway, warning that even one objection can delay a project by months, and criticized objectors who dropped out before the Municipal Board. Moved to deny parking cuts at 295 Lowen Boulevard (2016), voted to deny 80 Brant Street (2017), and said a closed-meeting motion to hear the public on the golf course land deal was defeated 4–3. In 2018 he moved the Maplewood denial ('we have to listen to neighbors') and seconded the funeral home denial.

Took part in 28 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Moved the denials of the Hanover Street apartment (2019, 'this is why public hearings matter') and the Ellice Avenue six-plex (2020), and the approval of 44 First Street over six speakers, saying a small turnout may not represent the area. Welcomed the 2019 objection threshold as 'good'; opposed Bill 48's developer appeals. On Clearspring Greens he argued an open community session would not reach consensus. In 2021–2022 he moved most contested approvals (Wyndham Estate, 115 First Street, 6 First Street, the Days Inn), citing staff advice, the plan and the rule of law, moved the one 2021 denial, and argued a hearing on the industrial-park school would change nothing.

Took part in 37 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Moved the denials of Third Street and the Tower Avenue truck yard, opposed adjourning Third Street so the developer could consult, and voted against Home Street because the people nearby objected; he also voted against its fourth storey. Seconded the 189 Hanover Street denial ('we do need to listen to neighbours'). Pressed for full parking at 635 Main Street and 185 Friesen Avenue, but moved the 185 Friesen adjournment on staff's advice. Approved 48 units on McKenzie Avenue because no variances were requested, and 12 units on Henry Street, saying council should not 'make up extra rules'; he told Stone Bridge residents that 'public hearings do matter'.

Took part in 30 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistent across three councils: neighbours' views weigh most when a change is large or needs exceptions, compliance with the plan and by-law weighs most when it doesn't. He welcomed fewer Board referrals and expected developers, not the city, to consult first.
