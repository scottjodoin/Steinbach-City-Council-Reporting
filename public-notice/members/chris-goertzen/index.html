Public notice and having a say · Council member

Chris Goertzen: public hearings and residents' objections

What did Chris Goertzen say and do when residents objected at public hearings on rezonings, variances and plan changes between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

In short

Mayor Chris Goertzen chaired nearly every hearing from December 2015 to the 2018 election, asking three times for objectors and explaining to the public how a spoken objection becomes a formal one and when a by-law must wait for the Manitoba Municipal Board. He told applicants that when a neighbourhood is changing they should go and talk to the people around them.

He generally supported approvals over objections, including three-storey apartments on Hanover Street in 2016, but voted to deny the 2017 plan change that would have allowed offices at 80 Brant Street, saying the reasons for keeping the block residential still existed 'as we heard from the neighborhood'. He opposed variances that lacked a unique reason or broke a development agreement, even with no objectors, and in 2017 promised that the community plan review would include public consultation.

“When things are changing and things are evolving in a neighborhood, you go talk to your people around you and you make sure they're good with it, or you make adjustments accordingly.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · On applicants consulting neighbours before a hearing · Jul 19, 2016 · 0:32:28

Term by term

2014–2018

Chaired hearings and explained the objection process (Barkman Avenue 2015, Bridge Park 2016, First Street 2018, where two objectors sent townhouses to the Municipal Board). Voted to deny the 80 Brant Street plan change after neighbours objected (2017) and spoke against an after-the-fact paving variance that staff opposed, though it passed. In 2018 he voted with the majority to refuse a front-yard variance on First Street (no uniqueness), to keep a $5,200-a-year parking condition on a church, and to close the Harmony Lane reserve over two neighbours' objections. He supported the 2017 golf course land purchase, decided without a public comment period, and promised public consultation in the plan review.

Took part in 6 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

A steady chair who kept the hearing process orderly and explained it, expected developers to consult, and usually approved over objections, while siding with residents where a change cut against the community plan.
