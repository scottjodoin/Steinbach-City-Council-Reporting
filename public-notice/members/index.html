Council members on public hearings and objections

How did each council member vote and speak when residents objected at public hearings on rezonings, variances and plan changes between December 2015 and November 2026?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. Most hearing decisions were unanimous, and every member voted both with and against objecting neighbours at some point. The differences show up in the split votes, and in what each member said a hearing is for.

  • Jac Siemens and Jake Hiebert sided with objectors most often. Siemens voted against 44 First Street at every reading (2019), the Clearspring Greens levy (2020) and the 205 McKenzie fourplex (2022–2024); Jake Hiebert was the lone vote against several infill and parking decisions and moved the 2026 denial on McKenzie Avenue.
  • Michael Zwaagstra moved most decisions in both directions. He moved most denials after big turnouts and said neighbours who live nearby matter most, but also moved most approvals, discounted small turnouts, and welcomed the 2019 rule that a few objectors could no longer send a rezoning to the Municipal Board.
  • Susan Penner was the most consistently in favour of infill (the lone vote against denying the Maplewood townhouses in 2018; mover of Home Street in 2024), while pushing for formal channels: a reset of the Clearspring Greens levy, a hearing for shelters, and counting objections on the record.
  • Earl Funk changed roles. As a councillor in 2018 he said council must “defend” Maplewood residents; as mayor he chairs every hearing, lets everyone speak, and votes case by case, including the deciding vote for Home Street.

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

As a councillor he said council must 'defend' objecting residents; as mayor he chairs every hearing, lets everyone speak, and votes case by case, including the deciding vote for Home Street.

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Chaired hearings, explained how a single objection reached the Municipal Board, expected applicants to talk to neighbours first, and sided with residents on 80 Brant Street.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Moved most hearing decisions in both directions: denials after big turnouts, approvals where a project met the plan, and conditions answering neighbours; welcomed the 2019 rule that a few objectors could no longer reach the Municipal Board.

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

The member who most often voted against contested approvals, usually on plan, setback and traffic grounds; moved first reading of 80 Brant Street 'to hear the public', then its denial.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

The member most consistently in favour of infill over objections, who also pushed for formal channels: a reset when most owners objected to a levy, hearings for shelters, and counting objections on the record.

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Weighs neighbourhood turnout openly but decides on principle; voted with the neighbours on Home Street and against adjourning Third Street for the developer, and moved 48 units on McKenzie as 'a balancing act'.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Voted with the majority on almost every contested hearing; broke with it against 44 First Street (2019) and on parking at 185 Friesen Avenue (2026), and seconded the 330 McKenzie denial.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

The member who most often voted with objecting neighbours since 2018, especially on traffic and parking; moved the 2026 denial on McKenzie Avenue.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

Supported most applications, saying no three-storey project is ever popular with neighbours, and defended closed-door land deals; in 2018 voted with objectors on a funeral home and the Maplewood townhouses.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Chaired the 2018 Maplewood hearing as Deputy Mayor and voted to deny it; voted to close the Harmony Lane reserve over two neighbours' objections.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; no record on this topic yet. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.

Every recorded split vote on hearings and having a say

Most decisions were unanimous, and councillors take turns moving routine motions, so moving a motion is not the same as championing it. Split votes are the clearest evidence of where members differed. Some split votes were on applications nobody objected to; they show members’ own planning judgment.
