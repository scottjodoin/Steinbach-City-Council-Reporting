Public notice and having a say · Council member

John Fehr: public hearings and residents' objections

What did John Fehr say and do when residents objected at public hearings on rezonings, variances and plan changes between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

In short

John Fehr supported most applications over neighbours' objections in 2016–2017, arguing that traffic was manageable and that no three-storey development is ever popular with its neighbours. At Bridge Park he asked for a wider side yard and a delay but voted yes, saying he hoped the developer would respect the neighbourhood. He moved the 2017 golf course land purchase and defended negotiating land deals behind closed doors.

In 2018 he voted with objectors on two of the year's biggest files: he supported denying a funeral home with a history of parking problems and the 44-unit Maplewood Street townhouses. He also voted to refuse a First Street front-yard variance over drainage, against closing the Harmony Lane public reserve, and was the lone vote against narrower side yards on Alderwood Crescent.

“No new development, you know, of a 3-story nature is popular with its neighbors. It never is.”

Councillor John Fehr · Supporting three-storey apartments on Hanover Street over neighbours' objections · Feb 2, 2016 · 1:03:40

Term by term

2014–2018

Supported the Hanover Street apartments (2016) and the 333 Lowen Boulevard rezoning, calling it 'the objector's friend' compared with the existing commercial zoning (2017). At Bridge Park he asked for a wider side yard and a delay but voted yes. Moved the 2017 golf course land purchase and defended closed-door negotiations. In 2018 he voted to deny the funeral home ('a history of parking issues') and the Maplewood townhouses, voted against the First Street front-yard variance and the Harmony Lane reserve closing, and cast the lone vote against narrower side yards on Alderwood Crescent.

Took part in 15 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

From a reliable vote for development over objections in 2016–2017 to siding with neighbours on several 2018 files, his last year on council.
