Jac Siemens: public hearings and residents' objections

What did Jac Siemens say and do when residents objected at public hearings on rezonings, variances and plan changes between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Jac Siemens backed most applications, but he cast more votes against contested approvals than any other member. He voted against 44 First Street at every stage (2019), against charging the Clearspring Greens neighbourhood most of a pathway's cost (2020), against the 205 McKenzie Avenue fourplex three times (2022–2024), and was the lone vote against six storeys at Stone Bridge Crossing (2026). He usually grounded those votes in the community plan, setbacks and traffic rather than in the number of objectors.

He wanted hearings held: in 2017 he moved first reading of a plan change on Brant Street so the public could be heard, and in 2021 he and Susan Penner were the two votes to let an industrial-park school rezoning go to a hearing. He criticized developers who had not consulted neighbours (Hanover Street 2019). He also voted against delaying decisions, alone opposing the 2026 adjournment at 185 Friesen Avenue, where he urged council to honour a 1989 green-space agreement before supporting the seniors' building.

“I'll vote against the motion. I'll take the position of the people that live in that area and know that area.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Voting against the 205 McKenzie Avenue fourplex after 22 letters of objection · Nov 1, 2022 · 1:24:53

Term by term

2014–2018

Moved first reading of the 80 Brant Street plan change 'to hear what the public has to say', then moved its denial after neighbours objected (2017). Seconded the parking denial at 295 Lowen Boulevard (2016) and moved the 2018 denials of a funeral home and a First Street lot split. Raised a sidewalk for Albert Street after residents' safety concerns at 333 Lowen Boulevard, and voted against closing the Harmony Lane reserve (2018). He supported the 2017 golf course land purchase.

Took part in 18 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Most often with objectors on infill: moved to deny 44 First Street and voted against it at every stage, calling it a precedent for the south side (2019). Voted against the Clearspring Greens levy after his cheaper version failed, and against the Acres Drive levy on a disability-services charity (2020). Criticized the Hanover Street applicant for not consulting neighbours. With Susan Penner, wanted the industrial-park school rezoning to go to a hearing (2021). Voted against denying an after-the-fact side yard on Coral Crescent, preferring a fine, and against the 6 First Street plan change, wanting a 30-foot buffer from PTH 52 (2022).

Took part in 29 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Moved to deny the 205 McKenzie fourplex and voted against it at second and third reading ('I'll take the position of the people that live in that area'). Voted against the 6 First Street plan change and lane closing on setback grounds, but seconded the Home Street approval. Voted with residents on Third Street and the truck yard, moved the 189 Hanover Street denial ('10 years too early'), voted alone against adjourning 185 Friesen Avenue, and cast the lone vote against six storeys at Stone Bridge Crossing, saying such buildings belong downtown.

Took part in 23 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

The council's most frequent dissenter on contested hearings across three terms, steady in reasoning: follow the community plan, keep setbacks and traffic in mind, involve the neighbourhood, and decide on the night.
