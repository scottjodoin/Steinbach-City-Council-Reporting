Jake Hiebert: public hearings and residents' objections

What did Jake Hiebert say and do when residents objected at public hearings on rezonings, variances and plan changes between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Jake Hiebert has voted with objecting neighbours more often than any other member since 2018. He sided with them on 44 First Street (2019) and the Clearspring Greens levy (2020), cast the lone vote against 115 First Street because he wanted a traffic study first (2021), voted against 32 units on Home Street and its fourth storey (2024–2025), and was the lone vote against a 12-unit building on Henry Street (2026). He moved the 2026 denial of 20 units at 330 McKenzie Avenue, saying council needs to listen to the objecting citizens.

His objections are usually about traffic, safety and parking: he voted against a zero front setback on Loewen Boulevard over sight lines and against reduced parking at 635 Main Street. He did not oppose every contested project: he voted for the 205 McKenzie Avenue fourplex, thanking petition signers, and supported the 185 Friesen Avenue and Stone Bridge Crossing seniors' buildings while asking for more parking.

“So I appreciate every person that came here today and gave their story. And what's important, what are their values? And I've heard you loud and clear.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert · Voting against the Home Street rezoning, which passed 4–3 · Mar 19, 2024 · 1:40:54

Term by term

2018–2022

Sided with objectors on 44 First Street (2019) and the Clearspring Greens levy and its scope (2020); seconded the Ellice Avenue denial, citing the petition; cast the lone vote against a front-yard fence on Rosewood Place. In 2021 he was the most frequent dissenter: alone against a zero front setback on Loewen Boulevard (sight lines) and against 115 First Street, where his motion to deny until a traffic study was done found no seconder. Moved to deny the Murano Cove variance, then moved its approval after the staff report (2022).

Took part in 17 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Voted for the 205 McKenzie fourplex (thanking those who signed petitions) and against the 6 First Street plan change at second reading (both November 2022), was absent for Third Street and the truck yard, and voted against Home Street ('I've heard you loud and clear') and its fourth storey. Voted against the First Street lane closing, reduced parking at 635 Main Street, an uncontested variance on Kroeker Avenue and the 288 Henry Street 12-plex. Moved the 330 McKenzie Avenue denial (2026); supported 185 Friesen Avenue and Stone Bridge Crossing.

Took part in 17 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Increasingly the council's vote with objecting neighbours, focused on traffic, parking and fit with single-family blocks; with Jac Siemens gone, he is the returning member most likely to side with objectors.
