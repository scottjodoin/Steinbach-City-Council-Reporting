Public notice and having a say · Council member

Susan Penner: public hearings and residents' objections

What did Susan Penner say and do when residents objected at public hearings on rezonings, variances and plan changes between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

In short

Susan Penner has been council's most consistent supporter of infill housing when neighbours object. She was the lone vote against denying 44 townhouses on Maplewood Street in 2018, moved the approval of 32 units on Home Street over a 43-name petition in 2024, and moved the Home Street fourth storey, the 635 Main Street parking reduction and the 185 Friesen Avenue seniors' building, arguing that the city's plans call for this density and that the benefits outweigh the costs.

She has also pushed for formal ways for residents to be heard. In 2021 she moved to cancel the Clearspring Greens levy after more than half the owners objected, and wanted an industrial-park rezoning to go to a hearing. In 2023 she asked that objections be formally counted, and in 2024–2025 she championed making emergency shelters a conditional use so neighbours can officially object. She has said split votes are 'democracy in action'.

“Having said that, I just think big picture for the community, for that area, for our downtown, for the hospital, I think the, the benefits outweigh the costs in this particular situation.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Moving the Home Street rezoning, which passed 4–3 over a 43-name petition · Mar 19, 2024 · 1:29:53

Term by term

2014–2018

In 2015–2017 she can be identified with certainty only seconding the Quail Ridge garage approval and the 333 Lowen Boulevard second reading, moving Bridge Park's third reading, and serving on the 2016 arts centre survey task force; other 'Councillor Penner' votes, including the lone recorded dissent on Hanover Street apartments, cannot be assigned. In 2018 she credited a First Street developer for talking to neighbours, backed its front-yard variance against staff advice, and was the lone vote against denying the Maplewood townhouses, pointing to the plan's collector street.

Took part in 10 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Moved approval of a front-yard fence on Rosewood Place over a neighbours' petition, citing a counter-petition and compassion (2020). Joined the Ellice Avenue denial because the plan was 'too aggressive', not because of the zoning. Moved to cancel and redesign the Clearspring Greens levy after 78 objections (2021), and with Jac Siemens wanted the industrial-park school rezoning to go to a hearing. Supported the high-density Old Tom Road neighbourhood (2022).

Took part in 24 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Asked that objections at Third Street be formally counted (2023). Moved the Home Street approval over a 43-name petition (2024) and its fourth storey (2025), and declined an objector's fence condition at 600 Main Street. Championed making emergency shelters a conditional use so neighbourhoods 'have the opportunity to speak to council'. Moved the 635 Main Street parking reduction and the 185 Friesen Avenue approval (2026), and told Stone Bridge residents housing and drainage work could go together.

Took part in 19 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Steady across the decade: a vote for infill and the plan's density goals even against large turnouts, paired with support for formal channels that let residents object on the record.
