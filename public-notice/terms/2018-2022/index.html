Public notice and having a say · By term

2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk’s first council

How did Steinbach City Council tell neighbours about planning changes and weigh their objections from November 2018 to October 2022, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • Objections changed 6 of 32 contested results, four of them in 2019. Council denied an eight-suite apartment on Hanover Street (a 54-name petition), a six-plex on Ellice Avenue and two small variances, and trimmed two more. Large petitions did not always win: 56 names on Maplewood Street (2019) and 58 on Wyndham Estate Drive (2021) lost.
  • “Did the developer talk to the neighbours?” became the test. Council denied the Hanover Street apartment partly because nobody had been consulted, and approved projects on First Street and Maplewood Street where developers had canvassed the block. The city’s own notice stayed the mailed minimum.
  • A handful of objectors no longer triggered a Board hearing. Under a 2018 change to the Planning Act, by 2020 four or five objections were “not sufficient.” Councillor Zwaagstra called the change good; council opposed a separate provincial bill letting developers appeal, saying it would take power from neighbourhoods.
  • Money gave residents the most leverage. When 78 of 146 Clearspring Greens owners objected to a $4,280-a-lot pathway levy, council cancelled it and charged at most $1,610 a lot.
  • Requests for more information got little. A neighbour’s request for a traffic study on First Street was answered with the annual traffic count; the mayor cut short a business owner raising access at a setback hearing.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2019: consultation decides, and the Board bar rises

Nine of 24 hearings drew objectors. In July council unanimously denied an eight-suite apartment at 216 Hanover Street after three letters, a 54-name petition and neighbours who said they had never been asked:

“I am disappointed in the fact that, again, just to repeat it, but the fact that the neighbourhood wasn't consulted in advance.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Jul 2, 2019 · 0:51:19

Two weeks later an eight-unit building at 44 First Street, whose applicants had contacted the 16 owner-occupants nearby, passed 4–3 over two letters and six speakers:

“we can't assume that the relatively small number of people that we have here represents the whole area.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jul 16, 2019 · 0:55:30

Councillor Siemens, voting against, called it a precedent for the south side of the street. Because there were not enough objections for a Board hearing, third reading followed in August. Councillor Zwaagstra welcomed the new rule:

“now it takes more than just 1 or 2 people who might object to trigger a municipal board hearing.…And this new process is good because it means that if there are a couple of If it's not a significant number, the decision of council remains.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Aug 20, 2019 · 0:09:12

A 56-name petition did not stop nine units at 126 Maplewood Street; no objector renewed the objection to the Board. Council also denied a 1-foot garage setback on Alexander Way after one neighbour objected, limited a front-yard fence on Granite Park Boulevard after another wrote, and refused a parking cut on Brandt Street that neighbouring owners opposed.

2020: who pays, and who decides

About 100 Clearspring Greens residents had asked for a safe pathway; the plan council brought back would charge each of 146 lots about $4,280. At a packed January hearing residents objected to the cost and said they had no say beforehand. Council passed it 5–2, and the mover pointed objectors to the formal routes:

“There's still ability for this project to not go forward by the means of a municipal board hearing or 2/3 of the affected residents objecting before 3rd reading, and I do strongly encourage them to look at these options if they do not want this to proceed.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Jan 7, 2020 · 1:29:32

Two weeks later council moved the lighting cost to the city, 6–1. In June council objected to the province’s Bill 48, which would let applicants appeal council’s land-use decisions:

“And it also takes the decision away from neighbourhoods, because if we decide to listen to the neighbourhood and we turn something down, or council decides to put a condition on, and then a municipal board decides not to, not to go ahead with that, that can have a big impact on our decisions”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jun 2, 2020 · 0:38:23

Hearings continued in person through COVID-19 and were livestreamed; the recordings suggest speakers were brought into the chamber one at a time. In August council denied a six-plex on a narrow lot at 14 Ellice Avenue after a petition and six speakers; the mayor invited a reworked plan, and a four-unit version passed in 2021. A redesigned 72-suite project on the Maplewood Street lots refused in 2018, from an applicant who had consulted the neighbourhood, passed with no formal objectors (May–July).

2021: 78 objections, and a hearing that never happened

By January 2021, 78 owners had formally objected to the Clearspring Greens levy, enough to send it to the Municipal Board. Councillor Susan Penner moved to cancel it:

“I've come to the conclusion that given the fact that over half the lot owners have objected to the plan means we should reconsider what we are proposing.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Jan 5, 2021 · 0:35:38

The new plan, with no lights and the city paying half, drew six or seven objections and was built that year. In May, staff recommended refusing even first reading of a rezoning for a school in the industrial park, which would end it before any hearing. Councillors Susan Penner and Siemens wanted the neighbours asked; the majority did not:

“Yeah, I would like to open this up and hear from the area to hear if the businesses in the surrounding area have issues with this or take issue with this.”

Councillor Susan Penner · May 4, 2021 · 0:19:19

“And so I think it's pretty obvious the reason the administration is recommending that we deny first reading is that if we go to a public hearing, the answer is going to be exactly the same.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · May 4, 2021 · 0:20:21

In September a neighbour at 115 First Street said few people had seen the notice, and another asked for a traffic study before more units. Councillor Jake Hiebert agreed, moved to deny and found no seconder:

“And I wonder at what point do we actually stop multifamily dwellings in that area for the purposes of getting a proper traffic study done.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert · Sept 7, 2021 · 0:37:37

With only two registered objections, third reading followed the same night. Ten of about 26 planning hearings in 2021 drew objectors; one was denied, a side-yard addition built too close to a neighbour, which staff had also recommended refusing. A 58-name petition on Wyndham Estate Drive sent that subdivision to the Municipal Board, which imposed no conditions.

2022: a notice error, and drainage on trust

At Old Tom Road and Loewen Boulevard, neighbours raised creek flooding, traffic and density; a farmland owner said an earlier request had “fell on deaf ears.” Council approved after the developer’s engineers said drainage would improve. Staff then found an error in the notice for one of the by-laws:

“…unfortunately there was an error discovered in the public notice, which was only discovered after the public hearing and meeting was completed.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager, on By-law 2213 · Sept 20, 2022 · 0:33:34

Council rescinded second reading and re-advertised; nobody came to the new hearing on November 1. Council tabled a side-yard variance on Murano Cove for two weeks so staff could answer neighbours’ fire and insurance questions, then approved it. None of the six contested 2022 applications was clearly changed by objections.

Where members differed

Councillors Jac Siemens and Jake Hiebert cast most of the votes against contested approvals: both against 44 First Street (with Bill Hiebert) and the Clearspring Greens levy, Siemens against the Acres Drive levy on a disability-services charity, Jake Hiebert alone against 115 First Street and a zero-setback building on Loewen Boulevard. Susan Penner and Siemens were the two votes to let the industrial-park school rezoning go to a hearing.
