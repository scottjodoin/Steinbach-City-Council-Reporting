Health and wellbeing · Council’s reasons

Council’s reasons, in its own words

What reasons has Steinbach City Council given since 2015 for not doing more on doctors, mental health and addictions, seniors’ care and healthy living, and how well do those reasons hold up?

In short

The first reason is the strongest and the most repeated: health care is the province’s responsibility. It is true, and most Manitoba councils say the same. But most of them also spend some money on recruiting doctors and clinics, and Steinbach’s neighbours raise special levies for their hospital and clinics. The weaker points are where a reason was used to set aside a cheap or fully funded option (the overdose fund), or where decisions moved into unrecorded study sessions and never came back.

1Health care is the province’s job

“It was, but this is more on infrastructure than it is healthcare. Healthcare is a provincial and a federal responsibility. It's not a municipal responsibility.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, on a regional lobby group, August 2024 · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:39:17

“Yeah, I agree. I mean, healthcare is a provincial, federal— that's what they take care of. It's their responsibility. Yet we do the seed money, the seed funding, plus we have to do all the lobbying for it as well.”

Mayor Earl Funk, replying · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:39:36

“Healthcare is a provincial responsibility, so obviously municipalities, we can't— we don't run the hospital, we can't dictate health policy, but we could— we can recommend in a focused way some specific solutions”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on the Brandon medical campus letter · Oct 21, 2025 · 0:59:41

“this is a problem of provincial responsibilities, in this case healthcare, being downloaded to municipalities. … we expect, we expect the healthcare to be provided by the province, by the health authority, as opposed to the municipality.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on ambulance lift assists · Jun 6, 2023 · 0:42:49
Context

The principle is right, and other councils say it too: Portage la Prairie’s mayor said in 2026 that “health does not fall under municipal jurisdiction”. Even so, according to the Association of Manitoba Municipalities, “more than 90 per cent” of municipalities report doctor shortages and “nearly 95 per cent are allocating financial resources to recruit and retain health practitioners” (AMM). Winkler and Morden each charge a special levy of about $91 a year for their share of the regional hospital and clinic expansions (see How Steinbach compares). By 2026 council was describing a defined role inside provincial health care: Councillor Susan Penner said the city has “a responsibility as a city to understand the problem, bring the right people together, and advocate for practical solutions.”

2Health and social programs aren’t the city’s role

“The reality is that government works best when it does fewer things, not more things.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, opening remarks · May 2, 2023 · 0:00:14

“And is this a social issue that we have to now deal with, start to be involved with? How many tax dollars do we put towards social issues.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, on encampments, September 2024 · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:39:54

“There are many factors related to homelessness, and it can run an extremely broad range of specific issues that the city has no knowledge of, experience of, or expertise in, in dealing with.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager, on encampments · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:45:28
Context

This reason decided the clearest health choice council made. In November 2024 Steinbach Community Outreach asked the city to apply to the federal Emergency Treatment Fund, which only municipalities could apply to, for a mobile outreach unit with addictions counselling, detox referrals and naloxone (about $142,000 to March 2026), with the money passed through to the non-profit. The City Manager’s memo said health and social programs are “generally outside the scope of its legislated obligations as a municipality”, and that the city would be answerable for the program. Council resolved to take no action, rather than keep the request open for a later intake. The group said no mobile outreach program existed in the area. Two years later RCMP Mental Health Act calls were up from 251 to 311 year to date, and the city’s own wellbeing plan lists more addiction services among its goals, with no costs or timelines. Council did endorse The Bridge on Third supportive housing in 2026, at no cost to the city.

3Only narrow, targeted steps

“I would have a concern about going down the road of giving a grant for regular 55+ housing because it's not supportive— it's not assisted living or supportive care.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on a seniors’ housing rebate request, 2016 · Oct 4, 2016 · 0:24:39

“And one of my big concerns about delving too far into this area is, of course, this is a provincial issue, but it affects municipalities. What I appreciate about the recommendations is that they're very targeted in scope. … Did they give any indication as to do they stay, do these doctors that they recruit stay beyond the 4 years?”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on the doctor task force’s report · Jul 16, 2024 · 0:39:14
Context

Targeting has served the city: the $10,000 residents’ retreat reached every family-medicine resident in Manitoba at the moment they choose where to practise. And the question whether recruited doctors stay is a fair one. Peers answer it with contracts: Clarington, Ontario pays $25,000 per doctor, matched by the clinic, for a five-year commitment and a roster of at least 1,000 patients, with prorated repayment if a doctor leaves early; Strathmore, Alberta pays toward new clinic offices, repayable if the doctor practises in town less than five years. The task force’s own package was narrow (about $20,000 a year), but it was never decided in public.

4It costs too much, and infrastructure comes first

“The price tags are very high, and so no one should look at this and assume that, okay, all of this is going to happen in the near term. … When you add this all together, I think it added up to $11 million in total. That won't be happening anytime soon.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on the Active Transportation Plan · May 6, 2025 · 1:03:48

“No municipality should be cutting infrastructure in order to pay for recreation projects or, you know, other sort of grant programs and such. We are not doing that.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, at the 2025 budget hearing · Mar 4, 2025 · 0:31:04

“Currently, the aquatic center is running a substantial deficit in excess of $800,000 per year. The directive from previous council council's to administration was to run approximately a 66% cost recovery, which has not been achieved over the last 2 years.”

Councillor Damian Penner, moving the 2019 pool fee increase · Feb 5, 2019 · 0:11:31
Context

Cost is real, and the 2024 and 2025 floods made sewers the priority. But the comparison matters: the city found just over $30 million for the event centre and about $700,000 a year to run it, while the Active Transportation Plan’s pathways are costed at $11,775,000 in asphalt ($6,645,000 in limestone), with the plan itself setting only a “long-term horizon”. The consultant told council the federal Active Transportation Fund can match 60% of a project, up to $5 million. Winkler, Morden and the RM of Stanley built a 10 km pathway, which also reaches their regional hospital, with a municipal share of $750,000 in all; it opened in June 2026. Pool fees rose in 2019 (children aged 3–7 went from free to $5), and fees have since been set to recover costs, with no recorded discussion of help for low-income families.

Context

Steinbach is a regional centre, and its hospital, pool and event centre serve the whole southeast; in 2016 a councillor said most Aquatic Centre users live outside the city, though no data was presented. Asking neighbours to share is fair, and Steinbach’s neighbours to the west show it can be done: Winkler, Morden and the RM of Stanley jointly pledged $3.6 million to their regional hospital’s foundation in 2023. Steinbach joined the Eastman Regional Municipal Committee in 2024–2025, which has raised hospital staffing with the Health Minister; no shared health funding has come of it yet.
