Steinbach City Council · Dec 2015 – Nov 2026 · Topic: Health and wellbeing

Health and wellbeing: what has council done, and what more could it do?

What role has Steinbach City Council played since December 2015 in building health-care capacity (family doctors, clinics, the hospital, emergency and ambulance services, mental health and addictions, seniors’ care) and in helping residents live healthier lives (recreation, active living, healthy environments)? Where has it held back and why, how did each council member act, and how do other councils compare?

Written for residents. Start with the summary, then choose what to read. Last updated Nov 7, 2026.

Executive summary

Over eleven years and three councils, Steinbach treated health care mainly as the province’s job. Its own part was money for the hospital’s foundation, approvals for care homes, advocacy, and recreation buildings.

  • The biggest health contribution was money for the hospital’s foundation. The city’s grant lists show $500,000 to the Bethesda Foundation for 2018 and $250,000 a year from 2019 to 2022, part of a pledge the mayor described as $2 million over seven years. The foundation’s 2023 request for $3 million over ten years toward the hospital expansion has no recorded decision. STARS air ambulance has received about $1 per resident a year since 2021.
  • Doctors came late to council’s agenda. Doctor recruitment barely appears in council meetings before 2023. Since then council paid up to $10,000 toward a 2023 residents’ retreat and created the Mayor’s Doctor Recruitment Task Force in 2024. The task force’s ask of about $20,000 a year was never decided in public. Its idea of reserving medical-school seats for rural students became a priority for Manitoba’s cities in 2025 and led to council’s support for a medical campus in Brandon.
  • “Health care is the province’s job” was the main reason for holding back. Members gave this reason throughout the decade, and from 2023 on in nearly the same words. In 2024 council took no action on a federal overdose-response fund that only a municipality could apply to, after staff advised that health programs are outside the city’s legislated duties.
  • On seniors’ care, council approved sites and advocated. Council made care-home beds its advocacy goal in 2016 and approved care homes by land-use votes: 333 Loewen Boulevard (2017), the Rest Haven expansion against staff advice (2018) and Maplewood Manor (2026). In October 2026 it created a seniors task force, “Rooted in Community”, on residents being placed in care homes outside Steinbach.
  • Healthy living meant buildings. The Southeast Event Centre cost the city just over $30 million, and spending on recreation facilities rose from $4.6 million (2023) to $6.2 million (2026). Walking and cycling paths were built one neighbourhood at a time. The 2025 Active Transportation Plan, with about $11.8 million of paths, has no project funded at a recorded meeting.

The story in three councils

Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council made care-home beds its health goal, kept grants narrow and spent on parks, playgrounds and the pool. Mayor Earl Funk’s first council paid steady grants to the Bethesda Foundation and STARS, committed to the event centre, and through COVID-19 wrote to the province urging it to reopen. His second council put doctors on the agenda for the first time, relied on advocacy more than money, and ended its term focused on seniors’ care. The council elected in October 2026 inherits a seniors task force, an undecided recruitment package and an unfunded walking and cycling plan.

About this report

Quotes are verbatim from automatic transcripts and link to the meeting videos. Council members, the mayor and staff are named; residents, delegations and members of community groups are identified by initials. The analysis and plain-language summaries are ours.

Methods, coverage and what we couldn’t confirm
  • Transcripts. Meeting quotes come from automatic transcripts of the official videos and are left as transcribed, including repeated words and false starts. The one exception is the city’s name, which the transcription often misspells and which quotes show correctly as Steinbach. Ellipses mark trimmed text; no sentence breaks were added. Each quote links to the start of the speaker’s turn in the video; check there before quoting elsewhere.
  • Speakers and names. Every quote from a council member or staff member is from a speaker identified with high confidence: from hand-checked speaker maps for meetings from January 2022, and for earlier meetings from how speakers were addressed and from the minutes. Council members, the mayor and city staff are named in full. Residents, delegations, task-force community members, physicians and other members of the public are identified only by initials, including in quote citations. Organizations and businesses are named. In 2015–2017 the two Councillors Penner often cannot be told apart in the recordings, and their remarks are not attributed unless certain.
  • Coverage. Every council meeting from December 15, 2015 to November 3, 2026 with a recording or minutes was searched: transcripts of 243 meeting videos and minutes from January 2018 on (the city’s older minutes are not online). Not covered: the first four 2015 videos (not yet transcribed); four videos that failed to download (April 4 and October 17, 2017; September 15, 2020; July 6, 2021), where the minutes were used if they exist; the April 5, 2022 budget hearing, which has no audio; and the November 5, 2024 meeting, which has no transcript, so the overdose-fund decision comes from the minutes and the agenda package. Budget study sessions and the Strategic Priorities Committee are closed and unrecorded, so some funding decisions may have been made there.
  • What counts. 111 items: doctor recruitment and clinics, the hospital and the Bethesda Foundation, ambulance and STARS, mental health and addictions, seniors’ care and housing for seniors, recreation and active living, health charities’ requests, and healthy-environment items such as drinking water. Policing and homelessness have their own reports and appear here only where framed as health. Classifying how an item ended (done, partly, no public result, declined) and tagging council’s reasons is our judgment; the reasons are recorded for each item in the data.
  • Votes before 2018. There are no minutes online for 2015–2017. Votes were by voice and counts were rarely announced, so who voted which way is taken from what members said. The April 2016 multiplex vote (5–2) is from SteinbachOnline’s report; the December 2017 golf-club vote (about 4–3) is inferred from members’ stated positions.
  • Bethesda Foundation money. The city’s 2018 grants list shows $500,000 to the Bethesda Foundation, but at the 2019 budget hearing Councillor Susan Penner described it as a $900,000 grant, and the list’s subtotal only adds up with $900,000. We could not confirm which is right. A November 2018 news report (MySteinbach) said the city had just made the last payment of $1.5 million committed over five years; the city’s 2017 grants list shows no Bethesda grant, so we could not confirm those earlier payments. When the $2 million pledge was approved is not in the meetings searched. No grants list after 2022 was found in the minutes, and the 2026 financial plan’s grants slide shows no Bethesda line. The foundation’s September 2023 request for $3 million over ten years is from SteinbachOnline; we found no decision on it.
  • Other figures not confirmed. The outcome of the doctor task force’s 2024 funding ask; the STARS grant amounts for 2024 and 2025 (the 2026 financial plan shows $18,000); the outdoor pool grant applications (2025 and 2026); the Southeast Cancer Support Services request ($40,000, 2024); the city’s final share of the event centre (the federal release gave about $22.6 million in 2021, council said $23 million in November 2022 and “just over $30 million” in December 2023); and whether the 2023 Bethesda Wellness land will hold clinic space. Counts of residents without a family doctor come from different lists (1,700 in 2018, 3,962 in 2024, about 8,000 by a councillor’s count in 2025) and are not a trend.
  • Data. The data downloads list every item, every council member’s part in it, every split vote, and every quote with its timestamp and link. Quotes in the downloads come only from council members and staff identified with high confidence. Every quote was checked word for word against its transcript.
  • Council change. The council elected in October 2026 held its inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026, with no health items. Quotes are from members serving at the time, some of whom are no longer on council. Election results are from SteinbachOnline (unofficial counts).
