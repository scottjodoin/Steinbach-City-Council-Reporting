Health and wellbeing · Council member

Chris Goertzen: health care and healthy living

What did Chris Goertzen say and do about health care and healthy living in Steinbach (doctors and clinics, emergency services, mental health, seniors, recreation) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

In short

Chris Goertzen opened 2016 by naming care-home beds as the city's advocacy priority with the province, and proposed renewing a city grant program for assisted-living and supportive seniors' housing. His council approved the private care home at 333 Loewen Boulevard and Rest Haven's expansion. No public report on the care-bed advocacy followed.

He praised STARS, the Bethesda Foundation and the seniors' centre, but kept the city's grants 'simplified and focused', so STARS' request for $1 a resident and the Pat Porter centre's request for a grant in lieu of taxes were not funded, and health charities' letters were taken as information. On recreation he opposed dropping the multiplex in 2016 and backed the golf club land deal, saying golf is 'important for people's health'.

“But obviously we do recognize STARS as an important piece of what— of our healthcare emergency services. And they're more and more, especially in the southeast, they've been being used, and, and they certainly do save lives. But we'll take that as information for now.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · To STARS, which asked for $1 a resident a year; no pledge followed · Feb 20, 2018 · 1:05:01

Term by term

2014–2018

Named personal-care-home beds the 2016 priority and chaired approval of two care-home projects. Held the line on grants: Headway, STARS, the seniors' centre and children's health charities received no city grant in public. Opposed the 2016 motion dropping the multiplex and backed the golf club land purchase as a 'win-win'.

Took part in 21 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

His council treated health care as a matter for the province and for donors, with the city's part in land use and advocacy. His successors kept that view but added standing grants to the Bethesda Foundation and STARS.
