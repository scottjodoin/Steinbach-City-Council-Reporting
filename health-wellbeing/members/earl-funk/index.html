Earl Funk: health care and healthy living

What did Earl Funk say and do about health care and healthy living in Steinbach (doctors and clinics, emergency services, mental health, seniors, recreation) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

In short

As a councillor, Earl Funk said little about health care, but in the 2018 budget debate he contrasted the golf club's land deal with the seniors' active living centre, which had followed the right process and 'merely received a pat on the back'. He backed the Rest Haven care-home expansion and voted against that budget.

As mayor he described the city's $2 million pledge to the Bethesda Foundation, led the event centre agreement, and in 2020 and 2022 wrote to the Premier urging the province to reopen and lift COVID-19 restrictions, while thanking health-care workers and urging vaccination. From 2023 he drove the doctor agenda: he chose the residents' retreat, created the task force, pressed ministers for medical seats for rural students and announced the province's response. He agreed health care is provincial, and sent the task force's spending, STARS and most health groups' requests to study sessions whose outcomes are not public. In October 2026 he created the seniors task force.

“he got an increase in seats to 10, but we need— we need that to triple. … we're never going to fix this if we don't start taking and dedicating seats to our local students.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Backing a Brandon medical campus that would prioritize rural students · Oct 21, 2025 · 1:01:50

Term by term

2014–2018

Backed the Rest Haven variance ('this land has been set aside for an expansion just like this'), asked whether the 333 Loewen care home's height would affect its chances of provincial funding, and supported the 2016 recreation principles that dropped the multiplex. Voted against the golf club land deal and the 2018 budget, contrasting the club's treatment with the seniors' centre's unanswered request.

Took part in 14 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Described the $2 million Bethesda Foundation pledge and called the pool key to the region. Led the event centre agreement ($10 million plus about $500,000 a year). Wrote the June 2020 letter urging a full reopening, framed around faith and 'the mental and physical health of Manitobans', and proposed the February 2022 letter asking the province to repeal restrictions, while urging vaccination and thanking ER workers and STARS during the second wave. Sent STARS' and the seniors' centre's requests to study sessions, and personally backed bringing Adult & Teen Challenge to Steinbach.

Took part in 20 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Chose the 2023 residents' retreat, created the doctor task force, and said he wanted to 'act now' but sent its spending to the budget, where no public decision followed. Took the reserved-seats idea to Manitoba's cities and reported the minister's favourable letter in January 2026. Agreed health care is provincial while noting municipalities 'do the seed money'. Promised a cancer-support charity an answer that does not appear in the minutes. Supports walking and cycling but not designated bike lanes. Created the 'Rooted in Community' seniors task force in October 2026.

Took part in 33 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

He went from an occasional critic on council to its main health advocate as mayor. His method stayed the same: advocacy, partnerships and events rather than standing budget lines, with money questions sent to closed study sessions. The new council will show whether the task force package and the seniors task force's recommendations get funded.
