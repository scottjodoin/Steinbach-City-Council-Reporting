Health and wellbeing · Council member

Michael Zwaagstra: health care and healthy living

What did Michael Zwaagstra say and do about health care and healthy living in Steinbach (doctors and clinics, emergency services, mental health, seniors, recreation) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Michael Zwaagstra moved or seconded much of council's health business over three terms: care-home rezonings, the Rest Haven variance, the event centre, the wellbeing plan, the lift-assist resolution and the 2024 resolution to take no action on the federal overdose fund. He supported the 2023 residents' retreat because it targeted 'something specific', and the reserved-seats advocacy.

He was the clearest voice for limits: health care is provincial, government 'works best when it does fewer things', grants for seniors should go only to care beds and supportive housing, and infrastructure comes before recreation. He asked whether recruiter-placed doctors stay beyond four years, and warned that the Active Transportation Plan's paths 'won't be happening anytime soon'. During COVID-19 he led calls to loosen restrictions, using hospital capacity as his test. He did not run in 2026.

“Healthcare is a provincial responsibility, so obviously municipalities, we can't— we don't run the hospital, we can't dictate health policy, but we could— we can recommend in a focused way some specific solutions”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Supporting the Brandon medical campus letter · Oct 21, 2025 · 0:59:41

Term by term

2014–2018

Said the city had committed money toward care-home beds but opposed grants for regular 55+ housing. Seconded the 2016 recreation principles, moved the city's opposition to the provincial pesticide ban (citing Health Canada and cost), asked the province about new mosquito research, and moved the Rest Haven variance. Called the golf land deal 'a bailout'.

Took part in 21 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Moved the event centre agreement and most of its later motions, the 2020 pool renovation, care-home visitation shelters and the silica sand water review. Asked staff to look at pool lifts for people in wheelchairs. Led councillors' calls to reopen in 2020 and to end restrictions in 2022.

Took part in 17 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Moved the lift-assist resolution, the wellbeing plan ('no city tax dollars') and the no-action resolution on the overdose fund. Supported the residents' retreat and the reserved seats, but worried about 'delving too far' into a provincial issue. Warned of the Active Transportation Plan's cost and said recreation must never displace infrastructure. Voted against the apartment beside the hospital and against reduced parking for a seniors' building.

Took part in 32 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistent across all three terms: yes to targeted, low-cost or partner-funded steps; no to open-ended health or social spending. His departure removes council's most articulate voice for that limit.
