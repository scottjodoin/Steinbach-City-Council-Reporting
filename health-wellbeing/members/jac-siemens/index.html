Jac Siemens: health care and healthy living

What did Jac Siemens say and do about health care and healthy living in Steinbach (doctors and clinics, emergency services, mental health, seniors, recreation) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Jac Siemens pushed for walking and cycling routes for a decade, from the 2016 sidewalk debate to the Clearspring Greens pathway, which he voted against when residents were asked to pay most of it. He moved the money for the 2023 residents' retreat, sat on the 2024 doctor task force, asked STARS about a helipad at the hospital, and took hospital staffing to the Health Minister through the Eastman regional lobby group.

He also drew a firm line on the city's role: health care is provincial and federal, he said in 2024, and he asked how many tax dollars should go to social issues. In 2025 he moved to deny an addiction program's industrial-park site on land-use grounds, and in 2026 he cast the only vote against a six-storey seniors' building. He did not run in 2026.

“Recreation Services provide our quality of life in our community. We need more active transportation routes, we need more green space, we need more opportunities for people to get out and enjoy our community.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · At the 2024 budget hearing · Apr 2, 2024 · 0:23:20

Term by term

2014–2018

Moved the soccer park washrooms ('long overdue') and a seniors' housing rezoning, asked Eden Health how it avoids overlap with other agencies, and seconded the golf land purchase. At the 2018 budget he named grants for seniors and youth programs and more pathways as gaps.

Took part in 13 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Moved the Deerfield pathway and pool grant applications, voted against the first Clearspring Greens levy (residents paying 'the majority of the cost'), then backed the 50/50 plan as fair. Asked that STARS' 2020 request go to the study session. Moved the 2022 Hospital Street rezoning for the hospital's growth.

Took part in 10 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Moved the residents' retreat money, served on the doctor task force, cited 'approximately 8,000 names' waiting for a doctor in 2025, and called recreation a quality-of-life service needing more active transportation. Said health care is 'not a municipal responsibility' (2024). Moved denial of Adult and Teen Challenge's site and voted alone against the Bridgepark seniors' building.

Took part in 28 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

His record pairs practical advocacy (pathways, doctors, a helipad) with a firm view of the city's limits. That made him both a driver of the retreat and the task force and a skeptic of social spending.
