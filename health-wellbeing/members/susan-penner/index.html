Susan Penner: health care and healthy living

What did Susan Penner say and do about health care and healthy living in Steinbach (doctors and clinics, emergency services, mental health, seniors, recreation) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

In short

Susan Penner's health work grew over the decade. In 2018–2022 she pointed out the Bethesda Foundation grant, rebuilt the Clearspring Greens pathway into a plan the neighbourhood accepted, and used opening remarks on seniors' care and palliative care. On the 2024 doctor task force she championed reserving medical-school seats for local students, the idea Manitoba's cities adopted and that the mayor credited for the province's 2026 letter.

In 2025–2026 she led on seniors: Maplewood Manor, two seniors' buildings, and the 'Rooted in Community' task force, which she chairs. She called loneliness a public-health problem and framed the event centre as a wellbeing investment. During COVID-19 she was among the councillors most critical of restrictions. She topped the 2026 poll and is Deputy Mayor.

“Ultimately, many of the decisions and resources involved fall under provincial healthcare, but I believe that we have a responsibility as a city to understand the problem, bring the right people together, and advocate for practical solutions.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Proposing the seniors task force she would chair · Oct 6, 2026 · 0:00:12

Term by term

2014–2018

Moved the 2016 recreation principles that dropped the multiplex (per SteinbachOnline), seconded the 333 Loewen care-home rezoning, and voted against the 2018 budget over the golf deal. Several Penner remarks in this term cannot be attributed to her with certainty.

Took part in 6 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Pointed out the 2018 Bethesda Foundation grant at the budget hearing. Proposed a community day of prayer in March 2020 and argued in openings that 'disproportionate restrictions' would do more damage than the outbreak. Redesigned the Clearspring Greens pathway as a 50/50 split. Moved the Silver Lining assisted-living approval and spoke for wider access to palliative care.

Took part in 13 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Task force member who championed reserved seats; moved the Brandon campus letter; met prospective doctors with the mayor and named clinic space as the next need. Argued the apartment beside the hospital would help health-worker recruitment and seniors. Moved the Maplewood Manor and seniors' housing approvals, and proposed and chairs the seniors task force. Raised loneliness and opposed extending assisted dying to mental illness in openings.

Took part in 26 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From a councillor who spoke about health in opening remarks to the member who set council's health agenda. As Deputy Mayor she carries the seniors task force and the medical-seats advocacy into the 2026–2030 term.
