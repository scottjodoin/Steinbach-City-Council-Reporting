Health and wellbeing · Council member

Bill Hiebert: health care and healthy living

What did Bill Hiebert say and do about health care and healthy living in Steinbach (doctors and clinics, emergency services, mental health, seniors, recreation) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Bill Hiebert said a new recreation centre was one of the main reasons he ran, and seconded the 2019 event centre agreement. He moved the grants list that included the 2018 Bethesda Foundation grant, and used opening remarks to describe the Bethesda expansion and the foundation's and Haven Group's part in the new Rest Haven home.

He spoke on health in debate less often than most colleagues. He supported Maplewood Manor ('to see our elderly stay in the community is worth so much') and hoped an addiction program would find a site in Steinbach, voted for the apartment beside the hospital, and voted against reduced parking for a seniors' building. In 2026 he asked STARS about a helipad near the hospital.

“My final note is without healthcare, we don't function well as a people or a community.”

Councillor Bill Hiebert · Opening remarks on the Bethesda hospital expansion · Dec 7, 2021 · 0:00:08

Term by term

2018–2022

Seconded the event centre agreement; moved the 2018 grants list with the Bethesda grant; described the 2021 Bethesda expansion (23 beds and six dialysis stations) and Rest Haven's fundraising in openings. Several of his turns cannot be told apart from Jake Hiebert's in the recordings.

Took part in 7 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Moved the event centre's 2023 change order, supported Maplewood Manor and the Bridgepark building, voted for the apartment beside the hospital and against 1:1 parking for the Steinbach Housing seniors' complex, and raised a helipad with STARS.

Took part in 11 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A supporter of health facilities and seniors' care who spoke mostly through openings and votes, with parking as his recurring concern.
