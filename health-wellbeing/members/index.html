Council members on health and wellbeing

How did each council member vote and speak on health care and healthy living in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. Every health-care decision in this record was unanimous, so the differences between members show up in who raised health issues, who led the work, and a few split votes, none of them on health care itself.

  • Susan Penner became council’s leading health voice. She championed the rural medical-seats idea on the 2024 doctor task force, and led on seniors’ care in 2026, including the seniors task force she chairs.
  • Earl Funk drove the doctor agenda as mayor: the residents’ retreat, the task force and the advocacy with the province. He also sent the task force’s spending and many health groups’ requests to unrecorded budget sessions.
  • Michael Zwaagstra and Jac Siemens set the limits. Both supported targeted steps (Siemens sat on the task force and pushed for pathways), and both said plainly that health care is the province’s job. Neither ran in 2026.
  • The split votes were about money for paths and about land use. Siemens and Jake Hiebert voted against charging the Clearspring Greens neighbourhood most of a pathway’s cost (2020); later splits were over an apartment beside the hospital and the parking and height of seniors’ buildings.

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

As mayor he put doctors on council's agenda and carried the rural medical-seats idea to the province; the task force's spending ask, like most health groups' requests, went to unrecorded budget sessions.

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Named care-home beds as his council's health priority, but held to a narrow grants policy that left STARS and the seniors' centre unfunded.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Backed targeted health steps and moved many of them, while saying more plainly than anyone that health care is the province's job.

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Council's steadiest advocate for pathways and a doctor task force member, who also said flatly that health care is 'not a municipal responsibility'.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

Council's leading health voice by 2026: the rural medical-seats idea, the care-home push and the seniors task force she now chairs as Deputy Mayor.

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Moved the doctor task force and the 2026 budget 'for seniors aging at home'; his split votes were about land use.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Credited the Bethesda Foundation and Rest Haven in his openings and ran partly for a new rec centre; raised a hospital helipad in 2026.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Voted against charging a neighbourhood most of a pathway's cost, and said people in encampments need addiction and mental-health treatment first.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

Praised the Bethesda Foundation's advocacy and backed Rest Haven's new beds; held that the city does not give operating grants.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Deputy mayor from November 2017; chaired meetings where health charities' requests were taken as information.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; no record on this topic yet. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.

Every recorded split vote on health and wellbeing

Most decisions were unanimous, and councillors take turns moving routine motions, so moving a motion is not the same as championing it. Split votes are the clearest evidence of where members differed. On this topic they were mostly about recreation spending, who pays, and land use.
