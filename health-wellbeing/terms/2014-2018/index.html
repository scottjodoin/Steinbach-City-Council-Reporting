Health and wellbeing · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

What did Steinbach City Council do about health care and healthy living from December 2015 (where the recordings begin) to October 2018, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • Care-home beds were the one health-care goal. The mayor named them as the 2016 advocacy priority, and council approved two care-home projects: a private 56-bed home at 333 Loewen Boulevard (2017) and Rest Haven’s 142-bed expansion, against staff advice (2018).
  • Doctors, the emergency room and ambulances barely came up. Doctor recruitment was mentioned once, in a 2017 letter about federal tax changes. No task force, grant or letter to the province on doctors appears.
  • A narrow grants policy kept health groups out. The seniors’ active living centre asked for a grant equal to its property taxes, and STARS air ambulance for $1 a resident, both in 2018; neither was funded. Requests from Headway, Katie Cares and other charities were received as information.
  • Recreation got steady attention: soccer park washrooms, playground renewals, a pool reserve, extra pool days and a new pathway. Big projects divided council: the multiplex was dropped over the mayor’s objection (2016), and the golf club got $500,000 through a land deal.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr.

What happened

2016: care-home beds and a recreation reset

The mayor opened 2016 by listing care-home beds among council’s goals with the province:

“you'll see us move forward with advocating for our city, and specifically for PCH beds with the province.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen, opening the first meeting of 2016 · Jan 5, 2016 · 0:00:04

In October, when a seniors’ housing group asked for a development-fee rebate, Councillor Zwaagstra said the city had already committed money toward care-home beds, but wanted city help kept to care beds and supportive housing. The amount of that commitment was never stated in public, and no report on the advocacy followed in 2016–2017.

“We clearly want more personal care home beds, and the city's already made a commitment in that regard for a city contribution.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on a seniors’ housing request · Oct 4, 2016 · 0:24:39

Council welcomed a change to Manitoba’s Mental Health Act that let someone other than a police officer wait with a person needing an assessment at the hospital, an issue the city’s RCMP advisory group had raised. Headway, a new program steering at-risk youth to mental health and addictions services, asked the city to help close a $20,000 gap; the mayor promised a reply under the grants policy, and its later letters were received as information.

On recreation, council dropped the long-planned multiplex in April. A Councillor Penner moved five principles (SteinbachOnline named Susan Penner), including continuing to put 1.241 mills a year into the recreation reserve. Councillor Zwaagstra seconded it, and the mayor opposed it:

“That does not mean that we stop planning in regards to recreation, but it does mean that we stop the planning for a large multiplex that is outside the feasibility of the city. Recreation remains a priority. Affordable recreation is a priority.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, seconding the recreation principles · Apr 5, 2016 · 0:53:02

“We have private contributors that are desperate to see just a master plan. Show us that master plan. And we're almost there.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen, opposing the motion · Apr 5, 2016 · 0:56:50

The motion carried 5–2 (Goertzen and Fehr against, per SteinbachOnline). Council also built washrooms at the soccer park, which had relied on portable toilets for about ten years, and renewed neighbourhood playgrounds with provincial grants. When the Fly-In Golf Club asked for a $50,000 yearly grant and pitched golf as health, the mayor agreed:

“The golf course is an important part of what goes on in our community. It's an important connecting point. It's important for people's health, and we appreciate what you do.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen, to the golf club’s delegation · Jul 5, 2016 · 0:43:37

2017: a private care home, and the first mention of doctors

With the province’s care-home building on hold, a private developer proposed a nine-storey retirement community at 333 Loewen Boulevard with 56 long-term care beds and 56 supportive-living units. Council rezoned the site unanimously:

“I do like the idea of having additional personal care home beds and supportive housing and that sort of thing. I mean, that's— we need that at this time. We urgently do.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on the 333 Loewen Boulevard proposal · Jul 18, 2017 · 0:42:59

Eden Health Care Services, which runs subsidized suites and a drop-in for people with mental illness, told council its longest waitlist was for single adults needing one-bedroom suites. Council asked questions and took no action. In September a councillor cited doctor recruitment as a reason to oppose federal tax changes for incorporated professionals, the only mention of recruiting doctors in 2015–2017. The Aquatic Centre began opening on Louis Riel Day and Victoria Day after families asked, although it already ran a deficit of about $800,000 a year.

In December council bought 40 acres of the golf course for $500,000 and leased it back for $1 a year, after refusing the club a grant. Councillors Zwaagstra, Funk and one Councillor Penner spoke against it.

2018: the seniors’ centre, STARS and Rest Haven

In February the Pat Porter Active Living Centre (Serving Seniors Inc.) asked for a yearly grant equal to its property taxes, saying its fitness, foot care, meal and health programs eased the load on “limited health resources” and that Southern Health covered only 24% of its costs. At the budget hearing the mayor confirmed the grants policy had not changed:

“…the reality is that there hasn't been a change in the policy of the grants of the city, and so that will not be included in this budget.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen, at the 2018 budget hearing · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:16:26

Councillor Funk, voting against the budget over the golf deal, drew the contrast:

“If we compare this to the same request made by the Pat Porter Centre, on one hand we had an organization, our seniors, that followed the right process, made public requests, and to date have merely received a pat on the back.”

Councillor Earl Funk, at the 2018 budget hearing · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:24:42

“There are items that are not in this budget, like additional grant funds for seniors or youth programs that play a vital role in the quality of Steinbach. I'd like to see more in pathways— that's a personal thing— and bike paths connecting different parts of Steinbach, like a pathway to Southland Estates.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, at the 2018 budget hearing · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:38:13

STARS told council it had flown to Steinbach 24 times in a year and asked the city to join about 40 Manitoba municipalities with a yearly pledge, many at $1 a resident. The mayor praised the service and took the request as information; no pledge was made in 2018 or 2019.

“But obviously we do recognize STARS as an important piece of what— of our healthcare emergency services. And they're more and more, especially in the southeast, they've been being used, and, and they certainly do save lives. But we'll take that as information for now.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen, to STARS · Feb 20, 2018 · 1:05:01

In April, council approved a setback variance for Rest Haven’s 142-bed expansion although administration recommended denial:

“This is something that we have long had much pressure to add personal care beds in this community.”

Councillor John Fehr, seconding the Rest Haven variance · Apr 3, 2018 · 0:27:33

The 2018 budget, passed 4–3 over the golf deal, included a $250,000 connector pathway to Park Hill. Before the October 2018 election, council also put a cannabis retail question on the ballot.

Where members differed

Every health-care decision in this term was unanimous or passed without stated dissent. The divided votes were over big recreation and spending choices: dropping the multiplex, the 2017 budget (over the arts centre), the golf club land deal and the 2018 budget.
