2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk’s first council

What did Steinbach City Council do about health care and healthy living from November 2018 to October 2022, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • Money went to the hospital’s foundation and to STARS. Grant lists show $500,000 to the Bethesda Foundation for 2018 and $250,000 in each of 2019–2022, part of a pledge the mayor described as $2 million over seven years. STARS air ambulance got its first city grant in 2021, about $1 a resident ($15,829).
  • The hospital grew by provincial decision. The province announced the Bethesda expansion in 2021; council’s part was rezoning Hospital Street lots quickly and unanimously (2019 and 2022).
  • On COVID-19, council’s collective voice was for reopening. The mayor wrote to the Premier in June 2020 urging a full reopening and full-capacity church services, and in February 2022 council asked the province to repeal all restrictions, while the mayor urged vaccination and thanked health-care workers.
  • Healthy living meant the event centre and one hard-won pathway. The city committed $10 million plus about $500,000 a year to the event centre (2019), and its share reached about $23 million by 2022. The Clearspring Greens pathway produced the term’s only split votes, over who pays.
  • Doctor recruitment did not come up in a council decision. The only mention was the Chamber of Commerce’s report of a rural recruitment summit in October 2022.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2019: a $2 million pledge and an event centre

At the 2019 budget hearing council accepted the 2018 grants list, which included the Bethesda Foundation. The list shows $500,000; Councillor Susan Penner called it $900,000, and the list’s subtotal only adds up with $900,000. We could not confirm which is right.

“You'll notice there's a $900,000 grant to the Bethesda Foundation, and I just want to acknowledge that, how we support what they are doing.”

Councillor Susan Penner, at the 2019 budget hearing · Mar 19, 2019 · 0:42:42

In October the mayor described the commitment and tied it to the 143-bed Rest Haven care home announced that year. When council approved the pledge is not in the meetings we searched.

“what we've pledged as a city is a total of $2 million. That's a lot of money. … One of those projects is the PCH that was announced this year for 143 new beds.”

Mayor Earl Funk, on the Bethesda Foundation pledge · Oct 1, 2019 · 0:00:05

The same night council signed the Southeast Event Centre agreement: $10 million and land from the city, plus up to half a mill (about $500,000) a year toward running it, for a $42.5 million arena and community centre. Public swim fees rose for the first time in about ten years (adults $6.50 to $7.50; children 3–7 from free to $5), because the pool ran a deficit of over $800,000 a year. A parent’s request for lifts at the outdoor pool and hot tub was sent to staff; no report back was found.

2020: COVID-19 and a pathway fight

The Clearspring Greens neighbourhood had asked for a safe walking and cycling route to schools, the pool and the park. The first plan charged each of 146 homes about $4,280. Councillors Siemens and Jake Hiebert voted against it:

“But this is the first time that we're asking residences from any area to pay the majority of the cost of a pathway to connect them to our community.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, voting against the first pathway plan · Jan 7, 2020 · 1:39:10

When the pandemic closed the pool, council moved its $1.48 million renovation up a year. Council approved visitation shelters at three care homes so residents could see family through the winter. From April, councillors used their opening remarks to question restrictions, and in June the mayor read his letter to the Premier into the record:

“all churches and other faith-based institutions play a vital role in promoting community health and public well-being. … religious gatherings are integral to the mental and physical health of Manitobans.”

Mayor Earl Funk, reading his letter to the Premier · Jun 16, 2020 · 0:43:13

In November, during the second wave, the mayor reported that STARS had flown COVID-19 patients out of Steinbach 9 times in 11 days, and that he had delivered $5,000 raised for Bethesda’s emergency staff:

“I got to see firsthand what, what's going on in our ER and, and what, what our ER workers are facing on a daily basis because of COVID”

Mayor Earl Funk, during the second wave · Nov 17, 2020 · 1:20:42

STARS had asked again in September 2020 for a yearly contribution; council sent it to the fall study session, and the first STARS grant appears in 2021.

2021: a pathway finished, a hospital announced

Councillor Susan Penner restarted the Clearspring Greens pathway with a cheaper plan that capped each home’s share at $1,610, the sidewalk fee on a new lot. Objections fell from 78 to a handful, and it was built that year.

“I would like council to consider doing a new plan for the sidewalk that would see the lights removed from the project and then splitting the cost of the asphalt path 50/50. This would bring down the cost dramatically and hopefully would get the support of the neighborhood.”

Councillor Susan Penner, proposing the new pathway plan · Jan 5, 2021 · 0:35:38

In October the province announced the Bethesda expansion: 23 acute-care beds and a six-station dialysis unit, which Councillor Bill Hiebert described as a $32 million project. He credited the Bethesda Foundation and ended his opening remarks:

“My final note is without healthcare, we don't function well as a people or a community.”

Councillor Bill Hiebert, opening remarks · Dec 7, 2021 · 0:00:08

Council handed Handi-Transit to Serving Seniors Inc., which runs the Pat Porter Active Living Centre, at about the same cost, and joined six neighbours in paying for an independent review of the Vivian silica sand mine’s risk to the aquifer the region drinks from. Requests from Katie Cares and Ronald McDonald House Charities were received as information and never funded.

2022: reopening, the event centre and STARS

In February council agreed by consensus that the mayor write to Premier Heather Stefanson thanking the province for the vaccination campaign and asking it to repeal all health restrictions. The mayor encouraged everyone eligible to be vaccinated.

“Council, I realize we need to protect the vulnerable in our community, and we also must support our tired and overwhelmed, overworked healthcare workers, but I do believe it's time to move on.”

Mayor Earl Funk, opening remarks · Feb 1, 2022 · 0:02:46

The 143-suite Rest Haven home opened in May, and council rezoned three more Hospital Street lots for the hospital’s growth. The mayor described the event centre’s walking track as a draw for seniors downtown:

“what people are telling me is what they're excited to see is that it's walkable. So that seniors from— that live in Fernwood or in the downtown area in condos can go walk on the track and they can walk to it.”

Mayor Earl Funk, on the event centre · Apr 19, 2022 · 0:24:08

In September STARS asked council to raise its support from $1 to $1.50 a resident, saying Steinbach had more missions than almost any other Manitoba community and that air transfers keep Bethesda’s emergency doctor and nurse in town. The mayor sent it to a study session; the 2022 grant stayed at $15,829.

“And what we'll do is we will take this request. Yeah, we'll take it to our study session and there'll be an answer soon after that.”

Mayor Earl Funk, to STARS · Sept 20, 2022 · 0:17:31
