Health and wellbeing · By term

2022–2026: Mayor Earl Funk’s second council

What did Steinbach City Council do about health care and healthy living from November 2022 to October 2026, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • Doctors reached the agenda. Council paid up to $10,000 toward a 2023 family-medicine residents’ retreat and created the Mayor’s Doctor Recruitment Task Force in 2024. It found 10 doctors were needed and 3,962 residents had no primary care provider, and asked for about $20,000 a year. That ask was never decided at a recorded meeting.
  • Advocacy was the main tool, and it travelled. The task force’s idea of reserving medical-school seats for rural students became a priority of Manitoba’s cities in 2025 and led to council’s support for a Brandon medical campus. In January 2026 the mayor reported a favourable letter from the Health Minister.
  • Mental health and addictions stayed with others. Council joined the province’s wellbeing plan on staff time only, took no action on a federal overdose-response fund (2024), refused an addiction program’s new site on land-use grounds (2025), and endorsed a supportive-housing project (2026).
  • Seniors’ care became the leading health issue. Council approved two seniors’ buildings and the Maplewood Manor expansion in 2026, and created a seniors task force in October.
  • Recreation buildings, not paths. The event centre opened in 2025 at a city cost of just over $30 million and about $700,000 a year to run. The Active Transportation Plan was accepted the same year with no funded project.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2023: a residents’ retreat and the first recruitment money

In June council sponsored a resolution for Manitoba’s cities asking the province to stop ambulance “lift-assist” calls being pushed onto city firefighters, which the mayor said had grown from 1–3 a year to a possible 40–60. In July, asked by Southern Health to take part in recruitment events, council chose the provincial Family Medicine Resident Retreat, held in Steinbach that September, and paid up to $10,000 from the 2023 surplus:

“It is important that we do whatever we can to recruit more doctors to our city. We know this is a significant challenge, and what I like about this, the motion here, is that we're targeting something specific.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on the residents’ retreat · Jul 18, 2023 · 0:56:19

Council also rezoned land beside the Steinbach Family Medical Centre so Bethesda Wellness Inc. could build future health-care facilities. In September, according to SteinbachOnline, the Bethesda Foundation asked the city for $3 million over ten years toward the hospital expansion; we found no decision on it in the minutes. When STARS presented that month, Councillor Siemens asked about a helipad at the hospital; STARS said the request had to come from the community, and no one followed up. The event centre’s cost reached $69.153 million, with the city’s share “just over $30 million”, in Councillor Siemens’ words.

2024: the task force, and the overdose fund

In March council created the Mayor’s Doctor Recruitment Task Force: Councillors Susan Penner and Jac Siemens, a Steinbach Economic Development director as chair, three physicians, and advisers from a clinic and Southern Health. It reported in July:

“Southern Health and Santé Zuid estimates that for the Steinbach area, 10 full-time physicians or the equivalent would need to be recruited to begin addressing the current needs. 3 new full-time physicians in addition to filling the current 7 vacancies. … the recent stats as of June 27th, 2024 indicate that 3,962 citizens do not currently have a primary care provider”

M.B., chair of the Mayor’s Doctor Recruitment Task Force, presenting its report · Jul 16, 2024 · 0:19:10

It recommended advocacy for medical-school seats for local students, a reception and host families for family-medicine residents, a community liaison at $15,000 a year (about $20,000 a year in all), and an optional recruiter at $90,000 per doctor. Councillor Susan Penner championed the seats:

“The reality is that if doctors are from here, they're more likely to stay. And if they're not from here, they're more likely to leave. … There are many local qualified students who've been trying to get into med school for years now, um, but it's not happening for whatever reason.”

Councillor Susan Penner, task force member · Jul 16, 2024 · 0:33:41

The mayor said he wanted to act, and sent the spending to the budget:

“I think we need to take this, the rest of this recommendation, this package, and look at it and look at it from a budget point of view so that we can see what we need to do on council to start doing some of these things … I want to act now and get this done and look at the healthcare and increase the healthcare for our community right now.”

Mayor Earl Funk, on the task force’s report · Jul 16, 2024 · 0:37:53

No funding vote followed at any recorded meeting, and the 2026 budget showed no recruitment line. In August, discussing a regional lobby group, Councillor Siemens and the mayor agreed that health care is a provincial and federal responsibility (see council’s reasons).

In September Councillor Jake Hiebert, after visiting a Main Street encampment, said the people there needed long-term addiction and mental-health treatment before housing and work. In November, Steinbach Community Outreach asked the city to apply to the federal Emergency Treatment Fund, which only municipalities could apply to, for a mobile outreach unit (about $142,000 to March 2026) offering addictions counselling, detox referrals and naloxone. The City Manager’s memo said health and social programs are “generally outside the scope of its legislated obligations as a municipality”. Council resolved unanimously to take no action, rather than keep the request open for a later intake. The meeting has no recording, so any debate could not be checked.

The same year a new charity, Southeast Cancer Support Services, asked for $40,000 a year to help patients get to treatment; the mayor promised an answer, and none appears in the minutes. Council applied for a provincial grant for half the cost of renewing the outdoor pool, and rezoned the empty former Family Medical Centre for a Bible college without discussing health uses.

2025: the cities take up Steinbach’s idea

In April Manitoba’s ten-city caucus adopted the reserved-seats idea as one of five advocacy priorities, and in October council unanimously backed a four-year medical campus at Brandon University that would prioritize rural students:

“he got an increase in seats to 10, but we need— we need that to triple. … we're never going to fix this if we don't start taking and dedicating seats to our local students.”

Mayor Earl Funk, on the Brandon medical campus · Oct 21, 2025 · 1:01:50

The event centre opened in March with a city subsidy of about $700,000 a year. Councillor Susan Penner framed it as a health investment:

“And while loneliness is not a municipal responsibility, the fact is the health and well-being of, of our community is important to us. That's why spaces like our new Southeast Event Centre matter. … So while the financial investments may not always make sense, the investment in the well-being of our city does.”

Councillor Susan Penner, opening remarks · Jun 17, 2025 · 0:00:47

In May council accepted the city’s first Active Transportation Plan, with pathways costed at about $11.8 million. Members stressed it was long-term and should not hinder driving; the mayor said designated bike lanes “don’t work” but promised “some things every year”. No project from it was funded at a recorded meeting through October 2026. Council refused Adult and Teen Challenge’s move to the industrial park on land-use grounds, while praising the program, and allowed a weekly farmers’ market at the event centre.

2026: doctors arriving, and seniors at the centre

“now there's a new letter out from the minister and it's very favorable and we will be seeing rural doctors being streamed for family medicine.”

Mayor Earl Funk, year-end remarks · Jan 6, 2026 · 0:09:16

Councillor Susan Penner said that month the city needed more clinic space, and floated a clinic combined with a daycare (SteinbachOnline); no city initiative followed at a recorded meeting. Council had ended its economic development grant at the end of 2025, whose director had chaired the task force, without discussing recruitment. The wellbeing plan was published in March; its health actions have no costs or timelines, and in July a $200,000 provincial safety grant was deferred to an unrecorded special meeting. In May council endorsed The Bridge on Third, a second supportive-housing project for people with mental-health needs.

Seniors’ care took over the agenda. Council approved two large seniors’ buildings, splitting only on parking and height, and in July a 19-bed assisted-living wing at Maplewood Manor:

“I'm hearing on a regular basis from families in our community whose aging parents are being placed all over the Southern Health Region as they're waiting for a care home placement. … it is not uncommon to wait 2 years plus in a community that you didn't— that's not Steinbach.”

Councillor Susan Penner, on the Maplewood Manor expansion · Jul 7, 2026 · 0:36:58

At its last meeting before the election, council created the Mayor’s Task Force on Keeping Aging Seniors in Steinbach, chaired by Councillor Susan Penner:

“And you're right, we're not looking at building more PCHs at $1 million a bed. We're looking at simple cost-saving solutions.”

Mayor Earl Funk, on the seniors task force · Oct 6, 2026 · 1:45:28

In September the RCMP reported Mental Health Act calls up from 251 to 311 year to date; council asked no follow-up question. In October council applied again for a provincial grant for the outdoor pool, now about $3.85 million.

Where members differed

Every health-care decision in this term was unanimous, including the doctor task force, the Brandon campus letter and the overdose-fund decision. The split votes were about land use, parking and building height: a children’s charity’s book sales in the industrial park, an apartment beside the hospital, and two seniors’ buildings.
