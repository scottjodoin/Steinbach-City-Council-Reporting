Health and wellbeing · By term

The story by term

How did Steinbach City Council’s approach to health care and healthy living change from one council to the next between December 2015 and November 2026, and what does the new council inherit?

In short

One view held across all three councils: health care belongs to the province, and the city’s part is to support, approve and advocate. What changed was how far council went within that view. Mayor Goertzen’s council kept grants narrow and talked about care-home beds. Mayor Funk’s first council gave steady money to the hospital’s foundation and to STARS, and its loudest health message, during COVID-19, was to reopen. His second council took on the doctor shortage directly, mostly through advocacy, and ended with seniors’ care at the top of its list. Healthy-living spending went to buildings in every term.

2014–2018

Mayor Goertzen’s council

Care-home beds as the advocacy goal; a narrow grants policy that left the seniors’ centre and STARS unfunded; the multiplex dropped and the pool kept up.

31 items · doctor recruitment mentioned once

2018–2022

Mayor Funk’s first council

A $2 million pledge to the Bethesda Foundation, STARS at $1 a resident, the event centre agreement, the Clearspring Greens pathway, and letters urging the province to lift COVID-19 rules.

28 items · split votes only on the pathway

2022–2026

Mayor Funk’s second council

A residents’ retreat, a doctor task force and the rural medical-seats push; no action on an overdose fund; care homes and a seniors task force.

52 items · the busiest term on health

2026–2030: the new council

The council elected on October 28, 2026 is the first change in membership since 2018. Susan Penner, who topped the poll and is now Deputy Mayor, led most of council’s health work in the last term: the reserved-seats idea, the care-home push and the seniors task force. Michael Zwaagstra, who most often said health care is a provincial matter while backing targeted steps, and Jac Siemens, who sat on the doctor task force and pushed for pathways, did not run. Ron Falk, a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years (14 as chair), and Greg Carlos, a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, are new.

What the new council inherits:

  • The seniors task force, “Rooted in Community”, created October 6, 2026 and chaired by Susan Penner, to measure how often seniors waiting for a care-home bed are placed outside Steinbach and to recommend low-cost fixes such as transitional care beds. Councillor Penner said the Health Minister agreed to look at its final report.
  • The doctor task force’s spending package (about $20,000 a year, plus an optional recruiter), never decided in public, and the clinic-space shortage members named in January 2026.
  • Hospital money. The Bethesda Foundation’s 2023 request for $3 million over ten years has no recorded answer, and the expanded hospital opened in March 2026.
  • The outdoor pool, waiting on a provincial grant for half of about $3.85 million, and the Active Transportation Plan, with no project funded yet.
  • The wellbeing plan’s health actions (more practitioners, addiction services, a mental-health space at the hospital outside the emergency room), which have no costs or timelines, and rising Mental Health Act calls (251 to 311 year to date, 2025 to 2026).

The new council’s inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026 had no health items. Its first budget, for 2027, is where the task force package, STARS and any Bethesda grant would be decided.
