Services in new neighbourhoods · Council member

Damian Penner: services in new and fringe neighbourhoods

What did Damian Penner say and do about services in new subdivisions and fringe areas (grading, sidewalks, parks, paving, wells and septic) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Damian Penner described how services follow development: Steinbach works 'on a developer-driven system,' and in 2023 he backed using reserves to finish paving Park Road West rather than wait. He championed the runoff and path-connection policies in the new Official Community Plan and the active transportation plan, which he said lets the city set land aside for paths as the edges develop. He seconded the denial of an unserviced lot split, saying two-acre lots make it 'very expensive' to extend services later.

On Clearspring Greens he agreed the path 'should have been planned for' when the low-service zoning was approved, but moved the plan that had residents pay about 80% and voted down the cheaper limestone version, before seconding the lighting concession. In 2026 he declined Councillor Siemens's request to add a sidewalk to a McKenzie Avenue development agreement ('I also am not an engineer'), voted for the Sterling Crescent variance, and moved the Clearspring Greens sewer reroute.

“we work on a developer-driven system … We can piggyback onto that and complete the project to what the fullest design should be, uh, without waiting for more development to come down further closer to industrial to extended all the way there.”

Councillor Damian Penner · On paying to finish Park Road West · Mar 21, 2023 · 0:50:58

Term by term

2018–2022

Moved the 2020 Clearspring Greens local improvement with residents paying about 80% and opposed the limestone amendment, while saying the path should have been planned when the area was approved; then seconded moving lighting to the city ('the whole community helping out a neighborhood'). Seconded the Hespeler Street South denial on servicing cost and supported Hampton Village's last phase as normal 'growing pains.'

Took part in 12 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Backed paving Park Road West with reserves, promoted runoff limits and path connections in the Official Community Plan and active transportation plan, and praised modern detention ponds. Declined the McKenzie Avenue sidewalk amendment as its mover, voted for the Sterling Crescent variance, and moved the Clearspring Greens reroute award as investment 'into our future infrastructure.'

Took part in 25 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A planner's view of growth: set the rules in plans and let development deliver services, with the city stepping in where reserves allow. He was reluctant to add conditions to individual approvals.
