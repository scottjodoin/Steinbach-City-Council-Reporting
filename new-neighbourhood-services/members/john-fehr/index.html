Services in new neighbourhoods · Council member

John Fehr: services in new and fringe neighbourhoods

What did John Fehr say and do about services in new subdivisions and fringe areas (grading, sidewalks, parks, paving, wells and septic) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

In short

John Fehr raised the Home Street North swale in December 2015, saying 'I would sure like to see us entertain doing something there to rectify that,' and as Deputy Mayor chaired the 2016 swale exception, which he called something council would not be 'doing every time.' He held firm on the drainage-plan policy for an owner who asked for relief ('we need to stick with our guns'), and said water near Old Tom Road had to be dealt with downstream first.

After the 2018 Brighton Lane sidewalk dispute he asked the key question of this report: 'when we approve these subdivisions, do we know where the sidewalks go?' The answer was that sidewalks come at 75–80% build-out. He moved the 2018 rule letting businesses on gravel roads use an all-weather surface, saying the city should 'lead by example,' and voted against closing part of the Harmony Lane green space.

“when we approve these subdivisions, do we know where the sidewalks go? I'm trying to avoid What happened about a month ago regarding the sidewalk.”

Councillor John Fehr · After a dispute over a new sidewalk's location · Jun 5, 2018 · 0:07:45

Term by term

2014–2018

Raised the Home Street swale (2015), chaired the 2016 swale exception as Deputy Mayor, held the drainage-plan policy (2016), and said Old Tom Road drainage had to start downstream at the golf course. Said cost would only rise if Old Tom Road's paving was delayed. Asked how sidewalk locations are set in new subdivisions (2018), moved the gravel-road surfacing rule (4–2), wanted a more permanent fix for the Cottonwood walkway, and voted against the Harmony Lane reserve change (4–3).

Took part in 18 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

A practical voice who raised residents' problems and asked how the system worked, while holding owners to drainage rules and easing paving rules for small businesses.
