Services in new neighbourhoods · Council member

Chris Goertzen: services in new and fringe neighbourhoods

What did Chris Goertzen say and do about services in new subdivisions and fringe areas (grading, sidewalks, parks, paving, wells and septic) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

In short

Mayor Chris Goertzen framed local improvements on older streets such as Barkman Avenue as a long-term plan to stay 'ahead of problems.' He defended the city's standards: he voted against letting businesses on gravel roads skip paving in 2018, saying 'going away from the high standard that we have is a mistake,' and confirmed that new subdivisions must keep 'net zero outflow' of water. He did not take part in the 2016 swale exception, having declared a perceived conflict.

On fringe requests he usually pointed to cost or the future: an Old Tom Road sidewalk might fit regional pathways once annexed land joined the city; the Home Street North swale, inherited from Hanover, 'would be over $1 million to correct'; earlier Clearspring Greens path options had 'a fairly hefty price tag.' He voted for closing part of the Harmony Lane green space in 2018.

“this is something that we inherited from Hanover when we took over that area. It's designed the way it is. We wouldn't design it that way now, but it is how it is, and to correct it would be over $1 million to correct.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · On the Home Street North swale · Dec 5, 2017 · 1:27:59

Term by term

2014–2018

Presented Barkman Avenue's rebuild as part of a long-term renewal strategy (2015). Pointed Old Tom Road residents to future regional pathways rather than a sidewalk (2016), and confirmed the 'net zero outflow' rule for new subdivisions (2016). Absent from the 2016 swale exception (declared a perceived conflict). Closed the Home Street North swale file as too costly (2017), recalled a 'fairly hefty price tag' for a Clearspring Greens link (2018), voted against relaxing paving for gravel-road businesses (2018), and voted for the Harmony Lane reserve change (2018).

Took part in 17 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

A consistent defender of the city's construction standards and planned renewal, who treated older and fringe problems as expensive legacies to be fixed when streets are rebuilt or new land develops.
