Council members on services in new neighbourhoods

How did each council member speak and vote on what developers must build, on drainage complaints, sidewalks, parks and paving, and on older and fringe areas, between December 2015 and November 2026?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. New neighbourhood phases were approved unanimously, so the differences show up in who pays for older areas, in variances that touch drainage, and in what members said.

  • Who pays divided council most. Jac Siemens and Jake Hiebert voted for smaller charges on Clearspring Greens owners (2020); Michael Zwaagstra and Damian Penner argued fairness to other taxpayers; Susan Penner found the 50/50 compromise all seven backed (2021).
  • Standards and exceptions. Zwaagstra was the steadiest voice against exceptions, from the 2016 swale exception to the 2025 Sterling Crescent variance, where Mayor Earl Funk and Jake Hiebert joined him and lost 4–3. Siemens and Cari Penner backed the 2016 exception; Cari Penner later stood alone for paving rules.
  • Services up front. Only Siemens proposed making a developer build a sidewalk (2026); Damian Penner, the mover, declined it. Zwaagstra, Susan Penner and the mayor defended sidewalks after build-out and leaving drainage to development agreements.

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

Held owners to drainage rules as a councillor; as mayor he explains the sidewalks-after-build-out rule and sends drainage questions to development agreements that council doesn't see.

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Defended high urban standards and planned renewal of older streets; on fringe requests pointed to cost or to future annexation.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

The steadiest defender of uniform standards: no exceptions, no add-on conditions at hearings, sidewalks planned but built after most homes are up.

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Council's most frequent dissenter for lower resident charges, and the only member to propose making a developer build a sidewalk.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

Brokered the Clearspring Greens 50/50 compromise and pushed to finish Park Road West; defended not promising sidewalks in bays and crescents.

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Named the 'developer-driven system' and used reserves to finish a road; moved the 80/20 Clearspring plan and declined a sidewalk condition.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Mostly seconded routine items; voted with the majority on Clearspring Greens and for the Sterling Crescent variance.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Pushed to get the Clearspring Greens path built cheaply and soon; sided with drainage advice on variances, against the 4–3 majority in 2025.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

Raised the Home Street swale and asked how sidewalks are planned in new subdivisions; held firm on drainage plans but moved relaxing paving on gravel roads.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Argued to relax city specs for the 2016 swale exception, but was the lone vote to keep paving rules for businesses on gravel roads.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; no record on this topic yet. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.
