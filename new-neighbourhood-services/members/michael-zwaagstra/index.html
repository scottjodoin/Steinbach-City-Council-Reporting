Services in new neighbourhoods · Council member

Michael Zwaagstra: services in new and fringe neighbourhoods

What did Michael Zwaagstra say and do about services in new subdivisions and fringe areas (grading, sidewalks, parks, paving, wells and septic) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Michael Zwaagstra took part in more of these items than any other member and moved most of the subdivision, sidewalk and local improvement approvals. He consistently defended uniform rules: he voted against the 2016 swale exception because it 'creates a precedent,' refused to exempt a charity from the Acres Drive local improvement, sided with staff on drainage variances, and in 2025 voted with the city engineer against the Sterling Crescent variance. He explained that development fees pay for new-area sidewalks, which go in 'after most of the development has happened,' and that 'we don't put a sidewalk in every single street.'

On Clearspring Greens he argued the rest of the city should not pay more ('a difference of opinion over who pays and how much'), then moved the lighting concession and backed the 50/50 plan. At Williams Creek in 2026 he refused to add conditions beyond the zoning by-law 'on an ad hoc basis,' and he warned that the $11 million active transportation plan would take a long time.

“My understanding is that it's pretty standard practice in the city that we always have sidewalks that are planned, but they don't go in right away. It's just a matter because of the fact that it goes in after most of the development has happened.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · On a request for sidewalks in Park Hill · Dec 16, 2025 · 0:13:25

Term by term

2014–2018

Voted against the 2016 Steinbach North swale exception on precedent grounds and opposed an after-the-fact paving variance in 2017 ('the ignorance is the excuse'), though he backed the 2018 gravel-road surfacing rule. Explained that the development fee's sidewalk levy covers new neighbourhoods while older ones lag. Moved Barkman, McKenzie Avenue and sidewalk-program items, kept the Cottonwood walkway ('we're in the business of building sidewalks'), and moved the Harmony Lane reserve change for 'fewer variances' (4–3).

Took part in 18 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Defended staff drainage advice in refusing a Lexington Village variance. On Clearspring Greens he argued against raising everyone else's share, noting the low-service category had been dropped, then moved the lighting concession and supported the $1,610 cap as what anyone buying a new lot pays. Refused to carve Envision out of the Acres Drive levy, and supported finishing Hampton Village despite construction traffic.

Took part in 19 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Said developers had provided more green space than required when Stonebridge got back part of its Wyndham park land. Held firm on servicing standards and drainage records (Woodhaven denial, lot grading conditions), voted against the Sterling Crescent variance, warned the active transportation plan's price tags are 'very high,' explained sidewalks-later at Park Hill, and moved Williams Creek's approval without adding conditions.

Took part in 24 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Unchanged across eleven years: apply the standards evenly, let fees and the capital program deliver sidewalks over time, and keep conditions out of individual hearings. That consistency protected the drainage rules, but it also meant residents' requests for services up front were turned down.
