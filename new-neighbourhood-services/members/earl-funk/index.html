Services in new neighbourhoods · Council member

Earl Funk: services in new and fringe neighbourhoods

What did Earl Funk say and do about services in new subdivisions and fringe areas (grading, sidewalks, parks, paving, wells and septic) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

In short

As a councillor, Earl Funk held individual owners to the city's drainage rules: in 2016 he urged council not to waive a homeowner's drainage plan, because 'the water that we don't take care of here becomes someone else's problem.' He asked for costed options for the Home Street swale, called relaxing paving rules for businesses on gravel roads 'like for like,' and voted against closing part of a Harmony Lane green space because the buyers had relied on city maps.

As mayor since 2018 he has chaired every item in this report. He conceded that Clearspring Greens owners got less service than their taxes implied and backed moving from an 80/20 to a 50/50 split, and he backed paying to finish Park Road West. But his recurring answers to residents are that roads and sidewalks follow development ('80% plus full'), that drainage 'will be dealt with through a development agreement,' and, at Williams Creek in 2026, that 'council doesn't get involved in the development agreement.' He sided with the city engineer against the Sterling Crescent variance in 2025 and lost 4–3.

“So technically right now, we can't comment to drainage because that will be dealt with through a development agreement that will be drawn up between developer and our engineer and our city planner, okay?”

Mayor Earl Funk · Answering neighbours of an infill project who asked about runoff · Dec 16, 2025 · 0:38:47

Term by term

2014–2018

As a councillor he held the drainage-plan policy for an owner who asked to be excused (2016), asked for costed options for Home Street North's swale (2017), and seconded the 2018 change letting businesses on gravel roads use an all-weather surface instead of paving ('it's like for like'). He voted against closing part of the Harmony Lane public reserve (4–3, 2018) because the homeowner had done 'everything that we tell them to do.' He moved the 2018 grant for Giesbrecht Park's playground.

Took part in 8 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Elected mayor in 2018. He told the Clearspring Greens delegation in 2019 that the budget had no room, then said the area's 'level of service does not reflect your tax assessment,' backed moving the lighting cost to the city and the 50/50 plan, and said 'this is the council that got it done.' He objected to unserviceable rural lots on Hanover's side of the line, and on fringe roads and phasing said development comes first: 'we let the development happen and then we can work on the road system.' On Hampton Village he said the city was 'developing basically at the speed of light.'

Took part in 13 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He backed paving Park Road West from reserves and objected to Hanover allowing businesses below Steinbach's standards on the city's edge. He explained the sidewalk rule (built at 80% build-out to avoid construction damage), told residents that drainage is settled in development agreements, and at Williams Creek said council doesn't get involved in the agreement and that one neighbour's problem seemed 'isolated on your properties.' He voted with the engineer against the Sterling Crescent variance and asked for a crossing near the new Hampton Village playground.

Took part in 22 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From enforcer to explainer: as a councillor he insisted owners follow the drainage policy; as mayor he defends a system in which services follow development and the details sit in agreements council doesn't see, while making exceptions with city money when a neighbourhood's case is strong (Clearspring Greens, Park Road West).
