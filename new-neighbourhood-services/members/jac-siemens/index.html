Services in new neighbourhoods · Council member

Jac Siemens: services in new and fringe neighbourhoods

What did Jac Siemens say and do about services in new subdivisions and fringe areas (grading, sidewalks, parks, paving, wells and septic) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Jac Siemens pushed most often for walking links and for residents who felt left out: he asked for reports on Albert Street and Clearspring Greens sidewalks, voted against making Clearspring Greens owners pay most of their pathway ('the first time that we're asking residences from any area to pay the majority'), and voted against charging Envision for Acres Drive. He pressed for lot grading plans on old and new lots, checked that trail corridors survived subdivision changes, moved the active transportation plan, and in 2026 asked to add sidewalk construction to a McKenzie Avenue development agreement. It was not adopted.

He was not always on the side of stricter rules. He moved the 2016 swale exception as 'a one-off,' moved a smaller Lexington Village side yard alone, and moved the 2025 Sterling Crescent variance against the engineer's stormwater advice. At Williams Creek he said council 'doesn't know the ins and outs of all the different drainage' and invited neighbours to raise their concerns in the next zoning review.

“So a friendly amendment I'm requesting is that they also add sidewalk construction to the development agreement.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · At the hearing for 48 apartments on McKenzie Avenue · Apr 7, 2026 · 1:29:11

Term by term

2014–2018

Moved the 2016 swale exception, calling it a one-off rather than a change in specifications. Moved the request for an Albert Street sidewalk report (2016) and asked to put it back on the agenda (2017); said Clearspring Greens had no safe access by foot or bike and moved it to a study session (2018). Complained council had no staff report before the Brighton Lane sidewalk decision, and voted against closing part of the Harmony Lane green space so residents' voices would count.

Took part in 16 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

The term's most frequent dissenter: voted alone for a smaller Lexington Village side yard, proposed a cheaper limestone Clearspring Greens path, and voted against the 80/20 plan, the lighting change and charging Envision for Acres Drive. Then supported the 50/50 plan as 'sellable' because $1,600 equals the sidewalk fee. Moved the Deerfield pond and pathway tender and the denial of an unserviced Hespeler Street South lot split.

Took part in 17 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Pressed for lot grading plans on variances (noting the city lacked one on file), checked that path corridors survived the Wyndham reserve closing and a drain takeover, and praised developer-built paths in Lang Hill Farms. Moved the Sterling Crescent variance (4–3). Proposed the McKenzie Avenue sidewalk amendment, which was not adopted. At Williams Creek he deferred drainage to engineers and the agreement, and later said Steinbach's flat streets 'become the collector' because bigger pipes are not affordable. Voted against the First Street lane closing over the related zoning.

Took part in 20 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A persistent advocate for residents' walking links and lower charges on neighbourhoods that were short-changed, who was also willing to relax rules for builders case by case. His 2026 sidewalk amendment was the clearest attempt in eleven years to make a developer build a sidewalk.
