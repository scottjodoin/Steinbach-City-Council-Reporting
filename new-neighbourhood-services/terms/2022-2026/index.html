Services in new neighbourhoods · By term

2022–2026: Mayor Earl Funk’s second council

What did Steinbach City Council say and do about services in new and fringe neighbourhoods from November 2022 to October 2026?

In short

  • Sidewalks-later became stated policy. The City Manager called it “a targeted and specific strategy” (2025). Three requests to require sidewalks at approval were turned down: a Park Hill letter (2025), Councillor Siemens’s McKenzie Avenue amendment (2026) and the Williams Creek neighbours (2026). The city built Wyndham Estate Drive’s sidewalk itself in 2026.
  • “The development agreement will deal with it.” Neighbours’ drainage questions at hearings were answered that way, and in August 2026 the mayor said plainly that council doesn’t get involved in the agreement.
  • Parks later, or from residents. Hampton Village’s play structure came in 2023 after neighbours worked with the city; in 2024 its developer got back part of its donated park land; Williams Creek’s first 153 lots pay a fee, with any park left to phase 2.
  • After the storms, more drainage rules. 2024 complaints were found built “according to what was spec’d.” Council then passed Land Drainage By-law 2274 with inspections and fines (2026) and signed to take over the Manning Canal, but listed the lot grading policy review only as a possible future project, and approved a variance 4–3 against the engineer’s stormwater advice.
  • Fringe areas patched. Park Road West paved from reserves (2023), a small Clearspring Greens drainage job and a council-initiated sewer reroute ($396,500, 2026). Wells, septic tanks and ditches got no servicing plan.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert, the same seven as the previous term.

What happened

2023: a paved gap and a playground

Part of Park Road West was being paved by a developer. Rather than wait for more development to finish it, council paid $1,970,097 from reserves to pave the last 260 metres to Industrial Road:

“we work on a developer-driven system … We can piggyback onto that and complete the project to what the fullest design should be, uh, without waiting for more development to come down further closer to industrial to extended all the way there.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Mar 21, 2023 · 0:50:58

In Hampton Village, council refused a three-way stop by the new park and middle school (6–1, Councillor Susan Penner against) and added a no-stopping zone. Asked whether the park was in use, the City Manager said:

“The park is being used. The picnic shelter structure was installed late last summer. My understanding is that the play structure that is also intended for the site, that installation for that began this spring.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Jun 20, 2023 · 0:27:20

“the intent was always just a passive green space, but now the neighbors have gotten together, gotten … worked with the city”

Mayor Earl Funk, naming the park · Sept 5, 2023 · 0:23:39

The final Hampton Village phase had been approved in 2022, so the playground arrived about a year later, as a neighbourhood initiative. Council kept attaching lot grading plans to variances, including for older homes built before grading rules; in one case the city had no plan on file:

“Okay, so does the city have that lot grading plan? Apparently we don't have it in stock or in our records, so you'd have to supply that as well.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Jun 20, 2023 · 0:11:05

2024: park land returned, complaints checked against the plan

In January 2024 Stonebridge Development Corporation, which had used three approved lots for a temporary turnaround, asked to turn part of the park land it had donated on Wyndham Estate Drive back into a building lot. A neighbour asked whom this benefited. Council agreed unanimously:

“over the course of the multiple phases of this development, the developer has provided more public green space than the city required pursuant to the city's development policies.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jan 16, 2024 · 0:37:06

After a wet spring, Chrysler Gate and Harmony Lane owners wrote about standing water. Council referred it to staff in May; in July staff reported back:

“So in effect, they were built according to what was spec'd. As a result, there were no remedial measures to the drainage swales that were necessary”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Jul 16, 2024 · 0:09:06

Staff expected the problem to resolve as the ground dried, and suggested a minor fix on one private lot. Creekside Drive residents, all on private wells beside a runoff ditch, helped defeat a truck yard next door in January 2024; their wells were not discussed. In December council moved the new Official Community Plan forward: runoff no greater than before development, path connections in new areas, and no private wells for buildings that can connect to city water. It also objected when the RM of Hanover let businesses build just north of the city below Steinbach’s standards.

“any new developments must control its runoff water so that its contribution to the city's land drainage system is not greater than its pre-development conditions. So something that's done in principle that's written in here as well.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Dec 3, 2024 · 0:18:59

2025: a path plan, a split vote and “a targeted and specific strategy”

At the April 2025 hearing on the new Official Community Plan, the city’s planning consultant explained the private-well rule. It applies to new buildings; no plan for existing well and septic areas was discussed.

“We added a policy that prohibits the use of private wells for buildings that are connected to or have the potential to connect to the municipal water system.”

K.S., WSP, the city’s planning consultant · Apr 1, 2025 · 0:21:06

Council accepted its first active transportation plan in May 2025 as a guide, with no budget attached. Councillor Siemens praised developer-built paths in Lang Hill Farms; Councillor Zwaagstra warned about cost:

“The price tags are very high, and so no one should look at this and assume that, okay, all of this is going to happen in the near term. … When you add this all together, I think it added up to $11 million in total.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · May 6, 2025 · 1:03:48

Days after the September 2025 storm, a builder asked to shrink a front yard in the new Sterling Crescent subdivision. The city engineer advised against it because bigger footprints add runoff beyond what the subdivision’s stormwater system was designed for. Council approved it 4–3:

“I also agree overall that potentially the question is answered with the overall design of the complete subdivision.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, moving approval · Sept 16, 2025 · 0:15:18

“the engineer's report is speaking quite loud to me.”

Mayor Earl Funk, voting against · Sept 16, 2025 · 0:20:41

In December a written objection to Park Hill’s 41-lot Rosebrook Trail phase asked for sidewalks on listed streets. The answer was no:

“However, as a matter of practice, sidewalks are generally not required by developers at time of development of subdivision, and that is a targeted and specific strategy of the city. However, that then is something that the City of Steinbach proceeds to install at a later date in and on streets that are appropriate for the needs and the uses that are occurring in the neighborhood.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Dec 16, 2025 · 0:14:34

“It's very common for bays and crescents, et cetera, not to have sidewalks in them. So to make that commitment doesn't seem consistent with how we generally operate.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Dec 16, 2025 · 0:17:33

At the same meeting, neighbours of a First Street infill project asked about runoff:

“So technically right now, we can't comment to drainage because that will be dealt with through a development agreement that will be drawn up between developer and our engineer and our city planner, okay?”

Mayor Earl Funk · Dec 16, 2025 · 0:38:47

2026: sidewalks built late, one proposed early, and Williams Creek

The 2026 capital works program built 320 metres of sidewalk on Wyndham Estate Drive, where neighbours had asked for one in 2021, paid from the operating fund, the capital development reserve and the gas tax reserve. In March staff listed a review of the lot grading policy as a project that “could be looked at in the future.” In April, at the hearing for 48 apartments on McKenzie Avenue, a neighbour asked who would pay for a south-side sidewalk, since owners had paid for the north side as a local improvement:

“So a friendly amendment I'm requesting is that they also add sidewalk construction to the development agreement.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Apr 7, 2026 · 1:29:11

“I share that concern as well. However, I also am not an engineer, and to put that into development agreement when it might not be feasible or be appropriate it is not something that I want to put into an amendment.”

Councillor Damian Penner, the mover · Apr 7, 2026 · 1:30:42

The City Manager said such a condition was possible but should be a condition of approval; none was added. In July, at a hearing near Stonebridge Crossing, the City Manager read the standard drainage clause aloud, and Golfview Crescent residents asked for independent review of developers’ studies, since the consultants work for the developer:

“Further, that the owner will be responsible to ensure that drainage in the immediate area of these lots functions properly and that a professionally prepared lot grading plan will be submitted and approved by the city prior to the issuance of the first building permit. These are standard requirements.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Jul 21, 2026 · 0:44:00

That night council awarded the $396,500 reroute of the Clearspring Greens low-pressure sewer, which the City Manager called “entirely council-initiated.” On August 4 council heard Williams Creek, 153 lots south of Loewen Boulevard. Neighbours from Wyndham Estates and Ash Grove Lane asked for drainage first, walkways and playgrounds right away, and to see the engineered drawings.

“Drainage is number one, has not been dealt with. I'd like to see drainage before anything proposed proceeds, because if we can't do the drainage, where's the water going to go?”

G.W., resident, Wyndham Estate Drive · Aug 4, 2026 · 0:16:04

“Well, sidewalks are usually put in after the, um, the development is full, 80% plus full, probably fuller. So the sidewalks are put in afterwards so that they're not damaged by the construction and the bigger vehicles.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Aug 4, 2026 · 0:13:36

“council doesn't get involved in the development agreement.”

Mayor Earl Funk, when a neighbour asked to see the drainage drawings · Aug 4, 2026 · 0:42:58

“So I'm not going to, on an ad hoc basis, add things that go beyond what the zoning bylaw is.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving approval · Aug 4, 2026 · 0:49:43

The developer said a 30-foot concrete-bottomed swale would carry water behind the neighbours’ lots and that phase 1 would pay a fee instead of providing park land, with any park in phase 2. Council approved the rezoning and subdivision unanimously, subject to a development agreement, with no condition on drainage timing, sidewalks or playgrounds. The same night it passed Land Drainage By-law 2274:

“It has been broadened to deal with general stormwater and local drainage with respect to private property, what is permitted and what is prohibited. It also does set the parameters for city administration to proceed with an inspection and compliance process”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Aug 4, 2026 · 1:19:29

Earlier in the year council had approved an agreement to take over the Manning Canal from the province, which drains the city’s east and north and had held up the Old Tom Road and Loewen Boulevard development since 2022. The transfer still needed provincial cabinet approval in November 2026.

Where members differed

Five votes on this topic divided council. Only one turned on drainage in a new subdivision: the 4–3 Sterling Crescent variance, where the mayor, Councillor Zwaagstra and Councillor Jake Hiebert sided with the city engineer and lost.
