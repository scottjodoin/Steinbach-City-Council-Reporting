Sewer backups and flooding · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

How did Steinbach City Council deal with drainage, sewers and flooding from December 2015 to November 2018, what did it do with residents’ requests, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • No storm on the agenda. No storm or basement backup was discussed at council in this period. The record is about everyday drainage: swales, ditches, lot grading and runoff from new buildings.
  • Residents’ requests went to reports, not work. A Home Street North resident’s request for a culvert was studied for two years and dropped at about $500,000. Complaints about a provincial drain at Lowen Boulevard and runoff onto the Fly-In Golf Course also ended without city work.
  • Rules for new land. Council’s main tool was development policy: new subdivisions may release no more water than before (“net zero outflow”), and every lot needs an approved drainage plan, a rule council would not waive even for one homeowner.
  • But a rule relaxed when it wanted development. In 2016 council let a commercial subdivision use roadside swales instead of curb, gutter and storm sewer, over the City Manager’s precedent warning.
  • Steady pipe work. Old sewer and water mains were replaced street by street, the Elmdale Drain returned to the 2018 budget, sewer rates rose 11% in 2018, and the mayor named lagoon expansion the next big hurdle.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr. This record starts in December 2015, the first meeting with a transcript.

What happened

2015–2016: renewing pipes, and setting rules for runoff

The city replaced old sewer and water mains as part of street rebuilds, starting with Barkman Avenue in 2016 ($2.14 million, partly paid by federal-provincial grants). The mayor described it as a plan:

“this is part of a long-term strategy that we've had and that we've been implementing over the last quite a number of years, and that is to renew our underground infrastructure and making sure that we are ahead of problems rather than reacting to problems.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen, on the Barkman Avenue rebuild · Dec 15, 2015 · 0:09:53

At the same meeting council heard a resident’s email about Home Street North, where an open drain is designed to send water across the road near Lowen Boulevard, leaving water and ice that can last weeks. Councillor Fehr asked for a fix; council asked staff to monitor it and report back:

“I would sure like to see us entertain doing something there to rectify that.”

Councillor John Fehr · Dec 15, 2015 · 0:30:27

Neighbours who feared a new apartment project on Hanover Street would back up their 60-year-old sewer were told gravity mains are designed for future connections; the project was approved. A resident at Lowen Boulevard and Old Tom Road was told the drain flooding his lot was the province’s. In May 2016 council let the Steinbach North commercial subdivision use roadside swales instead of the city’s curb-and-gutter or storm sewer standard, as a one-time exception. Councillor Zwaagstra opposed it, and the City Manager warned:

“If this exception is granted for one developer, I would fully expect that all future developers would also expect the same treatment.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · May 3, 2016 · 0:29:50

A month later Councillor Susan Penner asked the question that would return a decade later:

“So when the rare but major storm comes, it is difficult for our system to keep up. This begs the question, should our drainage infrastructure meet the worst-case but rare scenario, or is it enough that it is based on average precipitation with a bit of a buffer?”

Councillor Susan Penner, opening remarks · Jun 7, 2016 · 0:00:08

No policy change followed. The Fly-In Golf Course asked council for a partnership and compensation for runoff it blamed on development; the mayor said new subdivisions must keep “net zero outflow” and much of the water came from the neighbouring municipality, and talks on holding water upstream had stalled. In September council refused to waive a homeowner’s $2,000–$3,000 drainage plan:

“the water that we don't take care of here becomes someone else's problem, and that's why we have these development plans.”

Councillor Earl Funk · Sept 20, 2016 · 0:25:23

2017: Home Street North, studied and left as it was

In February 2017 the Home Street resident, K.B., returned with a neighbours’ petition for a culvert, and council asked for options and costs. The report came in December: a piped crossing would mean raising the road about a metre, possibly buying property, and costing about $500,000. No work was done; the city stepped up monitoring and maintenance instead.

“It's designed the way it is. We wouldn't design it that way now, but it is how it is, and to correct it would be over $1 million to correct.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Dec 5, 2017 · 1:27:59

The City Manager had put the cost at about $500,000 plus property; he called the mayor’s higher figure “pretty, pretty close.”

2018: the lagoon, the Elmdale Drain, and basements on First Street

The mayor named expanding the sewage lagoon as the city’s “next major hurdle”; staff said the 2007 expansion gave about 12–20 years. The 2018 budget put back the Elmdale Drain rebuild ($850,000 from reserves), which council had held for years until development called for it, and the sewer rate rose 11%.

At a May hearing on First Street townhouses, a neighbour said the area had seen “major sewer backup and flooding” and asked what more fixtures would do. Councillors answered that developers pay for sewer upgrades:

“our staff do not sign off on development agreements if it's going to have— if it's going to cause direct flooding in a neighborhood, if it's going to impact these things.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · May 1, 2018 · 0:48:53

The rezoning passed unanimously. The townhouses’ shorter front yards were refused 4–3; Councillor Fehr was the one to cite water:

“it's very hard to explain it to somebody that has had a considerable amount of water in their basement when we've had flooding events. where the properties were not able to carry the water.”

Councillor John Fehr · May 1, 2018 · 0:50:18

In August council denied a 44-unit rezoning on Maplewood Street 5–1. Drainage was one of the reasons given:

“Drainage is a big one for me. We already have Drainage that's not working on Home Street, which is very, very close, and if any water goes down that way, it's just not going to work.”

Councillor Earl Funk · Aug 21, 2018 · 1:02:04
