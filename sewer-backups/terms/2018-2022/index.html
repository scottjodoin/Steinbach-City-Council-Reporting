Sewer backups and flooding · By term

2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk’s first council

How did Steinbach City Council deal with drainage, sewers and flooding from November 2018 to October 2022, how did it respond to storms and neighbours’ worries, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • One rule for runoff. At nearly every hearing where neighbours raised water, staff and council cited the rule that a developed site may not drain more or faster than before, enforced through development agreements and lot-drainage plans.
  • Neighbours’ flooding worries never stopped a large development. Maplewood (2019, 2020) and the 128-acre Old Tom/Loewen development (2022) were approved on engineers’ assurances. Requests for a retention pond, an overall impact study and a concrete swale were not adopted.
  • Small variances were refused when staff flagged drainage. Council denied a side-yard variance (2019), a rezoning (2020) and an after-the-fact addition (2021) on staff’s or the city engineer’s warnings, with Michael Zwaagstra moving and Jac Siemens dissenting.
  • Steady pipe and pond work. Elmdale Street, Kroeker Avenue, Brandt Street relining, Loewen Boulevard and Brookdale Crescent sewers, three stormwater ponds, and sewer rate rises of 8.5% (2019) and 9.5% (2020).
  • Storms barely registered. A 2019 storm of “5 inches over 2 days” was mentioned in passing; after spring 2022 flooding, council asked for disaster aid three months later. Sanitary sewer backups, backwater valves, sump pumps and Georgetown were not discussed in this term’s meetings.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert. Jake Hiebert was council’s representative on the regional watershed district throughout.

What happened

2019: a passing storm, and a firm line on small variances

Steinbach joined the new Seine Rat Roseau Watershed District, rebuilt downtown Elmdale Street with new sewer and water lines, tendered the Elmdale Drain ($657,530) and built the Deerfield detention pond under budget. The sewer rate rose just over 8.5%, as the Public Utilities Board had approved. When a 56-signature petition against a Maplewood apartment raised ditch water, staff restated the rule for every development:

“The requirement for development is the drainage from a building site cannot be any greater or more rapid than what was the case prior to development.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · May 21, 2019 · 0:25:06

In August council denied a homeowner’s request to build a garage 1 foot from the lot line, because staff said it could block a shared swale:

“And water is a major issue in Steinbach. You know, we deal with so many cases where, you know, different homes certainly in the past weren't always built to proper specifications, and decades later we're dealing with those problems.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Aug 6, 2019 · 0:15:20

The mayor backed the denial by pointing to a recent storm. It is the only mention of that storm in the meetings of this term; no report on its damage or the city’s response came to council:

“If you look at the last water event we had, 5 inches over 2 days, got a lot of emails, a lot of phone calls. We had water problems.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Aug 6, 2019 · 0:19:06

Council also applied for federal-provincial grants for three street and sewer rebuilds (Kroeker Avenue, Brandt Street and Loewen Boulevard); whether any grant came through was not reported.

2020: ponds, engineers and private property

The sewer rate rose 9.5%, Kroeker Avenue’s mains were replaced, and land for a Harmony Lane retention pond was dedicated:

“It's, uh, important in the long-term planning of a city that has the topography of a postage stamp that we have water stormwater management in place.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Apr 21, 2020 · 0:13:54

When a Home Street North resident asked that new Maplewood buildings reduce runoff, the mayor said he shared the concern but would rely on the engineers:

“We have engineers on staff. I think we need to trust them to do a good job at this”

Mayor Earl Funk · May 19, 2020 · 0:49:52

At an Ellis Avenue hearing, where neighbours said the lot floods every year, Councillor Siemens described the limit of the city’s approach:

“The problem with our development agreement and and with our water problems, standing water, is the fact that they can solve the issue on their property, but it might get pushed to other properties, and that'll just extend it. And the city does wash their hands of it somewhat because it's all private property.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Aug 18, 2020 · 1:05:28

That rezoning, and a First Street rezoning the city engineer said would worsen a neighbour’s drainage, were denied.

2021: relining Brandt Street

Council relined about a kilometre of the Brandt Street sewer with trenchless technology and replaced the water main, adding $300,000 from the utility reserve when bids came in over budget:

“when it comes to underground infrastructure, timing is key. If you wait too long, the cost goes up significantly.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Dec 15, 2020 · 0:38:22

An after-the-fact house addition 1.9 feet from the lot line was refused 6–1 over drainage and fire risk. Neighbours’ request that a new Harmony Lane phase include a concrete-bottom swale was not made a condition; staff said the earlier swale had been the developer’s idea, not a city requirement.

2022: rain, a late aid request, and the Old Tom/Loewen development

The 2022 capital program included a Loewen Boulevard sewer extension and Brookdale Crescent sewer and water replacement. After April–May rains, the province opened Disaster Financial Assistance (DFA) for overland flooding. Council passed the resolution Steinbach owners needed to apply in August, three months later:

“This was fairly general across Manitoba, and for the most part, city administration wasn't aware that there was any significant damage that occurred within Steinbach until more recently.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Aug 16, 2022 · 0:29:52

Staff expected 5 to 10 claims. In September neighbours of the 128-acre Old Tom Road and Loewen Boulevard development said the low corner and creek already flood and asked for a retention pond and an overall impact study; one mentioned sewer backups west of Hespeler Avenue, which council did not take up. Council approved all four items unanimously:

“we've had some really bad drainage things that have happened and it hasn't happened that it was engineered wrong. It was people come in and landscape, they over-landscape and then they impede water from running.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Sept 6, 2022 · 0:57:13

“I understand, you know, so many of the concerns that are raised, but I also do have to accept when there is, you know, evidence that's presented in terms of many months of work, engineering analysis both by the city and by engineers working for the developer, review by the province.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Sept 6, 2022 · 0:50:18

A new water and sewer rate plan, about 3–4% a year for 2023–2026, was sent to the Public Utilities Board.

Where members differed

Every capital, pond and rate decision was unanimous. The splits were on small drainage variances and one rezoning, with Jac Siemens the usual dissenter.
