Steinbach City Council · Sept 2025 – Sept 2026

Sewer backups: what council said, and how Steinbach compares

After sewage backed up into hundreds of basements in September 2024 and again in September 2025, what has council committed to? What reasons has it given for not going further? And is it doing as much as other cities?

Quotes come from transcripts of the meeting videos, and each links to its timestamp on YouTube. The comparison uses published municipal programs and design standards. Last updated Oct 13, 2026 (climate plan status corrected).

Bottom line

  • On big pipes and pumps, Steinbach is doing well. It is doubling Lift Station 1's capacity, twinning the force main and replacing the Millwork Drive main. More than $20 million is budgeted for wastewater in 2026, with no mill-rate increase. Most of this was planned in 2022, before the floods.
  • On helping homeowners directly, it is behind its peers. Brandon, Winnipeg, Toronto, Ottawa and Edmonton subsidize backwater valves and sump pumps. No Steinbach program was found in public sources, and none came up in a year of council meetings.
  • On finding the cause, it is behind. Sewage backing up in heavy rain means rainwater is getting into the sanitary sewer. Steinbach has not published a study of where that water enters the Georgetown system.
  • On compensation, it is in line with its peers. Canadian cities rarely pay for backups outside insurance or negligence claims. Steinbach pressed the province for disaster aid twice. Homeowners are now suing.

Key findings

1Capital work is strong, but mostly pre-planned

Steinbach is building the right kind of projects, and tenders are coming in under estimate. But the core three were on the capital plan before either flood (Reason 5). Bigger lift-station capacity helps everyone downstream. It does not by itself stop rainwater from getting into a 1988 neighbourhood line.

2The “1-in-100-year storm” argument only partly holds

Council's main reason for not doing more is that no system is built for storms this big (Reason 1). That is partly right:

  • Storm pipes are typically sized for a 1-in-5-year storm. Lethbridge, Markham, Fort Erie and Nanaimo all use this standard.
  • The overland system (streets, ponds, ditches) is expected to carry a 1-in-100-year storm. Lethbridge's standard says it “shall safely handle a one in 100 year event without causing flooding of private property or erosion damage.” That rule applies to new developments, and older areas like Georgetown were not built to it.
  • The sanitary sewer is meant to carry no rainwater at all. The City Manager said so himself, and the system was built as a closed system. When sewage backs up during rain, the usual cause is rainwater getting into it through sump pumps, weeping tile, leaky pipes and open manholes.

So a record storm explains overland flooding. It doesn't fully explain repeated sewage backups in one neighbourhood. Residents report Georgetown has had them in 2002, 2014, 2019, 2024 and 2025. That pattern points to a problem specific to that system, which is what residents asked council to investigate.

3Homeowner subsidies: the clearest gap

Backwater valves and sump pumps are widely seen as the most cost-effective protection against sewer backup. Most mid-size and large Canadian cities help pay for them. Several raised their support after major storms.

Context

Many Georgetown homes already have backwater valves, and residents say sewage still came in. A subsidy alone would not fix their problem, but it would help the rest of the city. Steinbach's 2026 sewer tenders came in roughly $1.8 million under estimate: about $0.7M on Millwork Drive, $0.9M on the force main and $0.2M on the reroute. A Brandon-style program for a few hundred homes would cost a fraction of that. This is a rough illustration, not a costed proposal.

4Sump pump enforcement arrived late, and without help

Steinbach has banned sump pumps draining into the sewer since 2003. The new 2026 bylaw finally adds inspections and fines, and council was careful not to penalize older homes. Elsewhere, enforcement usually comes with money to do the work: Halton Region covers 100% of exterior disconnection up to $6,500, and Southwest Middlesex covers 100% of sump disconnection. The homeowners' lawsuit claims the city knew about illegal connections by 2015 but relied on “education and self-regulation.” That claim is unproven.

5No study of the cause, and studies promised but not shown

Residents repeatedly asked why Georgetown backs up, and the reroute went ahead without a study (Reason 5). Flow sensors and a drainage master plan were listed only as “could be looked at in the future.” Measuring flows in the affected sewers before and after a fix is how most cities tell whether it worked.

6Compensation: normal practice, now in court

Canadian cities generally don't pay for sewer backups unless they are found negligent. Steinbach's choice to press the province for disaster aid is the usual route (Reason 3). Council did more than most here, passing two resolutions and challenging the Premier publicly. Homeowners filed suit in Manitoba's Court of King's Bench on Aug 13, 2026, alleging negligence and nuisance. SteinbachOnline reports 13 plaintiffs; CTV reports 22. The claims include late activation of the auxiliary pumps and new subdivisions tied into a strained line. None of this has been proven, and the suit hasn't come up in any recorded council meeting.

What “doing all it can” would add

Based on what peer cities do, these are the steps Steinbach hasn't taken, roughly from cheapest to most involved:

  • A backwater valve and sump pump subsidy modelled on Brandon's, retroactive to the 2024 flood.
  • Funded sump pump and weeping-tile disconnection alongside the new bylaw's inspections.
  • Flow monitoring and smoke and dye testing of the Georgetown system, with results made public. This would also show whether the reroute worked.
  • Adopting and publishing the climate risk assessment, with a written flood plan and dates.
  • An independent engineering review of why Georgetown backs up, which residents have asked for since October 2025.
