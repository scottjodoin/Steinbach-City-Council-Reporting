Jac Siemens: sewer backups, flooding and drainage

What did Jac Siemens say and do about basement sewer backups, flooding and drainage in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Jac Siemens moved the 2016 exception that let a commercial subdivision use roadside swales instead of curb, gutter and storm sewer, against staff's precedent warning, and later explained the Elmdale Drain's return to the 2018 budget. In the following terms he was the member most likely to vote against denying a small variance on drainage grounds, preferring a compromise or a fine. He also said in 2020 that development agreements fix water on one lot but can push it onto neighbours, while the city 'does wash their hands of it somewhat because it's all private property.'

He explained Lift Station 2 as the way to let east-side subdivisions proceed, and in April 2024 said Steinbach had 'a lot fewer issues' with infrastructure than other communities, while some renewal projects were spread into later years. He moved the November 2024 request for disaster aid and in 2025 asked why it was denied when the city seemed to meet the criteria. In 2026 he gave the only public account of council's two-day flood study session, voted alone against the Bridgepark building (on height, not sewers), and said 'we can't build pipes big enough to take all the water all the time.' He did not run in 2026.

“we can't build pipes big enough to take all the water all the time. There's just not financially feasible.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · At the Giesbrecht Street hearing, September 2026 · Sept 1, 2026 · 0:42:49

Term by term

2014–2018

Moved the one-time swale exception for the Steinbach North commercial subdivision ('a one-off'); supported the golf club land purchase citing the course's flood-mitigation spending; explained the Elmdale Drain rebuild's return to the 2018 budget; voted to deny the Maplewood rezoning over overland flows toward Home Street.

Took part in 9 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Voted against the denials of the Alexander Way and Coral Crescent variances (wanting a compromise or a fine), and against a First Street variance because more hard surface could worsen known water problems. Moved the Deerfield detention pond, under budget. Said in 2020 that the city stays out of lot-to-lot water because it is private property.

Took part in 17 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Tied Lift Station 2 and the force main to east-side subdivisions. Before the 2024 storm said Steinbach had fewer infrastructure problems than other communities. Moved the 2024 disaster-aid request and the climate-plan grant application; questioned the 2025 denial. Described the November 2025 flood study session, asked how residents would be told what to do with pumped water under the new by-law, voted alone against Bridgepark on height, and said bigger pipes for every storm are not financially feasible.

Took part in 25 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Practical and cost-minded throughout: comfortable with lighter drainage standards and small variances, and frank that the city cannot build for every storm. He asked the sharpest question about the 2024 aid denial.
