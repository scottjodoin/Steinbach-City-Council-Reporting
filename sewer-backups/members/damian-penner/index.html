Sewer backups and flooding · Council member

Damian Penner: sewer backups, flooding and drainage

What did Damian Penner say and do about basement sewer backups, flooding and drainage in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Damian Penner argued from his first term that a city 'with the topography of a postage stamp' needs stormwater management, and that redeveloped lots usually improve drainage for their neighbours because they must have approved drainage plans. He moved the 2022 resolution asking for Disaster Financial Assistance after spring flooding, crediting residents who told the city about the damage. In 2023 he and Jake Hiebert voted against holding development and sewer impact fees to 5% a year, saying growth-related capacity should not fall on the mill rate.

After the storms he moved the 2026 budget and described its flood plan, saying 'over $20 million' was budgeted for wastewater; he moved the Millwork Drive and Clear Spring Greens contracts and the Land Drainage By-law's third reading ('first educate and then enforce'), and pressed the province on aid. At the Bridgepark hearing he said council 'can't stop developing the city in perpetuity' until residents feel okay with it.

“the largest focus in this budget is the renewal of our wastewater system, with over $20 million budgeted for wastewater renewal and new infrastructure.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Moving the 2026 budget · Mar 17, 2026 · 0:38:22

Term by term

2018–2022

Spoke for stormwater control in a flat city (Harmony Lane pond, 2020); wanted a 2020 First Street rezoning heard in public so the developer could explain how it would handle drainage; moved the 2022 disaster-aid resolution; said engineered canals had improved water flow near the Old Tom/Loewen development.

Took part in 15 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Voted against 5% development and sewer impact fees at every reading (2023). Moved the Water Services Board request for the force main design (2024) and highlighted the community plan's no-added-runoff rule. After 2025 he moved the 2026 budget, the Millwork Drive and reroute contracts and the drainage by-law's third reading; called a second aid denial unacceptable; said at Bridgepark that development cannot wait indefinitely.

Took part in 23 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistent: growth should pay for its capacity, engineered development improves drainage, and the answer to flooding is capital work rather than slowing development. After 2025 he became the main presenter of council's flood plan.
