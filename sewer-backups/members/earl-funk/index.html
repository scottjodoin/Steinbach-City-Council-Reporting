Sewer backups and flooding · Council member

Earl Funk: sewer backups, flooding and drainage

What did Earl Funk say and do about basement sewer backups, flooding and drainage in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

In short

As a councillor, Earl Funk said drainage in older neighbourhoods was the complaint he heard most. He refused to waive a homeowner's drainage plan in 2016, asked for costed options for the Home Street North swale in 2017, and voted to deny the Maplewood rezoning in 2018, saying 'Drainage is a big one for me.' As mayor from 2018 he chaired every item in this record. He often said Steinbach is flat and water must be watched, but at hearings he deferred to the city's engineers, and in 2022 he blamed recent backyard flooding on residents who 'over-landscape.'

He mentioned a 2019 storm of '5 inches over 2 days' only in passing, and after the September 2024 storm he asked no questions of the staff report. After the 2025 storm he promised council would 'leave no stone unturned', called the 2026 budget the delivery of that promise, and led council's challenges to the province's two refusals of disaster aid. At development hearings in 2026 he said the sewer system is designed for growth, that no city can prepare for a 1-in-125 rain, and that one neighbour's drainage problem seemed isolated to their properties. He did not answer residents' question about compensation or commit to the independent study they asked for.

“We'll leave no stone unturned to see what we can do to make this work.”

Mayor Earl Funk · The meeting after the September 2025 storm · Sept 16, 2025 · 0:04:06

Term by term

2014–2018

Said drainage in older areas was the most common concern brought to him and opposed waiving a homeowner's drainage plan ('the water that we don't take care of here becomes someone else's problem'). Seconded the 2017 motion for costed options on the Home Street North swale, and voted to deny the 2018 Maplewood rezoning partly because Home Street drainage was already not working.

Took part in 7 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Chaired every item as mayor. Cited a recent '5 inches over 2 days' storm and its many complaints when supporting the denial of a small variance (2019), but no report on that storm came to council. Said he shared neighbours' drainage worries but that council must trust its engineers (2020), and in 2022 said recent backyard flooding came from residents over-landscaping, not bad engineering.

Took part in 10 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Received the September 2024 storm report without questions. After the September 2025 storm promised council would 'leave no stone unturned' and pointed to the coming climate plan as a 'roadmap'; said the 2026 budget delivered on the promise. Called the province's disaster-aid criteria 'very vague' and backed the request to reopen both files. At the 2026 development hearings he kept debate to the application, said the sewer system is designed for growth and that no city can prepare for a 1-in-125 rain; every development he chaired was approved.

Took part in 32 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From the councillor who called drainage his biggest complaint to the mayor who chaired a slow first response in 2024 and a faster one after 2025. His approach to development stayed the same throughout: drainage is settled by engineers in the development agreement, not by refusing a project.
