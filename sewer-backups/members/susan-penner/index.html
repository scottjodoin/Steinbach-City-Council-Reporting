Sewer backups and flooding · Council member

Susan Penner: sewer backups, flooding and drainage

What did Susan Penner say and do about basement sewer backups, flooding and drainage in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

In short

In 2016 Susan Penner asked in her opening remarks whether drainage should be built for 'the worst-case but rare scenario' or for average rainfall with a buffer, because on flat land 'the rare but major storm' overwhelms the system. For years after that she mostly moved and seconded routine items, supported infill development, and backed lower development and sewer impact fees in 2023 for affordability.

Her Georgetown home flooded in both 2024 and 2025. At the first meeting after the 2025 storm she rejected calling the storms outliers, said many neighbours had flooded several times, and asked for action 'Not in the 5-year plan, not in the 10-year plan, but now.' She backed the Georgetown delegation ('We need safety from water too'), moved the request asking the province to explain its disaster-aid criteria, and said many residents could not get insurance after flooding. She also supported the Bridgepark building near Georgetown, saying growth and flood work are 'both', not 'one or the other.'

“There is a drainage issue and we need to do something about it. Not in the 5-year plan, not in the 10-year plan, but now.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Opening remarks after her basement flooded a second time · Sept 16, 2025 · 0:00:00

Term by term

2014–2018

Raised the design question of building drainage for rare major storms (2016). Moved the First Street infill rezoning and variance in 2018, and was the only vote against denying the Maplewood rezoning, saying drainage could be handled in the development agreement.

Took part in 5 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Supported sewer renewal on cost grounds ('timing is key'), treated a new lift station as a cost of development, asked how disaster aid relates to private insurance (2022), and called the Old Tom/Loewen land hard to engineer because of water but supported it.

Took part in 14 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Backed 5% development and sewer impact fees in 2023 and framed the 2025 force main as growth capacity. After her own basement flooded twice, demanded urgent action, told Georgetown residents council was '100% committed', moved the disaster-aid clarification and the force main contract, seconded the 2026 budget, and supported the Bridgepark building ('It's both').

Took part in 19 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

The clearest change of any member: from routine support for growth-related sewer work to the council's most urgent voice on flooding after 2025. She still voted with the majority on every development, arguing flood work and growth can go together.
