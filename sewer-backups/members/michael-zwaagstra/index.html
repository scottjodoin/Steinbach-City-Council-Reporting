Sewer backups and flooding · Council member

Michael Zwaagstra: sewer backups, flooding and drainage

What did Michael Zwaagstra say and do about basement sewer backups, flooding and drainage in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Michael Zwaagstra moved or seconded more sewer and drainage items than any other member: street rebuilds with new mains, Lift Station 2, the second force main to the lagoon, the Lift Station 1 upgrade, the Land Drainage By-law and most development approvals. He was the most consistent voice for following staff when they warned about drainage, moving denials of small variances and one rezoning in 2019, 2020 and 2021 ('water is a major issue in Steinbach'). In 2023 he cut staff's proposed yearly increase in development and sewer impact fees from 7.5% to 5% for affordability, while saying even 7.5% would not cover the cost of growth.

After the 2025 storm he called the Clear Spring Greens reroute 'huge' and the pace of the response 'lightning speed', said the province's disaster-aid decision looked 'political', and moved the request to reopen both files. When residents asked council to hold back development until flooding was fixed, he said a site outside the Georgetown sewer catchment could not be refused 'because of what happened in a different area', and that a McKenzie Avenue backup was caused by a blockage, not by density. He did not run in 2026.

“this is not part of the Georgetown catchment area. … We can't say no to a development that happens to be nearby because of what happened in a different area”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · At the Bridgepark Manor hearing, July 2026 · Jul 21, 2026 · 0:54:01

Term by term

2014–2018

Opposed letting a commercial subdivision use open ditches instead of storm sewers, as a precedent (2016); argued a binding lot-grading plan, not a refusal, would protect drainage on Fernwood Bay; asked what a Home Street North fix would cost. At the 2018 First Street hearing he said developers pay for sewer upgrades and staff will not sign off on direct flooding. Moved to deny the Maplewood rezoning for lack of a clear plan.

Took part in 9 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Moved most sewer capital work (Elmdale, Kroeker, Brandt Street relining: 'not doing this project is not an option'; the 2022 capital program). Moved the denials of the Alexander Way and Coral Crescent variances and the First Street rezoning when staff or the city engineer warned of drainage, and approved large developments where engineers had signed off, including Old Tom/Loewen in 2022.

Took part in 18 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Moved Lift Station 2, the force main and Lift Station 1 work, all framed as basic infrastructure and capacity. Amended development and sewer impact fees down to 5% a year. After the storms he backed the reroute, moved the request to reopen the disaster-aid files ('We're just asking to be treated fairly') and second reading of the Land Drainage By-law, saying the city does not intend to penalize owners who add backup protection. Moved approval of the Bridgepark, McKenzie, Williams Creek and Giesbrecht developments over residents' flooding objections.

Took part in 29 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

The same approach throughout: fund pipes as basic infrastructure, follow staff on drainage, and keep drainage questions out of decisions on whether a development goes ahead. After 2025 he defended the response as fast rather than arguing for more.
