Sewer backups and flooding · Council members

Council members on sewer backups and flooding

How did each council member vote and speak on basement sewer backups, flooding and drainage in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. Every vote on the 2024 and 2025 storms, disaster aid, the flood plan and the drainage by-law was unanimous, so the differences between members show in what they said, what they moved, and a few split votes on development drainage and growth fees.

  • Susan Penner changed most. She asked in 2016 whether drainage should be built for rare major storms; after her own basement flooded in 2024 and 2025 she demanded action “now”, while still backing nearby development.
  • Michael Zwaagstra and Damian Penner carried the response. They moved most of the capital work, the budget and the drainage by-law, and argued that flooding elsewhere was no reason to refuse a development.
  • Jac Siemens was the cost realist and the usual dissenter on small drainage variances; he questioned the 2024 aid refusal and said the city cannot afford pipes for every storm. He and Zwaagstra left council in 2026.
  • Mayor Earl Funk called drainage his biggest complaint as a councillor, received the 2024 storm report without questions, then promised to “leave no stone unturned” in 2025 and led the challenges to the province.
  • Damian Penner and Jake Hiebert voted against holding growth fees, which pay for sewer and lagoon capacity, to 5% a year in 2023.

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

As a councillor he called drainage his most common complaint; as mayor he promised to 'leave no stone unturned' after the 2025 storm, pressed the province on disaster aid, and kept development hearings to the application in front of council.

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Framed sewer renewal as staying 'ahead of problems'; sent residents' drainage requests to staff reports and relied on development agreements and the net-zero outflow rule.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Moved most sewer capital decisions for a decade; deferred to staff when they flagged drainage, called the 2026 pace 'lightning speed', and argued flooding was no reason to refuse development outside the Georgetown sewer area.

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Presented sewer work as capacity for growth; the usual dissenter on small drainage variances; after the storms he questioned the aid denial and said the city cannot afford pipes big enough for every storm.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

Asked in 2016 whether drainage should be built for the rare major storm; after her own basement flooded in 2024 and 2025, the strongest voice for acting 'now', while still backing nearby development.

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Moved the 2022 disaster-aid request and the 2026 budget that put over $20 million into wastewater; voted for higher growth fees in 2023; said development cannot wait until residents feel okay.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Mostly seconded sewer and drainage items, including the 2024 disaster-aid request and the reroute contract; said in 2026 that drainage is a big issue after two years of major rains.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Council's watershed district representative and chair; voted against low growth fees in 2023; said he had been flooded himself but backed the Bridgepark building.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

First to ask for a culvert at Home Street North; council's conservation district voice; opposed a 2018 infill variance citing runoff and basements flooded in past storms.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Seconded the 2016 swale exception, saying swales work well; as Deputy Mayor told Maplewood residents the city regulates drainage, then voted to deny the rezoning.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; no record on this topic yet. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.

Most decisions were unanimous, and councillors take turns moving routine motions, so moving a motion is not the same as championing it. Split votes are the clearest evidence of where members differed. None was on the storm response itself.
