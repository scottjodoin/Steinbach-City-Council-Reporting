Sewer backups and flooding · Council member

Jake Hiebert: sewer backups, flooding and drainage

What did Jake Hiebert say and do about basement sewer backups, flooding and drainage in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Jake Hiebert was council's representative on the Seine Rat Roseau Watershed District from 2018 and became its chair, reporting on regional projects that hold water back to prevent downstream flooding; the mayor credited him for a water-detention project at the museum. At hearings he asked about runoff from the south and a problem culvert (2022), and moved a 2023 variance denial because poor drainage could harm undeveloped neighbouring land.

In 2023 he and Damian Penner voted against holding development and sewer impact fees to 5% a year, calling it unfair that taxpayers pay the difference. After the storms he moved first reading of the Land Drainage By-law, said Steinbach had 'gone by the rules' and should get disaster aid, and at the Bridgepark hearing said he lives in the area and has been flooded, before voting for the building.

“I happen to live in the area. I've been flooded.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert · At the Bridgepark Manor hearing, July 2026 · Jul 21, 2026 · 1:03:13

Term by term

2018–2022

Watershed district representative and chair; moved the by-law joining the new district. Voted against a 2019 First Street variance; asked about runoff and the Bush Farm Road culvert in Lexington Village; supported the Old Tom/Loewen development as better engineered (medium-confidence identification).

Took part in 11 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Moved the watershed district expansion; voted against 5% development and sewer impact fees at every reading; moved a variance denial over drainage. After the storms moved first reading of the drainage by-law, backed the request to reopen the aid files, and voted for Bridgepark while saying he had been flooded.

Took part in 19 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Focused on regional water management and on growth paying its share; after 2025 he spoke of his own flooding but, like the majority, did not link it to development decisions.
