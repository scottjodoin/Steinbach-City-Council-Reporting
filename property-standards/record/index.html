Every decision and debate on property standards

What has Steinbach City Council decided, debated and promised about property standards and enforcement since December 2015, and how did each item end?

In short

Each row is one decision, debate, promise or request on the topic, from the minutes and the meeting videos. Filter by term, year, theme, council member or result. “No public result” means we found no public decision or follow-up; some work happens in closed study sessions and committees.

YearDecision or debateResult
2016One community services officer enforces city by-laws throughout 2016–2020Every financial plan presentation from 2016 to 2020 listed a single community services officer within protective services, alongside 16 RCMP officers and about 40 firefighters. In 2017 the presentation described the officer as the person 'who enforces city bylaws'. Council made no change to by-law enforcement staffing in this period.Done Staffing stayed at one officer across both terms; no motion or request to add officers was found. Weed, parking, animal and other by-law work fell to the same officer (2020 resident referred to him as 'the bylaws officer').
2016–20Community services officer appointed weed inspector each year (routine)As required by The Noxious Weeds Act, council each year appointed the city's community services officer, Trevor Schellenberg, as municipal weed inspector. The items passed without debate beyond questions about the provincial cosmetic-pesticide ban (2016) and training (2016).Done Appointed for 2016 (transcript), 2018 (R18-052), 2019 (R19-121) and 2020 (R20-038), all unanimous. No 2017 appointment was found (the Apr 4, 2017 video is missing and no 2017 minutes before July exist). No enforcement figures were reported with any appointment.R18-052; R19-121; R20-038 · vote: unanimous
2017Province's Community Safety Officer program letter sent to the RCMP advisory groupCouncil received a Manitoba Justice letter on the new Community Safety Officer program. Councillor Funk asked what such officers would cost compared with the province-funded community constables; the City Manager said CSOs have limited powers and he was unsure Steinbach's conditions matched Thompson's pilot. The letter was taken as information and sent to the RCMP advisory group.No public result No report back or decision on community safety officers was found in transcripts through Dec 2020.vote: consensus
2017Administrative Penalty By-law 2076 adopted, covering parking and traffic fines onlyBecause the province's Municipal By-law Enforcement Act moved parking tickets out of provincial court, council adopted By-law 2076 (first reading Apr 11, 2017; second June 20; third July 4) to handle penalty notices with a municipal adjudicator. It applied at first to the traffic and accessible parking by-laws; By-law 2091 (Jan–Feb 2018) added the parking meter by-law. No property-standards, junk or weed by-law was added to the penalty schedule in this period.Done Adopted July 4, 2017 (transcript); amended by By-law 2091 (R18-010, R18-011 Jan 16, 2018; R18-024 Feb 6, 2018; unanimous). The penalty system was not extended to yard or property upkeep by Dec 2020.R18-010; R18-011; R18-024 (By-law 2091) · vote: unanimous
2018Resident asks who enforces weed by-laws on a vacant Main Street lot; no answer givenAt the July 17, 2018 hearing on the new zoning by-law and community plan, M.G. pointed to weeds on a vacant property on Main Street and an unsigned car lot, and asked whether anyone was enforcing the by-laws. Staff answered other questions; the enforcement question was not addressed.No public result No response on the record at the hearing or later in 2018.
2018Maplewood St rezoning (By-law 2101) denied 5–1 after neighbours say the owner let his rental houses run downAn owner of a row of old rental houses at 135–191 Maplewood St asked to rezone them for 44 townhouses; his representative called most of the houses derelict. Neighbours (49 objection letters, 80-plus signatures) said he had owned some for up to 10 years without maintaining them and had not earned their trust. Council denied second reading 5–1; members cited unclear plans, drainage and lack of trust, while Councillor Susan Penner argued the new complex would be professionally maintained.Done Second reading denied (R18-191), so the by-law died. Council did not use any property-standards tool on the existing houses; Councillor Fehr said the properties 'really need to have some attention'.R18-191 · vote: 5-1 (against: Susan Penner)
2018Residents say neighbours leave garbage outside for months; mayor rules it off topicAt the Dec 4, 2018 hearing on renewing the garbage collection levy, two residents objected to the flat fee and also complained of litter and of neighbours leaving household garbage outside for months, drawing animals. The mayor said they were off topic; council renewed the levy unanimously without addressing the upkeep complaint.No public result Levy by-law given second reading; the garbage-left-outside complaint got no response or referral.vote: unanimous (levy renewal)
2019Neighbours' complaints about a run-down rental at 126 Maplewood; council approves a 9-unit replacementAt the hearing on By-law 2120 to rezone 126 Maplewood St for a 9-unit building, a 56-name petition opposed it. Council approved second reading unanimously, saying a new building would replace an old, run-down one.Declined Neighbours' request to refuse was declined: second reading carried unanimously (R19-118, Zwaagstra/Siemens). 2019Councillor asks about the state of a rental at an 8-plex hearing; owner says upkeep is the tenant's jobAt a hearing for an 8-unit building on a C-1 lot at Elm Ave and Hanover St, Councillor Zwaagstra raised objectors' concerns about the current state of the property. The owner, who had owned it about two years, said his tenant knew they had to keep it up. Council denied the conditional use and variance unanimously for lack of consultation and neighbourhood character, not upkeep.No public result Application denied (R19-140, R19-141, unanimous). The upkeep question got no follow-up; a Councillor Hiebert said the 'older place' was 'not well maintained'.R19-140 · vote: unanimous
2020Dwelling Units Safety Standards By-law 2157 given first reading (smoke alarms, CO alarms, escape exits)After a May 12, 2020 Strategic Priorities Committee report that a growing number of homes, mainly rental units, did not meet the Manitoba Building and Fire Codes or the zoning by-law, council directed staff to draft a by-law. On Sept 1, 2020 council unanimously gave By-law 2157 first reading. Members said it was 15 to 20 years in the making, limited to basic life-safety items (smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms, egress), and that the city did not know where all secondary suites were.Done First reading Sept 1, 2020 (R20-167). Second and third readings were given Jan 5, 2021 (R21-007, Zwaagstra/Siemens; R21-008, Siemens/J. Hiebert; both unanimous), just after this period. No public input on the by-law was found in the Sept–Dec 2020 transcripts or minutes; the Sept 15, 2020 meeting video is not available.R20-167 · vote: unanimous
2020First St rezoning approved; derelict houses to be replaced by apartmentsAt the hearing on By-law 2148 (78 First St, 10 units), a nearby resident welcomed the removal of derelict houses on First Street but worried about the pace of development. Councillor Siemens said the area's derelict housing showed it was under revitalization, as the community plan intended. Council gave second and third readings unanimously.Done Second and third readings carried unanimously (R20-127, R20-128), subject to a development agreement.R20-127 · vote: unanimous
2020–21Dwelling Safety Standards By-law 2157 adopted: alarms and safe exits in every home, aimed at secondary suitesCouncil gave second and third reading to By-law 2157 on Jan 5, 2021 (first reading Sept 1, 2020). It sets minimum life-safety standards for every dwelling unit: smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms and a safe exit, with secondary (basement) suites the main target. Staff said compliance was expected to be mostly voluntary, with inspections when owners or tenants ask; council members said the rules were basic and not onerous.Done Adopted unanimously. A public awareness campaign followed in 2021. By Aug 2024 staff reported only 'several dozen' of possibly several hundred suites had come forward, and announced proactive enforcement (see 2024-secondary-suite-enforcement).R21-007 (2nd), R21-008 (3rd) · vote: unanimous
2021–26Community services/safety officer appointed weed inspector every yearEach spring council appointed the city's community services officer (from 2026, two community safety officers) as municipal weed inspector under the provincial Noxious Weeds Act. Members treated it as housekeeping.Done Appointed every year 2021-2026: Trevor Schellenberg (2021-2023), Grant Ryland (2024-2025), Grant Ryland and Kevin Williams (2026). No report on weed complaints or orders came to council.R21-056, R22-073, R23-048, R24-052, R25-076, 'R25-052' (2026) · vote: unanimous each year
2021–26By-law enforcement staffing: one community services officer, two by 2026Financial plan presentations in 2021 and 2025 listed a single community services officer 'to enforce city bylaws'. In 2023 council set up a self-funded Community Safety Officer program (for vehicle weight limits). By March 2026 two community safety officers were appointed weed inspectors, and in July 2026 staff proposed buying a second CSO truck with a provincial safety grant; council deferred the grant to committee.Partly A second officer was added by 2026. The grant allocation (including the $65,000 truck) was deferred to a committee meeting; no public decision found by Nov 2026. Council received no enforcement statistics (complaints, notices, cleanups) in this period.R23-036 (CSO program) · vote: unanimous
2021Administrative Penalty By-law 2173: more by-laws brought under ticket-style penalty noticesBy-law 2173 amended Schedule A of the Administrative Penalty By-law 2076 so that more city by-laws can be enforced with penalty notices under the provincial Municipal By-law Enforcement Act. Staff said it gives 'additional tools to our bylaw and animal control'; Councillor Zwaagstra said it lets fines be adjudicated more quickly.Done First reading May 4, second and third June 1, 2021, all unanimous. Which by-laws were added was not stated in the meeting; the existing report notes the Dwelling Safety Standards by-law is on the penalty schedule ($1,000, $500 early payment) while the Junk, Weeds & Nuisance By-law 1663 is not.R21-092 (1st), R21-121 (2nd), R21-122 (3rd) · vote: unanimous
2021–26Neighbours raise run-down rentals, long grass and garbage at development hearingsAt hearings from 2021 to 2026 neighbours judged proposals by how existing rentals on or near the site were kept: rentals with police visits (Hanover St 2021, Third St 2023), low upkeep and blowing garbage (First St and Home St 2024), 18-inch grass and weeds (Henry St 2025), garbage spilling onto a church yard (McKenzie Ave 2026) and renters who 'aren't going to look after it' (Giesbrecht St 2026). Council decided each file on land-use grounds; none led to a property-standards response.No public result Outcomes: Hanover St variance approved 2021 (R21-189); Third St rezoning denied 2023 (R23-160, unanimous, on density/consultation grounds); Home St second reading 4-3 2024 (R24-061); First St variance approved 2024 (R24-063); Henry St variance and rezoning approved 2025 (R25-093, R25-094/095); McKenzie Ave approved 2026 (R26-068); Giesbrecht St second reading 2026 (R26-179). No enforcement review or report followed.R21-189, R23-160, R24-061, R24-063, R25-093/094/095, R26-068, R26-179 · vote: unanimous except R24-061 4-3 (against: D. Penner, J. Hiebert, Zwaagstra)

2024Voluntary compliance with By-law 2157 judged insufficient; proactive suite enforcement and $1,000/day fines backedOn Aug 20, 2024 the City Manager reported that of possibly several hundred secondary suites only several dozen had come forward since 2021, and that several recent house fires involved non-compliant basement suites. The city would contact owners believed to have suites, place work orders on title if needed, possibly decommission suites, and run a media campaign. Every member spoke in support, including fines of up to $1,000 a day; Councillor Siemens warned low-rent units could be lost.No public result No resolution (an update; minutes record members 'voiced their support'). No report on suites contacted, inspected, ordered, decommissioned or fined came back to council through Nov 3, 2026.vote: consensus
