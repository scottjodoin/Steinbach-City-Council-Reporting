Property standards · Council member

Susan Penner: property standards and enforcement

What did Susan Penner say and do about property standards and their enforcement in Steinbach (yards, garbage, rental buildings, secondary suites) between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

In short

Susan Penner cast the only recorded vote against council's majority on this topic. In August 2018 she voted against denying the Maplewood Street rezoning, acknowledging 'broken trust' over the owner's upkeep but arguing the new complex would be maintained by the company rather than unit by unit.

She seconded first reading of By-law 2157 in 2020, saying 'everybody has a right to safe housing' and that the rules also protect firefighters, and in 2021 valued the new route for tenants to request an inspection. In 2024 she called the low voluntary uptake 'disappointing' and supported enforcement. In July 2026 she asked that the provincial safety grant, including a second officer's truck, go to committee before a decision.

“Yes, I would just like to comment that everybody has a right to safe housing.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Seconding first reading of the Dwelling Safety Standards By-law · Sept 1, 2020 · 0:22:59

Term by term

2014–2018

She was the only member to vote against denying the 2018 Maplewood Street rezoning, arguing that a professionally managed complex would fix the upkeep problems neighbours described.

Took part in 2 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

She seconded first reading of By-law 2157 (2020), saying everyone has a right to safe housing and that firefighters need proper exits, and in 2021 welcomed the by-law as 'an avenue for tenants' to make sure they live in a safe situation.

Took part in 6 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

She moved the routine weed-inspector appointments in 2024 and 2026. In August 2024 she said it was 'disappointing' that only a few dozen landlords had come forward and backed enforcement for tenants and firefighters. In July 2026 she asked to discuss the $200,000 safety grant, including a $65,000 truck for the second officer, at committee.

Took part in 2 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

She framed property standards as a matter of tenants' safety from the start, and as Deputy Mayor from 2026 carries that record into the new council.
