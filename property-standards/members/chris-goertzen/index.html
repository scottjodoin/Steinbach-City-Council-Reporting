Property standards · Council member

Chris Goertzen: property standards and enforcement

What did Chris Goertzen say and do about property standards and their enforcement in Steinbach (yards, garbage, rental buildings, secondary suites) between December 2015 and November 2026?

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

In short

Property standards were rarely on council's agenda while Chris Goertzen was mayor. The one community services officer enforced all city by-laws and was reappointed weed inspector without debate.

In 2017 he backed the Administrative Penalty By-law 2076, saying it would lessen the cost of court cases and 'streamline the whole process'. It applied to parking and traffic fines; no yard, junk or weed rule was added. The same year his council took a provincial letter on community safety officers as information and sent it to the RCMP advisory group.

“it also will lessen the challenges for municipalities and specifically Steinbach when it comes to costs incurred in regards to court cases. And so this should streamline the whole process.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · On the new administrative penalty by-law · Jun 20, 2017 · 1:00:12

Term by term

2014–2018

He chaired the 2017 adoption of By-law 2076, which moved parking and traffic tickets out of provincial court, and the referral of the province's community safety officer letter to the RCMP advisory group. The Maplewood Street hearing of August 2018 was chaired by the deputy mayor. No property-standards by-law was proposed in his term.

The trajectory

His council built the ticketing system Steinbach still uses, but used it only for parking and traffic.
