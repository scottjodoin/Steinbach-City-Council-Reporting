Property standards · Council member

Earl Funk: property standards and enforcement

What did Earl Funk say and do about property standards and their enforcement in Steinbach (yards, garbage, rental buildings, secondary suites) between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

In short

As a councillor, Earl Funk asked in 2017 what provincial community safety officers would cost the city, and in 2018 voted to deny a Maplewood Street rezoning because neighbours no longer trusted the owner to look after his rental houses. As mayor from November 2018 he chaired every property-standards item, including a December 2018 hearing where he ruled residents' complaints about garbage left outside 'off topic'.

He backed By-law 2157 from the start, calling its alarm and exit rules provincial standards that are 'not onerous' and predicting full compliance could take 'maybe a decade or two'. In August 2024 he supported proactive enforcement and fines of $1,000 a day, answering worries about lost rentals by saying alarms and exits are 'small, small costs'. At development hearings he more often pointed to redevelopment, fenced dumpsters, the city's bin rules or the RCMP than to the junk and weeds by-law.

“And even though the fines are significant, the value of life is very significant. And that's why I think I would support these strong fines of $1,000 per day for noncompliance.”

Mayor Earl Funk · On proactive enforcement of the suite-safety by-law · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:18:06

Term by term

2014–2018

As a councillor he asked in March 2017 what the province's new community safety officers would cost the city, compared with province-funded community constables; the letter went to the RCMP advisory group and nothing more came of it. In August 2018 he voted with the majority to deny the Maplewood Street rezoning, saying the neighbourhood's lack of trust in the owner had to be repaired first.

Took part in 3 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Elected mayor in November 2018. He ruled residents' complaints about garbage left outside for months off topic at a December 2018 levy hearing, and in 2019 backed a nine-unit building because it would replace a run-down rental. He supported By-law 2157 in 2020 and 2021, calling the standards provincial and 'not onerous' and expecting compliance to take 'a decade or two'. In March 2022 he told a resident that littering near apartments was a matter for the RCMP and that the new building's dumpster would be fenced.

Took part in 6 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

In August 2024 he backed proactive enforcement of By-law 2157 and fines of $1,000 a day, and answered Councillor Siemens's fear of losing low-rent units by saying the costs of alarms and exits are small. In 2025 he told a Henry Street neighbour that a new building's dumpster would end loose garbage, and in October 2026 that the city has 'strict regulations' on bins. No report on the 2024 enforcement came to the council he chaired.

Took part in 3 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

He moved from voting on how one owner kept his rentals to backing a city-wide safety rule with real fines, while still treating yard and garbage complaints at hearings as something redevelopment would solve.
