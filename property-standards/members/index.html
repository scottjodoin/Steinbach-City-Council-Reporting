Property standards · Council members

Council members on property standards

How did each council member speak and vote on yards, garbage, rental upkeep, secondary-suite safety and by-law enforcement in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. Council divided only once on an item in this report, so the differences between members show up mostly in what they said. Most motions here are routine, and councillors take turns moving them.

  • Suite safety had everyone’s vote. Jac Siemens moved By-law 2157 in 2020 and Michael Zwaagstra moved its second reading in 2021; all seven members voted for it. In 2024 every member who spoke backed enforcing it.
  • Different emphases. Susan Penner framed suite safety as a tenant’s right and a firefighter’s protection; Jake Hiebert was “disgusted” by unsafe suites; Bill Hiebert wanted the rule to go further; Jac Siemens named the cost to low-rent housing; Mayor Earl Funk called the costs “small.”
  • Redevelopment as the answer. Faced with run-down rentals at hearings, the mayor and Councillors Zwaagstra, Siemens and Damian Penner pointed to new buildings replacing old ones. No member proposed a property standards or derelict-building by-law, rental licensing, or adding yards to the ticket schedule.
  • The one split. In 2018 Susan Penner was alone in voting against denying the Maplewood Street rezoning, which neighbours opposed over an owner’s upkeep of his rentals.

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

Backed the 2021 suite-safety by-law and, in 2024, $1,000-a-day fines; at hearings he usually answered upkeep complaints with redevelopment, new dumpsters or the RCMP.

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Backed the 2017 administrative penalty by-law to cut court costs; property upkeep itself did not come to council in his term except at one rezoning hearing.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Moved the suite-safety by-law and most penalty-schedule updates, calling minimum safety standards 'fair' and life safety 'absolutely fundamental'.

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Moved the suite-safety by-law, saying it had been '15 to 20 years' in coming, and gave council's clearest reason for going slow: the fear of losing low-rent units.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

Argued for suite safety as a tenant's right and a firefighter's protection, and cast the only vote against denying a 2018 rezoning that neighbours opposed over rental upkeep.

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Set out the city's 'educate, then enforce, then penalize' approach in 2026 and described infill as the fix for areas 'neglected for years'.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Supported enforcing the suite-safety by-law in 2024 and suggested going further, with egress windows in family basements too.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Said he was 'disgusted' by the unsafe secondary suites he saw while campaigning in 2018 and was '100% in favor' of enforcement in 2024.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

Seconded the 2018 denial of the Maplewood Street rezoning while saying the run-down row of houses 'really need to have some attention'.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Chaired the 2018 Maplewood Street hearing as Deputy Mayor; said the project would renew a neighbourhood that needs help, but voted to deny it.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; no record on this topic yet. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.
