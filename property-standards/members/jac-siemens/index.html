Property standards · Council member

Jac Siemens: property standards and enforcement

What did Jac Siemens say and do about property standards and their enforcement in Steinbach (yards, garbage, rental buildings, secondary suites) between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Jac Siemens moved first reading of By-law 2157 in September 2020 and gave its fullest explanation: the issue had been discussed 'likely dating back 15 to 20 years', the city did not know where all secondary suites were, and renters in substandard units would likely pay more once landlords upgraded. In January 2021 he noted the estimated cost of about $3,000 a suite and said the by-law 'protects some of the marginalized'.

In August 2024 he supported enforcement but explained why council had held off: 'potentially there will be a loss of many low-end rental units'. He also tied derelict houses to redevelopment, welcoming demolition permits in 2024 because 'derelict homes' would come out, and in 2025 asked whether the city could limit vehicles per rental unit.

“I think one of the reasons that council has held off to put compliance and enforcement in play is potentially there will be a loss of many low-end rental units in Steinbach”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Supporting suite enforcement while explaining council's caution · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:21:43

Term by term

2014–2018

He moved the routine weed-inspector appointment in 2018 and voted to deny the Maplewood Street rezoning, citing drainage and green space.

Took part in 3 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

He seconded the 2019 Maplewood approval and linked derelict housing on First Street to the revitalization the community plan intended. He moved first reading of By-law 2157 (2020) and third reading (2021), warning that renters might pay more and that the issue went back 15 to 20 years.

Took part in 9 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

In August 2024 he backed enforcement ('it is time to step up') while saying the fear of losing low-end rentals was why council had held off. In December 2024 he welcomed three demolition permits for 'derelict homes', and in November 2025 asked whether a development agreement could limit vehicles per rental unit; staff said the city does not enforce on private property.

Took part in 3 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

He championed the suite-safety rule and named its trade-off most plainly, and saw redevelopment as the answer to derelict houses.
