Property standards · Council member

Michael Zwaagstra: property standards and enforcement

What did Michael Zwaagstra say and do about property standards and their enforcement in Steinbach (yards, garbage, rental buildings, secondary suites) between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Michael Zwaagstra moved more of the property-standards motions than any other member, most of them routine: the yearly weed-inspector appointment and four amendments to the administrative penalty schedule (2018, 2021, 2022 and 2025), each adding fines for whichever by-laws council had just revised. None added the 2003 junk and weeds by-law.

He moved second reading of By-law 2157 in January 2021, saying it was 'reasonable' and 'fair' to expect every owner to meet minimum safety standards, and in 2024 backed enforcing it where voluntary compliance failed. At rezoning hearings he raised neighbours' concerns about the state of existing rentals, moved to deny the 2018 Maplewood Street rezoning, and in 2019 moved to approve a building that would replace a rental he said 'really shouldn't be there now'.

“I think it's, it's reasonable, it's fair to expect any property owner to make sure that all units within their building have these minimum safety standards.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Moving second reading of the Dwelling Safety Standards By-law · Jan 5, 2021 · 0:24:10

Term by term

2014–2018

He moved By-law 2091 (2018), which added the parking meter by-law to the new penalty system, and moved to deny the 2018 Maplewood Street rezoning after neighbours objected to how the owner had kept his rental houses.

Took part in 3 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

He moved the 2019 approval of a nine-unit building on Maplewood Street to replace a run-down rental, and asked another applicant about the state of his rental before moving to deny that project on consultation grounds. He moved second reading of By-law 2157 (2021) and first and second readings of By-law 2173, which he said would let fines be 'adjudicated more quickly'.

Took part in 10 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He moved the 2022 and 2025 penalty-schedule amendments, saying without them new by-laws would be 'very hard to actually enforce', and the yearly weed-inspector appointment in 2022 and 2023. In August 2024 he backed proactive suite enforcement: 'it is important that we protect life and safety'. He moved third reading of the 2026 zoning by-law, which requires screened garbage areas.

Took part in 3 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistently for clear rules and workable enforcement, he did most of the routine work of keeping the penalty system current, but did not propose extending it to yards and junk.
