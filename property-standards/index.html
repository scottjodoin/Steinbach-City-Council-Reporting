Steinbach City Council · Dec 2015 – Nov 2026 · Topic: Property standards

Property standards: keeping homes, yards and rentals in shape

What rules has Steinbach had since 2015 to keep homes, yards and rental buildings in decent condition, including garbage at apartment sites and properties whose owners live elsewhere? How has the city enforced them, and has council considered stronger tools such as rental licensing? How did each council member act, and how do other Manitoba and Canadian cities compare?

Written for residents. Start with the summary, then choose what to read. Last updated Nov 9, 2026. This report replaces our October 2026 page on 2022–2026 and extends it back to 2015, with peer by-laws re-checked in November 2026.

Executive summary

Steinbach keeps yards and rentals in shape with two by-laws: a 2003 junk-and-weeds rule with no ticket, and a 2021 rental-safety rule with stiff fines whose enforcement has never been reported back to council.

  • The yard-and-junk rule is from 2003 and has no ticket. By-law 1663 covers junk, rubbish, grass over 6 inches and nuisances on any property, including apartment buildings. The city can post a 21-day notice, then clean up and add the bill to the owner’s taxes, or go to court. Council created a penalty-ticket system in 2017 (By-law 2076) and amended it five times to 2026 (By-laws 2091, 2173, 2209, 2259, 2282), each time for by-laws it had just revised; By-law 1663 was never added, as far as we could confirm. There is no by-law for the condition of buildings themselves and none for derelict buildings.
  • Rental safety is the strong point, on paper. By-law 2157 (January 2021), adopted unanimously after an issue members said went back “15 to 20 years,” requires smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms and escape exits in every home, with a $1,000 fine for each day an order is ignored and inspections at a tenant’s request. Members warned that rents could rise; the mayor expected compliance to take “maybe a decade or two.”
  • Enforcement is small, voluntary-first and unreported. One community services officer enforced all city by-laws from 2016 until a second was added by 2026. In August 2024 staff said only “several dozen” of possibly several hundred secondary suites had come forward, and every member who spoke backed proactive enforcement. No results came back to council through November 2026, the city publishes no enforcement figures, and council has no enforcement policy.
  • Upkeep reaches council through rezoning hearings. From 2018 to 2026 neighbours described run-down rentals, 18-inch grass, garbage left out and overflowing dumpsters at more than ten hearings. Council decided each case on land-use grounds and usually answered that redevelopment would replace neglected houses. No one, on council or off, proposed rental licensing or a landlord registry at any recorded meeting.
  • Peers have more tools. Brandon (2023) and Portage la Prairie (2021) set standards for building exteriors, both have derelict-building by-laws, and Brandon tickets untidy premises ($100, or $200 after 15 days). Winkler adopted an enforcement policy in 2025, and Winnipeg publishes every by-law investigation as open data. No Manitoba peer licenses long-term rentals; Waterloo, London, Oshawa and Toronto do. Almost every vote in this record was unanimous; the one split vote that turned on upkeep was a 2018 rezoning (5–1).

The story in three councils

Under Mayor Chris Goertzen, the city built its ticketing system for parking and traffic, and rental upkeep reached council only through one contested rezoning. Mayor Earl Funk’s first council passed the suite-safety by-law, its one new property-standards rule in eleven years. His second council backed enforcing it, kept the yard rules as they were, and heard neighbours’ upkeep complaints at hearing after hearing. The council elected in October 2026 inherits an enforcement push with no reported results.

Mayor Funk’s second council

“Several dozen” of several hundred suites, a crackdown backed by all who spoke, a second officer, and screened bins in the new zoning by-law.

2026–2030

The new council

Michael Zwaagstra and Jac Siemens are gone; Ron Falk and Greg Carlos are new. Open files: suite-enforcement results, the junk by-law and a second officer’s truck.

About this report

Quotes are verbatim from automatic transcripts and link to the meeting videos. Council members, the mayor and city staff are named; residents, applicants and delegations are identified by initials, and private property owners are never named. Residents’ descriptions of specific properties are their own and unproven. The analysis and plain-language summaries are ours.

Methods, coverage and what we couldn’t confirm
  • Transcripts. Meeting quotes come from automatic transcripts of the official videos and are left as transcribed, including repeated words, false starts and odd capital letters mid-sentence. The one exception is the city’s name, which the transcription often misspells and which quotes show correctly as Steinbach. Ellipses mark trimmed text; no sentence breaks were added. Each quote links to the start of the speaker’s turn in the video; check there before quoting elsewhere.
  • Speakers and names. Every named quote from a council member or staff is from a speaker identified with high confidence: from hand-checked speaker maps for meetings from January 2022, and for earlier meetings from how speakers were addressed and from the minutes. Remarks identified only with medium or low confidence are described, not quoted: a 2017 remark by a Councillor Penner (Cari or Susan), a 2019 remark by a Councillor Hiebert (Bill or Jake), and comments probably by Councillors Zwaagstra and Siemens in 2020. Council members, the mayor and city staff are named in full. Residents, applicants, delegations and other members of the public are identified only by initials (first and last), including in quote citations. Organizations such as Steinbach Community Outreach are named. Property owners who are private individuals are not named; properties are identified only by street.
  • Coverage. Every council meeting from December 15, 2015 to November 3, 2026 with a recording or minutes was searched (transcripts of 243 meeting videos, and minutes from January 2018 on; the city’s older minutes are not online), plus agenda packages where available. Not covered: the first four 2015 videos (not yet transcribed) and four videos that failed to download (April 4 and October 17, 2017; September 15, 2020; July 6, 2021), so the 2017 weed-inspector appointment was not found. The Strategic Priorities Committee, where By-law 2157 was first discussed in May 2020, is not recorded.
  • What counts. 26 items: by-laws on junk, weeds, nuisances and suite safety; the penalty-ticket schedule; enforcement staff; residents’ complaints about specific properties, rentals and garbage; and charges added to tax bills. Apartment density, height and parking are covered in the housing report. Classifying how an item ended (done, partly, no public result, declined) and tagging council’s reasons is our judgment; the reasons are recorded for each item in the data.
  • Numbers. The city publishes no enforcement statistics: no counts of complaints, notices, cleanups, suites inspected or penalty notices. Council minutes regularly add unpaid city invoices to tax bills (amounts from $15 to about $24,700), but do not say what they were for, so cleanup charges under By-law 1663 cannot be counted. Suite estimates are staff’s (“up to several hundred” in 2024; 300 to 600 reported by SteinbachOnline in 2021).
  • Not confirmed. The text of By-law 2282 (August 2026), so whether it added By-law 1663 to the penalty schedule (staff presented it with the new Land Drainage By-law, and the statement that yard fines are not on the schedule rests on By-law 2259 of 2025); the original date of By-law 2076; whether any enforcement figures exist inside city hall; the outcome of the July 2026 proposal to buy a second officer’s truck; and whether the Manitoba Residential Tenancies Branch inspects rental units. Peer items we could not confirm are listed on the comparison page.
  • Data. The data downloads list every item, every council member’s part in it, and every quote with its timestamp and link. Quotes in the downloads come only from council members and staff identified with high confidence, and none names a member of the public. Every quote on these pages was checked word for word against its transcript.
  • Council change. The council elected in October 2026 held its inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026, with no property-standards items. Quotes are from members serving at the time, some of whom are no longer on council. Election results are from SteinbachOnline (unofficial counts).
