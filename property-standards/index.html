Steinbach City Council · Jan 2022 – Sept 2026 · Topic: Property standards & rental upkeep

Property standards: keeping rentals and yards in shape

What rules does Steinbach have to keep homes, yards and rental buildings in decent condition, including garbage at apartment sites and properties whose owners live elsewhere? How does the city enforce those rules, and has council looked at stronger tools such as rental licensing? How do Steinbach's rules and enforcement compare with other Manitoba and Canadian cities?

Quotes link to their moment in the official meeting videos; by-laws and peer sources are linked. Last updated Oct 14, 2026.

Bottom line

  • The yard-and-junk rule dates from 2003 and hasn't changed. By-law 1663 lets the city post a 21-day notice, then clean up and add the bill to the owner's taxes. It has no ticket, and it doesn't cover the condition of roofs, siding or windows. Brandon, Winkler and Portage la Prairie regulate all three.
  • Rental fire safety is the strong point. By-law 2157 (2021) requires smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms and escape windows in every home, with fines of $1,000 a day. In August 2024 staff said only “several dozen” of possibly several hundred suites had confirmed they comply, and council backed a proactive crackdown. No results have been reported to council since.
  • Enforcement is small and mostly complaint-driven. The city had one community services officer through 2025 and two by 2026, and they enforce many other by-laws too. Council has adopted no enforcement policy or response targets. Winkler adopted one in 2025, and Winnipeg promises inspections within 10 days.
  • Residents raised upkeep at seven development hearings. They spoke about run-down rentals, long grass, overflowing dumpsters and blowing garbage. Council decided those files on size and fit. Its stated hope is that redevelopment will replace neglected rentals.
  • Rental licensing has never come to council. We found no Manitoba peer that licenses long-term rentals. Waterloo, Oshawa and Toronto do, and charge landlords fees for it.

Apartment density, height and parking are covered in Housing and development. Encampments and the community safety officers' work with unhoused people are covered in Homelessness in Steinbach.

Part 1 · The council record

The rules on the books

Steinbach has no single property standards by-law. Upkeep is covered by several older by-laws and the new zoning by-law:

By-lawWhat it coversHow it is enforced
Junk, Weeds & Nuisance, No. 1663 (June 2003)Junk and rubbish on any “land, residence, apartment building, store” or other premises; grass or weeds over 6 inches (12 inches on vacant land); junk inside a home that is a health or safety hazard; odours, vermin and stagnant water; any property kept in violation of other city by-laws.A notice is posted on the property and mailed to the owner, with 21 days to comply and a right to appeal to council. After that the city may do the work itself and add the cost to the property taxes, or go to court. The by-law sets no fine, and it is not on the city's penalty-notice schedule.
Dwelling Safety Standards, No. 2157 (Jan 2021)Every dwelling unit, including basement suites: smoke alarms (tested yearly and for each new tenant), carbon monoxide alarms, and escape windows or exits.Inspection on complaint or request, then an order with at least 7 days to comply. A fine of $1,000, with each day a separate offence, collectable with taxes. Penalty notice $1,000 ($500 if paid early).
Derelict Vehicles, No. 1641 (2003)Storing derelict vehicles on private property.Penalty notice $100 ($50 if paid early).
Zoning, No. 2245 (Jan 2026)Anything larger than a duplex needs a landscaping plan showing “screened refuse containment areas”; site plans must show garbage collection areas. Owners must keep required landscaping “free from refuse and debris and with a neat appearance” (s. 7.5).Through development approval and zoning enforcement.
Context

By-law 1663 was not amended between 2022 and September 2026. The city's on-line by-law library (searched October 2026) lists nothing for “unsightly”, “vacant”, “unsafe” or “rental”. Council updated the penalty-notice schedule three times in this period. By-law 2209 (2022) added the burning, business licence and pool by-laws, By-law 2259 (January 2025) added fines for the traffic and animal by-laws, and By-law 2282 (August 2026) accompanied the new land drainage by-law. None was presented as adding the junk and weeds by-law. Weed complaints also fall under the provincial Noxious Weeds Act. The city's weed-control page says it “makes inspections and follows up on complaints” and charges cleanup costs to the owner “through the tax rolls.” Inside a rental, repairs are mainly a provincial matter: landlords have an obligation to repair under section 59 of the Residential Tenancies Act, and tenants can go to the Residential Tenancies Branch.

Rental safety: the 2024 push on basement suites

In August 2024 the City Manager told council that three years of voluntary compliance with By-law 2157 had not worked:

“Based on our estimation, there could be up to several hundred of these secondary suites present in Steinbach that we are aware of. However, only several dozen to date have taken voluntary measures and contacted the city to ensure that, that they do comply.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:13:28

“Even though we still believe that the best method of compliance is voluntary, The city will be moving forward with these more proactive measures effective immediately.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager, after describing letters to owners believed to have suites, work orders on title and possible decommissioning · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:14:49

“And even though the fines are significant, the value of life is very significant. And that's why I think I would support these strong fines of $1,000 per day for noncompliance.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:18:06

“This isn't only for tenants, which is obviously very important 'cause they live there on a day-to-day basis, but it's also for our firefighters.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:20:53

“But especially in rental units where people are paying rent, to have safety is so important.”

Councillor Bill Hiebert · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:24:09
Context

The minutes record that council members “voiced their support of proposed measures,” including fines. The item was an update, so no resolution was passed. Staff said there had been “several recent house fires” in rental properties whose basement suites did not comply, with no serious injuries. The city's suite page says renters can ask the city to inspect their unit, and that a passed inspection earns a Certificate of Compliance valid for three years. There is no suite registry. Between August 2024 and September 2026 no report came back to council on how many suites were contacted, inspected, ordered to fix problems or fined.

Who enforces, and how

“Every year we appoint our community service officer as our weeds inspector. Makes sense to continue doing so.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Mar 7, 2023 · 0:13:31

“Protective Services includes RCMP, 47 firefighters and 8 fire trucks, As well as a community services officer to enforce city bylaws.”

Brian Hrehirchuk, Senior Manager, Finance, presenting the 2025 financial plan · Mar 4, 2025 · 0:06:04

“Recommendations being that $65,000 be allocated to procurement of a second CSO truck now that we do have 2 CSOs”

Adam Thiessen, Senior Manager, Corporate Services, on a $200,000 provincial safety grant · Jul 21, 2026 · 1:22:18
Context

Budget presentations from 2023 to 2025 list one community services officer “to enforce city bylaws.” In March 2026 two community safety officers were appointed weed inspectors, and the 2026 plan refers to “community services officers.” The officers also enforce weeds, parking and other by-laws, and respond to concerns about encampments. Council deferred the grant decision, including the truck, to a committee meeting in August 2026. Council received no enforcement statistics in 2022–2026: no counts of yard notices, city cleanups or charges added to taxes. It also did not adopt an enforcement policy saying which complaints come first or how fast they are answered.

N.P., Whitby Cres resident behind the Woodhaven Ave apartments · Mar 1, 2022 · 0:43:22

“There will be a fence around the dumpster from what I can understand.”

Mayor Earl Funk, on the new building being approved that night · Mar 1, 2022 · 0:48:21

“The dumpster's in one spot. Usually there is a fence around it. Maybe that hasn't happened yet, but—”

Mayor Earl Funk, on multi-family sites generally · Mar 1, 2022 · 0:48:37

“There is a matter of the proposed garbage collection and garbage bin area. While it's shown on the drawing, it does not appear to be adequate based on the typical needs that are required for projects of this size…”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager, relaying the city engineer's review of a seniors' building plan · Mar 17, 2026 · 1:23:29
Context

The city collects garbage from businesses and supplies front-load bins of 4 or 6 cubic yards (city page). Bin areas are checked when a site plan is reviewed, and the 2026 zoning by-law requires screened refuse areas. Once a building is open, overflowing bins and litter fall under By-law 1663's 21-day notice process. No rule sets how often apartment bins must be emptied or what size they must be. Residents raised garbage again at hearings in March 2024, May 2025 and April 2026.

Renters and supporters spoke at the same hearings. Some said renting was their only option (see Housing and development). Their reasons for not doing more

1Tougher enforcement could cost low-rent housing

“I think one of the reasons that council has held off to put compliance and enforcement in play is potentially there will be a loss of many low-end rental units in Steinbach…”

Councillor Jac Siemens, who supported enforcing By-law 2157 · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:21:43
Context

The mayor answered the same night: “But what does it cost to put in an egress? What does it cost to put in a fire alarm, a fire extinguisher? These are small, small costs that should not affect the cost of the rental that much” (0:24:32). The by-law was itself a compromise. Staff said holding all rental suites to the National Building Code would have meant “some significant degree of decommissioning,” so council adopted basic life-safety standards instead (0:10:53). The concern about losing low-rent units is real, but it explains a slow start, not the lack of any report on results.

2Voluntary compliance comes first

“Well, like I said, the method of voluntary compliance is always much preferred.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:16:41
Context

Peers say the same: Winkler's policy calls education “the preferred first step.” The difference is the next step. For yards and junk, Steinbach's only tools after a notice are a city cleanup billed to taxes or a court case. Brandon, Winnipeg and Winkler can also fine owners. For suites, three and a half years of voluntary compliance produced “several dozen” confirmations out of an estimated several hundred.

3Redevelopment will fix neglected properties

“Generally, when we see infill applications come to us, we see them in more areas that are on the verge of needing redevelopment, areas that have been neglected for years, sometimes decades, and are in need of redevelopment.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Mar 19, 2024 · 1:43:10

“That means those houses, derelict homes, are going to come out.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, on three demolition permits · Dec 17, 2024 · 0:49:09

“I would imagine with the new garbage pickup, the dumpster, there wouldn't be garbage bags lying around. It would be in the dumpster. So that would be much cleaner that way.”

Mayor Earl Funk, answering C.M.'s concern about the Henry St rental · May 6, 2025 · 0:15:57
Context

Redevelopment can end a problem at one address. Residents' point was different, though: the city already had a rule against 18-inch grass and garbage, and the property stayed that way for years until a rezoning came along. In the Third St and Henry St cases, the run-down houses were being rented out while the owners waited to redevelop. Neighbours then see a rezoning hearing as the only place to raise upkeep, even though upkeep is not a land-use question. A new building also brings its own bins, which residents at three later hearings worried about.

4Litter and nuisance behaviour are police matters

“…the littering, the theft, um, that is something that will have to be taken up with the RCMP.”

Mayor Earl Funk, replying to N.P. · Mar 1, 2022 · 0:46:47
Context

Theft is a police matter. Overflowing bins and rubbish on private land are not: By-law 1663 makes owners of an “apartment building” responsible for rubbish on their premises and the lanes beside them. The city's property standards web page links weed control, graffiti and drainage pages and a general contact form, but has no page on junk or garbage on private property.

5Rental licensing: no reason given, because it never came up

No council member, staff report or delegation proposed rental licensing or a landlord registry in any of the 103 meetings. No councillor has spoken against it either, so there is no stated reason to report. Concerns about cost to landlords and rents (reason 1) would likely apply.

Key findings

1The core by-law is 23 years old and has no ticket

By-law 1663 reaches almost every yard problem residents described: long grass, weeds, garbage, junk. But its only remedies are a city cleanup billed to taxes, which takes 21 days and staff time, or a court case. Steinbach already uses penalty notices for derelict vehicles, noise, animals and suites, so adding yard and junk contraventions to the schedule would be a small step. It would answer the “voluntary first” approach (reason 2) with a next step short of court. By-law 1663 also says nothing about the condition of a building itself. Brandon and Portage la Prairie both rewrote their rules recently, in 2023 and 2021, to cover roofs, walls, windows and pests.

2The right rental-safety rule, with unknown results

By-law 2157 is stronger than most Manitoba peers' rules on paper. It covers every unit, carries a $1,000 daily fine and lets renters request inspections. The 2024 crackdown was a good step. What's missing is a public count of suites found, inspected, fixed, closed and fined. A count like that would also show whether the low-rent units Councillor Siemens worried about (reason 1) were actually lost.

3Upkeep complaints end up at rezoning hearings

Seven hearings in this record turned partly on how existing rentals were kept. Rezoning can't fix upkeep, and council rightly decides land use on other grounds. But with no visible enforcement record, neighbours fall back on the hearing (reason 3). Published response times and enforcement statistics would give them somewhere else to go.

4Licensing isn't the only model

Manitoba cities have tackled problem rentals with maintenance-and-occupancy standards, derelict-building by-laws and fines, not licensing. Licensing in Ontario comes with annual fees and inspections that landlords may pass on to tenants (reason 5). The Ontario programs are also narrower than they sound. Waterloo licenses low-rise rentals, Oshawa only rentals near its college and university, and Toronto only buildings of 3+ storeys and 10+ units.

What “doing all it can” would add

  • Replace By-law 1663 with a community standards by-law that covers building exteriors, pests and boarded homes, as Brandon (2023) and Portage la Prairie (2021) did.
  • Add yard, junk and garbage contraventions to the penalty-notice schedule, so officers have a step between a warning and a city cleanup.
  • Report on By-law 2157 every year: suites identified, inspected, fixed, decommissioned and fined, and how many low-rent units were lost. A free, voluntary suite list built from Certificates of Compliance would help renters check a unit before they sign.
  • Adopt a council-approved enforcement policy with complaint priorities and response times, as Winkler did, and publish yearly counts of notices, cleanups and charges added to taxes.
  • Pass a vacant and derelict building by-law, so empty houses awaiting redevelopment have to be secured and kept up.
  • Set garbage standards for multi-unit sites: bin size per unit, enclosure upkeep and collection frequency, written into development agreements.
  • Study rental licensing before deciding. Compare a light registry with Ontario-style licensing, including the cost per unit and who would pay it.

About this page

  • Transcripts. Meeting quotes come from automatic transcripts of the official videos.   • Speakers. Every quote is from a speaker identified with high confidence in the speaker maps. Two labels were left out.   • Residents' claims. Descriptions of specific properties, tenants or police visits are the speakers' own and are unproven. They were not tested at the hearings.
  • Coverage. All 103 regular council meetings from January 2022 to September 15, 2026 were searched, along with their minutes and motions. Committee meetings are not recorded. The city publishes no enforcement statistics, so this page can't say how many yard notices, cleanups or suite orders were issued. Resolutions adding “accounts receivable” to the tax roll don't say what the charges are for.
  • Unverified items. The text of By-law 2282 (Aug 2026) was not reviewed. Brandon's and Winnipeg's fine amounts and Waterloo's licence fees could not be confirmed and are left out. The Portage la Prairie derelict-building by-law's number could not be confirmed.
