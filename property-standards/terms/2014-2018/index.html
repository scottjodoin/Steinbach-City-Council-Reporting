Property standards · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

What did Steinbach City Council say and do about yards, junk, rental upkeep and by-law enforcement from December 2015 to October 2018?

In short

  • Old rules, one officer. The 2003 junk-and-weeds by-law was in force and not discussed. One community services officer enforced every city by-law and was reappointed weed inspector each year without debate.
  • A ticket system, for parking. In 2017 council adopted Administrative Penalty By-law 2076 so the city could issue and adjudicate its own tickets. It covered parking and traffic; no yard or junk rule was added.
  • Upkeep arrived through a rezoning. In 2018 neighbours said the owner of a row of Maplewood Street rentals had let them run down. Council denied his rezoning 5–1, the only split vote on this topic, but used no enforcement tool on the houses.
  • Questions went unanswered. A resident asked in 2018 who was enforcing weed rules on a vacant Main Street lot; no one answered on the record.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr. This record starts in December 2015, the first meeting with a transcript.

What happened

2016–2017: one officer, and tickets for parking

Every financial plan from 2016 to 2020 listed a single community services officer within protective services. In March 2017 the province wrote about its new Community Safety Officer program. Councillor Earl Funk asked about cost:

“So do these— would these CSOs, so to speak, would they— what kind of cost would they have to us?”

Councillor Earl Funk, on the province’s community safety officer program · Mar 7, 2017 · 0:30:08

The letter went to the RCMP advisory group, and no report came back. Later that year a provincial change moved parking tickets out of court, and council adopted By-law 2076 to handle them itself:

“it also will lessen the challenges for municipalities and specifically Steinbach when it comes to costs incurred in regards to court cases. And so this should streamline the whole process.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Jun 20, 2017 · 1:00:12

The system began with the traffic and accessible-parking by-laws; in February 2018 By-law 2091 added parking meters. The junk-and-weeds by-law, whose only remedies are a city cleanup billed to taxes or a court case, was not added.

2018: weeds, and the Maplewood Street houses

At the July 2018 hearing on the new zoning by-law, a resident asked who enforces the rules already on the books:

“Are we making amendments and bylaws and nobody's carrying them out or checking out on some of these things?”

M.G., Fernwood Bay resident · Jul 17, 2018 · 1:12:54

A month later the owner of a row of old rental houses on Maplewood Street asked to rezone them for 44 townhouses. Neighbours sent 49 objection letters and over 84 signatures, saying he had owned some of the houses for up to 10 years without maintaining them. (These are the neighbours’ claims; the owner agreed the houses were old and “not worth fixing up.”) Members were torn:

“This is something that always tears me up because we have a row of properties here that really need to have some attention, and here's somebody that comes in and wants to do something with it, and then it's met with some resistance”

Councillor John Fehr · Aug 21, 2018 · 0:57:39

“I recognize that there's been broken trust, it sounds like, with the developer in terms of the upkeep of the existing properties or lack thereof, but the other thing I appreciate about this plan is that the developer's company will actually be taking care of the properties instead of each individual unit…”

Councillor Susan Penner · Aug 21, 2018 · 0:58:29

“And the biggest one for me is that the lack of trust for the developer, and I feel that I don't know what the developer can do to gain trust of the neighborhood again, but that needs to happen before we can go forward”

Councillor Earl Funk · Aug 21, 2018 · 1:02:04

Council denied second reading 5–1, with Councillor Susan Penner against. Members also cited unclear plans and drainage. The houses stayed as they were; no property-standards tool was used on them.
