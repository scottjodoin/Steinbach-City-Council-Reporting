Property standards · By term

2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk’s first council

What did Steinbach City Council say and do about yards, garbage, rental upkeep and secondary-suite safety from November 2018 to October 2022?

In short

  • One new rule, long overdue. The Dwelling Safety Standards By-law 2157 (first reading September 2020, adopted January 2021) requires smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms and safe exits in every home, aimed at secondary suites. Members said the issue went back “15 to 20 years.” Every vote was unanimous.
  • Deliberately modest. Council chose basic life-safety items, not the full building code, warned that rents could rise, and expected compliance to be mostly voluntary, with inspections when owners or tenants ask. The mayor expected it to take “maybe a decade or two.”
  • Upkeep complaints met redevelopment. Neighbours described a backyard that was “an absolute disaster,” garbage left outside for months and overflowing dumpsters. Council approved new buildings to replace old rentals, ruled one complaint off topic and sent littering to the RCMP.
  • The yard rules did not change. Two penalty-schedule updates (2021 and 2022) added more by-laws to the ticket system; the junk-and-weeds by-law was not among those named. One officer still enforced every by-law.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2018–2019: rentals judged at rezoning hearings

A month after the new council took office, two residents at a hearing on the garbage levy complained that neighbours left household garbage outside for months, drawing animals. The mayor said they were getting off topic, and the levy was renewed without a reply on upkeep.

In May 2019 a 56-name petition opposed replacing a rental on Maplewood Street with a nine-unit building. Neighbours said the landlords were slow with repairs; the owner said urgent repairs were done within two days. (These are the speakers’ own claims.)

“if any one of you would go into the backyard, you would immediately hire someone to get the whole backyard cleaned up. It is an absolute disaster.”

W.C., next-door neighbour · May 21, 2019 · 0:21:55

“This here, we're talking about 9 extra units and replacing a house, you know, a facility which really shouldn't be there now with a new facility.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving second reading · May 21, 2019 · 0:26:11

Council approved it unanimously. In July 2019 Councillor Zwaagstra asked another applicant about the state of his rental; the owner said his tenant knew to keep it up. That project was denied for lack of consultation, and the upkeep question got no follow-up.

2020–2021: the Dwelling Safety Standards By-law

In May 2020 a closed committee heard that more and more homes did not meet basic standards. In September council gave By-law 2157 first reading:

“over several years, the city has become aware of a growing number of residential properties, primarily residential units that are found not to be in compliance with the minimum standard requirements of Manitoba Building Code, Manitoba Fire Code, and the city zoning bylaw.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Sept 1, 2020 · 0:19:40

“This is the first reading of the bylaw, but it's been a long time coming. … And likely dating back 15 to 20 years.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, moving first reading · Sept 1, 2020 · 0:21:02

“The downside— there is a downside to this, and that if there is substandard housing today in Steinbach, those same renters would be likely required to pay more to the landlord simply because upgrades to those rental units could be substantial in some cases.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Sept 1, 2020 · 0:21:02

“…this will be a long project that'll That'll happen over time, maybe a decade or two, but we will get, we will get everyone into safe housing”

Mayor Earl Funk · Sept 1, 2020 · 0:25:00

On January 5, 2021 council gave second and third readings, unanimously. Councillor Siemens cited an estimated cost of “plus or minus $3,000” a suite and about 500 staff hours in the first year; staff reported about a dozen public comments, mostly in favour. The mayor called the requirements “not onerous.” Staff described how it would work:

“generally what we are hoping for, at least the majority of compliance will be voluntary. Yes. The city does have a list of potential properties that we would like to be able to inspect to verify whether or not particular properties comply.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Jan 5, 2021 · 0:27:56

“So even if the landlord hasn't taken care of the property or hasn't made the appropriate changes to make it safe, this does provide an avenue for tenants now to make sure they're living in a safe situation.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Jan 5, 2021 · 0:28:45

A fine of $1,000 applies for failing to obey an order, each day a separate offence; on the penalty-ticket schedule it is $1,000, or $500 if paid early. In 2021 By-law 2173 added more by-laws to that schedule; which ones was not stated at the meeting.

2022: garbage and basement suites at one hearing

At the March 2022 hearing for Steinbach Community Outreach’s 24-unit affordable building, its director described a former client who raised her daughter in a basement suite heated only by plug-in heaters and an open oven door; By-law 2157 covers alarms and exits, not heat. A neighbour of existing apartments nearby described the bins:

“…obviously the apartments are needing to have like garbage and recyclable bins dumpsters. … And, and those dumpsters are typically really full. Um, I just went by one of them yesterday and it's— it was overflowing again.”

N.P., Whitby Cres resident · Mar 1, 2022 · 0:43:22

“the littering, the theft, um, that is something that will have to be taken up with the RCMP.”

Mayor Earl Funk, replying · Mar 1, 2022 · 0:46:47

The mayor added that the new building’s dumpster would be fenced. No action on the existing overflowing bins was recorded. In May, By-law 2209 added fines for the new burning, business licence and pool by-laws.

Where members differed

Every by-law vote on property standards in this term was unanimous. Rezoning votes at hearings where upkeep came up turned on other grounds and are covered in the housing report.

No recorded split votes on this topic in this period.
