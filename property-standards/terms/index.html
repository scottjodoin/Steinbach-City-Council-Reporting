Property standards · By term

The story by term

How did Steinbach City Council’s rules and enforcement for yards, garbage and rental buildings change between December 2015 and November 2026, and what does the new council inherit?

In short

Over eleven years council adopted one new property-standards rule: the Dwelling Safety Standards By-law 2157, passed in January 2021 after a September 2020 first reading. The 2003 junk-and-weeds by-law stayed as it was, and the ticket system built in 2017 was extended several times, but never to yards. Enforcement rested on one community services officer until a second arrived by 2026. Residents raised rental upkeep and garbage mainly at rezoning hearings, and council answered mainly with redevelopment. The 2024 push to enforce suite safety is the biggest open file.

2014–2018

Mayor Goertzen’s council

One officer for every by-law, a ticket system built for parking, and the Maplewood Street rezoning denied over neighbours’ distrust of an owner’s upkeep.

6 items · one split vote (5–1)

2018–2022

Mayor Funk’s first council

By-law 2157 for alarms and exits in every home; run-down rentals, garbage and overflowing dumpsters answered with redevelopment.

14 items · every by-law vote unanimous

2022–2026

Mayor Funk’s second council

Voluntary suite compliance judged a failure, a crackdown backed and never reported on, two officers, and screened bins in the zoning by-law.

2026–2030: the new council

The council elected on October 28, 2026 is the first change in membership since 2018. Michael Zwaagstra, who moved the 2021 suite-safety by-law and most penalty-schedule updates, and Jac Siemens, who moved its first reading and named its trade-off with low-rent housing, did not run. Ron Falk, a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years (14 as chair), and Greg Carlos, a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association in his first elected office, are new. Susan Penner, who argued for suite safety as a tenant’s right, is Deputy Mayor.

What the new council inherits:

  • The suite-safety crackdown’s results. Since August 2024 staff were to contact owners believed to have suites, place work orders on title if needed and possibly close suites. No count of suites contacted, inspected, fixed, closed or fined had come to council by November 3, 2026.
  • A 2003 junk-and-weeds by-law with no ticket. It is not on the penalty-ticket schedule, so an officer’s only steps after a notice are a city cleanup billed to taxes or court.
  • Enforcement capacity. Two community safety officers for all city by-laws, and a July 2026 proposal to spend $65,000 of a $200,000 provincial safety grant on a second officer’s truck, deferred to committee with no public decision found.
  • Gaps no council has discussed: standards for building exteriors, a derelict or vacant building by-law, published enforcement figures, an enforcement policy with complaint priorities, and rental licensing or a registry.

The new council’s inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026 had no property-standards items.
