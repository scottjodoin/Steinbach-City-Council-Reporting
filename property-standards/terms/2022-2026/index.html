Property standards · By term

2022–2026: Mayor Earl Funk’s second council

What did Steinbach City Council say and do about yards, garbage, rental upkeep, secondary suites and by-law enforcement from November 2022 to October 2026?

In short

  • Voluntary compliance fell short. In August 2024 staff said only “several dozen” of possibly several hundred secondary suites had come forward since 2021, and that several recent house fires involved suites that did not comply.
  • A crackdown everyone backed. Staff announced letters to owners, work orders on title and possible closures “effective immediately.” Every member who spoke supported it, including $1,000-a-day fines; Councillor Siemens warned low-rent units could be lost. No results came back to council by November 2026.
  • Neighbours kept raising upkeep at hearings: poorly kept rentals, 18-inch grass, blowing garbage and bins spilling onto a church yard. Council decided each file on land use and pointed to redevelopment, new dumpsters and the city’s bin rules.
  • Small changes to enforcement. A second community safety officer by 2026, screened garbage areas in the new zoning by-law, and two more penalty-schedule updates for traffic, animals and drainage, not yards. Council adopted no enforcement policy and received no enforcement figures.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert. All six councillors and the mayor were re-elected in 2022.

What happened

2023–2024: upkeep at the hearings

Rezoning hearings were where residents raised the condition of rentals. These are their own descriptions, not tested at the hearings, and this report does not identify any owner.

“if we cannot trust you or your mother to properly manage and maintain that one property that causes a lot of grief in our area, how can we trust you to manage a 32-unit complex?”

L.A., Third St resident, to the applicant for a 32-unit building · Aug 1, 2023 · 0:43:00

Council denied the Third Street rezoning unanimously, citing the number of units, consultation and the community plan, not upkeep. In March 2024 residents described “lower upkeep” of nearby rentals and garbage blowing into yards. Councillor Damian Penner described where infill tends to go:

“Generally, when we see infill applications come to us, we see them in more areas that are on the verge of needing redevelopment, areas that have been neglected for years, sometimes decades, and are in need of redevelopment.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Mar 19, 2024 · 1:43:10

August 2024: the suite-safety push

“Based on our estimation, there could be up to several hundred of these secondary suites present in Steinbach that we are aware of. However, only several dozen to date have taken voluntary measures and contacted the city to ensure that, that they do comply.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:13:28

Staff said the city would write to owners believed to have suites, place work orders on title if needed, close suites that could not be fixed, and run a media campaign, while still preferring voluntary compliance. Councillor Jake Hiebert said he had been “quite disgusted” by the suites he saw while campaigning in 2018. Others agreed:

“You know, it is disappointing to hear that only a few dozen landlords have voluntarily applied.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:20:53

“I think one of the reasons that council has held off to put compliance and enforcement in play is potentially there will be a loss of many low-end rental units in Steinbach…”

Councillor Jac Siemens, who supported enforcement · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:21:43

“But what does it cost to put in an egress? What does it cost to put in a fire alarm, a fire extinguisher? These are small, small costs that should not affect the cost of the rental that much.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Aug 20, 2024 · 0:24:32

The item was an update, so no resolution was passed; the minutes record that members “voiced their support.” Councillor Damian Penner made no recorded comment. No report on suites contacted, inspected, fixed, closed or fined came back to council in the following two years.

2025: Henry Street, and “not something the city does”

In May 2025 a neighbour of a rental house to be replaced by a sixplex described the yard:

“Like, we gotta look at lawn that is sometimes 18 inches high in the summer, weeds blowing all over the place. … So are the landlords going to be absentee landlords, or are they going to take care that this property is going to be maintained?”

C.M., Henry St resident · May 6, 2025 · 0:14:28

“I would imagine with the new garbage pickup, the dumpster, there wouldn't be garbage bags lying around. It would be in the dumpster. So that would be much cleaner that way.”

Mayor Earl Funk, replying · May 6, 2025 · 0:15:57

Grass over 6 inches already breaks By-law 1663. The applicant agreed the house had “fallen into a state of neglect,” and council approved the project unanimously. In November, asked whether a development agreement could limit vehicles per rental unit, the City Manager said that would mean enforcement, “not something the city does on private property.”

2026: two officers, screened bins and “educate first”

The new Zoning By-law 2245 (January) requires screened garbage areas for anything larger than a duplex and landscaping kept “free from refuse and debris.” In March two community safety officers were appointed weed inspectors. In July staff proposed spending part of a $200,000 provincial safety grant on a second officer’s truck:

“Recommendations being that $65,000 be allocated to procurement of a second CSO truck now that we do have 2 CSOs”

Adam Thiessen, corporate services · Jul 21, 2026 · 1:22:18

Council deferred the grant to a committee meeting at Councillor Susan Penner’s request; no public decision was found. In August By-law 2282 added fines for the new land drainage by-law:

“the history of the city has been, and I hope will continue to be, to first educate and then enforce and then penalize.”

Councillor Damian Penner, moving the by-law · Aug 4, 2026 · 1:25:40

Residents raised garbage again at hearings in April and October 2026; in October the mayor told a neighbour the city has “strict regulations” on bins.

Where members differed

We found no divided vote on a property-standards question in this term. Two rezoning votes at hearings where upkeep came up were divided on other grounds (Home Street 4–3 in 2024, a variance near PTH 52 5–2 in 2025); they are covered in the housing report.

No recorded split votes on this topic in this period.
