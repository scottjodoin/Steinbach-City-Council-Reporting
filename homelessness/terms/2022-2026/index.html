Homelessness · By term

2022–2026: Mayor Earl Funk’s second council

What did Steinbach City Council say and do about homelessness from November 2022 to November 2026, as encampments became visible and a volunteer group tried to open a shelter?

In short

  • Requests heard, not funded. Steinbach Community Outreach’s $65,000 request (2022) and Southeast Helping Hands’ $245,000 request (2024) were taken as information. In November 2024 council took no action when SCO asked the city to apply for federal Emergency Treatment Fund money for a mobile outreach worker.
  • Encampments handled case by case. The 2024 Main Street encampment ended with trespass notices. Council asked staff how other cities respond and the mayor asked for a written policy; neither appeared at a recorded meeting.
  • Zoning was the main decision. After The INN announced a shelter, council made emergency shelters a conditional use (directed 2024, in force January 2026), against its planning consultant’s advice, and then added transitional housing, also as a conditional use (June 2026). The INN still had no permit in September 2026.
  • Support in principle grew. Members called transitional housing “critical” and needed, endorsed the 30-unit Bridge on Third, and the mayor met the provincial housing minister. No city money, land or fee waivers followed, and the mayor said a homelessness strategy “doesn’t fall under municipal jurisdiction.”
  • Enforcement capacity grew. A second community safety officer and private overnight patrols were added; for a 2026 safety grant where homelessness was eligible, staff proposed a truck and cameras.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert, all re-elected in 2022.

What happened

2022–2023: The Bridge opens; “not exempt from homelessness”

In November 2022 SCO wrote asking for $65,000 for The Bridge. Council took it as information with no discussion, and no later city grant to SCO was found. The Bridge’s 24 tenants moved in on November 30, 2023. That fall a councillor used his opening to describe people living in tents behind churches and businesses:

“Are we as a society enabling this culture to grow, or should our efforts and focus be more on addressing long-term issues such as mental health, shelters, and unemployment. This is much more expensive, requires more trained professionals, and a willingness of government to address the situation head-on”

Councillor Jake Hiebert, opening remarks · Oct 3, 2023 · 0:00:30

In November 2023 a new volunteer group, The INN, announced Steinbach’s first emergency warming shelter for a donated lot at 447 Main St, with mats for 30 to 40 adults six months a year (SteinbachOnline).

2024: an encampment, a fund declined, a zoning change

In February council joined the province’s Community Safety and Well-Being plan with up to two staff hours a week, stressing that “there’s no city tax dollars” involved; Southeast Helping Hands’ $245,000 request was taken as information the same night. In August Councillor Siemens warned that enforcing secondary-suite safety rules could cost low-end rentals “when there are a lot of homeless people already.”

From August about 12 people camped at the fire-damaged building at 447 Main St and behind a car dealership. At the September 17 meeting Councillor Jake Hiebert, who had visited the camps, reported what people told him:

“They identified their needs as being long-term places to live, A job, mental health and drug addiction intervention, along with immediate medical care.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert, opening remarks · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:00:08

“We cannot have people simply encamping, um, in, uh, in, in areas like that in Steinbach. It's not safe, uh, not appropriate, and that's the universal feedback that I've heard from people, and I, I agree with that feedback.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:35:44

“did serve a formal trespass notice to all of the individuals that were present on the property as of September 5th, and it is my understanding that as of Monday, September 16th, that all individuals had left or otherwise vacated that property.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager, on the property owner’s response · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:37:15

“But I would like to have, I think, a documented written policy.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:44:09

By consensus council asked administration to report on how other municipalities handle homelessness and encampments. No report or policy appeared at any later recorded meeting through November 3, 2026; it may have gone to the unrecorded Strategic Priorities Committee.

In November SCO asked the city to apply for the federal Emergency Treatment Fund, which only municipalities could apply to, and pass the money to SCO for a 16-month mobile outreach program: an addictions-trained worker, a vehicle, naloxone and harm-reduction supplies, and referrals to detox and treatment (draft budget $142,208.34). The City Manager’s memo said such services are “generally outside the scope” of a municipality’s legislated obligations. Council resolved unanimously to take no action (R24-215). There is no transcript of that meeting, so any debate is unknown.

In December, approving draft zoning changes, council made “emergency residential shelter” a conditional use, which needs a public hearing, instead of a permitted social-service use:

“The need for this change was recognized when the Inn announced that they intend to build an overnight homeless shelter on Main Street… First, it will allow those most impacted by the addition of an emergency residential shelter to have a way to officially voice their concerns.”

Councillor Susan Penner, moving the change · Dec 3, 2024 · 0:16:38

2025: the zoning hearing

At the April 1 hearing on the new zoning by-law, most speakers came about shelters. The city’s planning consultant confirmed it had recommended keeping shelters a permitted use. The INN’s board said it was now planning transitional housing, not an emergency shelter, and warned about the label:

“this council needs to know that the Manitoba and Canadian government will not fund emergency shelters. And so we would be looking at losing over $600,000 in potential funding if we are categorized as an emergency shelter.”

L.D., board member, The INN · Apr 1, 2025 · 1:23:50

Staff said transitional housing was not defined in the by-law. Other speakers were divided: some neighbours supported a hearing for safety reasons, while others said conditional use leaves room for discrimination (their view, not tested). The Carillon reported 49 written objections and 10 letters of support. Asked whether council had a homelessness strategy, the mayor answered:

“Well, I mean, there are things I am working on with Minister Smith. But a direct homeless strategy, it doesn't fall under municipal jurisdiction.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Apr 1, 2025 · 1:42:22

First and second readings passed unanimously. In February council had also denied, on land-use grounds, Adult & Teen Challenge’s request to move its thrift store and addiction-counselling offices into the industrial park. From July, private overnight security patrols were hired from unspent RCMP money; homelessness was not mentioned in those debates.

2026: transitional housing, a hearing required

Objections sent the zoning by-law to the Manitoba Municipal Board, which ordered no changes; third reading passed on January 20. In February, The Carillon reported, city staff refused The INN’s attempt to apply as “assisted living” for a 12-room, $1.75 million project with about $650,500 in government funding that would be paid only once a permit is issued; the mayor said The INN wanted to “skirt around the public hearing.” The safety plan released in March set a goal to “Create a shelter space/emergency housing in Steinbach”; the mayor said it “really just summarizes what’s already happening.”

In April, on an application made for The INN, council defined transitional housing but, on administration’s advice, made it a conditional use in seven zones rather than the permitted use The INN asked for:

“So while I agree there should be transitional housing, I think it should still be a conditional use because it's really important that we get feedback from the people who actually live in the area where the project will potentially go.”

Councillor Susan Penner, moving first reading · Apr 21, 2026 · 0:47:38

“The transitional housing in Steinbach is critical. It's needed. Should have happened a long time ago. Wish it would have come to council a long time ago.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, moving third reading · Jun 2, 2026 · 1:12:48

No one spoke at the June hearing. In May council endorsed SCO and Eden’s Bridge on Third, 30 supportive units; the mayor said it would “directly address a critical gap in our community.” The endorsement carries no city money, land or fee waivers. In June community safety officers checked on unhoused people near Loewen Boulevard after residents wrote in, and the City Manager said such interactions “go on daily”; the mayor and Councillor Susan Penner met the provincial housing minister on June 30. In July a $200,000 provincial safety grant could fund “affordable housing and homelessness,” but staff recommended a $65,000 truck for safety officers and $135,000 for cameras; the item was deferred, and its final use has not been made public. In September the RCMP reported Mental Health Act calls up from 251 to 311 year to date, and The INN told CBC that a city building permit was “the only thing missing.”

Where members differed

Every recorded vote on homelessness in this term was unanimous. The differences were in what members said: Councillor Jake Hiebert doubted shelters met the need, Councillor Siemens called transitional housing overdue, and Councillor Zwaagstra was firmest that any such project needs a hearing.

No recorded split votes on this topic in this period.
