Homelessness · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

What did Steinbach City Council say and do about homelessness from December 2015 to November 2018?

In short

  • Not a council file. Homelessness never appeared as an agenda item, motion, staff report or budget line in this term. No shelter, outreach or warming-space proposal reached council, and no count or estimate was mentioned.
  • Charities were the answer. Members credited churches and charities such as Today House, Soups On, Helping Hands and Agape House. City money for them was token: $300 for Today House and $200 for Eden Foundation in 2017.
  • The one housing decision went against the need. In 2016 council denied, on parking grounds and staff advice, extra one-bedroom suites that Eden Health wanted for its clients.
  • One councillor named the problem. In 2018 Councillor John Fehr described a man sleeping behind his business as “one of many homeless people in this community.” No discussion followed.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr. This record starts in December 2015, the first meeting with a transcript. There are no minutes online before 2018.

What happened

2016: charities credited, suites denied

In a July opening, Councillor John Fehr described the help people in need received from churches and charities:

“These organizations and churches minister outside of their walls to help the less fortunate, make a difference for those that are less fortunate in our community. They provide social help, practical help, and spiritual help.”

Councillor John Fehr, opening remarks · Jul 19, 2016 · 0:00:06

In December a builder asked to put 22 suites instead of 15 on a Loewen Boulevard lot, with 26 parking stalls instead of the 36 required, because Eden Health wanted one-bedroom suites for its clients; the applicant said Eden had 40 applicants it could not house, only 8 needing parking. Neighbours objected over parking. Staff recommended denial:

“it does not appear that this is compatible with the surrounding neighborhood. The recommendation from administration is that council deny the application for variance.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Dec 20, 2016 · 0:08:09

“The fact is that we have a zoning bylaw in this case for a reason, and specifically, we require 1.5 parking stalls per dwelling unit plus 10% visitor parking.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving to deny · Dec 20, 2016 · 0:22:06

“we do not have a long-term agreement here as to who will be using these condos or the apartments, although there is an ongoing need”

Councillor Jac Siemens, seconding · Dec 20, 2016 · 0:22:59

The variance was denied with no opposition heard. Addiction came up the same year only through policing: the RCMP staff sergeant told council in April that Steinbach had “a meth issue” driving petty theft.

2017: Eden explains the gap

In November the mayor invited Eden Foundation and Eden Health Care Services to brief council. They ran 48 subsidized or affordable suites and a mental-health drop-in, and said single adults needing one-bedroom suites were their hardest to house:

“probably our longest waiting list is for single individuals needing one-bedroom apartments. That's usually our longest list, and mostly because Manitoba Housing doesn't provide housing for subsidized housing.”

B.D., Eden Housing and Supports Program · Nov 7, 2017 · 0:21:28

“is there a long-term strategy to overcome that by possibly having something built, or, or what are some thoughts on that going forward?”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Nov 7, 2017 · 0:22:46

The question was put to Eden, not to the city, and no action followed. Councillor Siemens asked how Eden avoided overlap with Today House, Agape House and Helping Hands. In December, Agape House’s request to mark December 6 was handled by asking the deputy mayor to attend its event.

2018: “one of many homeless people”

In March council approved its 2017 grants list unanimously: $887,371.78 in all, including $300 for Today House and $200 for Eden Foundation. The same evening Councillor John Fehr opened the meeting with an account of a man he had found sleeping behind his business:

“He told me how last October he had lived in our backyard at the shop for 5 days without food or water.”

Councillor John Fehr, opening remarks · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:00:36

In the same remarks he called the man “one of many homeless people in this community” and gave his own view of why this man had become homeless; we have not quoted that part because it concerns a private individual, but you can watch the full opening (Mar 20, 2018, from 0:00:36). It was the first time a council member said on the record that Steinbach has many homeless people. No discussion or motion followed. In May Councillor Earl Funk asked the RCMP how meth was affecting the city, and in July a resident at the Official Community Plan hearing asked whether the plan addressed the need of “seniors, homeless, and families” on low incomes; council did not answer on homelessness.

Where members differed

No vote on this topic divided council in this term. Before 2018 there are no minutes online, so votes are taken from the recordings.

No recorded split votes on this topic in this period.
