Homelessness · By term

The story by term

How did homelessness move from a passing mention to a regular item on Steinbach City Council’s agenda between December 2015 and November 2026, and what does the new council inherit?

In short

Before 2021 the record on homelessness is thin. In 2015–2020 it never appeared as an agenda item, motion, staff report or budget line; it surfaced in openings, agency briefings, residents’ comments at housing hearings, and RCMP reports on meth. It arrived as a council decision in November 2021, when council steered a provincial housing grant to Steinbach Community Outreach for The Bridge. From 2024 it became a regular item: an encampment on Main Street, a federal outreach fund council declined to apply for, and zoning changes that made shelters and then transitional housing need a public hearing, prompted by The INN’s plans.

2014–2018

Mayor Goertzen’s council

Charities credited, suites for Eden Health clients denied over parking, token grants, and one councillor’s account of a man sleeping behind his business.

9 items · homelessness never an agenda item

2018–2022

Mayor Funk’s first council

Today House’s repair request taken as information; then the $1.34 million provincial grant and a parking variance for The Bridge.

4 items · every vote unanimous

2022–2026

Mayor Funk’s second council

Encampments, “take no action” on federal outreach money, shelters and transitional housing made conditional uses, and the Bridge on Third endorsed.

19 items · every vote unanimous

Year by year: how the issue arrived

YearWhat happened at council
2016A councillor’s opening credits churches and charities for helping “the less fortunate.” Council denies, on staff advice, a parking variance that would have added one-bedroom suites Eden Health wanted for its clients. The RCMP tells council Steinbach has “a meth issue.”
2017Eden Foundation tells council single adults needing one-bedroom suites have its longest waiting list. Grants for the year include $300 for Today House and $200 for Eden Foundation.
2018Councillor John Fehr describes a man sleeping behind his business as “one of many homeless people in this community.” Today House’s request for one-time repair funding is taken as information (November).
2019–2020The mayor thanks the RCMP for work on “our meth crisis.” Residents raise homelessness and “vulnerable people” at rezoning hearings; council does not discuss it.
2021Council chooses Steinbach Community Outreach for a $1,337,500 provincial affordable-housing grant (November): the first council decision on homelessness in this record.
2022Parking variance for The Bridge approved against staff advice; grant awarded and By-law 2210 protects affordability for 20 years. SCO’s $65,000 request is taken as information.
2023Councillor Jake Hiebert: “Steinbach is not exempt from homelessness.” The Bridge’s tenants move in (November). The INN announces an emergency warming shelter for 447 Main St.
2024City joins the provincial safety and well-being plan on “no city tax dollars” terms. Main Street encampment: council asks staff how other cities respond, the mayor asks for a written policy (September). Council takes no action on SCO’s Emergency Treatment Fund request (November) and directs that emergency shelters become a conditional use (December).
2025Zoning by-law hearing: the planning consultant had advised keeping shelters a permitted use; The INN warns the label could cost it over $600,000 in funding. The mayor says a homelessness strategy “doesn’t fall under municipal jurisdiction.” Private overnight security patrols begin.
2026Zoning by-law passes after a Municipal Board review (January). City staff refuse The INN’s assisted-living permit route (February, reported by The Carillon). Safety plan released, with a goal to create shelter space (March). Transitional housing added as a conditional use (April–June). The Bridge on Third endorsed (May). $200,000 safety grant: homelessness eligible, staff propose a truck and cameras (July). SCO estimates about 50 homeless people (September).

2026–2030: the new council

The council elected on October 28, 2026 is the first change in membership since 2018. Michael Zwaagstra, who moved the 2021 grant choice and the 2024 “take no action” motion and was firmest against encampments, and Jac Siemens, who called transitional housing “critical,” did not run. Ron Falk, a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years (14 as chair), and Greg Carlos, a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association in his first elected office, are new. Susan Penner, who moved each of the shelter and transitional-housing zoning steps, is Deputy Mayor.

What the new council inherits:

  • The INN. Its builder was ready in September 2026, it said, and “the only thing missing” was a city building permit. Under the by-laws council passed, its site needs a conditional-use hearing, and none had appeared on a council agenda by November 3, 2026.
  • An encampment policy the mayor asked for in September 2024, and a staff report on how other cities handle encampments. Neither has appeared at a recorded meeting.
  • The first count. A regional point-in-time count led by the Brandon Neighbourhood Renewal Corporation was planned for fall 2026 in eight communities including Steinbach. Its results, if it took place, would be the first measure of the problem.
  • The safety plan’s shelter goal, “Create a shelter space/emergency housing in Steinbach,” with most timelines “to be determined,” and the $200,000 provincial safety grant, whose final use has not been made public.
  • The Bridge on Third, endorsed but dependent on provincial and other funding.

The new council’s inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026 had no homelessness items.
