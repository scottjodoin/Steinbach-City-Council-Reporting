Homelessness · By term

2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk’s first council

What did Steinbach City Council say and do about homelessness from November 2018 to October 2022?

In short

  • Three quiet years. From late 2018 to 2020 homelessness was still not an agenda item. Today House’s request for repair money was taken as information without discussion, and addiction came up only in RCMP reports.
  • The first real decision. In November 2021 council chose Steinbach Community Outreach for the whole of a $1,337,500 provincial affordable-housing grant, citing its work with homeless and low-income people.
  • Council overrode its staff to make it work. In March 2022 it approved The Bridge’s parking variance against administration’s advice, accepting that very low-income tenants rarely own cars.
  • Affordability protected, with no city money. By-law 2210 lent the grant as a 20-year forgivable mortgage so the 24 units stay affordable. Every vote was unanimous.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2018–2020: still not a council file

Two weeks after the new council took office, a letter from Today House asking for one-time funding for essential repairs came up. This was the whole discussion before it was taken as information:

“Okay, 12B, Today House, read support. What does council want to do on this?”

Mayor Earl Funk · Nov 20, 2018 · 0:20:24

No grant to Today House appears on the 2018 or 2019 grants lists. Addiction stayed a policing matter:

“working on the organized crime, working on our meth crisis and all those things.”

Mayor Earl Funk, thanking the RCMP · Apr 16, 2019 · 0:35:03

Residents raised the issue at general rezoning hearings. In 2019, when neighbours objected to a nine-unit building, Councillor Zwaagstra pushed back on how they described renters:

“Not everyone who rents an apartment is riffraff. And I think we need to recognize that there is a need for places where we have apartments throughout the city.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving the rezoning · May 21, 2019 · 0:26:11

“there are a lot of vulnerable people in Steinbach, whether people like to admit it or not. And vulnerable people cannot afford high-rise housing.”

C.S., resident, at a rezoning hearing · Jun 16, 2020 · 0:20:28

Council approved both rezonings unanimously, as general housing decisions, without discussing homelessness.

2021: the grant goes to Steinbach Community Outreach

The province gave Steinbach $1,337,500 to place with an affordable-housing project. Three proposals came in; administration suggested forwarding two. Council forwarded only SCO’s 24-unit proposal, so the whole grant went to one project:

“But proposal 3 with Steinbach Community Outreach, for many years now they have certainly led the way in working with the homeless, working with the low-income families.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Nov 2, 2021 · 0:13:06

“It's not just housing, it's more than that. It's support as well.”

Councillor Susan Penner, seconding · Nov 2, 2021 · 0:11:57

“So it looks like they're hoping it's going to be temporary, 2 or 3 years, and they'll be able to transition, you know, folks in need out of homelessness to this, to a home.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Nov 2, 2021 · 0:14:42

2022: parking, and a 20-year safety net

SCO moved the project to 224 Woodhaven Avenue and asked for 30 parking stalls instead of the 40 required. Administration recommended denial because of traffic on Woodhaven. SCO’s director told council its work had grown from 20 people in 2007 to 765 a year:

“Affordable housing is a need in Steinbach that goes way beyond the 24 units we are asking for. However, it is a start.”

I.K., executive director, Steinbach Community Outreach · Mar 1, 2022 · 0:17:05

“And it does not factor in that the lower someone's annual income is, the less likely they are to be able to afford a vehicle, let alone 1.5 vehicles.”

Councillor Susan Penner, on the parking by-law · Mar 1, 2022 · 0:55:17

“I think this is something our community needs desperately. This is a good start, and there will be more to come”

Mayor Earl Funk · Mar 1, 2022 · 0:58:10

The variance passed unanimously, limited to bachelor and one-bedroom suites (rents of $583 and $801 including utilities). In July and August council awarded the grant and passed By-law 2210, lending it as an interest-free second mortgage forgiven over 20 years so the units stay affordable. Councillor Zwaagstra noted that the money came from the province and hoped it was “the first of many projects”; Councillor Siemens called the by-law “a bit of a safety net that the city is building.” The Bridge’s tenants moved in on November 30, 2023.

Where members differed

Every recorded vote on homelessness in this term was unanimous.

No recorded split votes on this topic in this period.
