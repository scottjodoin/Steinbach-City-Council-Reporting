Homelessness in Steinbach: what has council done?

How many people are homeless in Steinbach? What has city council done about it since December 2015, through shelter and supportive-housing decisions, zoning, funding requests, encampments and lobbying? What reasons has it given for not doing more, how did each council member act, and what are councils in similar cities doing?

Written for residents. Start with the summary, then choose what to read. Last updated Nov 9, 2026. This report replaces our October 2026 page on 2022–2026 and extends it back to 2015, with peer figures re-checked in November 2026.

Executive summary

For six years homelessness was not a council file at all. Since 2021 council has helped agency-run housing get built with other governments’ money, and has used zoning to give neighbours a say over shelters, while holding that homelessness itself is not the city’s job.

  • No one has counted. Steinbach has never had a completed homelessness count. Steinbach Community Outreach (SCO) estimated about 30 people on the street in 2022 and about 50 homeless people in 2026; a regional count led by the Brandon Neighbourhood Renewal Corporation was planned for fall 2026 and includes Steinbach. There is no general overnight shelter.
  • Silent until 2021. From 2015 to 2020 homelessness never appeared as an agenda item, motion, staff report or budget line. It came up in one councillor’s opening remarks, agency briefings and residents’ comments; groups’ requests were taken as information, and the only city grants were $300 for Today House and $200 for Eden Foundation (2017).
  • Housing built with other governments’ money. Council chose SCO for a $1,337,500 provincial grant (2021–22), approved its parking variance against staff advice and tied the units to 20 years of affordability; The Bridge’s 24 units opened in 2023. In 2026 it endorsed the 30-unit Bridge on Third. No city money, land or fee waivers went to shelter or supportive housing.
  • Zoning became council’s main tool. After The INN announced a shelter on Main Street, council made emergency shelters (2024–26, against its planning consultant’s advice) and then transitional housing (2026) conditional uses that need a public hearing. The INN, planned since 2023, still had no permit in September 2026. In 2024 council took no action when SCO asked it to apply for federal Emergency Treatment Fund money for an outreach unit.
  • Council’s reasons, and what peers do. Members said a homelessness strategy “doesn’t fall under municipal jurisdiction,” that other governments are “downloading,” that agencies understand the needs better, and that neighbours deserve a say. Every vote was unanimous. Brandon offers per-unit grants, tax breaks and city land; Thompson runs federal homelessness funding; Lloydminster runs a cold-night warming shelter. Steinbach’s smaller Manitoba peers, like Steinbach, mostly leave it to agencies.

The story in three councils

Under Mayor Chris Goertzen, homelessness was something councillors mentioned, not something council decided. Mayor Earl Funk’s first council made the first real decision, steering a provincial housing grant to Steinbach Community Outreach. His second council faced visible encampments and a proposed shelter, and answered mainly with zoning and referrals. The council elected in October 2026 inherits The INN’s unfinished application and an encampment policy that was asked for but never produced.

About this report

Quotes are verbatim from automatic transcripts and link to the meeting videos. Council members, the mayor and city staff are named; residents, delegations and agency staff are identified by initials, and people experiencing homelessness are never identified. The analysis and plain-language summaries are ours.

Methods, coverage and what we couldn’t confirm
  • Transcripts. Meeting quotes come from automatic transcripts of the official videos and are left as transcribed, including repeated words and false starts. The one exception is the city’s name, which the transcription often misspells and which quotes show correctly as Steinbach. Ellipses mark trimmed text; no sentence breaks were added. Each quote links to the start of the speaker’s turn in the video; check there before quoting elsewhere.
  • Speakers and names. Every named quote from a council member or staff is from a speaker identified with high confidence: from hand-checked speaker maps for meetings from January 2022, and for earlier meetings from how speakers were addressed and from the minutes. One remark identified only with medium confidence is described, not quoted, and marked. Council members, the mayor and city staff are named in full. Residents, delegations, agency staff, RCMP officers and other members of the public are identified only by initials (first and last), including in quote citations. Organizations such as Steinbach Community Outreach, The INN, Agape House, Eden Health and Today House are named. People experiencing homelessness are never identified, and remarks in which members spoke about their own or their family’s health are not quoted. In 2015–2017 the two Councillors Penner often cannot be told apart, and their remarks are not attributed. Where we trim a remark, leave out a personal passage, or identify a speaker by initials, the full remarks can be watched at the linked moment in the official meeting video.
  • Editorial notes. Two passages are summarized rather than quoted. In his opening on Mar 20, 2018, Councillor John Fehr gave his own view of why one man he met had become homeless; we do not quote it because it concerns a private individual (watch from 0:00:36). In his opening on Oct 20, 2020, Councillor Jake Hiebert spoke about addiction and mental health in personal terms; we describe it only briefly (watch from 0:00:57).
  • Coverage. Every council meeting from December 15, 2015 to November 3, 2026 with a recording or minutes was searched (transcripts of 243 meeting videos, and minutes from January 2018 on; the city’s older minutes are not online), plus agenda packages where available. Not covered: the first four 2015 videos (not yet transcribed) and four videos that failed to download (April 4 and October 17, 2017; September 15, 2020; July 6, 2021). The November 5, 2024 meeting, where council declined the Emergency Treatment Fund request, has no transcript, so that decision is from the minutes and agenda package and any debate is unknown. The Strategic Priorities Committee and budget study sessions are closed and unrecorded.
  • What counts. 32 items: shelters and transitional or supportive housing, outreach partners, encampments and enforcement, zoning for shelters, funding requests from homelessness and addiction groups, the safety and well-being plan, residents’ letters and lobbying of the province. General affordable housing is covered in the housing report. Classifying how an item ended (done, partly, no public result, declined) and tagging council’s reasons is our judgment; the reasons are recorded for each item in the data.
  • Numbers. Steinbach has no official count; all local numbers are agency estimates reported in the news, and rates per 10,000 people are rough. The CBC/Abacus survey was online with 117 Steinbach respondents, so no margin of error applies. The number of letters at the 2025 zoning hearing (49 objections, 10 in support) is from The Carillon; the minutes say only “several items of correspondence.”
  • Not confirmed. Whether the fall 2026 regional count took place in Steinbach and when results will be published; whether Today House still offers overnight shelter; the status of The INN’s permit after September 23, 2026; whether the safety plan’s coordinator has been hired and whether council formally adopted the plan; how the $200,000 provincial safety grant was finally spent; the 2024 letter from 14 churches reported by The Carillon, which did not appear in any recorded meeting or minutes; and the claim that provincial programs will not fund overnight-only shelters, which we could not find on the program page. A speaker at the 2025 hearing referred to a human-rights case against another Manitoba municipality; we did not verify it, and no complaint or lawsuit against Steinbach on this topic was raised at council.
  • Data. The data downloads list every item, every council member’s part in it, and every quote with its timestamp and link. Quotes in the downloads come only from council members and staff identified with high confidence. Every quote was checked word for word against its transcript.
  • Council change. The council elected in October 2026 held its inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026. Quotes are from members serving at the time, some of whom are no longer on council. Election results are from SteinbachOnline (unofficial counts).
