Homelessness · Council’s reasons

Council’s reasons, in its own words

What reasons has Steinbach City Council given since 2015 for not doing more about homelessness, and how well do those reasons hold up?

In short

Council’s reasons fall into seven groups. Some rest on real limits: health care, income assistance, social housing and shelter funding are provincial in Manitoba, and the city has no social-services staff. Others are choices: to leave the work to agencies while rarely funding them, to require a public hearing for every shelter or transitional-housing site, and to answer encampments with trespass notices and patrols. Members also agreed on much: that homelessness is real in Steinbach, that The Bridge works, and that transitional housing is needed.

1“It doesn’t fall under municipal jurisdiction”

“Well, I mean, there are things I am working on with Minister Smith. But a direct homeless strategy, it doesn't fall under municipal jurisdiction.”

Mayor Earl Funk, asked whether council has a homelessness strategy · Apr 1, 2025 · 1:42:22

“city administration has to date approached this particular matter as it's a community-based issue. It's not a City of Steinbach as an organization issue.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:44:55

“And is this a social issue that we have to now deal with, start to be involved with? How many tax dollars do we put towards social issues.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:39:54

“I'm not saying that we have to put, like, we don't put money into social programs, but that we have a policy to deal with these”

Mayor Earl Funk, on encampments · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:44:09
Context

This is partly true. Health care, income assistance, social housing and shelter operating money are provincial in Manitoba, and federal homelessness money (Reaching Home) flows through designated communities and, for rural places like Steinbach, through the Brandon Neighbourhood Renewal Corporation. Steinbach is not alone in saying so: Selkirk’s mayor said in 2023 the city lacks the money and staff to build or run a shelter, and Brandon’s mayor said in 2025 that housing “isn’t a municipal responsibility.” But Brandon’s council still offers per-unit grants, tax breaks and city land, and Thompson’s runs federal homelessness funding (see Finding 3). The same reason was used in 2024 to decline even to apply for federal money: the City Manager’s memo on the Emergency Treatment Fund said such services are “generally outside the scope” of a municipality’s legislated obligations, though only municipalities could apply. In February 2026 the mayor “stood by comments he’s previously made over homelessness not being a municipal issue,” The Carillon reported.

2Other governments are “downloading”

“that's usually the province's— it's usually in their basket. But more and more they're looking to the municipalities to take care of that, right? And so we do have to be careful with those kinds of things.”

Mayor Earl Funk, on affordable housing · Mar 18, 2025 · 1:22:33

“But there's so much going on and downloading from other levels of government that there are things we are assuming that we shouldn't be assuming.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Apr 1, 2025 · 1:42:37

“This is much more expensive, requires more trained professionals, and a willingness of government to address the situation head-on”

Councillor Jake Hiebert, on mental health, shelters and jobs · Oct 3, 2023 · 0:00:30
Context

Council has acted when other governments paid: The Bridge was built with a $1,337,500 provincial grant council chose to place there. The province’s own plan, Your Way Home (January 2025), had not expanded beyond Winnipeg as of November 2025. Outside money exists, but some of it depends on the city: SCO received $101,130 in federal Reaching Home money for a housing support worker in 2025–26; the Emergency Treatment Fund in 2024 needed the city as the applicant; and The INN’s roughly $650,500 in applications is paid only once the city issues a permit. In June 2026 the mayor and Councillor Susan Penner took housing and mental-health concerns to the provincial minister, which is common practice.

3Agencies and churches understand the needs

“These organizations and churches minister outside of their walls to help the less fortunate, make a difference for those that are less fortunate in our community.”

Councillor John Fehr, opening remarks · Jul 19, 2016 · 0:00:06

“We mean well, but we don't know what we're dealing with.… If these people are to be helped, I think they need to be helped by an organization that understands their needs”

Mayor Earl Funk · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:47:40

“donate to good organizations such as Steinbach Community Outreach, Helping Hands, Soups On… because we don't want these encampments to continue.… because we can't ask our staff, they're not going to solve this problem”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:41:58

“Really, the— and I don't want to oversimplify it, but this plan really just summarizes what's already happening by wonderful organizations in our community.”

Mayor Earl Funk, on the safety and well-being plan · Mar 3, 2026 · 0:20:43
Context

The agencies are real and capable; The Bridge shows what one can do. But council has relied on them without funding them. The only grants found were $300 for Today House and $200 for Eden Foundation (2017). Today House’s repair request (2018), SCO’s $65,000 request (2022) and Southeast Helping Hands’ $245,000 request (2024) were taken as information, and council took no action on SCO’s Emergency Treatment Fund request (2024). The agencies say they cannot do it alone: SCO’s director told CBC in September 2026, “We have had people asking for a shelter … we don’t have the resources to do that … But it is definitely needed in our town.” The Carillon reported in March 2026 that 14 local churches had signed a 2024 letter calling on council to address homelessness; we found no record of it at a council meeting. The 2026 safety plan itself sets a goal to create shelter space in Steinbach, with most timelines “to be determined.”

4Neighbours deserve a say

“The need for this change was recognized when the Inn announced that they intend to build an overnight homeless shelter on Main Street… First, it will allow those most impacted by the addition of an emergency residential shelter to have a way to officially voice their concerns.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Dec 3, 2024 · 0:16:38

“Because within a designated zone, many smaller areas with certain characteristics and cultures can occur, which may not be conducive to have an emergency shelter in the near vicinity.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Dec 3, 2024 · 0:19:50

“As, as the project was announced, uh, there was, there was a lot of people that came to see councillors.… We put it as a conditional use so that we can actually hear from the people.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Apr 1, 2025 · 1:39:51

“This is the type of facility that could change the character of a neighbourhood, and when you have something that could change the character of a neighbourhood, it makes sense to have a public hearing.… So I would not support it being a permitted use”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on transitional housing · Apr 21, 2026 · 0:48:45
Context

A hearing is a normal tool, and the mayor argued for consistency: any multi-family or co-living use is conditional. But the city’s planning consultant had recommended keeping shelters a permitted use, and the change was made in response to one project. The INN’s board warned that the shelter label could cost it over $600,000 in funding:

“this council needs to know that the Manitoba and Canadian government will not fund emergency shelters. And so we would be looking at losing over $600,000 in potential funding if we are categorized as an emergency shelter.”

L.D., board member, The INN · Apr 1, 2025 · 1:23:50

We could not confirm on the provincial program page that overnight-only shelters are excluded. Residents were divided at the 2025 hearing: some neighbours supported a hearing for safety, while others argued conditional use leaves room for discrimination (their claim, not tested). The Carillon reported 49 written objections and 10 letters of support. The objections sent the by-law to the Municipal Board, delaying it to January 2026. When council created a transitional-housing category in June 2026, no one spoke at the hearing. The INN, planned since 2023, still had no permit in September 2026; the mayor told The Carillon in February 2026 that he thought The INN wanted to “skirt around the public hearing,” while The INN said it had submitted plans under the “assisted living” definition. Council also said in 2025 that it had seen no detailed plan from The INN.

5Shelters and handouts don’t meet the real need

“Are we as a society enabling this culture to grow, or should our efforts and focus be more on addressing long-term issues such as mental health, shelters, and unemployment.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert, opening remarks · Oct 3, 2023 · 0:00:30

“I could be convinced that places that provide food and people envisioning an overnight shelter on cold nights have the best of intentions.… However, this is not what the homeless are identifying to me as a need.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert, after visiting the Main Street camps · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:00:08

“Steinbach does not have such adequate resources.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert, on drug treatment and mental-health care · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:00:08
Context

Councillor Hiebert said the people he met wanted long-term places to live, jobs, and addiction and mental-health help. That matches the evidence for housing with support. In Canada’s five-city At Home/Chez Soi trial, 62% of Housing First participants were stably housed, against 31% with usual services, and for the highest-need group every $10 spent saved about $21.72 in other costs (Mental Health Commission of Canada). It is not the only model: Medicine Hat, once Canada’s example, dropped its Housing First program in 2022 for “Rapid Resolution,” and its chronic homelessness has risen since. The Bridge, the Bridge on Third and The INN’s later plans are all housing with support, not overnight-only shelters. People with lived experience consulted for the 2026 safety plan asked for “affordable and supportive housing, shelter space, and more outreach support.” Treatment is limited: SCO’s 2024 outreach proposal said one detox bed serves all of the Southern Health region, and council declined to apply for the federal money that would have paid for it.

6Encampments are not acceptable

“We cannot have people simply encamping, um, in, uh, in, in areas like that in Steinbach. It's not safe, uh, not appropriate, and that's the universal feedback that I've heard from people, and I, I agree with that feedback.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:35:44

“did serve a formal trespass notice to all of the individuals that were present on the property as of September 5th, and it is my understanding that as of Monday, September 16th, that all individuals had left or otherwise vacated that property.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Sept 17, 2024 · 0:37:15

“Even though this, this particular letter does highlight it for council's attention, these kinds of interactions and, and activities within the city, they go on daily.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager, on safety officers’ contact with unhoused people · Jun 16, 2026 · 0:57:26
Context

Residents and businesses complained to councillors about safety, Councillor Zwaagstra said, and in 2026 residents wrote about people near a transit shelter and pathway off Loewen Boulevard (their account; safety officers found no hazards). The city’s response has been case by case: property owners’ trespass notices, two community safety officers, and from 2025 private overnight patrols. The written policy the mayor asked for in 2024 has not appeared. Trespass moves people along; it does not give them somewhere to go. Manitoba’s plan in Winnipeg moves encampments “only once move-in ready housing is secured,” and in 2026 Brandon joined agencies in an encampment outreach program. For a 2026 provincial safety grant where “affordable housing and homelessness” was eligible, staff proposed a truck and cameras. Meanwhile Mental Health Act calls to the RCMP rose from 251 to 311 year to date between 2025 and 2026.

7The rules and the site come first

“The fact is that we have a zoning bylaw in this case for a reason, and specifically, we require 1.5 parking stalls per dwelling unit plus 10% visitor parking.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, denying suites for Eden Health clients · Dec 20, 2016 · 0:22:06

“I think— I don't think we have a choice but to deny it. For the biggest reason is that it runs with the land.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, on Adult & Teen Challenge’s industrial-park site · Feb 18, 2025 · 0:33:15
Context

Land-use rules protect neighbours and industrial land, and both decisions followed staff advice. But the rules bend when council trusts the project: in 2022 council cut The Bridge’s parking by a quarter against staff advice, and Councillor Susan Penner said the parking by-law “does not factor in” that people on low incomes are less likely to own a vehicle. The 2016 denial came while Eden Health said it had 40 applicants it could not house. Whether Adult & Teen Challenge found another site was not recorded at council.
