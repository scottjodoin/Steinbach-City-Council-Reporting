Homelessness · Council member

Chris Goertzen: homelessness and shelter

What did Chris Goertzen say and do about homelessness in Steinbach (shelters, transitional housing, outreach, encampments) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

In short

Homelessness was never an agenda item while Chris Goertzen was mayor. In December 2016 his council denied, on staff advice and on parking grounds, a variance that would have added one-bedroom suites Eden Health wanted to rent for its clients.

In November 2017 he invited Eden Foundation to brief council. When Eden said single adults needing one-bedroom suites had its longest waiting list, he asked whether there was 'a long-term strategy' to build something; the question was put to Eden, and no city action followed. Agape House's request to mark December 6 was handled by asking the deputy mayor to attend its event. Council's 2017 grants included $300 for Today House and $200 for Eden Foundation.

“is there a long-term strategy to overcome that by possibly having something built, or, or what are some thoughts on that going forward?”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · To Eden Foundation, on its waiting list for one-bedroom suites · Nov 7, 2017 · 0:22:46

Term by term

2014–2018

He chaired the 2016 denial of suites for Eden Health clients, invited Eden's 2017 briefing and asked about gaps and building plans, and looked for a councillor to attend Agape House's December 6 event. Homelessness did not come to a vote in his term.

Took part in 3 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

He listened to the agencies, but homelessness never became a council file in his term.

2016Variance to add one-bedroom suites for Eden Health clients denied over parkingDeWalt Custom Homes asked to build 22 suites instead of 15 at 295 Loewen Boulevard with 26 parking stalls instead of the 36 required, because Eden Health (a Mennonite program serving people with mental illness) wanted to rent one-bedroom suites for its clients. The applicant said Eden had 40 applicants it could not house, only 8 of whom needed parking. Staff recommended denial. Neighbours objected over parking and property values. Council denied the variance on parking grounds.Declined Variance V-2016-20 denied. Councillor Siemens acknowledged 'an ongoing need' but cited parking and no written long-term commitment from Eden.
