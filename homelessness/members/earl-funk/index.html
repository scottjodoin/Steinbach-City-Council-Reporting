Homelessness · Council member

Earl Funk: homelessness and shelter

What did Earl Funk say and do about homelessness in Steinbach (shelters, transitional housing, outreach, encampments) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

In short

As a councillor before 2018, Earl Funk raised addiction only as a policing question: in May 2018 he asked the RCMP how meth was affecting the city, and as mayor in 2019 he thanked the RCMP for working on 'our meth crisis.' In November 2018 he chaired the meeting that took Today House's request for repair money as information.

As mayor he chaired every homelessness decision of 2021 to 2026. He backed giving the whole $1.34 million provincial grant to Steinbach Community Outreach, supported its parking variance against staff advice, called such housing something the community 'needs desperately,' and in 2026 said the Bridge on Third would address 'a critical gap' and that the city needs transitional housing. He has also stated most plainly why the city does no more: a homelessness strategy is not municipal jurisdiction, other governments are 'downloading,' and people need 'an organization that understands their needs.' He asked staff for a written encampment policy in 2024 that has not appeared in public, and defended making shelters and transitional housing conditional uses 'so that we can actually hear from the people.'

“Well, I mean, there are things I am working on with Minister Smith. But a direct homeless strategy, it doesn't fall under municipal jurisdiction.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Asked at the zoning by-law hearing whether council has a homelessness strategy · Apr 1, 2025 · 1:42:22

Term by term

2014–2018

As a councillor, he asked the RCMP in May 2018 how meth was affecting the city. Addiction reached council as a policing question; he did not speak on homelessness or shelter in this term's record.

Took part in 2 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Elected mayor in November 2018. He chaired the meeting that took Today House's repair request as information (2018) and thanked the RCMP for its work on 'our meth crisis' (2019). In 2021 he backed choosing Steinbach Community Outreach for the provincial grant, describing The Bridge as a way to move people out of homelessness 'to a home,' and in 2022 he supported its parking variance against staff advice.

Took part in 4 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He chaired every item. Requests from Steinbach Community Outreach ($65,000), Southeast Helping Hands ($245,000) and Agape House were taken as information at his suggestion, and he voted with the rest of council to take no action on SCO's Emergency Treatment Fund request (2024). In September 2024 he asked staff for a written encampment policy while saying the city does not put money into social programs; in April 2025 he said a homelessness strategy 'doesn't fall under municipal jurisdiction.' He defended conditional-use hearings for shelters (2025) and transitional housing (2026) while saying the city needs transitional housing, and endorsed the Bridge on Third. In February 2026, The Carillon reported, he backed staff's refusal of The INN's assisted-living permit route. In June 2026 he and Councillor Susan Penner met the provincial housing minister.

Took part in 12 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

He moved from treating addiction as a police matter to backing supportive and transitional housing in principle, without changing his view that the city's job is land use and that the money and expertise must come from others.
