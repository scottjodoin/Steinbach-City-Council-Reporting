Homelessness · Council member

Jac Siemens: homelessness and shelter

What did Jac Siemens say and do about homelessness in Steinbach (shelters, transitional housing, outreach, encampments) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

In 2016 Jac Siemens seconded the denial of extra suites for Eden Health clients, acknowledging 'an ongoing need' but citing parking and the lack of a long-term commitment from Eden. In 2017 he questioned the overlap among the many charities doing similar work. He moved council's 2017 grants list, which included $300 for Today House and $200 for Eden Foundation.

From 2021 he was The Bridge's most active mover: he spoke for choosing Steinbach Community Outreach, which he said had 'led the way in working with the homeless,' and moved the parking variance, the grant and the by-law that keeps the units affordable for 20 years. In 2024 he warned that enforcing suite-safety rules could cost low-end rentals 'when there are a lot of homeless people already,' but also asked whether homelessness is a social issue the city should take on. In 2026 he said transitional housing 'should have happened a long time ago,' while voting to make it a conditional use. He did not run in 2026.

“The transitional housing in Steinbach is critical. It's needed. Should have happened a long time ago. Wish it would have come to council a long time ago.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · As council added transitional housing to the zoning by-law · Jun 2, 2026 · 1:12:48

Term by term

2014–2018

He seconded the 2016 denial of suites for Eden Health clients while acknowledging 'an ongoing need,' questioned overlap among community organizations at Eden's 2017 briefing, and moved the 2017 grants list (routine).

Took part in 5 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

He seconded the 2019 and 2020 small-apartment rezonings. He spoke for choosing SCO for the provincial grant (2021) and moved its parking variance, the grant and By-law 2210 (2022), accepting SCO's evidence that very low-income tenants rarely own cars.

Took part in 3 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He warned that suite enforcement could add to homelessness (August 2024), asked how many tax dollars should go to social issues (September 2024), moved the 2025 denial of Adult & Teen Challenge's industrial-park site on land-use grounds, moved second reading of the zoning by-law with shelters as a conditional use, and moved third reading of the transitional-housing by-law, calling it 'critical.' In June 2026 he reported that chronic homelessness and addiction were national themes at the FCM conference and asked what Steinbach should do.

Took part in 11 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistently sympathetic to the need and consistently cautious about the city's role: he moved the housing council could approve with other governments' money and questioned spending city taxes on social issues.
