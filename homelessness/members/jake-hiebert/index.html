Homelessness · Council member

Jake Hiebert: homelessness and shelter

What did Jake Hiebert say and do about homelessness in Steinbach (shelters, transitional housing, outreach, encampments) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Jake Hiebert, a former police officer, joined council in November 2018. He spoke about addiction and mental health in a 2020 opening, and in October 2023 used another opening to say Steinbach 'is not exempt from homelessness,' asking whether society was 'enabling this culture to grow' or should focus on mental health, shelters and jobs, which he said need trained professionals and government to act.

In September 2024 he described visiting people at the Main Street camps, who he said wanted long-term places to live, a job, and help with addiction and mental health; he doubted that food or an overnight cold-weather shelter met that need, and said Steinbach lacks adequate resources. He seconded the second and third readings of the zoning by-law and third reading of the transitional-housing by-law, and voted with the rest of council to take no action on SCO's Emergency Treatment Fund request. Re-elected in 2026.

“They identified their needs as being long-term places to live, A job, mental health and drug addiction intervention, along with immediate medical care.”

Term by term

2018–2022

He spoke about addiction and mental health in a 2020 opening, without a policy proposal; seconded the 2019 grants list (routine); and voted for The Bridge's grant and parking variance, seconding third reading of By-law 2210 (2022).

Took part in 3 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

His October 2023 and September 2024 openings gave council its most direct account of people living outside in Steinbach and what they said they need. He seconded the 2025 denial of Adult & Teen Challenge's industrial-park site, the second and third readings of the zoning by-law with shelters as a conditional use, and third reading of the transitional-housing by-law, and voted to take no action on the Emergency Treatment Fund request (2024).

Took part in 7 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From a general concern about addiction to council's most detailed account of what people living outside said they need, paired with scepticism about shelters and handouts.
