Every decision and debate on homelessness

What has Steinbach City Council decided, debated and promised about homelessness since December 2015, and how did each item end?

In short

Each row is one decision, debate, promise or request on the topic, from the minutes and the meeting videos. Filter by term, year, theme, council member or result. “No public result” means we found no public decision or follow-up; some work happens in closed study sessions and committees.

YearDecision or debateResult
2016Menno Mart thrift store approved; proceeds to go to Steinbach Community OutreachMenno Mart, a non-profit building-materials resale store, asked for a conditional use to open at 62 Life Sciences Parkway (M1 zone). Its presenter said profits would go to local charities, including Steinbach Community Outreach. Council approved without objection; Steinbach Community Outreach itself was not discussed.Done Conditional use CU-2016-01 approved the same night. This is the only time Steinbach Community Outreach is named at council in Dec 2015 – 2020 apart from a passing reference by a Headway presenter (Apr 5, 2016).vote: carried, no opposition heard
2016Meth and addiction raised mainly through RCMP reportsAddiction came up mostly in RCMP quarterly reports. In 2016 the staff sergeant said Steinbach had 'a meth issue' driving petty theft. In 2018 Councillor Earl Funk asked how meth was affecting the city, and the RCMP linked it to vehicle theft, mischief and shoplifting. In 2019 Mayor Funk thanked the RCMP for 'working on our meth crisis'. In 2020 Councillor Jake Hiebert spoke about addiction and mental health in an opening. Council kept 'drug use reduction' and organized crime among its annual RCMP priorities. Treatment or housing was not discussed.No public result Council confirmed RCMP priorities (organized crime, property crime, visibility, traffic) each year; no city action on addiction treatment, outreach or housing was proposed in 2015–2020.
2016Opening remarks credit churches and charities for helping 'the less fortunate'In his opening remarks, Councillor John Fehr described Steinbach as a benevolent city and credited churches and charities, naming Today House, Soups On, Helping Hands, Agape House and others, for the social, practical and spiritual help they give people in need.No public result Remarks only; no motion or follow-up. It states the period's prevailing view that help for people in need comes from churches and charities.
2016Variance to add one-bedroom suites for Eden Health clients denied over parkingDeWalt Custom Homes asked to build 22 suites instead of 15 at 295 Loewen Boulevard with 26 parking stalls instead of the 36 required, because Eden Health (a Mennonite program serving people with mental illness) wanted to rent one-bedroom suites for its clients. The applicant said Eden had 40 applicants it could not house, only 8 of whom needed parking. Staff recommended denial. Neighbours objected over parking and property values. Council denied the variance on parking grounds.Declined Variance V-2016-20 denied. Councillor Siemens acknowledged 'an ongoing need' but cited parking and no written long-term commitment from Eden. Eden's need for one-bedroom suites came back at its Nov 2017 delegation.vote: carried (motion to deny), no opposition heard
2017Eden Foundation briefs council on supportive housing and the singles waitlistAt council's invitation, Eden Foundation and Eden Health Care Services described their Steinbach programs: 48 subsidized or affordable suites (Penfield and Wilson Courts), a weekday mental-health drop-in, and help finding housing. The longest waitlist was for single people needing one-bedroom suites, whom Manitoba Housing did not serve. A planned building proposal was on hold. Mayor Goertzen asked about gaps and long-term plans; Councillor Siemens asked how Eden avoids overlap with Today House, Agape House and Helping Hands.No public result Information only; Eden made no request and council took no action. The city gave Eden Foundation a $200 grant for 2017 (R18-065).
2017Agape House (women's shelter) proclamation requests received as informationAgape House, the area's domestic-violence shelter, asked council each December to proclaim or support its Dec 6 event (National Day of Remembrance and Action on Violence Against Women). In 2017 Mayor Goertzen looked for a councillor to attend the event. In 2018 Mayor Funk said council would be at a meeting with the RM of Hanover and took the request as information. In 2019 the request was acknowledged in the minutes.No public result No proclamation resolution appears in the 2018 or 2019 minutes (2018-12-04 item 29, 2019-11-19 item 18: 'acknowledged'). Agape House is not on the 2017–2019 city grant lists.vote: received as information
2018Councillor Fehr describes a homeless man who lived behind his shopCouncillor John Fehr opened the meeting with an account of a man he had found sleeping behind his business, who told him he had lived in the yard for five days the previous October without anyone noticing. Fehr called him 'one of many homeless people in this community' and said he felt helpless to do more than offer coffee and toast.No public result The first time a council member said on the record that Steinbach has many homeless people. No discussion, motion or follow-up came from it in 2018–2020.
2018Token city grants to Eden Foundation ($200) and Today House ($300) for 2017, none afterCouncil's annual grants list for 2017 included $200 for Eden Foundation and $300 for Today House, out of $887,371.78 in grants. Neither appears on the 2018 or 2019 lists.Done R18-065 accepted the 2017 grants unanimously. The 2018 list (R19-073, $1,559,883.21) and the 2019 list (R20-064, $932,853.54) have no grants to Eden, Today House, Agape House or Steinbach Community Outreach.R18-065 · vote: unanimous
2018Residents raise homelessness and 'vulnerable people' at housing hearingsAt three general housing hearings residents raised homelessness or low-income need. At the 2018 Official Community Plan hearing, T.B. asked whether the plan addressed the need of seniors, homeless people and low-income families. At a 2019 rezoning for a 9-unit building, A.D. welcomed bachelor and one-bedroom units for homeless and young people. At a 2020 rezoning, C.S. said Steinbach has many vulnerable people who cannot afford $1,000 rents. Council did not discuss homelessness in reply; it approved both rezonings (Zwaagstra moved, Siemens seconded).No public result No answer on homelessness was recorded. Administration said the 2018 plan changes affected mainly annexed land. By-law 2120 got second reading unanimously on May 21, 2019 (R19-118; third reading July 2, 2019), and By-law 2148 got second and third readings unanimously on June 16, 2020 (R20-127, R20-128), as general housing decisions.R19-118; R20-127 (second readings) · vote: unanimous
2018Today House asks for one-time funding for essential repairs; received as informationToday House wrote asking for one-time, project-specific funding for essential repairs. Two weeks after the new council took office, the request was taken as information without discussion.No public result Minutes: 'Correspondence was acknowledged from Today House with a request of a one-time funding to cover project - specific funding for essential repairs.' No grant to Today House appears on the 2018 (R19-073) or 2019 (R20-064) grants lists.vote: received as information
2021Council picks Steinbach Community Outreach for the $1.34M provincial affordable-housing grantThe province gave Steinbach a $1,337,500 conditional grant (announced March 2021) to place with an affordable-housing project. Three proposals came in (Vogt Building 18 units, Habitat for Humanity duplex, Steinbach Community Outreach 24 units). Administration recommended forwarding both Habitat and SCO; council forwarded only SCO so the whole grant went to one project, citing SCO's work with homeless and low-income people.Done MHRC recommended approval (letter Dec 9, 2021, per Adam Thiessen on Jul 5, 2022); council formally awarded the grant Jul 5, 2022 (R22-161). The project became The Bridge (24 units at 224 Woodhaven Ave).R21-232 · vote: unanimous
2022Parking variance for The Bridge approved against staff adviceSCO moved its 24-unit project to 224 Woodhaven Ave after Bridge Park Manor withdrew support for the first site, and asked to build 30 parking stalls instead of the required 40. Administration recommended denial because of traffic on Woodhaven; council approved the variance unanimously, accepting SCO's evidence that very low-income tenants rarely own cars.Done Approved subject to bachelor/1-bedroom suites only, a grant funding agreement before a permit, and a development agreement (friendly amendment by Zwaagstra).R22-052 · vote: unanimous
2022$1,337,500 grant to SCO approved, secured by a 20-year forgivable mortgage (By-law 2210)After MHRC approval, council formally awarded the provincial grant to SCO and passed By-law 2210, lending the grant as an interest-free second mortgage forgiven over 20 years so the units stay affordable. No city money was involved.Done R22-161 (grant) and R22-163 (By-law 2210 first and second readings) Jul 5, 2022; R22-185 third reading Aug 2, 2022. The Bridge opened in 2023 (per the existing report; opening not discussed at a recorded meeting).R22-161; R22-163; R22-185 · vote: unanimous
2022SCO's $65,000 funding request for The Bridge received as informationSteinbach Community Outreach wrote asking for a $65,000 grant for The Bridge. Council took it as information with no discussion or funding motion.No public result Received as information (minutes item 17). No later city grant to SCO found in the minutes or transcripts.vote: not recorded (received as information)
2023Councillor Jake Hiebert's opening on homelessness in SteinbachIn his meeting opening, Councillor Jake Hiebert, a former police officer, described people he meets living in tents behind churches and businesses in Steinbach and asked whether society is "enabling" homelessness or should focus on mental health, shelters and unemployment, which he said need government to act.No public result Opening remarks only; no motion.
2024City joins the provincial Community Safety and Well-Being plan (staff time only); plan released 2026The province funded a Community Safety and Well-Being (CSWB) plan for 12 communities, run by the Canadian Centre for Safer Communities with Headway. Council agreed to take part with up to 2 staff hours a week, stressing the city is only a "conduit" and no city tax dollars are involved. The finished plan was released in March 2026 and received as information.Done Plan finalized and posted Mar 3, 2026 (received as information). The province funds $60,000 for plan priorities, distributed by the CSWB steering committee (Thiessen, Jul 21, 2026). Per the existing report, the plan's housing goals include creating shelter space/emergency housing in Steinbach; no council action on that goal found by Nov 2026.R24-025 · vote: unanimous
2024Southeast Helping Hands' $245,000 grant request received as informationSoutheast Helping Hands (food bank) asked the city for $245,000. Council took it as information without discussion.No public result Received as information (minutes item 14); no funding motion found later.vote: not recorded (received as information)
2024–25Agape House (domestic-violence shelter) requests received as informationAgape House asked for sponsorship of an International Women's Day event (Feb 2024) and for participation in its Ribbons of Hope campaign (Nov 2025); both were received as information.No public result Received as information both times (minutes Feb 20, 2024 item 24; Nov 4, 2025 item 18).vote: not recorded (received as information)
2024Secondary-suite safety enforcement: Siemens warns it could add to homelessnessCouncil backed administration's plan to enforce the 2021 secondary-suite safety by-law (2157), including fines up to $1,000 a day and decommissioning suites that cannot comply. Councillor Siemens supported it but said the fear of losing low-end rentals "when there are a lot of homeless people already" is why council had held off.Done Council voiced support (minutes 9.1); no resolution was required. No measure to rehouse displaced tenants was discussed.vote: consensus
2024Main Street encampment: council asks staff how other cities handle encampments; mayor wants a written policyAbout 12 people camped at the fire-damaged former business at 447 Main St and behind a car dealership from August 2024. Councillor Jake Hiebert, who visited the camps daily, said people wanted long-term housing, jobs and addiction and mental-health help rather than food drop-offs or a cold-night shelter. Councillor Zwaagstra called encampments unacceptable; the City Manager said the owner served trespass notices and all had left by Sept 16. By consensus council asked administration to report on how other municipalities handle homelessness and encampments; the mayor asked for a written policy.No public result No report or encampment policy appeared at any later recorded council meeting through Nov 3, 2026 (it may have gone to the unrecorded Strategic Priorities Committee). The City Manager confirmed there was no council-approved policy.vote: consensus (minutes item 18.2)
2024Council declines to apply for federal Emergency Treatment Fund money for SCO's mobile outreach unitSCO asked the city to apply to the federal Emergency Treatment Fund (only municipalities could apply) and pass funds to SCO for a 16-month mobile outreach program: an addictions-trained (preferably Indigenous) worker, a vehicle, naloxone and harm-reduction supplies, and referrals to detox and rehab for people who are homeless. The City Manager's memo offered three options and noted such services are "generally outside the scope" of a municipality's obligations. Council resolved to take no action.Declined R24-215 "take no action". Council did not choose the option of keeping the request open for a future intake. Application deadline was Nov 8, 2024.R24-215 · vote: unanimous
2024Council directs that emergency shelters become a conditional use, prompted by The INNIn approving the draft Official Community Plan and Zoning By-law changes, council added a definition of "emergency residential shelter" and made it a conditional use (public hearing required) instead of a permitted social-service use. Councillor Susan Penner said the need was recognized when The INN announced an overnight homeless shelter on Main Street.Done Carried into Zoning By-law 2245 (first/second reading Apr 1, 2025; third reading Jan 20, 2026).R24-234 · vote: unanimous (Mayor Funk and Councillor Jake Hiebert absent; Deputy Mayor Zwaagstra chaired)
2025Adult & Teen Challenge's thrift store and addictions office denied a site in the industrial parkAdult & Teen Challenge, a residential addiction-recovery program, had to leave 354 Main St and asked to run its thrift store (work therapy) with counselling offices at 86 Industrial Road, which needed conditional use for retail in the M1 zone. Council denied it unanimously to protect industrial land, while praising the organization.Declined Denied (R25-034). Whether the program found another site is not recorded at council.R25-034 · vote: unanimous
2025Asked if council has a homelessness strategy, the mayor says it is not municipal jurisdictionAt the zoning hearing a member of the public asked whether council has a homelessness strategy. Mayor Funk said he was working on things with the provincial minister but a homeless strategy "doesn't fall under municipal jurisdiction," and that other governments download responsibilities onto the city. He had made the same downloading point about affordable housing two weeks earlier.Declined No city homelessness strategy was adopted through Nov 2026. The CSWB plan (Mar 2026) is a community plan, not a city strategy. The mayor and Councillor Susan Penner met the provincial Housing Minister on Jun 30, 2026 (SteinbachOnline, per existing report; not discussed at a recorded meeting).
2025–26Zoning By-law 2245 adopted with emergency shelters as a conditional use, against the consultant's adviceAt the April 1, 2025 hearing most speakers came about the shelter change. The city's planning consultant (WSP) confirmed it had recommended keeping emergency shelters a permitted use. The INN's board said it was now a transitional-housing project, opposed the by-law, and warned that being classed as an emergency shelter would cost over $600,000 in government funding. Staff said transitional housing was not defined in the by-law. Council gave first and second readings unanimously; objections sent the by-law to the Manitoba Municipal Board, which ordered no changes, and third reading passed Jan 20, 2026.Done By-law 2245 in force from Jan 20, 2026 (R26-015). The gap for transitional housing was filled later by By-law 2275 (June 2026), also as a conditional use.R25-071; R25-072; R26-015 · vote: unanimous
2025Private overnight security patrols hired and continued into 2026Citing RCMP shortages and property crime, council hired a private security firm (pilot from Jul 2, 2025) funded from unspent RCMP budget, then continued nightly 10 pm - 6 am patrols to year-end and put $145,000 in the 2026 budget for consideration. Homelessness was not mentioned in either debate; included as context for the city's enforcement capacity.Done R25-118 (Jun 17, 2025) and R25-209 (Nov 4, 2025), both unanimous.R25-118; R25-209 · vote: unanimous

No site-specific conditional-use hearing for The INN at 447 Main St appeared on a council agenda through Nov 3, 2026.R26-084; R26-111; R26-112 · vote: unanimous
