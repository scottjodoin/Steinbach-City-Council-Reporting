Steinbach's 2026 council candidates' forum: every question, every answer

What did each of the 10 candidates for Steinbach City Council say about growth, infrastructure, taxes, business, quality of life and the city's next 20 years? And do the four sitting councillors' answers match what they have said and done on council since 2022?

Every answer is shown in full and links to its moment in the forum video (SteinbachOnline). The full transcript is on its own page. Election day is Oct 28, 2026. Last updated Oct 16, 2026.

Bottom line

  • Ten candidates for six seats. Four are sitting councillors: Damian Penner, Susan Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert. Six are new: Ron Falk, Mitesh Kumar, Gary Dyck, Greg Carlos, Nicholas Goertzen and Brad Unger.
  • Infrastructure means wastewater and flooding. Five candidates named the new wastewater treatment plant, estimated at over $200M, as the top priority. Four put flooding first; Brad Unger paired it with more RCMP officers. Damian Penner's answer was “the next project”.
  • On taxes, the incumbents defended the record: a low tax rate and repaying all borrowing within five years. Nicholas Goertzen suggested looking at 10–15-year borrowing for big projects. Susan Penner said she won't promise a tax freeze, and Brad Unger is open to “a small tax increase if it makes sense”.
  • New candidates raised issues the incumbents mostly didn't: public transit (Mitesh Kumar, Nicholas Goertzen, Gary Dyck), homelessness (Nicholas Goertzen, Gary Dyck), and children's safety on the way to school (Brad Unger).
  • Incumbent check: the four incumbents' answers largely match their council record. One clear tension: Jake Hiebert said growth should favour “expanding our city versus high density”, but in December 2025 he made the case for higher-density infill and moved to approve it. A fact check: Bill Hiebert's statement that Steinbach has the lowest mill rate of any Manitoba city is not correct; Winnipeg's and Winkler's are lower.
