Steinbach City Council · 2026–2030 term · Follow-up list

What the new council inherits

What decisions, promised reports and unanswered questions does the council sworn in on November 3, 2026 inherit from its predecessors, and which ones come up first?

Drawn from the research behind this site’s 16 topic reports and the advocacy report: council meetings to the inaugural meeting of November 3, 2026, and minutes to October 6, 2026. Each item links to the report that tells its full story. Last updated Nov 9, 2026.

The new council is the first change in membership since 2018. Mayor Earl Funk was acclaimed for a third term. Susan Penner, who topped the poll, is Deputy Mayor; Jake Hiebert, Damian Penner and Bill Hiebert also return. Michael Zwaagstra and Jac Siemens, both first elected in 2006, did not run. The two new councillors are Ron Falk, a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years (14 as chair), and Greg Carlos, a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, in his first elected office.

The inaugural meeting on November 3 dealt only with oaths and appointments. The mayor named Damian Penner to the RCMP advisory committee, Jake Hiebert to the Eastman Regional Municipal Committee and the watershed district board, Susan Penner to the joint committee with the RM of Hanover, Greg Carlos to the committee with La Broquerie, and Ron Falk to the Jake Epp Library Board; council ratified the appointments by resolution. The first regular meeting is set for November 17.

Bottom line

  • The largest file is the wastewater expansion. Council applied in April 2025 for federal help with a $132.5 million project, with a city share of at least $65 million. No funding decision has been reported. Members have since cited costs over $200 million, and the mayor has called a system of about $215 million his top priority for the term (SteinbachOnline). The city’s practice of repaying all debt within five years has never been tested by anything this large.
  • The first budget comes in a reassessment year. Property values rose about 9% in the 2027 reassessment. The 2027 financial plan has not been presented. The development fees set in 2023 run only to the end of 2027, and a review of the Chamber of Commerce’s share of the business tax was promised in 2019 and again in 2025.
  • One deadline is fixed: a $200,000 provincial public safety grant must be spent by March 31, 2027. Its use was sent to a closed committee meeting in August 2026, and no public decision has been found.
  • Several promised reports have not come back in public, including an encampment policy (asked for in September 2024), results of the secondary-suite safety enforcement (August 2024), the Park Hill School traffic report (April 2026) and the Sio Silica report (May 2026). The climate adaptation plan presented in August 2026 has not been adopted.
  • Of the 40 items on our pre-election list, none has a full public answer yet. One is partly answered, by the November 3 appointments; 39 remain open on the public record. Some may have been settled in unrecorded committee or study sessions, and a statement on the record would close them.

About this page

  • Sources. Items come from the research data behind this site’s 16 topic reports and the advocacy report: transcripts of council meetings to the inaugural meeting of November 3, 2026, minutes to October 6, 2026 (later minutes were not yet posted), agenda packages and the city’s published plans. A few figures come from SteinbachOnline and are attributed where used. Each row links to the report that gives the full story, with dated quotes and meeting links.
  • What counts as inherited. A decision that is pending, a report or reply that was promised or asked for, or a resident’s question raised at council, where no outcome appears in the public record by November 9, 2026. Most are items marked open or partly done for the 2022–2026 term in the topic data; older items are included only where they are still live. “Open” does not mean nothing was done: Strategic Priorities Committee meetings and budget study sessions are not recorded, and staff may have finished work without reporting it.
  • The earlier list. The 40 items were first published in October 2026 as questions for the outgoing councillors. Their status here was re-checked against the topic research; none had a later public result except the November 3 appointments. The archived list is kept as it was published.
  • Neutral by design. The page lists what is on the table; it does not ask anything of individual new councillors. Their backgrounds are as reported in the election coverage.
  • Names and quotes. Council members, the mayor and city staff are named. Residents, delegations and other members of the public are identified only by initials, and none is named here. The two quotes are copied verbatim from the published taxes and spending report pages, where they were checked against the automatic transcripts; the city’s name is shown correctly wherever the transcription misspells it. Full remarks can be watched in the linked meeting videos.
  • Litigation. A homeowners’ lawsuit over sewer backups is pending in the Court of King’s Bench. Its allegations are unproven, and nothing here comments on them.
