Steinbach City Council · 2022–2026 term · Follow-up list

Unfinished business: questions for Steinbach's outgoing councillors

Councillors Michael Zwaagstra and Jac Siemens, both first elected in 2006, are not on the ballot on October 28. Before they leave, which promises, referrals and decisions from the 2022–2026 term does the public record leave open, unexplained or unannounced? And for each one, what specific question could they answer with a date, a yes or no, a number or a document?

Based on the 103 regular meetings from January 2022 to September 15, 2026 (video transcripts, minutes and recorded motions), the August 18, 2026 minutes and the October 6, 2026 agenda. Quotes link to their moment on YouTube. Council's last meeting of the term is Tuesday, October 20, 2026. Last updated Oct 14, 2026.

Bottom line

  • 40 items are still open on the public record. In each one, council or staff said something would happen, or a decision was made, and no outcome appears in later minutes or meetings. Each comes with the question that would close it and example answers.
  • Six can still be settled by this council at its last meeting on October 20. They include a $200,000 provincial safety grant that must be spent by March 31, 2027, a council pay by-law that was withdrawn in August without explanation, and this year's RCMP priorities. Zwaagstra moved the RCMP priorities every year from 2022 to 2025, but no 2026 approval is on record.
  • The largest sums have no public update. The outcome of the April 2025 federal application for the wastewater expansion has not been reported, and the estimate has since grown from $132.5M to $215M. The event centre has no final cost report, after figures of $61M, $69M, $74M and $76M.
  • Both councillors are directly connected to most items. Zwaagstra moved, seconded or spoke to 19 of the 25 main items and Siemens to 15. Between them they cover 22 of the 25.
  • Many may have simple answers. Some may have been settled at a committee or study session, or done by staff without a report. An answer on the record closes the item. Council has delivered on many requests this term, and those are credited below.

How to use this list

  • These are questions about the record, not accusations. “Open” means no outcome was found in the public minutes or recorded meetings. The Strategic Priorities Committee (SPC) and budget study sessions are not recorded, so some items may have been dealt with there.
  • Each question asks for something checkable: a date, a yes or no, a number, or a document that can be posted.
  • The example answers are illustrative. They were written for this page, not said by either councillor. They show what each kind of answer would mean and what to ask next.
  • Timing. Zwaagstra said on September 1 that he will share his reflections “at the final council meeting in October”, which is October 20. Items in Part 1 could be settled there by resolution. The City Manager's office holds most of the documents that would close the other items.

“I don't plan to get into anything too personal at this point because I'll likely share some thoughts at the final council meeting in October. In addition, we still have several meetings to go before this council term ends, and I don't believe in saying goodbye until it's actually time to say goodbye.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, in his last turn giving a meeting's opening remarks · Sept 1, 2026 · 0:00:11

Siemens gave his own last opening on August 18. That meeting has minutes but no transcript, so his remarks are not quoted here.

Part 1 · Decisions this council can still close

Before the last meeting on October 20

These items need a council resolution or a public statement. The current council can provide either at its final meeting.

1The $200,000 provincial public safety grant

Open Since Apr 7, 2026 · Siemens raised the library twice; Zwaagstra asked for a library camera quote

In April the province gave Steinbach a $200,000 public safety grant. On July 21, staff recommended $65,000 for a second community safety officer truck and $135,000 for cameras at 10 city sites. The Jake Epp Library, which had raised security concerns, was not on the list. Council deferred the decision. The minutes record: “Consensus of council was to defer the decision until a Strategic Priority Committee meeting could be held on August 11, 2026.”

“…a key consideration in this grant is that, uh, funds must be spent before March 31st, 2027, uh, which does limit, uh, ability to pursue longer-term initiatives.”

Adam Thiessen, Senior Manager, Corporate Services · July 21, 2026 · 1:22:18

“…but the library, which has come forward a number of times with big security concerns, they're not on here. Can you answer why they're not on this list?”

Councillor Jac Siemens · July 21, 2026 · 1:28:26

“I would like to see the estimated cost of having cameras installed at the library so we can see that option alongside these and then we can make a decision.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · July 21, 2026 · 1:31:12

Since then: no resolution on the grant appears in the minutes of August 4, August 18, September 1 or September 15, and it is not on the October 6 agenda. The city's meeting calendar has no entry for August 11. See also Behind closed doors, Case 7.

Ask

What did the August 11 meeting decide about the $200,000? Was the library included? On what date will council pass a resolution allocating it?

Possible answers (illustrative)
  • “We agreed on a plan on August 11, and it comes to council on October 20.” Then ask what was agreed, and whether the library cameras were costed as Zwaagstra asked.
  • “Staff are going ahead with the July recommendation.” Then ask which resolution authorizes the spending. Under the Municipal Act, council acts only by resolution or by-law.
  • “It's for the new council to decide.” Then ask whether the money can still be spent by March 31, 2027, and what happens to it if not.

What would close it: a council resolution allocating the $200,000, recorded in the minutes.

2Remuneration By-law 2284, withdrawn without explanation

Open Aug 18, 2026 · Both councillors were present; Siemens opened the meeting

Council and mayor pay is set by by-law. First reading of Remuneration By-law 2284 was on the August 18 agenda. It was pulled when the agenda was adopted (R26-167):

“Item 9G Remuneration By-Law 2284 (1st reading) - Withdrawn”

Minutes of the Regular Council Meeting · Aug 18, 2026, item 4

Since then: no reason is given in the minutes, and no transcript of that meeting is available. The by-law was not on the September 1, September 15 or October 6 agendas, and the word “remuneration” appears in no other minutes since 2022.

Ask

What change to council and mayor pay did By-law 2284 propose? Why was it withdrawn? Will it come back before the election, or is it left to the new council?

Possible answers (illustrative)
  • “It was a routine adjustment, and we pulled it so the next council can set its own pay.” That is a common practice. Then ask for the draft and the staff report to be posted, so voters know what is proposed.
  • “It needed changes and will come back on October 20.” Then ask for the amounts and the date it would take effect.
  • “It has been dropped.” Then ask whether pay stays at current rates for the 2026–2030 term.

What would close it: the draft by-law and staff report posted, or a statement on the record.

3This year's RCMP priorities were never approved by council

Open 2026–27 · Zwaagstra moved every approval from 2022 to 2025 and chairs the RCMP advisory group

Each year council has approved the detachment's local priorities by resolution: R22-119 (2022–23), R23-079, R24-075 and R25-108 (“that the policing priorities be adopted for 2025-2026 as presented”). Zwaagstra moved all four. For 2026–27, the crime and policing report found priorities set with the Police Advisory Board, but no resolution in the minutes through September 15, 2026.

“As a council, in consultation with our staff sergeant and with our administration, we always determine our priority areas that we want police to focus on.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, chair of the RCMP advisory group · May 17, 2022 · 0:17:14
Ask

Has council approved the 2026–27 RCMP priorities? If not, will they be on the October 20 agenda?

Possible answers (illustrative)
  • “They were agreed at the advisory group, and the council resolution was missed.” This is easy to fix: bring them to council on October 20.
  • “They were approved at another meeting.” Then ask which meeting, and where its minutes are posted.
  • “The RCMP no longer needs a council resolution.” Then ask what the RCMP now requires, and how residents will see the priorities and results.

What would close it: a resolution adopting the 2026–27 priorities, as in past years.

(SEG), the city gives up to 0.5 mill a year, about $700,000, and SEG's annual operating budgets are subject to council review and approval. In June 2026 council lent SEG $400,000 for operating cash flow (By-law 2272), after the matter was “discussed previously at committee”. The same night, a separate SEG funding request was sent to the summer study session. That request does not appear in the June 16 minutes.

“council still retains oversight over the annual budget, and so council will review and approve proposed operating budgets.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jan 7, 2025 · 0:09:46

“We've got the 11E. This is the Southeast Event Group, the funding request. Council, I'm not expecting decisions today. I would say we recommend we put this on summer study session and take his information at this time.”

Mayor Earl Funk · June 16, 2026 · 1:01:15

Since then: no approval of an SEG operating budget appears in any minutes from January 2025 to September 2026, and the June request has no recorded outcome.

Ask

Has council approved SEG's 2025 and 2026 operating budgets, as the agreement requires, and on what dates? What did the June 2026 request ask for, and what was decided?

Possible answers (illustrative)
  • “We reviewed the budgets at committee.” Then ask for them to be approved by resolution and published, since about $700,000 a year of city money is involved.
  • “The June request was folded into the loan.” Then ask for the amount, and why it was left out of the minutes.
  • “It's still pending.” Then ask what was requested, so voters know what the new council faces.

What would close it: the approved budgets and SEG's 2025 financial statements tabled, and the outcome of the June request.

What was delivered

  • Traffic signals at Highway 52 and Giesbrecht Street. Council asked in February 2022 (R22-038, moved by Zwaagstra), the province delayed them repeatedly, and they were switched on October 13, 2026.

  • Economic development. Council decided in public, with reasons, to end the city's economic development pilot (R25-158, August 2025).
  • Library regionalization was resolved by council resolution in January 2026 (R26-009).
  • The new development plan and Zoning By-law were adopted in September 2025 and January 2026.

About this page

  • What it covers. All 103 regular council meetings from January 2022 to September 15, 2026 were searched: the video transcripts, minutes and recorded motions, plus the August 18, 2026 minutes and the October 6, 2026 agenda. A few meetings, including April 5, 2022 and August 18, 2026, have minutes but no transcript. Items were also drawn from this site's other reports.
  • What “open” means. No outcome was found in the public record. It does not mean nothing was done. SPC meetings and budget study sessions are not recorded or minuted in public, and staff may have finished work without reporting it. This page does not allege bad faith.
  • Example answers are written for this page to illustrate the kinds of answers possible and the natural follow-ups. They are not statements by either councillor or by the city.
  • Transcripts. Quotes come from automatic transcripts of the official meeting videos. Each links to its moment in the video; check there before quoting it elsewhere. The transcription often misspells the city's name, and the city's name is shown correctly here. Ellipses mark trimmed text; no words were changed. Minutes are quoted from the city's PDFs.
  • Speakers. Speakers were identified by hand from how they are addressed in each meeting, and every named quote here comes from a high-confidence identification. Council members and city staff are named. Residents and members of delegations are identified by initials only.
  • The candidates. The city's notice of nominations for the October 28, 2026 election lists 10 candidates for six council seats; neither Zwaagstra nor Siemens is among them.
  • Litigation. A homeowners' lawsuit over sewer backups was reported in August 2026. Its claims are unproven, and nothing here comments on them.
  • Unverified items. Whether the August 11, 2026 committee meeting took place, and what it decided, could not be confirmed. The STARS rate is inferred from a donation amount, not a stated decision. Whether the climate plan has been posted online could not be confirmed.
