Housing and development · Council member

Bill Hiebert: housing, apartments, infill and paying for growth

What did Bill Hiebert say and do about housing and development in Steinbach (affordable housing, apartments and infill, new neighbourhoods, and who pays for growth) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Bill Hiebert spoke less on housing than most members, and some of his remarks cannot be told apart from Jake Hiebert's in the recordings. He voted against the 44 First Street rezoning in 2019 but for nearly every other housing approval, praised developers for adjusting their plans 'to a market', and noted that a duplex needs no rezoning.

In the 2022–2026 term he voted with the majority for 5% fees and for the Home Street building, and to deny Third Street. In 2026 he voted against one stall per suite for the seven-storey seniors' building ('parking is a big issue'), seconded the 330 McKenzie denial, and opened a September 2026 meeting by noting that 755 of about 1,000 units permitted in three years were multi-family. Re-elected in 2026.

“This change in development is difficult for some people, but growth is a sign of a healthy community.”

Councillor Bill Hiebert · Opening remarks on the shift to apartments · Sept 15, 2026 · 0:03:05

Term by term

2018–2022

He voted against the 44 First Street rezoning (4–3) but for its site-width variance, seconded first reading of the 72-unit Maplewood project, and praised the Parkhill developer for 'adjusting to a market'. He voted for the 205 McKenzie fourplex, noting a duplex would need no rezoning, and for all other housing approvals in the term. In July 2022 he said the city had added 'over 1,000 new housing units' since 2018.

Took part in 17 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Mostly moved or seconded routine subdivisions and variances. He voted with the majority for 5% development fees and for the Home Street building, voted to deny Third Street, and in 2026 voted against the seniors' building's one-stall-per-suite parking. Per the minutes he seconded the 330 McKenzie denial, and he moved the objection to the RM of La Broquerie's plan for denser rural development at the city's edge. In September 2026 he said 'growth is a sign of a healthy community'.

Took part in 34 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A reliable majority vote on housing whose clearest stands were on parking and on one early infill file. Many of his remarks sit on recording labels that cannot be tied to him with confidence, so his record here rests mostly on the minutes.
