Housing and development · Council member

John Fehr: housing, apartments, infill and paying for growth

What did John Fehr say and do about housing and development in Steinbach (affordable housing, apartments and infill, new neighbourhoods, and who pays for growth) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

In short

John Fehr served only in the first term covered here. He supported the Hanover Street apartments and moved removing their commercial main floor, welcomed the Haven Group seniors' building and the Rest Haven care-home addition, and moved second reading for 18 three-bedroom family rentals on Brandt Street.

He sided with neighbours on some infill: he moved to deny the shorter First Street setback and seconded the Maplewood townhouse denial over drainage, while saying the lots 'really need to have some attention'. He was the lone vote against each of the city's 2018 purchases of Hanover Street houses: 'we are driving residences farther and farther from the downtown.'

“I will not support this purchase because I feel that we are driving residences farther and farther from the downtown.”

Councillor John Fehr · Voting against the city buying 336 Hanover Street · Jul 3, 2018 · 0:04:57

Term by term

2014–2018

He supported the 2016 Hanover Street apartments and moved the variance dropping their commercial main floor, saying no three-storey building is ever popular with neighbours. He welcomed the Haven Group seniors' building ('much more welcome' than other buildings of that size), backed the Bridge Park seniors' rezoning and the Rest Haven care beds, and moved second reading for the Brandt Street family rentals ('a great fit'). He moved to deny the 15-ft First Street setback and seconded the Maplewood denial, citing drainage. He was the lone vote against each 2018 purchase of downtown houses, and voted against the Harmony Lane redesign and a single-family-only side-yard variance.

Took part in 20 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

Served only in the first term covered. His record mixes support for seniors' and family rentals with attention to drainage and to keeping residents downtown rather than replacing their homes with city land.
