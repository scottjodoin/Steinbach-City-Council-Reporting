Housing and development · Council member

Jake Hiebert: housing, apartments, infill and paying for growth

What did Jake Hiebert say and do about housing and development in Steinbach (affordable housing, apartments and infill, new neighbourhoods, and who pays for growth) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Jake Hiebert tested infill by whether it 'transitions from the outside' rather than landing mid-block. He voted against 44 First Street and 6 First Street, was the lone vote against 115 First Street until a traffic study was done, and voted against the Home Street rezoning, suggesting a duplex or fourplex instead. He moved the 2026 denial of 330 McKenzie: 'not a transitional zone'.

He was also the most frequent dissenter on parking and access: against Home Street's fourth storey, the studio apartments' parking cut, a duplex setback and the 12-unit building at 288 Henry Street. On paying for growth he voted against the 5% development fees at every reading, calling it unfair that taxpayers cover what developers don't. He supported all greenfield subdivisions and several dense projects he saw as transitional, including 492–506 McKenzie, Bridgepark and the Steinbach Housing seniors' building. Re-elected in 2026.

“Because the development fee does not pay for the complete cost, the city pays the difference. Where does the city get their money? From the taxpayer. I think this is unfair.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert · Voting against 5% yearly development-fee increases · Nov 7, 2023 · 0:09:31

Term by term

2018–2022

He asked of each infill project whether it transitions from the edge or lands 'into the middle of a block' (216 Hanover, 14 Ellice). He voted against 44 First Street and its variance, and was the lone vote against 115 First Street (2021), wanting development there stopped until a traffic study. He also voted against the Clearspring Greens pathway levy and against 6 First Street. He supported 205 McKenzie after checking sight lines, seconded third reading of the 72-unit Maplewood project and moved third reading for 78 First Street.

Took part in 18 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He voted against the 5% development fees at every reading: 'I don't think it's up to the average taxpayer in town to pay' for development. He voted against the 6 First Street lane closing, the Home Street rezoning and its fourth storey, the studio apartments' parking cut ('focused just on a dollar value'), a duplex setback after the 2025 flooding, and every reading of 288 Henry Street over visitor parking and emergency access. He moved the 330 McKenzie denial. He supported 492–506 McKenzie, Bridgepark, the Steinbach Housing seniors' building and all greenfield approvals, calling subdivisions market-driven.

Took part in 31 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistent from 2019 to 2026: growth should pay its own way, infill should step in from the edges, and parking and access standards should hold. He is the member most likely to vote no on a particular apartment project while supporting growth overall.
