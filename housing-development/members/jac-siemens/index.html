Housing and development · Council member

Jac Siemens: housing, apartments, infill and paying for growth

What did Jac Siemens say and do about housing and development in Steinbach (affordable housing, apartments and infill, new neighbourhoods, and who pays for growth) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Jac Siemens made the case for infill more often than anyone: old downtown houses on large lots cannot be torn down and replaced with single homes, 'urban sprawl is a lot more expensive', and rental vacancy stays near zero however many units council approves. He moved the 72-unit Maplewood rezoning, seconded the Home Street building, moved The Bridge's grant and its reduced parking, and called transitional housing 'critical'.

He was also the member most often voting alone against particular projects: 44 First Street (2019), 6 First Street and its lane closing (setbacks beside the highway), the 205 McKenzie fourplex (school traffic) and the six-storey Bridgepark building (heights belong downtown). He moved the 189 Hanover denial ('I think you're 10 years too early'). He warned in 2016 that city land purchases took starter homes off the market, and in 2020 and 2024 that safety rules for basement suites could cost low-end rentals. He did not run in 2026.

“our vacancy rate is nil in Steinbach. You cannot find housing in Steinbach, and there's lots of multifamily being constructed currently. We need more.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Supporting 48 apartments on McKenzie Avenue · Apr 7, 2026 · 1:31:39

Term by term

2014–2018

He backed downtown density and seniors' housing, seconding the Hanover Street apartments, but voted against buying a Hanover Street house in 2016 because 'these are starter homes that we're taking off the market' with no plan for the land. A long-time advocate of more than 1.5 parking stalls per apartment, he seconded the Lowen Boulevard parking denial. In 2018 he held to the 25-ft infill setback on First Street, moved to deny the 404 First Street lot split and voted against the Harmony Lane redesign because the public hearing was residents' only chance for input. In 2017 he said growth's costs are generally covered by the taxes it adds.

Took part in 27 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

He voted against 44 First Street (4–3) because the community plan left the south side single-family, then supported 78 First Street while saying council had changed the plan 'one lot at a time'. He moved the 72-unit Maplewood rezoning, noting vacancy never rises however many units are approved, and moved the 2020 suite safety by-law while warning tenants could pay more. He moved The Bridge's grant and its 30-stall parking, though usually 'one of the first people to hold to that policy'. He moved to deny 6 First Street and 205 McKenzie (no seconder) and voted against both, and was the lone vote against moving the Clearspring Greens lighting cost to the city.

Took part in 31 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He seconded Home Street, arguing that large lots cannot be redeveloped one house at a time and that sprawl costs more. But he cast lone votes against the 6 First Street business-district designation and lane closing (a building could sit two feet from the highway), the 205 McKenzie third reading and the six-storey Bridgepark building, moved the 189 Hanover denial and wanted a council decision on the seniors' building rather than an adjournment. He agreed with 7.5% fees in principle but voted for 5%, warned that suite enforcement could mean 'a loss of many low-end rental units', and said in 2026 'our vacancy rate is nil'.

Took part in 39 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A consistent supporter of density in principle and of seniors' and supportive housing, with firm views on where and how: setbacks, heights and the community plan's lines. That made him both the strongest voice for downtown infill and the most frequent dissent on particular sites.
