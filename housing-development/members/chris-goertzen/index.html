Housing and development · Council member

Chris Goertzen: housing, apartments, infill and paying for growth

What did Chris Goertzen say and do about housing and development in Steinbach (affordable housing, apartments and infill, new neighbourhoods, and who pays for growth) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

In short

Chris Goertzen chaired council through the approval of the Hanover Street apartments, seniors' rezonings at Bridge Park and Woodhaven, a nine-storey care and seniors' building on Loewen Boulevard and 18 three-bedroom family rentals on Brandt Street. He cited a rental vacancy rate 'at probably a historic low' (2017) and a shortage of lots (2018), and said Steinbach's density policies were among 'the most aggressive in rural Manitoba'.

His support had limits. In May 2016 he said he would work with staff on renewing the city's seniors-housing incentive; no renewed program came back. He voted against a shorter setback for First Street townhouses so as not to 'usurp' the infill policy, sided with neighbours to keep a Brandt Street block residential, and asked Eden Housing about a long-term plan for one-bedroom units without proposing a city role. In 2018 he named lagoon expansion as the city's next major growth hurdle.

“obviously we're going to see infill and we're going to see increased density, which is part of the official community plan and our desire for our community.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · On the First Street townhouse rezoning, 2018 · May 1, 2018 · 0:41:28

Term by term

2014–2018

He framed downtown density near green space as a goal when council approved the Hanover Street apartments 4–1 (2016), relied on the infill policy to protect neighbours when rezoning land for seniors' housing, and welcomed mixed use 'where people can live and work'. He promised in May 2016 to look at renewing the seniors-housing incentive; it was still called 'the old program' that October and no new program followed. He voted against denying a 3rd Street lot split (tied 3–3), against the 15-ft First Street setback (2018), and for keeping 80 Brandt residential. He supported buying Hanover Street houses for future downtown use, said the city's density policies were aggressive for rural Manitoba, and in 2018 flagged lagoon expansion as the next big hurdle.

Took part in 25 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

He left office in 2018 having set the pattern his successors followed: density welcomed in principle and downtown, decided project by project through the infill policy, with the city's own role in affordability limited to approvals and a seniors' program that lapsed.
