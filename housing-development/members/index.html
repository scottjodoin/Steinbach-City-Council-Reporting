Housing and development · Council members

Council members on housing and development

How did each council member vote and speak on affordable housing, apartments and infill, new neighbourhoods and paying for growth between December 2015 and November 2026?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. Every member supported growth in general and almost every new subdivision. The differences show up on particular apartment sites, on parking, and on how much new homes should pay.

  • The strongest votes for density came from Susan Penner (the only vote against denying the Maplewood townhouses in 2018, and the mover of most affordability exceptions since) and, on main roads and near downtown, Damian Penner and Mayor Earl Funk.
  • The most frequent dissents on particular sites came from Jac Siemens (setbacks, heights, the community plan’s lines) and Jake Hiebert (traffic, parking, mid-block sites). Michael Zwaagstra moved the most housing files and was the firmest defender of parking minimums.
  • On paying for growth, Damian Penner and Jake Hiebert voted against the 2024–2027 development fees at every reading because they were too low; the other five chose 5% a year for affordability.
  • No member proposed a city affordable-housing incentive, a housing needs assessment or a federal Housing Accelerator Fund application in a public meeting.

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

As mayor he backed most apartments ('please don't fear multifamily') and lower fees for entry-level buyers, but joined denials where he judged a street 'not ready', and calls affordable housing a provincial job.

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Called infill and density 'our desire for our community' and pressed for seniors' housing, but the seniors-housing incentive he promised to revisit never came back.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Moved more housing files than anyone and argued for supply and 'developer pays'; led the choice of 5% fee increases and was the firmest defender of parking minimums.

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Argued hardest that big old downtown lots need apartments and that vacancy was 'nil', yet was the most frequent lone dissenter on particular infill sites, setbacks and heights.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

Council's most consistent vote for density and for exceptions that make housing cheaper: lower parking for The Bridge and studio apartments, and new affordable-housing policies in the community plan.

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Pressed hardest for growth to pay its way, voting against the 5% fees at every reading, and for density on main roads; yet voted against the Home Street rezoning and to deny 330 McKenzie.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Mostly a quiet vote with the majority: against 44 First Street in 2019, for Home Street in 2024, and against the seniors' building's reduced parking in 2026.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

The most frequent dissenter on infill details, traffic and parking; voted against the 5% fees as unfair to taxpayers and moved the 330 McKenzie denial.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

Supported family rentals and care beds, but cast lone votes against the city buying downtown houses, saying it pushed residents out of downtown.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Wanted infill to fit the whole street: voted for the First Street rezoning but against its shorter setback, and voted to deny Maplewood while calling the area in need of renewal.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; no record on this topic yet. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.
