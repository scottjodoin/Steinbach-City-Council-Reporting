Housing and development · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

How did Steinbach City Council handle housing, infill and the cost of growth from December 2015 to October 2018, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • Yes to density downtown and to seniors’ housing. Council approved apartments on Hanover Street (4–1), rezoned land for seniors’ buildings at Bridge Park and Woodhaven, and allowed a nine-storey care and seniors’ tower on Loewen Boulevard, calling seniors’ housing an urgent need.
  • But the rules held. It refused parking reductions (including 22 suites meant partly for Eden Health clients), kept the 25-ft infill setback on First Street 4–3, and denied 44 townhouses on Maplewood Street 5–1 after 84 neighbours signed against them.
  • Little city help for affordable housing. The mayor promised in 2016 to revisit the city’s seniors’-housing incentive; no renewed program came back. Eden Housing described long waitlists for one-bedroom units in 2017; council took no action.
  • Growth paid by fees kept near inflation. Development fees were renewed at 2.5% a year to 2019. In 2018 the mayor named lagoon expansion as the next major growth hurdle.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr.

What happened

2016: apartments downtown, seniors first, and a firm parking rule

New building was slow: 50 new homes were permitted in 2016, only 6 of them in multi-family buildings. The year’s main housing debate was CASL Developments’ plan for 24 apartments in two three-storey buildings on three house lots on Hanover Street, in the downtown “transition zone”. Neighbours objected to the height, a parking lot facing their homes and property values. Council approved it 4–1:

“We want to see additional people living in a higher density, and we want to see high-quality being built, and especially when you are near a green space that’ll allow for quality of life for a lot of people.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Feb 2, 2016 · 1:06:08

“I think 3 stories is too high for that particular area. Hanover is a really nice mature street, lots of character homes … But again, I can’t approve more than 2 stories even though I know we want to increase density downtown.”

Councillor Susan Penner, the vote against · Feb 2, 2016 · 1:02:15

The city kept buying Hanover Street houses for future downtown use. Councillor Siemens voted against one purchase:

“I think that these are starter homes that we’re taking off the market, that these are fixer-uppers for first-time homeowners in our downtown location … and I feel it’s not right for the city to be buying these properties when there’s no plan in place.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Apr 5, 2016 · 0:29:50

Seniors’ housing was the one type council called urgent. It rezoned land for Bridge Park and for a 61-unit, five-storey Haven Group building, relying on its infill policy to protect neighbours. In May the mayor said the city’s seniors’-housing incentive, renewed once before, had drawn applications both times:

“Then I’ll work with administration to look at what we have done in the past, what we can do in the future, so that we can certainly fit it into the budget, but also promote additional seniors housing as we move forward.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · May 17, 2016 · 0:33:14

No renewed program came back. When the Haven Group asked in October for a rebate of development fees for its 55+ building, staff said the old program had funded only provincially approved assisted-living or supportive projects, and Councillor Zwaagstra urged that city money stay “targeted”. In December council refused DeWalt Custom Homes’ request for 22 suites instead of 15 on Lowen Boulevard, with 26 parking stalls instead of 36, even though the applicant said Eden Health needed one-bedroom suites and few of its clients drive:

“We can’t get an absolute guarantee that that will always be the case. And the reality is, is that we have enough situations in Steinbach where there’s not enough parking.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Dec 20, 2016 · 0:22:06

Development fees were renewed for 2017–2019 with increases of 2.5% a year, which Councillor Zwaagstra said was “at approximately the rate of inflation”.

2017: a rental crunch noted, but no city role

Building picked up to 233 new homes, and CMHC’s vacancy rate fell to 0.3%. Council approved 18 three-bedroom family rentals on Brandt Street the night they were heard:

“Council, obviously we do need additional multifamily in our community. We’ve Seen the vacancy rate at probably a historic low in our city, and to see an investment happening in our city when it comes to 3 bedrooms, I think is important”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · May 2, 2017 · 0:14:12

It also rezoned 333 Loewen Boulevard, the first use of the residential mixed-use zone, for a nine-storey building with 56 care beds, 56 supportive-living units and 33 seniors’ apartments. In November Eden Health Care Services told council its longest waitlist was single adults needing one-bedroom units, because Manitoba Housing subsidized only seniors and families. The mayor asked about a long-term plan; council took no action. In December it refused two extra apartments on Chapel Drive over parking.

2018: infill tested, the plan updated, the lagoon flagged

Two infill projects divided council. On First Street, members unanimously rezoned six old lots for townhouses with suites, but split 4–3 against letting them sit 15 feet from the street instead of 25. On Maplewood Street, council denied 44 townhouses 5–1 after neighbours sent 49 letters with 84 signatures; Councillor Susan Penner alone voted to proceed.

“obviously we’re going to see infill and we’re going to see increased density, which is part of the official community plan and our desire for our community.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen, on First Street · May 1, 2018 · 0:41:28

After annexing land from the RM of Hanover, council adopted a new community plan and zoning by-law. Residents asked how the plan defined affordability and served low-income families and seniors; no new affordable-housing policy was added. Staff said infill had added almost 100 homes in existing neighbourhoods in under 10 years.

“We need to balance that with the fact that we have to respect what people’s wishes are within neighborhoods. … So we encourage the increased infill, but we can’t— we’re not going to mandate that everyone must change in that regard.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jul 17, 2018 · 1:33:27

Council bought three more Hanover Street houses for future downtown development; Councillor Fehr voted against each. In February the mayor looked ahead:

“I would suspect that the next major hurdle that we may see is actually the expansion of the lagoon or the waste removal.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Feb 6, 2018 · 0:21:25

Where members differed

The divided votes were about how to do infill, not whether to grow: setbacks, how large a change a street could take, and whether the city should buy downtown houses. Susan Penner was the most consistent vote for density; Jac Siemens and John Fehr most often sided with objecting residents.
