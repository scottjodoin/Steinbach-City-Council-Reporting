Housing and development · By term

2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk’s first council

How did Steinbach City Council handle housing, infill and the cost of growth from November 2018 to October 2022, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • First Street filled up, one lot at a time. Council rezoned lot after lot for apartments; by late 2020 staff counted up to 92 units permitted on the street. It approved most infill (44 First Street only 4–3) and refused three plans it called the wrong process or too big for the lot.
  • The market sets the mix and the rents. Members said rents are “completely out of our purview”, praised developers for adjusting to demand, and approved every greenfield subdivision. Vacancy stayed low however many units were approved.
  • Developer pays. Council refused a builder’s $10,000-a-lot demolition grant under a developer-pay policy dating from the 1980s, and called new lots “not a financial windfall”.
  • The first affordable-housing money came from the province. Council directed a $1.34 million provincial grant to Steinbach Community Outreach for 24 units (The Bridge) and cut its parking against staff advice. The city added no money or land of its own.
  • Basement suites made safer, not more common. A 2021 safety by-law set minimum standards for suites; no rule made new suites easier.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2019: renters defended, a 4–3 split, and “developer pays”

The new council heard a string of infill applications. At 126 Maplewood Street, where 56 neighbours signed a petition (“We do not want apartments,” R.W., a resident, told council), it approved nine small suites unanimously:

“I don’t know if there’s anyone in this room that didn’t start out by renting somewhere. I started out in an apartment, and I wasn’t riffraff when I rented. … And I don’t think we want to declare that Steinbach is a place where we don’t want renters.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · May 21, 2019 · 0:26:11

Eight units at 44 First Street divided council 4–3. Councillors Siemens, Jake Hiebert and Bill Hiebert said the community plan meant the south side of the street to stay single-family; the majority said the units were needed:

“We need to have affordable housing for our residents, and single-bedroom suites is something that is really we are desperate for in this city to be able to have single-income people be able to have their own residence.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Jul 16, 2019 · 0:58:48

“We left the south side of First Street as single-family homes, and I think we need to stick with that plan that we had, as I’ve stated earlier. … I think an 8-suite is too much there.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Jul 16, 2019 · 0:49:36

Council unanimously denied eight units at 216 Hanover Street because the developer had not consulted neighbours, and refused the First Street developer’s request for a demolition grant of $10,000 a lot:

“In the 1980s, we, the city of Steinbach, developed the developer pay policy, and we’ve been following it since.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Mar 19, 2019 · 0:52:18

New development-fee by-laws were passed in November 2019 at meetings that were not recorded.

2020: rents “out of our purview”, and a safety by-law for suites

Seventy-two garden apartments on Maplewood and ten more units on First Street passed unanimously. A First Street resident, C.S., objected that “vulnerable people cannot afford high-rise housing. They can’t afford the cost of $1,000 rent.” Council’s answer was that prices are for the market:

“As far as the issue of pricing and all that, that’s completely out of our purview here. … And frankly, usually we hear complaints that the apartments that are being built aren’t high-end enough.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jun 16, 2020 · 0:33:52

“It’s very expensive to develop these older lots, as Councillor Siemens said, these lots that need to be revitalized, very, very costly. And a side-by-side or a single-family dwelling just doesn’t pay the bills.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Jun 16, 2020 · 0:38:10

Council denied six rental units at 14 Ellice Avenue as “too aggressive” for a narrow lot, after neighbours raised drainage, traffic and the “type of people” in lower-income rentals (claims about future tenants are unproven), and refused 24 more units at 85 First Street at first reading on staff advice. It also introduced a safety by-law for secondary suites:

“The downside— there is a downside to this, and that if there is substandard housing today in Steinbach, those same renters would be likely required to pay more to the landlord simply because upgrades to those rental units could be substantial in some cases.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, moving the by-law · Sept 1, 2020 · 0:21:02

2021–2022: growth “at the speed of light”, and The Bridge

Building reached 302 homes in 2020 and 289 in 2021. Council approved the 68-acre Langill Farm neighbourhood, more First Street apartments and a fourplex on Ellice Avenue, and every apartment parking or height variance before it. Members noted the strain:

“Housing is on the verge and has already escaped the grasp of some of being unattainable for young families.”

Councillor Damian Penner, opening remarks · Sept 21, 2021 · 0:00:32

“It is not a financial windfall. It just simply isn’t. … The reason that we want to have new lots within the city is that we want people who want to be able to live in Steinbach to be able to do so. And that means our kids and grandkids are able to buy homes that aren’t priced out of the market.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Aug 17, 2021 · 1:04:15

In 2021 the province gave the city a $1,337,500 affordable-housing grant to pass on. Staff recommended forwarding both Habitat for Humanity’s duplex and Steinbach Community Outreach’s 24-unit building to the province; council sent only the latter, so it would get the full amount. In 2022 it secured the grant with a forgivable 20-year second mortgage and cut the building’s parking from 40 stalls to 30 against staff advice, limiting the site to bachelor and one-bedroom suites:

“And it does not factor in that the lower someone’s annual income is, the less likely they are to be able to afford a vehicle, let alone 1.5 vehicles.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Mar 1, 2022 · 0:55:17

“Like many communities, Steinbach is in short supply of affordable housing. … But the $1.3 million, Mr. Mayor, does not build this building.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, moving the grant · Jul 5, 2022 · 0:06:23

Steinbach Community Outreach asked council to write lower parking for low-income housing into the by-law; it did not. In the fall, 6 First Street and a fourplex at 205 McKenzie Avenue advanced 5–2, with Mayor Funk voting against the fourplex:

“I just don’t feel at this point I’m ready to go with that kind of development in this area.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Nov 1, 2022 · 1:25:44
