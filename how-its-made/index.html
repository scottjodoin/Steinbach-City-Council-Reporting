About this site

How these reports are made

This site is a civic service. It makes Steinbach's council meetings and their history searchable, so anyone can understand what council has discussed and decided over the years, and so voters can find clear, checkable answers instead of rumours.

Last updated Nov 14, 2026.

In short

  • Everything comes from the public record: the City of Steinbach's recorded council meetings and its published minutes.
  • Every quote links to the moment in the city's video where it was said, so you can hear it yourself.
  • AI helps with the reading and drafting; the rules are fixed and the evidence is shown. No quote stands without its source.
  • Residents who speak at council are not named.
  • The full transcripts are free for anyone to search, check or reuse: council meeting transcripts on GitHub.

Where the information comes from

  • Meeting videos. The city records its council meetings and posts them on YouTube. These recordings are the main source, from September 2015 onward.
  • Minutes. The city's published minutes are the official record of decisions, motions, movers and seconders, and recorded votes. When a report says what council decided, it relies on the minutes.
  • Agendas and agenda packages, to confirm figures and background.
  • Other cities' own published programs and by-laws, for the “how Steinbach compares” sections.

The site is independent. It is not affiliated with the City of Steinbach, any council member or any candidate.

From meeting to report

  1. Transcribe. Each meeting video is turned into text by a speech-to-text service, AssemblyAI. YouTube's own automatic captions are used as a second, independent check on the words.
  2. Name the speakers. Each voice is matched to a person from the meeting itself (the mayor handing the floor to a councillor by name, people introducing themselves) and from the minutes (who moved each motion, who asked which question). Every match records its evidence and a confidence level. Recorded votes are never used to decide who was speaking.
  3. Protect residents. Residents' names are removed from the transcripts and replaced with “Resident 1”, “Resident 2”; reports use initials. Council members, city staff, and people speaking for a business or organization are named.
  4. Research a topic. For each report, the transcripts and minutes for every meeting in the period are searched and read in full around each mention: every decision, debate, promise and request on the topic, what each council member said and did, and the reasons council gave.
  5. Write and check. The report is drafted from that evidence, with decisions taken from the minutes. A script then checks that every quote appears word for word in its transcript and links to its moment in the video, and that quotes published by name come only from council members and staff whose identification is high-confidence.
  6. Correct. When someone reports an error, it is checked against the video and the minutes, fixed, and the correction is noted.

What AI does, and what it doesn't

AI tools do much of the heavy lifting: speech-to-text, matching voices to names, searching hundreds of hours of meetings, and drafting the reports. That is what makes a site like this possible for a small independent project. We use Anthropic's Claude for the research and drafting.

  • AI works within fixed rules. Quote exactly, link the source, name what couldn't be confirmed, take no side, never guess at motives, and never use a recorded vote as evidence of who was speaking.
  • The evidence is always shown. Every quote links to its moment in the video, so a reader never has to take the AI's word, or ours.
  • An editor decides what is published. A person chooses the topics, sets the rules, listens to samples of the recordings, and decides what goes on the site.
  • AI makes mistakes. It can mishear a word or attach a line to the wrong person. How often, and what we do about it, is on the Data accuracy page.

The transcripts are free to use

The full speaker-labelled transcripts of every council meeting from November 2022 to November 2026 are published on GitHub, free for any use (CC0). Each meeting has:

  • a readable transcript, with a link to the video for every line;
  • the same transcript as data, with each line's speaker, role, confidence level and line type;
  • a speaker map: who each voice in the recording is, and the evidence for it.

Council meeting transcripts and speaker maps on GitHub. Earlier years will be added as they are rechecked.

Want to research council yourself? Ask your own AI about council explains two ways: point an AI chat assistant at this site, or download the transcripts and run an AI agent over them.

Privacy

Residents who speak at council do so in public, but this site doesn't make them searchable by name. The published transcripts are checked for residents' names before each release. Visits to this site are counted without cookies or personal tracking: see Privacy.
