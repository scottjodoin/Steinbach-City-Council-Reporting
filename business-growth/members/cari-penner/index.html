Business growth and investment · Council member

Cari Penner: business growth and investment

What did Cari Penner say and do about business growth and investment in Steinbach (economic development, industrial land, downtown, business taxes and rules) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

In short

Cari Penner supported several measures to help business investment: she argued in 2016 for relaxing the city's road specifications so a private commercial park could go ahead, spoke for the 2017 golf club land deal as keeping an economic asset, and moved two of the 2018 Hanover Street land purchases, saying council should have long-term vision for downtown.

She also stood for standards. She was the lone vote against the first third-party digital sign on Main Street, saying council should first decide how it wanted downtown to look, voted against excusing businesses on gravel roads from paving, and seconded the 2018 motion to put Sunday shopping on the ballot because it was time to hear from citizens. In 2015–2017 the two Councillors Penner often cannot be told apart in the recordings, so some of her votes are not attributed.

“I think now's the time for council to consider those types of things, because this is a precedent and we are setting a precedent. So I will not vote in favor of the motion.”

Councillor Cari Penner · Voting against the first third-party digital sign on Main Street · Sept 4, 2018 · 0:18:21

Term by term

2014–2018

Seconded the 2016 relaxed road standard for a private commercial park, moved the 2017 parking-fine increase, spoke for the golf club land deal, and moved two downtown land purchases in 2018. Seconded the Sunday-shopping ballot question (defeated 2–5). Voted against the gravel-road paving exemption (carried 4–2), the 2018 paving waiver on Park Road West (6–1) and the Main Street digital sign (6–1).

Took part in 11 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

Supportive of investment and of the city planning ahead, but protective of downtown's look and the city's standards, often alone in saying so.
