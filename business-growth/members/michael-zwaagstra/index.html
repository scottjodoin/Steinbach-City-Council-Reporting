Business growth and investment · Council member

Michael Zwaagstra: business growth and investment

What did Michael Zwaagstra say and do about business growth and investment in Steinbach (economic development, industrial land, downtown, business taxes and rules) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Michael Zwaagstra spoke on more business items than any other member. He drove the loosening of sign rules in 2015–2016 ('businesses should be able to advertise'), said he could not recall council ever denying an electronic sign, welcomed the shorter 2026 zoning by-law and its one-third cut to downtown parking, and in 2023 cut a proposed development-fee increase from 7.5% to 5% a year to keep building affordable. He said the best projects come from private developers rather than government.

He also held the line on fairness. He opposed one-off exceptions that would set a precedent (a private park's road standard in 2016, an industrial lot buy-back, an after-the-fact paving variance), called the 2017 golf club purchase 'a bailout that is dressed up as a land deal', and was the clearest voice for keeping retail out of the industrial park because the city subsidizes it for industry. In 2024 he asked the economic development office what its markers of success were, and in 2025 he questioned its unconsulted immigration pilot.

“There's a number of reasons why retail is not permitted automatically in industrial zoning, partly because our industrial parks are subsidized by the city because we're trying to attract more industry. So obviously we want to direct retail to commercial spaces which are not subsidized in the same way”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · On why retail is not allowed automatically in the industrial park · Oct 4, 2022 · 0:17:51

Term by term

2014–2018

Moved the 2015 year-long mobile sign permits and led the 2016 rewrite, choosing the least restrictive options and a 1-foot setback; kept signs out of the public right-of-way for safety. He opposed a relaxed road standard for one private commercial park, the buy-back of an unsold industrial lot and an after-the-fact paving variance, and led opposition to the golf club land deal. He moved several downtown land purchases, kept parking fines low so shoppers would not be scared off, refused a large home-based business, and voted against the Sunday-shopping ballot question because the threshold was 'a letter from one person'.

Took part in 28 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Moved industrial lot sales, the downtown event centre agreement, the Millbrook Market commercial plan and the Acres Drive extension, and said the city's voice in COVID-19 should be for reopening. He refused a landscaping waiver because the rule dated from 2010, opposed a school in the industrial park ('the obvious answer is no'), favoured parking flexibility downtown, and in 2022 voted against a clothing store in the industrial park while setting out why retail belongs on unsubsidized commercial land.

Took part in 19 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Allowed a narrow 'common sense' exception for charity book sales but defended the industrial park against broader retail and caretaker homes. He amended development fees to 5% a year, asked the economic development office for targets in 2024, challenged the immigration pilot in 2025, supported signs and the shorter zoning by-law, and moved the refusal of a cannabis plebiscite request. He did not run in 2026.

Took part in 22 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Steady over eleven years: deregulation where it lets businesses operate and advertise, and firm, uniform rules where an exception would favour one business or let the city's subsidized land compete with private landlords.
