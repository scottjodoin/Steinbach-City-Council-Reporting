Business growth and investment · Council member

Jake Hiebert: business growth and investment

What did Jake Hiebert say and do about business growth and investment in Steinbach (economic development, industrial land, downtown, business taxes and rules) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Jake Hiebert was the member most likely to vote against a business application. He voted against a clothing store and charity book sales in the industrial park ('retail is retail, light industrial is light industrial'), against a zero front setback on Loewen Boulevard for safety, and against moving industrial land into the downtown district. In 2023 he voted against holding development fee increases to 5% because, he said, when fees fall short the taxpayer pays the difference.

He seconded the 2021 economic development office terms. In August 2025, news coverage reported him saying its results were not there and that continuing to pay $65,000 would be futile. In 2024 he moved to let a developer drop main-floor commercial space it could not lease, but in 2026 he worried that another mixed-use project had become an apartment block in a commercial zone.

“Because the development fee does not pay for the complete cost, the city pays the difference. Where does the city get their money? From the taxpayer. I think this is unfair.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert · Voting against the 5% development-fee increase because he wanted more · Nov 7, 2023 · 0:09:31

Term by term

2018–2022

Seconded the 2019 thrift-store approval and the 2021 economic development office terms. Voted to hold a truck dealer to the tree-planting rule, against a zero front setback on Loewen Boulevard (6–1, safety), and against the Skirts and Co. clothing store in the industrial park (5–2). Called industrial subdivisions market-driven and welcomed Main Street patios.

Took part in 14 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Voted against the downtown-district expansion next to Loewen Windows and against charity book sales in the industrial park (both 5–2), and against the 5% development-fee increase because he wanted 7.5%. Moved the 2024 main-floor commercial waiver, seconded the refusal of retail on a new industrial lot and of the cannabis plebiscite request, and in 2025 was reported saying the economic development office's results were not there. In October 2026 he questioned an apartment block in a commercial corridor.

Took part in 14 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistent on land use and on who pays: keep each zone for its purpose, and make growth cover its costs; he went from backing the economic development office to judging it by results and finding none.
