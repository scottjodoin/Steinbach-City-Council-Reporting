Business growth and investment · Council member

Susan Penner: business growth and investment

What did Susan Penner say and do about business growth and investment in Steinbach (economic development, industrial land, downtown, business taxes and rules) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

In short

Susan Penner became council's main member on economic development after 2018, as its representative on the Steinbach Community Development Corporation. She presented the 2019 'Why Steinbach' investor website, worked with the mayor and the Chamber for about a year and a half on the 2021 economic development office, and moved the Acres Drive extension and the 2025 August Cove lots, saying business was booming and lots were short. She argued that investing in infrastructure is itself economic development.

She has also been the most persistent questioner of the business tax that goes to the Chamber of Commerce. In 2019 she said a large city grant could compromise a chamber that should be lobbying for lower business taxes; in 2025 she called the $225,000 'way out of line' and asked whether it should be capped. Each year she has still moved the levy, because businesses ask to keep it. She voted against the golf club land deal and against a Sunday-shopping ballot question, saying a day of rest is part of what makes Steinbach unique.

“So I feel this $225,000 is way out of line, and I think we need to review that. Again, that's not— it doesn't affect residential property taxes, but it does impact our businesses.”

Councillor Susan Penner · On the Chamber of Commerce's share of the business tax, at the 2025 budget hearing · Mar 4, 2025 · 0:19:31

Term by term

2014–2018

Warned in 2016 that a regional business draw does not mean a regional tax base ('Growth costs money'). Moved the 2017 letter asking Ottawa to drop its small-business tax changes. Voted against the golf club land purchase and the Sunday-shopping ballot question. Supported the downtown land assembly as low risk, and said she wanted more businesses downtown, not more parking lots. In 2015–2017 the two Councillors Penner often cannot be told apart in the recordings, so some of her sign votes are not attributed.

Took part in 13 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

As the city's development corporation representative she presented the 'Why Steinbach' website (2019) and helped design the economic development office (2021). She questioned the Chamber grant in 2019 and said a July study session would decide the business tax's future; no decision was made public. She moved the Acres Drive extension, Main Street patios and small-business variances, voted to waive street trees for a truck dealer, and wanted a public hearing before refusing a school in the industrial park.

Took part in 17 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Called the Chamber's $225,000 'way out of line' (March 2025) and asked whether it should be capped (April 2025), while moving the levy each year. Moved the August Cove industrial lots, the new zoning by-law and the six-storey Main Street building; argued against retail in the subsidized industrial park as unfair to commercial landlords and against caretaker homes there; supported the lower 5% development-fee increase. She was absent for the August 2025 vote ending the economic development grant. Deputy Mayor from November 2026.

Took part in 23 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From an occasional voice on business costs to the council member most involved in economic development and the one who keeps returning to the Chamber's share of the business tax, without, so far, a public change to it.
