Business growth and investment · Council member

Earl Funk: business growth and investment

What did Earl Funk say and do about business growth and investment in Steinbach (economic development, industrial land, downtown, business taxes and rules) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

In short

As a councillor, Earl Funk was one of the members most ready to loosen rules for business. He said the spirit of the sign by-law was to allow advertising, backed the 2018 change that excused businesses on gravel roads from paving their lots, and argued downtown parking rules were too strict when 25 to 30 Main Street businesses did not meet them. He voted against the 2017 golf club land deal as unfair to other business owners, and against putting Sunday shopping on the 2018 ballot.

As mayor since November 2018 he has described council's job as creating an environment for business to grow. He gave the city's development corporation an economic development role, helped design the 2021 economic development office with Susan Penner, and in 2025 backed ending its grant, saying economic development is a partnership and no single office could do it. He said in 2019 that he had always been 'conflicted' about the business tax going to the Chamber of Commerce, and agreed in 2025 that it needed review, but no review has come back in public. He supports keeping retail out of the industrial park and says downtown change is driven by developers.

“I do believe that economic development is a partnership. … And I think our— part of the nearsightedness was to expect that one office would do it. And really, it's a partnership, it's a team thing.”

Mayor Earl Funk · As council ended the economic development office grant · Aug 5, 2025 · 0:29:55

Term by term

2014–2018

A councillor who favoured flexibility for business. He brought the business community's complaints about the 2015 sign rules to council, argued in 2016 that the sign by-law should allow advertising, and supported the 2018 rule that businesses on gravel roads need not pave ('we shouldn't expect them to give us more than gravel'). He moved the first of three 2018 Hanover Street land purchases, wanted downtown busy beyond business hours, and said parking rules there were too onerous. He voted against the $500,000 golf club land purchase as unfair to other business owners and against a Sunday-shopping ballot question, saying he had not heard enough of a case for it.

Took part in 18 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Elected mayor in 2018. He tasked the Steinbach Community Development Corporation with starting economic development, credited federally funded industrial lots sold at cost, and signed the downtown event centre agreement. He said he had been conflicted about the Chamber's business-tax grant from the start but kept it. In 2020 he sent letters urging the province to reopen business, and voted to hold a truck dealer to the city's tree-planting rule. With Susan Penner he worked for about a year and a half on the 2021 economic development office, hoping it would bring 'many, many businesses'.

Took part in 22 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He backed holding development fee increases to 5% a year because it matched inflation, called the August Cove industrial lots proactive, and said the six-storey Main Street building showed downtown density is developer-driven. He warned that federal pilot programs end up funded by cities, agreed in 2025 that the Chamber's growing share of the business tax needed review, and in August 2025 supported ending the economic development grant, calling it 'nearsightedness' to expect one office to do the job. In 2026 he described his lunches with business owners and called council 'pro-growth and business-friendly'.

Took part in 21 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From a councillor who argued for fewer rules on signs, paving and parking to a mayor who frames council's role as setting conditions and partnering, with land, low taxes and the Chamber as the tools, after a five-year experiment with a dedicated economic development office that he helped start and then let end.
