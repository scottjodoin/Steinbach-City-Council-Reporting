Business growth and investment · Council member

Chris Goertzen: business growth and investment

What did Chris Goertzen say and do about business growth and investment in Steinbach (economic development, industrial land, downtown, business taxes and rules) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

In short

As mayor, Chris Goertzen usually framed business questions as a balance between what businesses need and how the city looks. He promised the Chamber of Commerce quick action on sign rules and supported year-long mobile sign permits, but voted against allowing mobile signs beside electronic boards (clutter) and against excusing a business that had not paved its lot as its development agreement required. In 2018 he voted against the change excusing businesses on gravel roads from paving, calling it a mistake to move away from the city's high standard.

He supported the long-running downtown land assembly, saying assembled lots gain value, and voted for the 2017 golf club land purchase, which passed 4–3. He opposed a Sunday-shopping ballot question in 2018 because he did not favour governing by referendum, and he favoured cooperation with neighbouring municipalities, saying their growth would be Steinbach's growth. He declared a perceived conflict and left the room for one 2016 decision on a private commercial park.

“I think going away from the high standard that we have is a mistake, and so I will not support this.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Voting against excusing businesses on gravel roads from paving · Jun 5, 2018 · 1:12:39

Term by term

2014–2018

Chaired the council that rewrote the mobile sign rules at the Chamber's request (2016), seeking 'a balanced approach' that respected businesses and the city's appearance; he voted against signs beside electronic boards. He backed buying land downtown and industrial land for strategic reasons, voted for the golf club land deal, and argued for high, uniform standards: against the after-the-fact paving variance (2017) and the gravel-road paving exemption (2018, carried 4–2). He opposed the Sunday-shopping ballot question as governing by referendum, and met the RM of La Broquerie's reeve to set up a joint committee after a dispute over commercial zoning at the border.

Took part in 21 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

Consistent through his last term: supportive of business growth and of the city buying land, but protective of standards and of council's own role in deciding, rather than by referendum.
