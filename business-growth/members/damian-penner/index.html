Business growth and investment · Council member

Damian Penner: business growth and investment

What did Damian Penner say and do about business growth and investment in Steinbach (economic development, industrial land, downtown, business taxes and rules) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Damian Penner moved the 2021 terms for the economic development office, calling it a wise decision and insisting it be run by a private board rather than city staff. He warned more often than any other member that the industrial lot supply was running short, argued that government creates jobs by creating the environment for them, and sought a provincial grant for the Hespeler Street industrial corridor. By 2024 and 2025 he was questioning the office's overlap with the Chamber and the secrecy of its immigration pilot.

On downtown he argued for density and against parking requirements ('parking lots don't bring people downtown'). He did not always side with lower costs for builders: in 2023 he voted against the 5% development-fee increase because he wanted 7.5%, so existing taxpayers would not cover the gap, and in 2026 he said the Chamber's ever-growing business-tax share may need to be looked at.

“And parking lots don't bring people downtown. Businesses do.”

Councillor Damian Penner · On the new zoning by-law · Dec 3, 2024 · 0:19:50

Term by term

2018–2022

Moved the 2019 business tax levy while leaving a review open, supported cutting a Main Street gym's parking requirement for an 'intimate feel' downtown, and moved to hold a truck dealer to the tree-planting rule. Moved the 2021 economic development office terms, warned in 2021 and 2022 that industrial lots were in short supply, voted against a school in the industrial park, and welcomed sidewalk patios. Spoke as an employer against the province's 2022 minimum-wage change.

Took part in 14 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Supported moving a small industrial remnant into the downtown district, said small industrial lots go very quickly, and voted against charity book sales in the industrial park and against the 5% development-fee increase (wanted 7.5%). Questioned Steinbach Economic Development's overlap with the Chamber (2024) and its immigration pilot (2025). Backed the zoning by-law's parking cut and the six-storey Main Street building, urged residents to buy local amid U.S. tariff threats, and in 2026 said the business tax growing 'in perpetuity' may need discussion. He was absent for the August 2025 vote ending the economic development grant.

Took part in 24 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From a strong backer of the economic development office to one of its sharper questioners, consistent throughout on two points: industrial land supply is the base of economic development, and growth should not be subsidized by existing taxpayers.
