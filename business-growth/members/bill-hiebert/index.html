Business growth and investment · Council member

Bill Hiebert: business growth and investment

What did Bill Hiebert say and do about business growth and investment in Steinbach (economic development, industrial land, downtown, business taxes and rules) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Bill Hiebert voted with the majority on almost every business item and spoke on few of them. He seconded the downtown event centre agreement in 2019, voted to waive street-edge trees for a truck dealer in 2020 (lost 3–4), and voted for a clothing store in the industrial park in 2022.

In 2024 he asked Steinbach Economic Development whether it was in touch with any manufacturer planning to come to Steinbach. In August 2025 he seconded the motion to end its grant; news coverage reported him saying he did not see the value he had expected. He supports the business tax that goes to the Chamber because it lets the Chamber promote local business.

“Are you presently in touch with somebody that's possibly coming to Steinbach to build, to set up a manufacturing facility of some sort?”

Councillor Bill Hiebert · Questioning Steinbach Economic Development about investment leads · Mar 5, 2024 · 0:43:46

Term by term

2018–2022

Few attributable remarks on this topic. Seconded the downtown event centre agreement and an industrial lot sale, voted to waive street trees for a truck dealer (lost 3–4), and voted for the Skirts and Co. clothing store in the industrial park (5–2). Several of his turns are hard to separate from Jake Hiebert's in the transcripts.

Took part in 11 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Moved the December 2022 industrial subdivision on Acres Drive, asked the economic development office about manufacturing leads (2024), said he felt for Adult & Teen Challenge while voting to refuse retail on a new industrial lot (2025), and seconded the motion ending the economic development grant. In 2026 he said the business tax empowers the Chamber to promote business.

Took part in 19 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A quiet member on this topic who voted with the majority, moving over two terms from supporting the economic development office to seconding its end.
