Business growth and investment · Council member

Jac Siemens: business growth and investment

What did Jac Siemens say and do about business growth and investment in Steinbach (economic development, industrial land, downtown, business taxes and rules) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Jac Siemens argued repeatedly that Steinbach had to work harder to attract industry. In 2016 he moved a relaxed road standard for a private commercial park, noting no major manufacturer had come in ten years, and praised small industrial bays as incubators. He pushed to sell long-listed city industrial land, asked for a full review of downtown parking, and moved the 2018 motion to put Sunday shopping on the ballot (defeated 2–5).

He was also the member most often in the minority. He voted against year-long mobile signs and the first downtown house purchase (no plan), against charging a charity for the Acres Drive road, and against moving industrial land into the downtown district. In 2025 he moved to end the economic development office grant because the hoped-for development had not come, saying landowners and business owners are the community's real economic developers; the same year he was the lone vote to allow a showroom in the industrial park and the lone vote against refusing a cannabis plebiscite request.

“the city has put in $65,000 a year for the last 5 years from 2021 to current … The, the reason I think we need to stop the funding is that we haven't seen the development that we would have liked to have seen”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Moving to end the economic development office grant · Aug 5, 2025 · 0:24:52

Term by term

2014–2018

Opposed year-long mobile sign permits ('a sign that's up for 365 days is a permanent sign') and the first 2016 downtown house purchase because there was no plan. Moved the relaxed road standard for a private commercial park, backed the paving variance for an implement dealer, praised industrial condos as incubators and seconded the golf club land deal. Moved the Sunday-shopping referendum, which would have cost nothing extra on the election ballot, and asked a future council for a complete downtown parking review.

Took part in 24 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Explained how the business tax came to fund the Chamber, voted to waive street trees for a truck dealer (lost 3–4) and against levying a charity for the Acres Drive road (5–1). Pushed to sell 45 Tower Avenue and enforce build deadlines on city lots, wanted a public hearing before refusing a school in the industrial park, and backed a downtown brewpub because downtown was 'fairly empty of people traffic'.

Took part in 13 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Voted against moving industrial land into the downtown district over Loewen Windows' objection, said Steinbach holds developers to a standard, and explained that the city sells industrial lots at or near cost. Moved to end the economic development grant in 2025 and said any future office should sit under the Chamber. Cast the lone vote to allow the Home Workshop showroom on Industrial Road and the lone vote against refusing TobaGrown's cannabis plebiscite request. He did not run in 2026.

Took part in 19 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

An independent streak throughout: eager for industry and jobs, sceptical of plans without a purpose, willing to put questions to voters, and in the end the member who moved to close the office council had hoped would bring that industry.
