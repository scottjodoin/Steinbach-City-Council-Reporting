Business growth and investment · Council member

John Fehr: business growth and investment

What did John Fehr say and do about business growth and investment in Steinbach (economic development, industrial land, downtown, business taxes and rules) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

In short

John Fehr generally argued for flexibility for businesses. He backed the looser mobile sign rules (while noting they were unfair to businesses that paid for permanent signs), wanted the Pistons' game-day signs allowed in the right-of-way, supported a hotel on industrial land after visitors to the 2016 Summer Games had to stay elsewhere, and moved the 2018 change excusing start-up businesses on gravel roads from paving. He moved the 2017 purchase of golf club land, saying council does not give operating grants but buys land.

On downtown he broke with the majority: he voted against each of the three 2018 Hanover Street land purchases, saying they drove residents away from downtown and that some business people feared being bought out (his own report, not verified). On Sunday shopping he deferred to business owners who did not want to be made to open.

“I will not support this purchase because I feel that we are driving residences farther and farther from the downtown. I believe it is counterproductive in our efforts to keep the downtown active.”

Councillor John Fehr · Voting against a downtown land purchase · Jul 3, 2018 · 0:04:57

Term by term

2014–2018

Seconded the year-long sign permits, supported the 2016 sign rewrite and was the vote against keeping mobile signs out of the right-of-way. As Deputy Mayor he chaired the 2016 decision to relax road standards for a private commercial park ('we are all looking for commercial development'). Backed the hotel on light-industrial land, paving relief for businesses on gravel roads and affordable incubator lots, and was uneasy that the Chamber slogan licence did not cover promoting business. Moved the golf land purchase; voted against all three 2018 downtown land purchases and against the Sunday-shopping ballot question.

Took part in 24 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

Consistent in his last term: practical help for individual businesses, scepticism of the city's long-range downtown land plans.
