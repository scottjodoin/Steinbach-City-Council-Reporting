Business growth and investment · Council members

Council members on business growth

How did each council member vote and speak on business growth and investment in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. Members broadly shared the same view (supply land, keep rules and taxes light, let the market lead), so the differences show up in individual applications and a few larger votes.

  • 2015–2018 was the divided period. Twenty split votes, on signs, paving, downtown land and the 2017 golf club land deal (4–3, with Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra and Susan Penner against). John Fehr alone opposed three downtown land purchases; Cari Penner alone opposed Main Street’s first digital billboard.
  • Since 2018 the splits are about land use and who pays. Jake Hiebert voted against retail in the industrial park most often; he and Damian Penner wanted higher development fees in 2023. Jac Siemens was most often on the applicant’s side, and the lone dissent in 2025 on a showroom and a cannabis plebiscite request.
  • Susan Penner led on economic development after 2018 and questioned the Chamber’s business-tax share most often. Michael Zwaagstra was the clearest voice for keeping the industrial park for industry. Jac Siemens moved to end the economic development grant in 2025; both he and Zwaagstra left council in 2026.

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

As a councillor he pushed to loosen sign and parking rules and voted against the golf deal; as mayor he built, then ended, the economic development office and calls council 'pro-growth and business-friendly'.

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Balanced business needs against the city's look: backed downtown land assembly and the golf land deal, but defended high standards on paving and signs and opposed governing by referendum.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Council's most frequent voice on business rules: freed up advertising and cut red tape, but insisted on uniform standards and led the case for keeping retail out of the subsidized industrial park.

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Pushed for flexibility to attract industry, moved the 2018 Sunday-shopping ballot question and, in 2025, the motion ending the economic development grant; often the lone dissent.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

Council's lead on economic development after 2018 and the member who has questioned the Chamber's business-tax share most often, while moving the levy each year because businesses want it.

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Moved the 2021 economic development office and warned most often about the industrial lot shortage; a downtown density advocate who wanted growth to pay more of its own way.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Rarely spoke on business items; asked the economic development office about manufacturing leads, seconded the motion ending its grant, and supports the Chamber's business tax.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

The most frequent dissenter on business land-use: against retail in the industrial park, for higher development fees, and in 2025 said the economic development office's results weren't there.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

A business owner who favoured flexibility on signs and paving and moved the golf land purchase, but alone voted against all three 2018 downtown land purchases.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Backed relaxed specs for a private commercial park and the golf land deal, seconded the Sunday-shopping ballot question, and was the lone vote against Main Street's first digital billboard.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; no record on this topic yet. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.
