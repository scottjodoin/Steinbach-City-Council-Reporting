Business growth and investment · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

What did Steinbach City Council do for business growth and investment from December 2015 to October 2018, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • Rules bent toward business. After businesses and the Chamber of Commerce complained, council moved mobile sign permits from 30 days to a full year and rewrote the sign rules, mostly choosing the least restrictive option and overriding management’s advice. In 2018 it stopped requiring businesses on gravel roads to pave their lots.
  • Land was the main tool. The city sold its own industrial lots (about $75,000 each), approved private industrial condos, and continued a 25-year strategy of buying houses downtown: eight lots in 2016–2018, with no plan adopted for them.
  • The most divided council on this topic. Twenty split votes, more than the next two terms combined: on signs, paving exceptions, downtown purchases (John Fehr alone against three) and above all the 2017 golf club land deal, which passed 4–3.
  • No dedicated economic development effort. The Chamber received the 0.5% business tax; the city licensed its slogan and backed its fight against federal tax changes. A regional tourism membership request got no decision.
  • No Sunday-shopping vote. A request to put Sunday shopping on the 2018 ballot was defeated 2–5.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr.

What happened

2015–2016: signs, at businesses’ request

When the city began enforcing its 2015 zoning by-law, businesses using mobile signs complained. Council replaced 30-day permits (with 30 days down between them and a 90-day yearly cap) with a single annual permit:

“We’re doing this because businesses should be able to advertise their products and their services. That’s why we’re doing this.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Dec 15, 2015 · 0:20:49

“I think a sign that’s up for 365 days is a permanent sign, and we should deal with it as such”

Councillor Jac Siemens, during the 2016 rewrite · Sept 6, 2016 · 0:53:23

The Chamber of Commerce asked for a joint committee to write a new by-law; none was struck, but council worked through the rules clause by clause with the Chamber’s written input. It kept year-long permits, allowed standard 9-by-10-foot signs downtown and for non-profits, and, after the Chamber showed signs being pushed into parking lots, cut the front setback to one foot:

“if the sign has to go back 10 feet and it’s in the middle of a parking lot, who does that benefit? What’s that for? Who are we helping?”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Oct 18, 2016 · 0:37:31

Two limits stayed: no mobile signs in the public right-of-way, and no lit digital mobile signs, which a local sign maker asked for and which were left to a future zoning review.

2016–2017: land, and exceptions for investors

The city was the main seller of industrial land, with unsold lots in 2017. Council allowed a charity resale store, a hotel and a church on light-industrial land, and approved two private industrial condominiums. For a private commercial park on PTH 12 North it allowed open-ditch drainage instead of curb and gutter, over staff’s warning:

“We have not had a major manufacturer move here in the last 10 years that will employ entry-level employees”

Councillor Jac Siemens · May 3, 2016 · 0:20:48

“If this exception is granted for one developer, I would fully expect that all future developers would also expect the same treatment.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · May 3, 2016 · 0:29:50

Downtown, council kept buying houses around the arenas and Hanover Street, renting them out until needed for recreation, parking or development. Jac Siemens voted against the first purchase in April 2016 because there was no plan for the land; none was adopted in this term.

2017: the golf club land deal

The Steinbach Fly-In Golf Club, in financial difficulty, asked council in closed session for a $500,000 grant and was refused. Council then bought 40 acres of the course for $500,000, paid over three years, and leased it back for $1 a year. It passed 4–3:

“It is a bailout that is dressed up as a land deal.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Dec 5, 2017 · 1:09:41

“This might be perceived as unfair to other business owners as we are using tax dollars to help the golf course out in their financial position.”

Councillor Earl Funk · Dec 5, 2017 · 1:11:37

Supporters called it a strategic land purchase that kept a regional attraction open; the mover, John Fehr, said council does not give operating grants but buys land. The same year council asked Ottawa to drop its proposed small-business tax changes at the Chamber’s request, and licensed the Chamber’s “More for You Here” slogan.

2018: paving, parking, a downtown block and Sunday shopping

With the new zoning by-law for nearly 3,000 annexed acres, council voted 4–2 to require paved parking only where a street is paved:

“A lot of times when you have startup business and they’re on a gravel road, a lot of times the property values are probably somewhat less, and a lot of times there isn’t a lot of money to do those things like asphalting or hard surfacing.”

Councillor John Fehr · Jun 5, 2018 · 1:11:11

Council bought three more Hanover Street lots to finish a downtown block, each over John Fehr’s objection that the city was pushing residents out of downtown. It let a church fill the long-empty former credit union building at 305 Main with 10 parking stalls instead of 100, and councillors said downtown parking rules were too strict:

“if you go down Main Street, you would probably find 25 to 30 businesses that do not comply with parking at this point. And if it’s too onerous, if our parking is too onerous in the central business district, then we need to change that”

Councillor Earl Funk · Jul 3, 2018 · 0:44:45

A former councillor, D.B., asked for a by-law allowing all stores to open on Sundays, then for a ballot question. Jac Siemens moved a non-binding referendum on the October ballot at no extra cost; it was defeated 2–5:

“I believe that a referendum may well favor Sunday shopping. I don’t know whether I’m going to put my fellow business people in a position where they have to be open when they don’t want to be open.”

Councillor John Fehr · Aug 7, 2018 · 0:34:00

“what people like about Steinbach is that we don’t have Sunday shopping. It’s a day for families. It’s a day of rest. It’s what makes Steinbach unique”

Councillor Susan Penner · Aug 7, 2018 · 0:36:09

Mayor Goertzen said the question was really whether to govern by referendum, which he did not favour. The province’s cannabis retail question did go on the same ballot.

Where members differed

This council split more often on business questions than the next two combined. The lines were not left and right: Michael Zwaagstra led on loosening sign rules but opposed one-off exceptions; Jac Siemens wanted flexibility for industry but not year-long signs; John Fehr opposed the downtown land purchases; Mayor Goertzen and Cari Penner defended paving standards.
