Business growth and investment · By term

2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk’s first council

What did Steinbach City Council do for business growth and investment from November 2018 to October 2022, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • Growth was the strategy. Asked about economic development, councillors pointed to building permits and infrastructure. The city’s development corporation got an economic development role and launched a “Why Steinbach” investor website (2019); no incentives or grants were created.
  • Industrial lots kept selling out. Federally funded lots sold at cost were gone by October 2019; councillors warned of low inventory again in 2021 and 2022. Council extended Acres Drive (built in 2022 without borrowing) and subdivided nine more small city lots.
  • A five-year economic development office. In 2021 council agreed to match private money, up to $65,000 a year for 2021–2025, for an office run by the Chamber of Commerce, with no measurable targets.
  • The Chamber grant was first questioned. In 2019 Susan Penner and the mayor raised a possible conflict in passing the business tax to the Chamber; a study-session review was promised, and nothing changed in public.
  • COVID-19 meant advocacy, not relief. Council lobbied the province to reopen business but offered no local tax, fee or patio relief.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2019: “Steinbach’s been thriving”

Economic development had been a campaign theme in 2018. The new council’s answer was that growth was already happening:

“We often hear a lot about economic development during the campaign and since, but clearly if you look back on our, well, the past decade, Steinbach’s been thriving.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Jan 15, 2019 · 1:08:45

The mayor gave the Steinbach Community Development Corporation, the city’s land company, an economic development role; in June it launched an investor website listing land and contacts. The city’s small “incubator” industrial lots sold out:

“We received approximately $1 million from the federal government, and that was to build the lots, and we could sell them at cost, and that— there’s no lots left at this point. They’re all been sold.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Oct 1, 2019 · 0:00:05

At the April business tax vote, the City Manager explained that when council cut the business tax from 1% about 15 years earlier, it began a yearly grant to the Chamber of Commerce roughly equal to the tax. Councillors questioned it:

“I do think there’s potentially a conflict there because in theory chambers should be advocating to reduce business tax, for example.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Apr 2, 2019 · 0:36:49

“I’ve been conflicted on this right from the start, but we’ve always gone forward because the chamber brings added value to our business community in many ways, or perceived value.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Apr 2, 2019 · 0:38:09

Susan Penner said a July study session would decide whether the tax continued. No decision was made public, and the tax and grant continued unchanged. In October council signed an agreement for a $42.5 million event centre on the downtown land it had assembled.

2020: private plans, COVID-19 and the next road

Council approved Millbrook Market, a 20-acre commercial and housing plan beside Superstore:

“the best projects aren’t governments always coming up with ideas. It’s the private developers who do it, and we certainly see that here.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Mar 3, 2020 · 0:21:57

When COVID-19 closed businesses, the mayor and Susan Penner met the Chamber, and the city sent the Premier business owners’ stories and, in June, a letter urging faster reopening. We found no city-level relief: no tax or fee deferral, patio program or business grant. The $947,679 federal-provincial Safe Restart grant was put in a reserve for a future project. In October council refused, 4–3, to waive street trees for a truck dealer. In December it gave first reading to extending Acres Drive into the industrial park, paid by frontage levies on the city’s development corporation and a disability charity:

“the CDC’s job is to keep developing, so that money won’t stay there. That money will develop another road, and it’ll open up more land.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Dec 15, 2020 · 0:32:05

2021–2022: an economic development office, and short supply

In March 2021 council adopted terms to match private funding for an economic development office run by the Chamber, up to $65,000 a year for five years, with councillors kept off its committee and a report to the city twice a year. The terms set no targets for jobs, investment or land sales:

“It is important to me that this office not be actively run by city administration but rather the proposed board of directors composed of private stakeholders and Chamber of Commerce administration.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Mar 2, 2021 · 0:27:27

“Business is booming in Steinbach and we need more lots. Our lot inventory is low and I know there’s interest by business owners already on lots that are going to be developed by this, the paving of Acres Drive.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Apr 20, 2021 · 0:27:21

The city sold 20 acres on Tower Avenue that had been listed for years, and its development corporation subdivided two lots in 2021 and seven more along the new Acres Drive in 2022. Downtown, council allowed a brewpub with 5 parking stalls instead of 11 and approved sidewalk patios one by one. It refused to rezone a church property in the industrial park for a school, 5–2:

“And if we were to take the names off this and simply look on a planning basis, does it make sense to put a school in the middle of an industrial park? The obvious answer is no.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · May 4, 2021 · 0:20:21

In 2022 council allowed a clothing store in one industrial unit (5–2) but refused blanket retail for a whole multi-unit site, setting out the rule it would follow for the next four years:

“There’s a number of reasons why retail is not permitted automatically in industrial zoning, partly because our industrial parks are subsidized by the city because we’re trying to attract more industry. So obviously we want to direct retail to commercial spaces which are not subsidized in the same way”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Oct 4, 2022 · 0:17:51

The Chamber reported in October 2022 that the office had become Steinbach Economic Development, a separate corporation, and had commissioned an investment-attraction study.

Where members differed

Most business items in this term were unanimous. The splits were over individual applications: paving and tree-planting waivers, a charity’s share of the Acres Drive road, and what belongs in the industrial park. Jac Siemens and Susan Penner were most often on the side of the applicant; Jake Hiebert most often against.
