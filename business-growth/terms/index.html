Business growth and investment · By term

The story by term

How did Steinbach City Council’s approach to business growth and investment change from one council to the next between December 2015 and November 2026, and what does the new council inherit?

In short

All three councils relied on the same tools: city-serviced industrial land sold at or near cost, light rules, low taxes, and the Chamber of Commerce as the city’s business partner. What changed was the pressure. Mayor Goertzen’s council spent its business time on signs, parking and land deals, and divided often. Mayor Funk’s councils, with the same six councillors for eight years, kept running out of industrial lots, tried a dedicated economic development office and ended it, and cut downtown parking rules in a new zoning by-law. Building permits grew from about $57 million a year in 2017–2018 to over $100 million in most years since 2021.

2014–2018

Mayor Goertzen’s council

Sign rules loosened at the Chamber’s request, eight downtown lots bought, paving relief on gravel roads, a 4–3 golf land deal and no Sunday-shopping vote.

36 items · 20 split votes, more than the next two terms combined

2018–2022

Mayor Funk’s first council

Industrial lots sold out and Acres Drive extended; a $65,000-a-year economic development office; the Chamber grant first questioned; COVID-19 letters but no local relief.

26 items · 7 split votes

2022–2026

Mayor Funk’s second council

The economic development office ended; August Cove lots; retail kept out of the industrial park; a shorter zoning by-law; the Chamber’s share questioned again.

28 items · 7 split votes

2026–2030: the new council

The council elected on October 28, 2026 is the first change in membership since 2018. Michael Zwaagstra, the clearest voice for keeping retail out of the industrial park, and Jac Siemens, who moved to end the economic development grant, did not run. Ron Falk, a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years (14 as chair), and Greg Carlos, a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association and new to elected office, have no record on this topic yet. Susan Penner, who has questioned the Chamber’s business-tax share most often, topped the poll and is Deputy Mayor.

What the new council inherits:

  • No industrial lots on the market. In February 2026 the mayor said the city had none left, with about 11–12 lots coming on Acres Drive from spring 2026; these are probably the August Cove lots approved in 2025 (we could not confirm). No land purchase ahead of demand has been discussed in an open meeting.
  • Wastewater capacity. The city’s wastewater system has room until about 2032; the expansion is estimated at $132.5 million, with half sought from the federal government. New industry needs that capacity.
  • No economic development office. The city’s grant ended on December 31, 2025, and nothing replaced it on the record. Steinbach Economic Development says it continues as a regional office on its reserves.
  • The Chamber’s share of the business tax. Reviews were promised in 2019 and 2025; the 2026 levy passed unchanged. The 2027 levy by-law comes each spring.
  • Development fees, including non-residential fees, are due for review in 2027 under the 2023 by-law.
  • Downtown. The new zoning by-law is in force. In November 2026 the mayor said he wants downtown busy “from morning into evening” but does not want to spend extra money on it if the city doesn’t have to (SteinbachOnline).

The new council’s inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026 had no business-growth items.
