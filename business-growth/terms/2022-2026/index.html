Business growth and investment · By term

2022–2026: Mayor Earl Funk’s second council

What did Steinbach City Council do for business growth and investment from November 2022 to October 2026, what did it stop doing, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • The economic development office ended. Council asked in 2024 how its success would be measured and got no targets back. In 2025 it questioned the office’s immigration pilot and request to double its grant, then ended the city’s $65,000 a year at the close of 2025. Nothing replaced it.
  • Land, again. The Acres Drive lots “sold basically as soon as they came on the market”; the city’s development corporation added 11 lots on August Cove (2025), and by February 2026 the mayor said none were left.
  • The industrial park stayed industrial. Council refused broad retail, a truck yard beside homes and a caretaker home, and allowed only narrow, tenant-tied exceptions.
  • Downtown rules eased. A six-storey mixed-use building on Main Street, main-floor commercial waived when tenants couldn’t be found, and a new zoning by-law (in force January 2026) that cut required downtown parking by a third.
  • The Chamber’s share questioned, unchanged. Councillors called the $225,000 grant “way out of line” (2025) and raised a cap (2025, 2026); the mayor promised a review; the 2026 levy passed unchanged.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2022–2023: lots, fees and a strategy without targets

In November 2022 council moved a small industrial remnant into the downtown district for apartments, 5–2, over Loewen Windows’ objection about traffic at a busy intersection:

“The Central Business District policy area that was created, it was done so to promote business in the downtown core area.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, voting against · Nov 1, 2022 · 0:36:15

“As we’ve seen, 1.5 and 2-acre lots in the industrial park go very quickly, and it’s a case of supply and demand, and our supply is low, the demand is high, so let’s try and balance that out.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Dec 6, 2022 · 0:16:33

In July 2023 Steinbach Economic Development presented an agribusiness-focused strategy; councillors asked no questions about targets. In October council applied for a provincial grant to rebuild Hespeler Street as an industrial corridor (no result found). It also raised development fees, including those on commercial and industrial building, by 5% a year instead of the 7.5% staff proposed:

“We do not— because we know that every increase here ultimately gets passed on to consumers. That’s the reality of it. But if we don’t have these development fees, or if we don’t increase them, if they stay the same, then that means that that puts more pressure on the mill rate.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Oct 17, 2023 · 0:08:47

“Because the development fee does not pay for the complete cost, the city pays the difference. Where does the city get their money? From the taxpayer. I think this is unfair.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert, voting against · Nov 7, 2023 · 0:09:31

2024: markers of success, a truck yard and a taller Main Street

In January council refused a truck yard on a city-developed industrial lot that backed onto homes, after about 17 letters from neighbours, while saying it wanted the business. In March, in year four of the five-year pilot, Steinbach Economic Development reported again:

“what are the markers of success that will indicate that this process has led to results? Like, what are you targeting in terms of whether it’s number of businesses or number of jobs created?”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, Deputy Mayor, chairing · Mar 5, 2024 · 0:37:54

The office said it would follow up; no targets came back on the public record. Its director, M.B., told council that investors want serviced land, not just available land, and that Steinbach was at a disadvantage there. In June council approved a six-storey building at 362–366 Main Street with shops on the ground floor and 32 apartments:

“It’s developer-driven. We had to wait until a developer wanted to do it.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Jun 18, 2024 · 0:38:18

In December council set the direction of a new zoning by-law, and objected to the RM of Hanover legalizing businesses built without approvals on the city’s northern edge.

“And parking lots don’t bring people downtown. Businesses do.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Dec 3, 2024 · 0:19:50

2025: August Cove, the Chamber’s share, and the end of the office

In January council approved 11 new industrial lots on August Cove for its development corporation, from under an acre to almost 8 acres:

“I think in 2022, we built Acres Drive. Ran that all the way through Keating, created 7 lots. They were sold basically as soon as they came on the market.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Jan 21, 2025 · 0:10:11

In February it unanimously refused retail on a new industrial lot for a charity thrift store, on staff’s advice:

“If a retail sales use is approved for this site, it has the potential of essentially requiring the Steinbach taxpayer to subsidize commercial development and use, and through taxpayer funding, the city has effectively become a competitor.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Feb 18, 2025 · 0:10:08

It later allowed the same charity in an existing building, tied to the tenant, and refused a kitchen-and-bath showroom 5–1. At the March budget hearing the Chamber’s grant drew the sharpest criticism yet:

“So I feel this $225,000 is way out of line, and I think we need to review that. Again, that’s not— it doesn’t affect residential property taxes, but it does impact our businesses.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Mar 4, 2025 · 0:19:31

Two weeks later Steinbach Economic Development reported that it had joined the federal Rural Community Immigration Pilot without consulting the city, a program with no federal administration money, and asked for its city grant to be doubled. Councillors questioned the lack of consultation and the risk of costs landing on the city:

“so usually that’s how they call it, pilot project, and then all of a sudden, it’s great for the community, you get involved, and before you know it, you’re funding it 100%.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Mar 18, 2025 · 1:22:05

In August, with both Councillors Penner absent, council voted unanimously not to renew the grant after 2025:

“the city has put in $65,000 a year for the last 5 years from 2021 to current … The, the reason I think we need to stop the funding is that we haven’t seen the development that we would have liked to have seen”

Councillor Jac Siemens, moving to end the grant · Aug 5, 2025 · 0:24:52

“I do believe that economic development is a partnership. … And I think our— part of the nearsightedness was to expect that one office would do it. And really, it’s a partnership, it’s a team thing.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Aug 5, 2025 · 0:29:55

Two weeks later council refused, 6–1, a request from TobaGrown to hold a cannabis retail plebiscite with the 2026 election; Jac Siemens voted against the refusal. No reasons are recorded in the minutes.

2026: a new zoning by-law and an unchanged business tax

The new zoning by-law took effect in January after a Municipal Board hearing: a third less required parking downtown, apartments allowed in mixed-use buildings there, offices and restaurants allowed as of right in the area around downtown, and a shorter by-law overall. In April councillors again raised the Chamber’s growing share and again renewed the levy:

“in general, normally I wouldn’t approve of a business tax, to be honest. I want to, you know, keep Steinbach as open for business as possible. … In fact, when we have wanted to cancel this tax in the past, businesses are the ones advocating that we keep it.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Apr 7, 2026 · 1:39:45

“Lower cost attracts business and residents. Lower cost creates a pro-growth environment, and Steinbach City Council has always been pro-growth and business-friendly.”

Mayor Earl Funk, previewing his speech to the Manitoba Chamber of Commerce · Apr 21, 2026 · 0:04:50

In October council let a developer replace most of a building’s required commercial space with apartments after failing to find tenants; Jake Hiebert worried it had become an apartment block in a commercial corridor.

Where members differed

Most business items were unanimous, including ending the economic development grant. The splits were on development fees (Damian Penner and Jake Hiebert wanted more), what belongs in the industrial park, and the downtown-district expansion. Jac Siemens cast the only votes against refusing the showroom and the cannabis plebiscite request.
