A Main Street bypass · Council member

Michael Zwaagstra: a bypass and through traffic

What did Michael Zwaagstra say and do about a bypass, truck routes and through traffic on Main Street and the highways between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Michael Zwaagstra was council's main voice for the yearly count of the highway intersections. He moved its acceptance in most years and explained why the city pays for it: the highways are provincial, but 'this data makes it possible' to advocate, and to show the province that growth merits intersection upgrades. He never suggested using the counts, or asking for new ones, to test whether traffic passes through.

He moved the 2016 Old Tom Road paving ('long overdue'), the Loewen rebuild's readings and the denial of a truck yard on Tower Avenue, backed twinning PTH 59, and seconded the 2024 objection to an RM of Hanover rezoning on PTH 12 North that ignored the 2018 access plan. In 2023 he timed a cross-town drive at almost 25 minutes, and in 2026 said McKenzie Avenue's main traffic is 'people going through'. He did not run in 2026.

“And we do this because we know the traffic numbers continue to increase within the city, and we want to make sure that we have evidence, uh, behind the request that we make to the province in regards to, uh, in regards to possible upgrades.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · On why the city pays for a yearly count of the highway intersections · Feb 18, 2020 · 0:42:12

Term by term

2014–2018

Moved the full Old Tom Road tender despite the overrun, moved the McKenzie Avenue local improvement plan (routine) and seconded its tender, and moved third reading of the 2018 development plan, which had no bypass or corridor policy.

Took part in 5 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Moved most yearly count acceptances, explaining that the city wants evidence behind its requests to the province; moved council's support for twinning PTH 59 (2019) and the readings of the Loewen and PTH 12 rebuild.

Took part in 4 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Backed the 2023 signal-timing request with his own timed trip, moved heavy-vehicle weight checks with the province (2023, routine), moved the denial of the Tower Avenue truck yard, seconded the objection to Hanover's PTH 12 North rezoning, said in 2024 the counts let a city advocate on provincial highways, and in 2026 moved acceptance of the count at which the bypass was dismissed and said McKenzie Avenue carries mostly through traffic.

Took part in 6 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistent across ten years: the province owns the highways, the city's job is to bring data and advocate for specific upgrades. The data he championed was never extended to the through-traffic question.
