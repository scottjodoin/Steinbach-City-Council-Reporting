A Main Street bypass · Council member

Jac Siemens: a bypass and through traffic

What did Jac Siemens say and do about a bypass, truck routes and through traffic on Main Street and the highways between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Jac Siemens is the only council member who addressed a highway bypass on the record in eleven years. Accepting the 2025 traffic count in February 2026, he said residents always ask why Steinbach isn't building one, but the yearly reports show 'traffic comes to Steinbach, it doesn't go through Steinbach. So a bypass Doesn't make sense to me.' In the same turn he warned that the counts are taken in the fall during construction. Nobody replied, and no request to measure through traffic followed.

He was also the member most aware of the informal bypasses. He read the counts as showing drivers using Park Road 'as a sort of bypass' (2020), said paving Old Tom had made it 'a way to bypass Highway 12 and our main street' (2022) and that there was 'not a definitive plan' for it, and credited Park Road West with redirecting traffic (2023). He recalled how Main Street was rebuilt as a four-lane highway with side streets upgraded for trucks, opposed a truck yard because of more trucks on Main Street, extended the engine-brake sign request to all four highways, and as regional delegate lobbied for wider highways into Steinbach. He did not run in 2026.

“One, one of the things I always look at on this report is the amount of traffic coming into Steinbach and the amount of traffic leaving Steinbach, because I always get that, why isn't Steinbach building a bypass to help with the traffic? But we see when in this, these reports, we see every year when we do these, we see traffic comes to Steinbach, it doesn't go through Steinbach. So a bypass Doesn't make sense to me.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · On the 2025 traffic count, the only direct remark on a bypass by a council member, 2015–2026 · Feb 17, 2026 · 0:24:02

Term by term

2014–2018

Held the over-budget Old Tom Road tender for a month (2016), moved the McKenzie Avenue tender citing Barkman Concrete's trucks, and in July 2018 told how Main Street was rebuilt as a four-lane highway through downtown, with McKenzie and other streets upgraded 'to provide alternate traffic routes'. A January 2017 remark calling Old Tom and Industrial Road 'unofficial bypasses' is probably his but couldn't be attributed with certainty.

Took part in 6 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Read the 2019 count as showing drivers using Park Road 'as a sort of bypass' of Loewen Boulevard, extended the engine-brake sign request to all four highway entrances, and at the 2022 Old Tom–Loewen hearing called the city's traffic 'horrendous' and Old Tom a bypass of Highway 12 and Main Street, said there was 'not a definitive plan', and seconded the approval.

Took part in 4 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Raised complaints about 'the amount of time it takes to drive through Steinbach' (2023), credited Park Road West with redirecting traffic, opposed the Tower Avenue truck yard over more trucks on Main Street, set the regional highway lobbying list (2024), resisted more lights on PTH 52, and in February 2026 dismissed a bypass.

Took part in 4 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Over the decade he described the problem more vividly than anyone on council, through traffic on Old Tom, trucks on Main Street and long drives through town, but his answer was always to fix the highways through the city and widen those into it. His 2026 verdict on a bypass rested on counts he himself said were taken during construction.
