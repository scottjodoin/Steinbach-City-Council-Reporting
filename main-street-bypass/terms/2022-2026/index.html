A Main Street bypass · By term

2022–2026: Mayor Earl Funk’s second council

What did Steinbach City Council say and do about a bypass, truck routes and through traffic from November 2022 to October 2026, including the one time a member spoke to a bypass?

In short

  • The only bypass remark, and it was a no. In February 2026 Councillor Jac Siemens said the yearly reports show “traffic comes to Steinbach” and a bypass doesn’t make sense. Nobody replied; no motion, budget line or request followed.
  • A second route north, paid for by the city. Council extended Park Road West to Industrial Road ($1,970,097) as an alternative to PTH 12 while Loewen Boulevard was rebuilt.
  • “How long it takes to get out of town” was answered with signal timing on PTH 12, a matter for the province.
  • Trucks stayed on Main Street. Council refused a truck yard partly over more trucks on Main Street, banned engine brakes, and heard residents report trucks on McKenzie and Friesen avenues, but didn’t review truck routes or count trucks.
  • Corridors in principle. Council objected when the RM of Hanover let development on PTH 12 North ignore the 2018 access plan, and its 2025 development plan protects land for “new corridors” in general, but maps no bypass.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2023: getting through town, and another way north

In January 2023 Councillor Siemens brought residents’ complaints to council:

“Some of the complaints I've heard are due to the amount of time it takes to drive through Steinbach on any given day, especially during peak hours.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Jan 3, 2023 · 0:19:46

Councillor Zwaagstra said a December trip across the city had taken him “almost 25 minutes because I'm getting caught at these lights.” Council’s answer was for staff to meet the province about PTH 12 signal timing and turn lanes, not a new route.

In March, council paid from reserves to pave Park Road West through to Industrial Road, a city route parallel to PTH 12:

“traffic that bypasses uses Park Road as a bypass now, could use Acres.…So I believe that redirected a lot of traffic, and that was kind of the plan, and it has worked.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, on the 2022 traffic count · Mar 21, 2023 · 0:46:22

“this is going to be a great bypass when we do the intersection at number 12 and Lowen Boulevard.”

Councillor Bill Hiebert, on the Park Road West extension (medium-confidence match; SteinbachOnline reported the same words as his) · Mar 21, 2023 · 0:51:51

“This is going to give another way to go north in our community.…it will be an alternative route to get to the north end of our commercial district.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Mar 21, 2023 · 0:52:21

2024: trucks on Main Street, and the highways council wants

In January 2024 neighbours objected to a trucking company’s yard on Tower Avenue. Councillor Susan Penner checked the routes; the city manager confirmed “trucks would be required to access it only from PTH 52, and as that is a designated truck route.” Council refused the yard unanimously, and Councillor Siemens said why:

“there will be more truck traffic on Main Street. And trucks generally go south on 12 or north on 12.…generally they're after the number 1 highway, they're going south to the border.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, on the truck yard · Jan 16, 2024 · 1:31:02

That September council joined the Eastman Regional Municipal Committee to lobby the province together, and Councillor Siemens, its delegate, set out what Steinbach wanted. Every item widens or resurfaces a highway into the city:

“to lobby for overlay of number 12 highway from McKenzie North to the Seine River. with paved shoulders. And second is continue to build the 4-lane highway on 59 to St. Mauro. And then after that, we would also need a 4-lane highway, 52 to the 59, and build a 4-lane highway between Steinbach and La Broquerie with paved shoulders.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Sept 3, 2024 · 0:21:33

In December council objected to an RM of Hanover rezoning just north of the city on PTH 12, where businesses had already been built:

“development that has been permitted to proceed on the subject property does not appear to have taken into consideration the provisions of the highway access management and transportation plan for PTH 12 and 52 that was completed in 2018 and jointly prepared and funded between the City of Steinbach, the RM of Hanover, and Manitoba Infrastructure and Transportation.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Dec 17, 2024 · 0:33:53

The outcome of the objection was not reported at a council meeting.

2025: rules for trucks, a plan without a corridor

Traffic By-law 2254 (January 2025) banned engine brakes in the city and kept heavy vehicles to designated routes, which the city lists as PTH 52, PTH 12 and McKenzie Avenue. In March, presenting the 2024 count, Councillor Siemens heard the same complaint again and resisted another set of lights on Main Street West:

“That's one of the biggest complaints that I hear how long it takes to get out of town because you have to stop at every light.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Mar 18, 2025 · 1:32:21

In September council adopted a new development plan (By-law 2244). It says land “shall be protected for … any future highway system enhancements, such as interchanges, highway realignments, or new corridors,” but names only a PTH 52 realignment at Bush Farm and Keating roads, and its maps show no bypass. No member spoke about highway corridors at the readings. Loewen Boulevard and PTH 12 reopened on November 24.

2026: “traffic comes to Steinbach”

On February 17, 2026 the city manager presented the 2025 count: nearly 3,500 vehicles an hour at the busiest intersection, Brandt and Main, and recommendations that “aren't must-haves.” The mayor described the morning rush from the west:

“there's a lot of vehicles coming in from the west, coming in every morning to work, and it really causes congestion in that area.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Feb 17, 2026 · 0:20:30

Then came the only direct comment on a bypass by a council member in eleven years:

“One, one of the things I always look at on this report is the amount of traffic coming into Steinbach and the amount of traffic leaving Steinbach, because I always get that, why isn't Steinbach building a bypass to help with the traffic? But we see when in this, these reports, we see every year when we do these, we see traffic comes to Steinbach, it doesn't go through Steinbach. So a bypass Doesn't make sense to me.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, on the 2025 traffic count · Feb 17, 2026 · 0:24:02

In the same turn he noted the counts are always taken in September or early October, during construction. Nobody replied. Council accepted the report unanimously (R26-037); unlike earlier years, the resolution didn’t say to send it to the province. Whether the counts can show what he said they show is examined in Council’s reasons.

That spring residents at two infill hearings described trucks on city streets. H.K., who has lived on McKenzie Avenue for 57 years, said many eastbound tractor-trailers turn toward La Broquerie on PTH 52; P.H. said Friesen Avenue “has become like a major hub for truck transport to come down, although it is not a truck route.” Councillor Zwaagstra agreed McKenzie carries through trips:

“The main traffic on the street are people going through. McKenzie is a thoroughfare, is a collector street.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Apr 7, 2026 · 1:24:17

Both projects were approved. No truck count or truck-route review was requested.

Where members differed

No divided vote on a bypass, truck routes or through traffic was found in this term. The truck yard was refused unanimously.

No recorded split votes on this topic in this period.
