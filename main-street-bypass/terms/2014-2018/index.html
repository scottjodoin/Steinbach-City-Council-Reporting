A Main Street bypass · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

What did Steinbach City Council say and do about a bypass, truck routes and through traffic from December 2015 to October 2018?

In short

  • An edge road paved, and a bypass by accident. Council paved Old Tom Road on the city’s east edge in 2016 despite a cost overrun. Within a year a councillor was calling it and Industrial Road “unofficial bypasses.”
  • Edge roads wait for developers. When PTH 12 construction pushed traffic onto gravel Park Road West, staff said road improvements are “developer-driven”; Mayor Goertzen asked whether the city had a share. It was paved in stages over the next seven years.
  • Trucks belong on the truck routes. McKenzie Avenue, the truck route to Barkman Concrete, was rebuilt in concrete, and Councillor Siemens recalled how Main Street itself was rebuilt as a four-lane highway in the 1990s with side streets upgraded to carry trucks.
  • The province called a bypass hypothetical. A 2018 access plan for PTH 12 and 52 prepared with the province mentioned a perimeter highway; a provincial official said it was “just a hypothetical concept.” No council member raised it, and the city said it would not adopt the plan until the province did.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr.

What happened

2016: Old Tom Road and Park Road West

The 2016 budget put $1 million toward paving Old Tom Road, a boundary road on the east edge of the city. The low tender came in about $200,000 over budget with engineering; council held it for a month, then approved it in May:

“I think we all recognize that the paving of Old Tom Road is long overdue.…We need to do this now, and we need to do this right.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving the tender · May 3, 2016 · 0:04:37

In June, with PTH 12 under construction, a Councillor Penner (the recording doesn’t say which) noted heavy detour traffic on gravel Park Road West and asked whether it would be surfaced. The answer set the rule for edge roads for the next decade:

“As a rule, any road or servicing improvements is developer-driven.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Jun 7, 2016 · 0:14:51

“Not only is that road something that needs to be paid for by some of the developers in that area, but is there a component that we are responsible for as well”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Jun 7, 2016 · 0:16:22

Park Road West was paved in stages as land along it developed; the city paid for the last link to Industrial Road in 2023 (see 2022–2026). The yearly count of the highway intersections, paid for by the city since 1997, was accepted as usual.

2017: “unofficial bypasses” and a truck route in concrete

Presenting the January 2017 traffic count, a councillor, probably Jac Siemens, described the newly improved Industrial Road and Old Tom Road as “unofficial bypasses” around Steinbach that would change how traffic turns at Park Road and PTH 12. Late in the year council approved rebuilding McKenzie Avenue between Lilac Bay and Brandt Street, part of the truck route between PTH 12 and Main Street East:

“that's a very, very heavy truck traffic road. Will that be done in concrete or asphalt?”

Councillor John Fehr · Dec 19, 2017 · 0:11:34

It was concrete. Councillor Siemens moved the $514,410 tender in March 2018, citing “a lot of heavy truck traffic there from Barkman Concrete.”

2018: the province’s “hypothetical” perimeter

In March 2018 SteinbachOnline reported that a new Highway Access Management and Transportation Plan for PTH 12 and 52, prepared and paid for by the city, the RM of Hanover and the province, mentioned a perimeter highway or bypass. A provincial official said it was “just a hypothetical concept being considered” and that he didn’t see it happening “anytime soon.” No council member raised it at a meeting.

In July, opening a meeting, Councillor Siemens told the history of Main Street, which he had worked on for the city: in the 1980s side streets were upgraded to take trucks, and from 1989 the city rebuilt PTH 52 through downtown as a four-lane highway with the province’s approval and money.

“McKenzie was upgraded to a 4-lane street, all to handle the truck traffic.…all in order to provide alternate traffic routes to the main street.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, opening remarks · Jul 17, 2018 · 0:00:00

At the same meeting’s hearing on the new development plan, the province’s planners asked the city to reference the access plan. The city planner said no:

“the Highway Access Management and Transportation Plan, the one has not yet been adopted by Manitoba Infrastructure. So until it's formally adopted by Manitoba Infrastructure, the city will not be adopting it until they have adopted it.”

Lacey Gaudet, City Planner · Jul 17, 2018 · 0:56:06

A city transportation plan was wanted “within the future,” she said, but not immediately. The development plan passed in October 2018, treating PTH 12 and 52 through Steinbach as “a major highway corridor” with no bypass or corridor policy. In August the city secured corner land at Loewen Boulevard and PTH 12 for future turn lanes:

“I commend the developer and city administration for working together and making a potentially problematic corner something that's going to be a lot more maneuverable because it is a trucking route there.”

Councillor John Fehr · Aug 7, 2018 · 0:18:42

Where members differed

No divided vote on the topic was found in this term. There are no minutes online for 2015–2017; the decisions above carried without opposition voiced in the videos.

No recorded split votes on this topic in this period.
