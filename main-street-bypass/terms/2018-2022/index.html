A Main Street bypass · By term

2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk’s first council

What did Steinbach City Council say and do about a bypass, truck routes and through traffic from November 2018 to October 2022?

In short

  • Residents named the bypass. Clearspring Greens residents said in 2019 that paving Old Tom had turned Park Road, Old Tom and Hespeler into a perimeter road; Loewen Boulevard residents in 2022 called it “a city bypass and speedway.” Council answered with streetlights and a pathway, not a plan for the traffic.
  • Roads follow development. Asked about gravel Bush Farm Road and the Old Tom–Loewen area, staff and the mayor said developers build the roads that front their land. The mayor promised a plan for the edge roads in 2020; none came to a public meeting.
  • The highway fixed, not avoided. The Loewen Boulevard and PTH 12 rebuild was financed to put traffic back “on our main road,” and the yearly count was used as evidence for the province.
  • Trucks: noise, not routes. Council asked the province for “no engine brake” signs at all four highway entrances. Truck routes themselves weren’t reviewed.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2019–2020: “a perimeter road” and the edge roads

In July 2019 a delegation of Clearspring Greens residents asked for a safe path along Old Tom and Park Road. One said Highway 12 was basically the only north–south road in the city, and that since Old Tom was paved, Park Road, Old Tom and Hespeler had become “a perimeter road around Steinbach,” busy and fast. The mayor said the work wasn’t stalled, only unfunded:

“Well, we're not stalled. We're working through our budgets. Our 2019 budget did not allow us to do anything in that area at all.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Jul 16, 2019 · 1:26:03

Council later added streetlights and a pathway, with residents paying part (the cost-sharing votes are covered in Traffic in Steinbach). Nothing addressed the volume of traffic using the edge roads. The next February, the yearly count showed the pattern on the north side too:

“So the way I would read that is that there's more people using that and down Park Road as a sort of bypass so they don't go through the Lowen Boulevard and 12 intersection.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, on the 2019 traffic count · Feb 18, 2020 · 0:45:31

In March 2020 a Bush Farm Road resident, G.H., objected that the gravel road on the southwest edge carried heavy traffic and that waiting for development to pave it “seems a little bit regressive.” Staff gave the rule; the mayor promised more:

“By and large, when it comes to accommodating traffic, the…it is the responsibility of the developer to develop any…generally any roads that are fronting proposed developments.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Mar 3, 2020 · 0:38:25

“I think we need to be looking at this and any…in the, in the future, come up with a plan for how we will mitigate these, these, these roads and how we're going to make sure that these roads will handle the traffic, because we are growing.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Mar 3, 2020 · 0:50:45

In 2021 council widened Bush Farm Road’s right-of-way for utilities and drainage (By-law 2181). No plan for the edge roads as a ring around the city has come to a public meeting.

2021: the Loewen rebuild puts traffic back on the highway

Loewen Boulevard and PTH 12 had topped the yearly counts’ problem list since 2016. In December 2021 council passed the local improvement plan for its rebuild ($8.275 million estimate, with provincial money for the surface works). K.P., speaking for the family that owns the corner property, told council drivers were cutting through Maplewood, Old Tom Road and their yard to avoid the queue. Councillors saw the rebuild as the cure:

“It will probably change traffic patterns to a certain extent and alleviate some of the arterial roads and put it on our main road.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Dec 7, 2021 · 0:29:15

“it moves a lot of traffic right now, but it'll be able to move a lot more traffic and it'll be able to move it more efficiently.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Dec 7, 2021 · 0:30:13

It reopened on November 24, 2025, at about $11.2 million, the city’s share about $6.1 million. The full story is in Traffic in Steinbach.

2022: through traffic, engine brakes and “not a definitive plan”

Accepting the 2021 count in February 2022, Councillor Damian Penner pointed to traffic from the west:

“as well as knowing that on the highway Mitchell is a growing community and growing quickly, our neighbors over in Hanover. And there's a lot more traffic going through there”

Councillor Damian Penner · Feb 15, 2022 · 0:24:02

In July, Councillor Bill Hiebert raised residents’ complaints about trucks’ engine brakes south of the city. Staff said signs on a provincial highway need the province and a council resolution, and the request grew:

“all 4 highways coming into Steinbach should have signage from Manitoba Highways on no Jake brakes, I believe.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Jul 19, 2022 · 0:20:43

Council sent the request (R22-177); no reply or installation was reported. In September, at the hearing for a 95-lot neighbourhood at Old Tom Road and Loewen Boulevard, residents asked for a traffic study first. R.F., who lives on Loewen, said “these roads are being used as a city bypass and speedway and not designed to handle this.” Councillor Siemens agreed with the diagnosis:

“The traffic around Steinbach is horrendous.…when we paved Old Tom 10 years ago, whenever that was, there was a lot of unintended consequences, and that becomes a way to bypass Highway 12 and our main street.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Sept 6, 2022 · 1:18:26

“But specifically to Old Tom and Lowen Boulevard, it's been talked about, it's part of our long-term planning strategies. There's not a definitive plan.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Sept 6, 2022 · 1:19:40

He seconded the approval, which passed unanimously without a traffic study. The mayor had set out the approach earlier in the hearing: “we let the development happen and then we can work on the road system.”

Where members differed

No divided vote on a bypass, truck routes or through traffic was found in this term. The 2020 split votes on the Clearspring Greens pathway were about who pays, and are in Traffic in Steinbach.

No recorded split votes on this topic in this period.
