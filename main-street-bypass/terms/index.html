A Main Street bypass · By term

The story by term

How did Steinbach City Council handle the idea of a bypass, truck routes and through traffic from one council to the next between December 2015 and November 2026, and what does the new council inherit?

In short

Across three councils the approach never changed, and it was never debated. Main Street and Brandt Street are provincial highways, and council treated them as the city’s main arteries to be fixed, not avoided. It paid for a yearly count of the highway intersections, rebuilt the worst corners with the province, kept trucks on the two highways and McKenzie Avenue, and lobbied for wider highways into Steinbach from the region.

Meanwhile the city’s own edge roads quietly became the bypass. Paving Old Tom Road in 2016 created what residents in 2019 called “a perimeter road around Steinbach,” and the 2023 Park Road West extension gave drivers “another way to go north,” in Mayor Earl Funk’s words. Each edge road was built when development reached it or when a detour needed it, not as part of a plan; the mayor promised one for the edge roads in 2020 and none came to a public meeting.

A highway bypass itself was raised by the province once (2018, as “just a hypothetical concept”) and by a council member once (2026, to dismiss it). No council asked anyone to measure whether traffic, or trucks, actually pass through.

2026–2030: the new council

The council elected on October 28, 2026 is the first change in membership since 2018. It loses Jac Siemens, the only member to speak to a bypass on the record and council’s delegate on regional highway lobbying, and Michael Zwaagstra, who moved most of the yearly traffic counts and framed them as evidence for the province. Neither ran. At the November 3 inaugural meeting the mayor named Jake Hiebert to the Eastman regional committee that lobbies on highways. Ron Falk, a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years (14 as chair), and Greg Carlos, a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, have no record on this topic yet. Susan Penner is Deputy Mayor.

What the new council inherits:

  • An unmeasured question. Whether traffic and trucks come to Steinbach or pass through it has never been measured. The yearly count records turns at intersections, not trips, and reports no trucks; no origin–destination survey has been asked for.
  • Unpublished provincial plans. The 2018 access plan for PTH 12 and 52, prepared with the province and the RM of Hanover, and its later update (the province offered up to $27,500 toward an update in 2022) shape access along both highways but are not public.
  • A corridor policy with no corridor. The 2025 development plan protects land for “new corridors” in general terms; the only highway change it names is a PTH 52 realignment at Bush Farm and Keating roads.
  • Edge roads without a plan. Old Tom, Park Road West, Bush Farm Road and Loewen Boulevard carry cross-town traffic, and residents along them have asked for studies. The edge-road plan promised in 2020 has not appeared.
  • Trucks on city streets. Residents’ 2026 reports of trucks on McKenzie and Friesen avenues got no follow-up, and the city has no truck count and no dangerous-goods route.
  • The province’s list. The province’s 2025/26 plan lists intersection improvements at PTH 12 and PTH 52 in Steinbach and work on PTH 52 in Mitchell; no bypass, realignment or twinning around the city.

The inaugural meeting had no bypass or truck-route items on its agenda.
