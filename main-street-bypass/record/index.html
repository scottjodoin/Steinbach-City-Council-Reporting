A Main Street bypass · Full record

Every decision and debate on a bypass and through traffic

What has Steinbach City Council decided, debated and promised about a bypass and through traffic since December 2015, and how did each item end?

In short

Each row is one decision, debate, promise or request on the topic, from the minutes and the meeting videos. Filter by term, year, theme, council member or result. “No public result” means we found no public decision or follow-up; some work happens in closed study sessions and committees.

YearDecision or debateResult
2016–26Annual traffic counts on PTH 12 and 52: used to lobby for intersections, never to measure through traffic or trucksEvery year since 1997 the city has paid for peak-hour intersection counts on PTH 12 and PTH 52 (MMM, then WSP). Council accepted each report (2016-2026) and usually forwarded it to the province with requests for signals or intersection work: Industrial Rd (2017), Loewen Blvd (2018-2020), Giesbrecht St (2022 onward). Members called the counts the evidence for lobbying on provincial highways. The studies count turning movements at about 20 intersections for a few weekday hours; council never asked for an origin-destination survey or a truck count. Members did read through-traffic patterns into them: Damian Penner noted more traffic 'going through' from growing Mitchell (2022).Partly Reports accepted each year (2018-01-02 consensus; R20-034, R21-046, R22-038, R23-056, R24-054, R25-060, R26-037). Requested intersection work followed (Industrial Rd signals, Loewen rebuild 2025, Giesbrecht signals 2026). No through-traffic or truck data was requested; the 2026 resolution, unlike earlier ones, did not say to forward the study to the province.R20-034, R21-046, R22-038, R23-056, R24-054, R25-060, R26-037 · vote: unanimous each year
2016Old Tom Road paved as a boundary road; councillors later call it an "unofficial bypass" of PTH 12The 2016 financial plan put $1M toward asphalting Old Tom Road on the city's east edge as part of its boundary (periphery) roads program, and council approved the full tender in May 2016 despite a roughly $200,000 overrun. In January 2017 a councillor, very likely Jac Siemens (medium-confidence attribution), described the newly improved Industrial Road and Old Tom Road as 'unofficial bypasses' around Steinbach that would change turning traffic at Park Road and PTH 12.Done Old Tom Road was paved in 2016. Over the following years residents and councillors described Old Tom, Park Road and Hespeler as a de facto perimeter route around Main Street and PTH 12 (see 2019-clearspring-perimeter-road and 2022-old-tom-loewen-bypass-route).vote: carried (no opposition voiced)
2016–23Park Road West: from gravel detour (2016) to a paved second route north (2023)In June 2016 a Councillor Penner noted heavy detour traffic on gravel Park Road West during PTH 12 construction and asked whether it would be surfaced as a peripheral road; the city manager said road upgrades are developer-driven and the mayor said the city's own share would be considered. Councillor Siemens later read the traffic counts as showing drivers using Industrial Road and Park Road 'as a sort of bypass' of the Loewen Blvd/PTH 12 intersection (2020). In March 2023 council paid from reserves to extend the paving to Industrial Road, and members described it as 'a great bypass' and 'another way to go north' during the Loewen rebuild.Done Park Road West was paved in stages with developer work; the city-funded extension to Industrial Road ($1,970,097, R23-057) was expected to open after Oct 13, 2023. Councillor Siemens said in 2023 that Acres Drive had also more than doubled its traffic when Park Road was closed.R23-057 · vote: unanimous
2017–18McKenzie Ave, a truck route between PTH 12 and the Barkman plant, rebuilt in concrete for heavy trucksIn late 2017 council approved a local improvement to rebuild McKenzie Avenue between Lilac Bay and Brandt Street (PTH 12); councillors said it would be concrete because of heavy truck traffic from Barkman Concrete. The tender went to Maple Leaf Construction in March 2018.Done Tender awarded (R18-060) and road rebuilt in 2018; McKenzie Ave remains a designated truck route.R18-060 · vote: unanimous
20182018 provincial highway access plan mentions a perimeter/bypass; province calls it 'hypothetical' and the city does not adopt the planA Highway Access Management and Transportation Plan for PTH 12 and PTH 52 was prepared and funded jointly by the City, the RM of Hanover and Manitoba Infrastructure and completed in 2018. Per SteinbachOnline (Mar 20, 2018, as cited in the existing report), it mentioned a perimeter highway or bypass, which a provincial official called 'just a hypothetical concept' with nothing planned 'anytime soon'. At the July 17, 2018 hearing on the new Official Community Plan (By-law 2099), the province's planning branch asked the city to reference the access plan and to plan internal roads for land along PTH 52; the city planner replied that the city would not adopt the access plan until Manitoba Infrastructure formally adopted it, and that a city transportation plan was wanted 'within the future' but not immediately. No council member raised the perimeter idea at a meeting.No public result OCP By-law 2099 passed third reading Oct 16, 2018 (R18-242). The access plan was not published; council next cited it in December 2024 (see 2024-hanover-pth12-north-objection). No bypass study followed.R18-116 (1st), R18-242 (3rd) · vote: unanimous
2018–24Loewen Blvd/PTH 12 intersection rebuilt with the province instead of moving traffic off the highwayLoewen Blvd at PTH 12 was the worst intersection in the yearly counts. Council secured corner land in 2018 (the corner was 'a trucking route', said Councillor Fehr), passed a $8.275M local improvement plan in 2021-22 with provincial funding for the surface works, and awarded construction in 2024. A neighbouring property owner's son (K.P.) told council that drivers were cutting through Maplewood, Old Tom Road and his yard to avoid the intersection. Councillor Damian Penner said the new design would 'alleviate some of the arterial roads and put it on our main road', and Mayor Funk said it would move more traffic more efficiently.Done By-law 2187 (LIP 2022-02) passed (R21-250/251, R22-067); construction awarded to Maple Leaf Construction for $9,981,284 (R24-107). The intersection reopened Nov 24, 2025 (about $11.2M, city about $6.1M, per the existing report).R21-250, R21-251, R22-067, R24-107 · vote: unanimous
2018How Main Street became a rebuilt highway through downtown, with side streets upgraded as alternate truck routes (history told by Councillor Siemens)In his opening remarks on July 17, 2018, Councillor Siemens recounted Main Street's reconstruction: in the 1980s Hespeler, Loewen and McKenzie were upgraded 'to handle the truck traffic' and Elmdale connected 'to provide alternate traffic routes to the main street', and from 1989 to the mid-1990s the city, with Manitoba Highways' approval and money for the highway portion, rebuilt Main Street (PTH 52) as a four-lane highway through downtown. The history shows the long-standing approach: keep the provincial highway through the centre and build city streets as relief routes.Done Historical account; no decision.vote: no vote
2019–21Residents: Park Road, Old Tom and Hespeler 'became a perimeter road around Steinbach'A July 2019 delegation of Clearspring Greens residents asked for a pathway because paving Old Tom Road had turned Park Road, Old Tom and Hespeler into a busy, fast route around the city. One resident said Highway 12 was basically the only north-south road and that the edge roads had become a perimeter road. The mayor said the 2019 budget did not allow work and a plan was coming.Partly Council added streetlights at Old Tom/Park Rd (R19-033) and later a pathway (cost split capped at $1,610 per lot, R21-012), per the traffic-congestion research; nothing was done about the volume of traffic using the edge roads as a bypass.R19-033; R20-005 (5-2); R20-012 (6-1); R21-012 · vote: R20-005 5-2 (against: Siemens, J. Hiebert) and R20-012 6-1 (against: Siemens), both on who pays for the pathway
2019–24Regional highway lobbying: twin PTH 59, four-lane PTH 52 east and west, PTH 12 overlay; no bypass on the listCouncil supported St. Pierre-Jolys' request to twin PTH 59 to PTH 52 (2019) and in 2024 joined the Eastman Regional Municipal Committee to lobby the province together. Councillor Siemens, appointed as delegate, listed his highway priorities: a PTH 12 overlay north of McKenzie, four-laning PTH 59, a four-lane PTH 52 to PTH 59, and a four-lane PTH 52 between Steinbach and La Broquerie. He later reported raising PTH 12, 52 and 59 with regional highway staff and Highways Minister Lisa Naylor (Oct 22, 2025). None of the asks was a bypass or truck route around Steinbach.No public result Memberships and appointment approved (R24-170, R24-176, R25-087); results of the ministerial meeting were not reported at a recorded council meeting. The province's 2026 PTH 12 resurfacing north of Park Road adds no lanes (existing report).R19-268; R24-170; R24-176; R25-087 · vote: unanimous
2020–21Bush Farm Road and other edge roads: upgraded only as development fronts themAt a March 2020 hearing on the next Parkhill phase, Bush Farm Road resident G.H. objected that the gravel road carries heavy traffic in the southwest and asked when it would be improved. The city manager said developers build the roads fronting their land, so no upgrade was required; Mayor Funk said council needed a plan for how its edge roads would handle growth. In 2021 council widened the Bush Farm Road right-of-way for utilities and drainage (By-law 2181).Partly Right-of-way widened (By-law 2181: R21-167, R21-168, R21-184). No plan for the edge roads as a ring around the city came to a public meeting; the 2025 development plan maps no ring road (existing report).R20-046, R20-047; R21-167, R21-168, R21-184 · vote: unanimous
2021–25Truck rules: through trucks kept on PTH 12 and 52; engine-brake signs requested, weight enforcement and fines addedSteinbach's designated truck routes are PTH 12 (Brandt St), PTH 52 (Main St) and McKenzie Ave (city truck-route page, 2026), so through trucks are routed along Main Street and Brandt Street. Staff told council in 2021 that trucks keep to designated routes except to reach permitted work sites. In 2022 council asked Manitoba Transportation and Infrastructure for 'no engine brake' signs on all four highway entrances after residents south of the city complained; in 2023 it joined the province's Community Safety Officer program to weigh heavy vehicles; in 2025 Traffic By-law 2254 banned engine retarder brakes and raised fines for parked heavy vehicles.Partly R22-177 (signs) sent; no reply or installation reported. R23-036 (weight enforcement) agreed. By-law 2254 adopted (R25-015, R25-016). No review of truck routes or count of trucks was made.R22-177; R23-036; R25-015, R25-016 · vote: unanimous
2022Residents say Old Tom and Loewen are 'a city bypass and speedway'; council approves new homes without a traffic studyAt the Sept 6, 2022 hearing for a 95-parcel neighbourhood at Old Tom Road and Loewen Blvd, residents asked for a traffic study and plans for the Loewen intersections; R.F. said the roads are 'being used as a city bypass and speedway'. Councillor Siemens agreed the city's traffic is 'horrendous' and that paving Old Tom had made it 'a way to bypass Highway 12 and our main street', said traffic flow is discussed at long-term planning sessions, but there was 'not a definitive plan'. Mayor Funk said roads follow development. Council approved unanimously.Declined No traffic study was required and no plan for Old Tom/Loewen was adopted; a 3-way stop at Loewen and Hespeler was defeated in 2024 and a roundabout not budgeted (existing reports).R22-205 · vote: unanimous
2023Complaints about the time to drive through Steinbach answered with signal timing, not a new routeIn January 2023 Councillor Siemens relayed complaints about 'the amount of time it takes to drive through Steinbach' on PTH 12 and asked that the province review signal timing and turn lanes; Councillor Zwaagstra said a trip from the south end to Clearspring Mall had taken almost 25 minutes. Council's consensus was for staff to meet Manitoba Transportation and Infrastructure. The same complaint, 'how long it takes to get out of town', returned with the 2024 and 2025 studies, which recommended retiming PTH 12 signals; Siemens opposed possible signals at Lund Rd ('3 sets of traffic lights in one block').Partly Staff met MTI (city manager's report, May 2, 2023 minutes); MTI was reviewing PTH 12 timing in March 2025 per staff; members were still calling the lights unsynchronized in February 2026.vote: consensus (minutes 2023-01-03 item 20)
2024Truck yard denied; Councillor Siemens worries about more trucks on Main Street, which 'go south on 12' to the borderCouncil unanimously denied a conditional use for a trucking company's yard at 45 Tower Avenue after neighbours objected. Councillor Susan Penner asked whether trucks would stay on PTH 12 and 52; the city manager said they would have to use PTH 52, a designated truck route. Councillor Siemens said his first concern was additional truck traffic on Main Street, adding that trucks generally go south on PTH 12 to the border.Declined Conditional use denied (R24-009); status refers to the applicant's request.R24-009 · vote: unanimous
2024City objects to RM of Hanover rezoning on PTH 12 North that ignored the 2018 highway access planIn December 2024 council filed an objection to an RM of Hanover rezoning of land just north of the city on PTH 12 North, where businesses had already been built. The city manager said the development did not appear to follow the 2018 Highway Access Management and Transportation Plan for PTH 12 and 52, prepared jointly by the City, Hanover and the province, nor Hanover's own development plan and the annexation agreement. Councillors Susan Penner and Zwaagstra said land next to the city must stay compatible with its growth and that highway upgrades should have been triggered.Done Objection filed (R24-246) ahead of Hanover's Jan 8, 2025 hearing; the outcome was not reported at a recorded council meeting.R24-246 · vote: unanimous
2025New development plan protects land for future highway works in general terms but maps no bypass or ring roadCouncil adopted a new Official Community Plan (By-law 2244) in 2025. Per the existing report, it says land 'shall be protected for … any future highway system enhancements, such as interchanges, highway realignments, or new corridors', names only a PTH 52 realignment at Bushfarm and Keating Rds, and treats PTH 12 and 52 as heavy-truck routes to be 'preserved as major highway corridors'. Its maps show no bypass or ring-road corridor. No council member spoke about highway corridors at the recorded readings.Partly Adopted (R25-069, R25-070, R25-184). General corridor protection only; the bypass question is left unsettled in the plan.R25-069, R25-070, R25-184 · vote: unanimous
2025–26Residents report trucks and cut-through traffic on McKenzie Ave and Friesen Ave; no truck-route reviewAt 2026 hearings on infill housing, residents described heavy trucks using city streets. On McKenzie Ave (a four-lane truck route), H.K. said many eastbound tractor-trailers turn right toward La Broquerie on PTH 52 East, using the curb lane. On Friesen Ave, P.H. said the street 'has become like a major hub for truck transport' although it is not a truck route, and drivers from Main Street use it as a shortcut. Councillor Zwaagstra said the main traffic on McKenzie is people going through, not residents. Council approved both projects; no truck-route review or truck count was requested. Earlier, a resident had asked (Dec 2025) about delivery trucks on First Street, and staff said traffic studies are required only for impacts on PTH 12 and 52.No public result Rezoning and housing approvals passed; the truck concerns received no response or follow-up at council.vote: not applicable (no motion on trucks)
2026'Traffic comes to Steinbach, it doesn't go through': Councillor Siemens dismisses a bypass as the 2025 traffic study is acceptedPresenting the 2025 WSP study, the city manager said PTH 12 at Main Street carried nearly 3,500 vehicles an hour at peak and that MTI manages all signals; the recommendations were 'not must-haves'. Mayor Funk said many vehicles come in from the west every morning to work. Councillor Siemens said he is always asked why Steinbach isn't building a bypass, but the yearly reports show 'traffic comes to Steinbach, it doesn't go through' so 'a bypass Doesn't make sense'; he also noted counts are taken in autumn during construction. Nobody responded, and council accepted the report. This is the only time a member of council spoke directly to a highway bypass on the record from 2015 to 2026.Declined Report accepted (R26-037, unanimous); no motion, budget item or request about a bypass, perimeter road, origin-destination survey or truck count followed by Nov 2026.R26-037 · vote: unanimous
