Steinbach City Council · Dec 2015 – Nov 2026 · Topic: A Main Street bypass

A bypass around Main Street? What council has said, and what the traffic data shows

Has Steinbach City Council considered a highway bypass or truck route that would take through traffic off Main Street (PTH 52) and Brandt Street (PTH 12) since December 2015? What reasons has it given, does the city’s own traffic data support them, how did each council member act, and how have other Manitoba and Canadian cities handled provincial highways through town?

Written for residents. Start with the summary, then choose what to read. Last updated Nov 9, 2026.

Executive summary

In eleven years and three councils, a bypass came up at a council meeting once, and was set aside in a sentence. Council’s answer to through traffic has been to fix the highways through town with the province, and to let city streets carry the overflow.

  • Never on the agenda. No motion, budget line, letter or study request about a bypass, ring road or truck route around the city was found at any recorded meeting from December 2015 to November 2026. The only direct remark was Councillor Jac Siemens’ on February 17, 2026: “traffic comes to Steinbach, it doesn't go through Steinbach. So a bypass Doesn't make sense to me.” Nobody replied, and council accepted the traffic report unanimously.
  • Fix the highway through town instead. PTH 12 and 52 are provincial, and in 2018 a provincial official called a perimeter route in a joint access plan “just a hypothetical concept.” Council worked on the highways it has: the Loewen Boulevard and PTH 12 rebuild (about $11.2 million, the city’s share about $6.1 million, reopened November 2025), signal timing, and lobbying to widen highways into Steinbach. Trucks are routed along Main Street and Brandt Street by design.
  • City streets became the bypasses. Old Tom Road (paved 2016) and Park Road West (extended to Industrial Road in 2023 for $1.97 million) carry drivers around the busiest corners, and members called them “unofficial bypasses” and “a great bypass.” Residents on Old Tom, Loewen, Friesen and McKenzie told council the cut-through traffic and trucks run past their homes. No traffic study, plan for the edge roads or truck-route review followed.
  • The data partly backs council, but can’t settle it. The city’s yearly counts show PTH 12 at the south edge carrying about a third of its mid-city traffic, and only about 225 drivers an hour turning between PTH 52 West and PTH 12 North at the 2024 evening peak. Provincial truck estimates (2019) allow at most about 755 trucks a day straight through north–south; the north-to-west truck movement isn’t measured. Nobody has asked for an origin–destination survey or a truck count.
  • Steinbach’s path is common, but others measured first. Winkler, Morden and Niverville are also rebuilding the highway through town. Portage la Prairie (1970) and Brandon (1959) were bypassed decades ago in provincial highway programs, and both still run trucks down the old route. Recent bypasses and truck routes elsewhere, such as Estevan (2015) and Yorkton (2012), were built for trucks, and Yorkton surveyed licence plates before deciding a full bypass wasn’t needed.

The story in three councils

Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council paved Old Tom Road and heard the province call a perimeter highway hypothetical. Mayor Earl Funk’s first council financed the Loewen Boulevard rebuild and heard residents describe the edge roads as a bypass. His second council paved a second route north, kept trucks on Main Street, and heard the bypass idea dismissed. No vote on the topic divided council in any term. For the wider traffic story, see Traffic in Steinbach.

About this report

Quotes are verbatim from automatic transcripts and link to the meeting videos. Council members, the mayor and staff are named; residents are identified by initials. The analysis and plain-language summaries are ours.

Methods, coverage and what we couldn’t confirm
  • Transcripts. Meeting quotes come from automatic transcripts of the official videos and are left as transcribed, including repeated words, odd capitals and misheard names (“Lowen” is Loewen, “Bartman” is Barkman). The one exception is the city’s name, which the transcription often misspells and which quotes show correctly as Steinbach. Ellipses mark trimmed text; no sentence breaks were added. Each quote links to the start of the speaker’s turn in the video; check there before quoting elsewhere.
  • Speakers and names. Quotes are from speakers identified with high confidence, with one exception: the March 21, 2023 “great bypass” remark was matched to Councillor Bill Hiebert with medium confidence, and SteinbachOnline’s report of that meeting attributes the same words to him. A January 2017 remark calling Old Tom and Industrial Road “unofficial bypasses” is probably Councillor Jac Siemens’ but isn’t attributed. Speakers were matched to names with AI assistance, from how they were addressed in each meeting and from the minutes. Council members, the mayor and city staff are named in full. Residents, delegations and other members of the public are identified only by initials. Organizations and businesses are named. Where we trim a remark, leave out a personal passage, or identify a speaker by initials, the full remarks can be watched at the linked moment in the official meeting video.
  • Coverage. Every council meeting from December 15, 2015 to November 3, 2026 with a recording or minutes was searched for bypass, perimeter, ring road, truck route, heavy trucks, dangerous goods, through traffic, origin–destination and the highway names: transcripts of 243 meeting videos and minutes from January 2018 on (the city’s older minutes are not online). Not covered: the first four 2015 videos (not yet transcribed); four videos that failed to download (April 4 and October 17, 2017; September 15, 2020; July 6, 2021), where the minutes were used if they exist; and the April 5, 2022 meeting, which has no audio. Council study sessions and closed meetings are not recorded; a councillor said in 2022 that traffic flow is discussed there.
  • What counts. 18 items: any mention of a bypass or perimeter road, truck routes and truck rules, through traffic, the province’s plans for PTH 12 and 52, corridor protection in the development plans, edge roads used as alternate routes, and the yearly traffic counts as they bear on through traffic. Intersections, signals, stop signs and speed limits are covered in Traffic in Steinbach. Classifying how an item ended (done, partly, no public result, declined) and tagging council’s reasons is our judgment; the reasons are recorded for each item in the data.
  • Votes. No divided vote on the topic was found in any term. Before 2018 there are no minutes online; votes were by voice, so who supported what is taken from what members said.
  • Figures. Traffic-count figures are from the city’s 2024 and 2025 WSP studies (agenda packages of March 18, 2025 and February 17, 2026); the flow figures were added up for this report from the studies’ tables. Truck figures are the province’s MHTIS 2019 estimates (Manitoba Highway Traffic Information System, run with the University of Manitoba), not counts. The Loewen Boulevard total is the final figure reported in December 2025. An earlier version of this report listed Acres Drive and Industrial Road as truck routes; the city’s current truck-route page lists only PTH 12, PTH 52 and McKenzie Avenue.
  • Not confirmed. What the unpublished provincial access plans for PTH 12 (2013) and PTH 12 and 52 (2018), and their later update, say about a bypass, through traffic or trucks; the 2018 plan’s perimeter wording is known only from news coverage. Also not confirmed: any provincial origin–destination survey of Steinbach (none found); the outcome of the city’s December 2024 objection to the RM of Hanover rezoning on PTH 12 North; any reply to the 2022 request for “no engine brake” signs; and any bypass position by 2026 candidates (none found).
  • Data. The data downloads list every item, every council member’s part in it and every quote with its timestamp and link. Quotes in the downloads come only from council members and staff identified with high confidence. Every quote was checked word for word against its transcript.
  • Council change. The council elected in October 2026 held its inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026. Quotes are from members serving at the time, some of whom are no longer on council. Election results are from SteinbachOnline (unofficial counts).
