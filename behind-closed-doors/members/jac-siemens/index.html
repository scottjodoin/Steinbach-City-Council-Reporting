Behind closed doors · Council member

Jac Siemens: decisions made outside public meetings

What did Jac Siemens say and do about decisions made outside public meetings (study sessions, committees, closed sessions) and about council's openness between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Jac Siemens was the member most open about how much council's work happens outside public meetings. In 2015 he said he would not re-move at council an amendment 'we did push' at SPC; in 2016 he said a change to city standards 'comes at an SPC meeting where we get full response from staff'; and in 2018 he counted 190 council meetings, 80 SPC meetings and 16 study sessions over two terms. He seconded the golf club land purchase and moved the 2018 budget.

In later terms he seconded and voted for sending the purchasing by-law to SPC (2019), explained the 2018 pay change citing a promise to be 'open and transparent', and described the budget as a year-long process built at study sessions. He was the main public dissenter on the Clearspring Greens levy (2020), tried to turn a same-day five-year ice agreement into a one-year trial before voting for it 'begrudgingly' (2025), and voted alone against the Bridgepark Manor variance (2026). He said that 'each word is recorded' shapes how he speaks.

“It's not a change in city specification at this point. That decision and that discussion doesn't come at a council meeting. That discussion comes at an SPC meeting where we get full response from staff.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · On a developer's request for an exception to city standards · May 3, 2016 · 0:32:46

Term by term

2014–2018

He described SPC as the place for policy discussion with full staff input, and noted an amendment he had pushed there rather than at council (2015). He moved to send a resident's sidewalk request to the study session (2018), seconded the golf club purchase and moved the 2018 budget, and voted to table the arts centre grant. In his 2018 farewell he gave the count of 190 council meetings, 80 SPC meetings and 16 study sessions over eight years.

Took part in 9 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

He seconded Jake Hiebert's motion to send the purchasing by-law to SPC and voted for it (defeated 3–4, 2019). He explained the council pay change in public because members had 'agreed to be open and transparent', described the budget as a year-long process that starts in May, pointed residents to the SPC report on the dwelling-safety by-law, and said traffic was discussed at 'long-term planning sessions'. He voted against the Clearspring Greens levy and moved an amendment to cut its cost.

Took part in 15 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He moved a one-year trial in place of a five-year ice agreement that reached council the same day, withdrew it when staff said council could only accept or reject the whole, and voted for it 'begrudgingly' (2025). He suggested sending the economic development grant request to the study session, then moved its non-renewal in public with reasons (2025). He described the two-day November 2025 study session on flooding, and voted alone against the Bridgepark Manor variance (2026).

Took part in 15 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Unusually candid about where council does its thinking: he named and counted the private sessions, and used the public meeting to dissent, amend and explain, especially when costs fell on particular residents.
