Behind closed doors · Council member

Chris Goertzen: decisions made outside public meetings

What did Chris Goertzen say and do about decisions made outside public meetings (study sessions, committees, closed sessions) and about council's openness between December 2015 and November 2026?

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

In short

Mayor Chris Goertzen chaired a council that did much of its preparatory work at the Strategic Priorities Committee (SPC) and in study sessions; by Councillor Siemens' 2018 count, he had chaired over 288 council meetings and 120 SPC meetings since 2000. He described the budget as worked on from July to November, ending at a public hearing where 'Normally it's a very empty room.'

On the term's two transparency disputes he sided with the majority. He rebuked councillors who took the $24.3 million arts centre estimate to the media before raising it with council (2016), and defended the $500,000 golf club land purchase as 'negotiated like every other land deal' (2017). He also showed good practice: when a councillor wanted mobile-sign rules hashed out at SPC, he kept the debate in the meeting, which 'is public, it is recorded' (2016), and he gave short public summaries of the summer study sessions.

“the reality is, is that this is a deal that is a land deal, very specifically, that has been negotiated like every other land deal.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · On the golf club land purchase · Dec 5, 2017 · 1:21:30

Term by term

2014–2018

He chaired the SPC and study sessions as well as council. In public he summarized the summer study sessions (2016, 2017), sent items such as the council pay report and a 2017 funding application to SPC or the July study session, and in 2018 suggested tabling the arts centre grant to the study session. He kept the 2016 mobile-sign debate in the public meeting. He rebuked members for going to the media with the arts centre estimate ('You couldn't wait a day and a half, 2 days before a council meeting'), voted for the golf purchase, and voted for the 2018 budget, calling it a compromise.

Took part in 9 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

He treated confidential negotiation as normal practice while keeping ordinary business in public; the golf deal, negotiated over a year of closed meetings, became the dispute that defined his last year in office.
