Behind closed doors · Council members

Council members on decisions made behind closed doors

What did each council member say and do between December 2015 and November 2026 about discussion and decisions in settings the public can’t attend: study sessions, the Strategic Priorities Committee and closed sessions?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. Every member took part in committee and study-session work, so the differences show up in who objected to closed processes, who sent business there, who disclosed what happened, and a small number of split votes.

  • 2016–2018 had a clear divide. Michael Zwaagstra, Earl Funk and Susan Penner objected to how the arts centre estimate and the golf club deal were handled and voted together against the majority of Mayor Chris Goertzen, John Fehr, Jac Siemens and Cari Penner. Fehr gave the majority’s plainest reason: “Land deals are always done in camera.”
  • In 2019 the line was about committee itself. Jake Hiebert, Jac Siemens and Bill Hiebert voted to send the purchasing by-law to the Strategic Priorities Committee; the mayor, Zwaagstra and both Penners kept it in public (3–4).
  • Members spoke for openness, and some disclosed. Susan Penner explained in-camera meetings (2019); Zwaagstra warned against “decisions behind closed doors” (2020); Jake Hiebert described the new council’s orientation and study session (2022); Damian Penner put the committee’s fee options on the record (2023); the mayor disclosed a meeting with a developer (2025). But no member proposed opening committee or study sessions, and council reaffirmed the by-law that closes study sessions unanimously in 2021 and 2023.
  • As chair, the mayor sets the path. Earl Funk, who as a councillor said “public business should be done in public”, has as mayor sent most funding requests to study sessions and announced closed meetings after adjournment with no closure in the minutes.

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

As a councillor he said 'public business should be done in public' and voted against the golf deal; as mayor he sends most money requests to closed study sessions.

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Defended the golf deal as 'negotiated like every other land deal', but also kept a debate in the 'public, recorded' meeting and summarized study sessions aloud.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Council's most persistent public voice for openness, who also defended committee talk as 'not a final decision'.

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Spoke openly of SPC and study sessions as where the real discussion happens, and counted them: 80 SPC meetings and 16 study sessions in eight years.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

Objected to the golf deal and the arts centre's handling, explained in-camera meetings ('the transparency has to come after the fact'), and asked for study-session decisions to be visible in the budget.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Sided with the majority on the arts centre and the golf deal, criticizing 'manipulation with the press'; opposed sending the arts grant to the study session.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

Gave council's plainest defence of closed negotiation: 'Land deals are always done in camera.'

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Put the committee's four development-fee options on the public record and objected to 'veils of transparency'.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Little on the record on this topic: voted to send the purchasing by-law to SPC (2019) and otherwise with the majority.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Moved to send the purchasing by-law to SPC so new councillors could ask questions (defeated 3–4), and later argued for the development fee discussed there.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; no record on this topic yet. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.

Every recorded split vote on the topic

Most decisions were unanimous, and councillors take turns moving routine motions, so moving a motion is not the same as championing it. Split votes are the clearest evidence of where members differed. Several here are on the substance (land, budgets, development) of cases where the process was in dispute.
