Behind closed doors · Council member

Earl Funk: decisions made outside public meetings

What did Earl Funk say and do about decisions made outside public meetings (study sessions, committees, closed sessions) and about council's openness between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

In short

As a councillor, Earl Funk was one of three members who objected to how the golf club land deal was handled. In November 2017 he asked staff when a matter discussed in closed meetings could become public. In December he said there had been 'too many' closed-door meetings on the deal, said he had backed a motion to hear the golf club at a public meeting (defeated 4–3 in a closed meeting), and voted against the $500,000 purchase. In March 2018 he voted against the budget that carried the first payment, saying 'public business should be done in public.'

As mayor since November 2018 he has chaired every meeting. He keeps regular meetings on the record: in 2019 he reminded an RCMP commander who asked to speak 'in camera' that the meeting was on video, in 2020 he thanked Councillor Zwaagstra for the reminder 'to keep public business done in public', and in 2025 he disclosed that he had met a developer. He also routinely sends groups' funding requests to the closed budget study session 'because it's a budget item', announced closed meetings after adjournment that left no trace in the minutes (2020, 2021 and 2026), and set the August 2026 meeting on a $200,000 grant after which no public resolution was found.

“When I was elected to council, I had a simple approach: public business should be done in public, and that's how we should operate.”

Councillor Earl Funk · Voting against the 2018 budget over the golf club land deal · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:24:42

Term by term

2014–2018

One of three members, with Michael Zwaagstra and Susan Penner, who objected to closed processes. He voted against reaffirming the arts centre's $7.5 million share when its estimate reached $24.3 million (2016), and later linked that project to 'closed meetings, numbers kept from the community'. On November 21, 2017 he asked staff when a matter discussed with an outside group becomes public. He backed the closed-meeting motion to have the golf club present in public, voted against the purchase, and voted against the 2018 budget and its tax by-law, contrasting the golf club's closed-door request with the seniors' centre, which 'followed the right process, made public requests'. He opposed tabling the arts centre grant to the study session (2018).

Took part in 10 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

As mayor he chaired every meeting. He voted against sending the purchasing by-law to the Strategic Priorities Committee (2019, defeated 3–4), told an RCMP commander who wanted to speak in camera that the meeting was on video, and endorsed Councillor Zwaagstra's 2020 warning against decisions behind closed doors. He also said council had 'deliberated' the Clearspring Greens cost split for two years and bought the lot 'privately and quietly' (2020), sent funding requests from STARS, an animal rescue and the seniors' centre to study sessions, and announced closed meetings after adjournment in May 2020 and October 2021 with no closure recorded in the minutes.

Took part in 15 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He proposed sending most money requests to the study session, from minor hockey (2023) to the curling and Agape House requests left for the new council (October 2026), and described the 2023 and 2026 budgets as worked out in long private sessions; asked about a sewer reroute, he told a resident 'It was the direction of council'. He disclosed on the record that he had met a developer (2025), welcomed a group's public request as 'a trail of paper' (2026), and rejected a resident's suggestion of favouritism as 'speculation' (2026). He announced closed meetings after adjournment in March and July 2026 with no closure minuted, and set the August 11, 2026 meeting on the $200,000 safety grant.

Took part in 16 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From critic to chair: as a councillor he objected to decisions shaped in closed meetings; as mayor he leads a council that does much of its deliberating in study sessions and committee, while keeping regular meetings on the record and disclosing his own contacts.
