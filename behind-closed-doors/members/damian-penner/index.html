Behind closed doors · Council member

Damian Penner: decisions made outside public meetings

What did Damian Penner say and do about decisions made outside public meetings (study sessions, committees, closed sessions) and about council's openness between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

In his first term Damian Penner voted against sending the purchasing by-law to SPC (2019), moved the pool fee increase that had been debated at SPC, and moved the original Clearspring Greens by-law before council changed course after meeting residents.

In 2023 he set out in public the four development-fee options staff had given council at SPC, including a staff note that 11% a year would be needed to restore past contribution levels, and voted against the 5% compromise. In 2025 he called an agency's withheld program numbers 'Veils of transparency'. In 2026 he cited the event centre's committee presentation when moving its loan, and moved the 2026 budget built at the November study session.

“When this was brought to SPC, administration gave us 4 options, 1 option to consider.”

Councillor Damian Penner · On development fees, voting against the 5% compromise · Nov 7, 2023 · 0:06:44

Term by term

2018–2022

He voted against sending the purchasing by-law to SPC (2019). He moved the pool fee increase after 'significant discussion and debate' at SPC, explaining it as a step toward cost recovery, and moved the Clearspring Greens by-law, later saying council had rethought it 'over this last 2 weeks' (2020). In 2022 he asked when the council chamber could return to normal after COVID-19.

Took part in 11 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He disclosed the SPC's development-fee options in public and voted against 5% at every reading (2023). He objected to 'Veils of transparency' when an economic development delegation would not share program numbers (2025). He cited the event centre's presentation 'in committee' when moving first reading of its loan, and moved the design standards 'generally agreed' at committee (2024) and the 2026 financial plan.

Took part in 13 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Re-elected in 2026, he has used the public meeting to put committee material on the record when he disagreed with the outcome, while moving much routine business that came from committee.
