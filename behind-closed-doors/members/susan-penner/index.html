Behind closed doors · Council member

Susan Penner: decisions made outside public meetings

What did Susan Penner say and do about decisions made outside public meetings (study sessions, committees, closed sessions) and about council's openness between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

In short

In the 2014–2018 term Susan Penner voted with Michael Zwaagstra and Earl Funk against the arts centre reaffirmation, the golf club land purchase and the 2018 budget. She said the arts centre estimate should have come to a council meeting, and that the golf deal showed how a group could 'keep taxpayers in the dark until it is too late'. She moved the arts centre matching grant in public and opposed tabling it to the study session.

Later she explained in public why council meets in camera (labour, legal and land), saying 'the transparency has to come after the fact' (2019). She rebuilt the Clearspring Greens plan after talking with residents (2021), asked in 2025 for grants agreed at the study session to be shown in the budget's grants line, and challenged an agency that withheld program numbers. In July 2026 she preferred to discuss a $200,000 safety grant at SPC rather than at the meeting. She became Deputy Mayor in November 2026.

“Essentially, the transparency has to come after the fact.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Opening remarks explaining in-camera meetings · Sept 17, 2019 · 0:00:14

Term by term

2014–2018

She voted against the December 2016 arts centre reaffirmation, saying 'if this had been brought to council meeting, we could have all discussed it in that forum'. She voted against the golf club land purchase and the 2018 budget that carried its first payment. She moved the arts centre matching grant without waiting for the November study session, where grant requests are normally decided, and opposed tabling it (tabled 4–3, approved unanimously in September 2018).

Took part in 6 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

She voted against sending the purchasing by-law to SPC (2019). Her 2019 opening explained in-camera meetings for labour, legal and land matters, and said decisions are made 'at public meetings or in camera'. Presenting for the community development corporation she said some study-session recommendations 'can't say right now publicly' (2019). She rebuilt the Clearspring Greens plan after meeting residents and added it to the agenda at the meeting (2021).

Took part in 12 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

She asked for a cost 'we talked about this at study session' to appear in the grants line, and for the Chamber of Commerce's business-tax grant to be reviewed at the study session (2025; no public result found). She pressed an economic development delegation for withheld numbers. In July 2026 she asked for the $200,000 safety grant to be discussed 'at an SPC or something like that', citing privacy; no public resolution on it was found afterwards.

Took part in 13 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

She has argued for decisions to be visible after the fact, and objected when they were not, while accepting that some discussion belongs in closed or committee settings. As Deputy Mayor she will help set the new council's practices.
