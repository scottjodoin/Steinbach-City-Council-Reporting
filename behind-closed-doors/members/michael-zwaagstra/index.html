Behind closed doors · Council member

Michael Zwaagstra: decisions made outside public meetings

What did Michael Zwaagstra say and do about decisions made outside public meetings (study sessions, committees, closed sessions) and about council's openness between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Michael Zwaagstra led the objections to the golf club land deal. He said the public should have been allowed to weigh in, that the motion to hear the club in public had been 'defeated 4-3' in a closed meeting, and that the deal was built in 'one closed-door meeting after another'; he voted against it and against the 2018 budget that paid for it. He had also objected that the arts centre's $24.3 million estimate first reached the public outside a council meeting. In February 2018 he opened a meeting by reading advice that council decisions 'are to be made publicly'.

In the next two terms he argued against sending the purchasing by-law to SPC (2019), and in 2020 warned that 'the temptation to make decisions behind closed doors' must be resisted, adding that SPC meetings were open to the public (no notice, agenda or minutes of any SPC meeting were found to confirm that). He moved many items that came from SPC, and in 2023 showed what he meant by 'not a final decision' by moving development fees of 5% rather than the 7.5% discussed there. In 2026 he asked whether a request needed a resolution rather than a consensus, set out the event centre loan terms in public, but also called the August 11 grant meeting 'an SPC meeting'.

“But if we are honest with ourselves, we recognize that the temptation to make decisions behind closed doors outside of public scrutiny is one that all politicians, particularly at the local level, must constantly resist.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Opening remarks as deputy mayor · Feb 4, 2020 · 0:00:17

Term by term

2014–2018

The leading critic of the golf deal and the arts centre process. He said the $24.3 million arts centre figure had 'never come to council as a vote' (2016); moved, at a closed meeting, to have the golf club present in public; called the deal a bailout built through closed meetings; and voted against the 2018 budget, saying he would not help 'sell a budget that hides a grant that was negotiated behind closed doors'. He accepted closed talks for 'a normal land deal'. He read George Cuff's advice on open decision-making to open the February 6, 2018 meeting, and seconded tabling the arts centre grant to the study session.

Took part in 10 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

He argued that the purchasing by-law was 'not complex' and should be decided in public rather than at SPC (2019). His 2020 opening warned against decisions behind closed doors, listed personnel, land and legal matters as the only things kept private, and said SPC meetings were open to the media and the public, a claim this review could not confirm. He moved many items that came from SPC or same-day packages, citing 'consensus' at SPC (2021), and departed from staff in public with reasons on the affordable housing grant (2021).

Took part in 16 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He moved development fees of 5% instead of the 7.5% discussed at SPC, saying the committee's figure was 'not a final decision' (2023). He deferred to the City Manager on the same-day ice agreement (2025), asked in 2026 whether a request needed 'a resolution or is a consensus sufficient?', stated the event centre loan terms in public, and called the August 11, 2026 grant meeting 'an SPC meeting'. In his farewell opening he said there was no 'deep, dark secret' and urged people to watch the meeting videos.

Took part in 20 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistent in word: he argued for public decisions for a decade. In practice he defended SPC as a place for preliminary discussion and moved much of the business that came from it, while making sure some of its outcomes were changed or explained in public.
