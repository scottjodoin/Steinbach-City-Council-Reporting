Behind closed doors · Council member

Jake Hiebert: decisions made outside public meetings

What did Jake Hiebert say and do about decisions made outside public meetings (study sessions, committees, closed sessions) and about council's openness between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Two months into his first term, Jake Hiebert moved to send Purchasing By-law 2108, which let the City Manager spend up to $350,000 without council approval, to an SPC meeting so new councillors could understand it. The motion failed 3–4 and the by-law passed in public. He later moved SPC-sourced items such as design standards and the traffic by-law with little public comment, and voted against the Clearspring Greens levy.

In November 2022 he told viewers about council's private orientation and its fall study session 'to determine spending priorities for the city', an unusually open account. In 2023 he argued for the 7.5% development fee discussed at SPC and voted against the 5% compromise; his remarks that day are identified with medium confidence, so they are not quoted here.

“There are a number of councilors on council that are new, a lot of things that we may not understand or fully know the intent and reasoning behind them. … And I'd like to make a motion that it be moved to an SPC meeting for further discussion.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert · Moving to send the purchasing by-law to the Strategic Priorities Committee · Jan 15, 2019 · 0:58:12

Term by term

2018–2022

He moved the 2019 referral of the purchasing by-law to SPC, citing 'a lot of money involved'. He moved the design standards agreed by 'consensus' at SPC (2020) and the traffic by-law directed at a committee meeting, saying 'I don't think there's any further to add'. He voted against the Clearspring Greens levy and seconded Siemens' amendment to cut its cost (2020).

Took part in 8 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He disclosed council's three-hour orientation and day-and-a-half study session in his November 2022 opening. He voted against 5% development fees at every reading, wanting the 7.5% discussed at SPC (2023). He moved the agenda change and third reading for the same-day road-widening by-law (2025) and agreed to send the Raiders' request to the study session (2025).

Took part in 14 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

He has seen SPC as the place to understand complex items before deciding, and has said so in public; his votes show he also accepts the public meeting as where the decision is made.
