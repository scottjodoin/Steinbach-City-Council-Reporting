Behind closed doors · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

From December 2015 to November 2018, how much of Steinbach City Council’s work happened in committee, study sessions and closed meetings, and where did its members disagree about it?

In short

  • Committee first was the normal way. By-laws arrived “as per recommendation from council SPC meeting”, budgets were built from July to November in study sessions, and grant requests were decided there. Councillor Jac Siemens counted 80 committee meetings and 16 study sessions beside 190 council meetings over two terms.
  • The arts centre estimate reached the media first. A $24.3 million estimate went to council outside a public meeting and then to the media. In December 2016 the mayor rebuked members for going to the press; the objectors said the figure had never been debated in public. Council reaffirmed its share 4–3.
  • The golf club deal was made behind closed doors. After about a year of closed meetings, council bought 40 acres from the golf club for $500,000 and leased it back for $1 a year. A motion to hear the club in public was defeated 4–3 in a closed meeting, known only because members said so. The deal was first public on the night of the vote, and carried 4–3.
  • The budget split on the same line. The 2018 budget, which carried the first golf payment, passed 4–3: “public business should be done in public” against “Land deals are always done in camera”.
  • Good practice too. Meetings were on YouTube from September 2015; the mayor kept a contested debate in the “public, recorded” meeting and summarized study sessions aloud; ordinary land purchases came to public meetings with prices; and the new zoning by-law went from committee to open houses and public hearings.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr. This record starts in December 2015, the first meeting with a transcript.

What happened

2015–2016: committee first, and an estimate that went to the media

Items often reached the public meeting already shaped by the Strategic Priorities Committee (SPC), the whole council sitting as a committee. A mobile-sign by-law came in December 2015 “as per recommendation from council SPC meeting”, and a councillor who disagreed with it explained why he would not argue the point again in public:

“…we did push an amendment at the SPC meeting, so we won't do it here”

Councillor Jac Siemens, on the mobile-sign by-law · Dec 15, 2015 · 0:16:59

“That decision and that discussion doesn't come at a council meeting. That discussion comes at an SPC meeting where we get full response from staff.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, on changing city servicing standards · May 3, 2016 · 0:32:46

Development fees were renewed in October 2016 after the mayor said council had “discussed a week back at Strategic Priority Committee”, and staff said the committee’s recommendations were written into the by-law. The budget followed the same path: worked on, in the mayor’s words, from July “all the way through till November”, then presented at a hearing where “Normally it's a very empty room” (February 2016). After the all-day summer study session the mayor gave a short public summary.

The mayor also kept business in public when he could. In September 2016 a councillor asked to work out sign rules in “a more informal setting, in an SPC or a special meeting”; he answered that council could do it there and then:

“We are able to hear, as we're all here, it is public, it is recorded.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Sept 6, 2016 · 0:24:16

In November 2016 a $24.3 million estimate for the performing arts centre, far above the outer limit of about $15 million that, by Councillor Zwaagstra’s account, council had in mind when it capped its share at $7.5 million, went to council outside a public meeting and then reached the media. No public meeting between October and early December mentioned it. On December 20 the mayor rebuked those who went to the press, and two councillors answered that the figure should have come to council in public first:

“You couldn't wait a day and a half, 2 days before a council meeting to maybe talk to council instead of talking to the media?”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Dec 20, 2016 · 0:57:11

“We have never had a vote on a $24.3 million performing arts centre. That has never come to council as a vote. The first that that was released in the public domain didn't happen at a council meeting.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Dec 20, 2016 · 0:44:53

Council reaffirmed its $7.5 million share with a June 30, 2017 deadline, 4–3, with Councillors Zwaagstra, Susan Penner and Funk against. The funding did not arrive and the project lapsed. Who first gave the estimate to the media is not on the record.

2017: a land deal made in closed meetings

Some business moved the other way. A citizen task force presented its report on council pay at a public meeting; the mayor sent it to an SPC agenda, and the by-law that followed was debated, amended and passed in public. At the November 7 organizational meeting, the procedure and organization by-laws were presented “for council’s information” and kept without change.

Two weeks later, Councillor Earl Funk asked a question that would matter within days:

“When council is in discussion with a group, at what time does the matter become public?”

Councillor Earl Funk · Nov 21, 2017 · 0:22:27

“If there are particular meetings of council that deal with closed matters, those matters are to remain closed until council as a body makes the decision to bring the matter into an open meeting.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Nov 21, 2017 · 0:23:28

On December 5, 2017 the Steinbach Fly-In Golf Club deal came to a public meeting for the first time. About a year earlier, in a closed meeting, the club had asked for a $500,000 grant and been refused; after more closed meetings it had become a $500,000 purchase of 40 acres of the course, leased back to the club for $1 a year for about 63 years. Supplementary information reached council the day before, and the lease was not in the printed package. Councillor Zwaagstra said he had moved, at one of the closed meetings, to have the club present its case in public:

“Perhaps the public should have the right to hear about this proposal and have a chance to weigh in. That's defeated 4-3, and so here we are.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Dec 5, 2017 · 1:09:41

“This is the first time the public is hearing about this deal, and we will vote on it tonight.”

Councillor Earl Funk · Dec 5, 2017 · 1:11:37

“Every time that we've negotiated a deal on any piece of property in the city anywhere, it has always been behind closed doors. We never discuss deals until we make them. because it's just not prudent to do so.”

Councillor John Fehr, moving the purchase · Dec 5, 2017 · 1:20:24

Mayor Goertzen said the deal had been “negotiated like every other land deal”. The purchase carried about 4–3, with Councillors Zwaagstra, Funk and Susan Penner against (the count is from members’ stated positions and SteinbachOnline; there are no minutes). Supporters said the land was worth more than the price; opponents said no appraisal had been done, a question the record does not settle. Councillor Zwaagstra’s claim that the payments were spread over three years to avoid Municipal Board review was disputed in the room and is unproven.

2018: the budget vote, and a different path for other items

Two months later Councillor Zwaagstra opened a meeting by reading from George Cuff’s guide for municipal leaders:

“The decisions of a council on public issues, with very few legislated exceptions, are to be made publicly.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, reading from a governance guide · Feb 6, 2018 · 0:00:08

The 2018 budget carried the first golf payment, about $167,000, and a 0.16 mill increase. Staff said removing the payment was not an option because the agreement was signed. The three opponents of the deal voted against the budget, and the exchange put both sides of this report’s question in two sentences:

“When I was elected to council, I had a simple approach: public business should be done in public, and that's how we should operate.”

Councillor Earl Funk, voting against the 2018 budget · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:24:42

“Land deals are always done in camera. They're always done behind closed doors. I'm not saying that. You cannot make a land deal in the public eye.”

Councillor John Fehr · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:28:07

“Yes, we do negotiate land deals in camera when it's actually a normal land deal, not when it's this weird, convoluted grant request/land deal sort of just slid into here.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:30:22

The budget passed 4–3 (R18-056). Councillor Funk contrasted the golf club with the seniors’ centre, which had asked for help in public and, he said, “merely received a pat on the back”.

Other 2018 business shows what a more open path looked like. The new zoning by-law and community plan were reviewed at committee (including the SPC of March 27), then went to open houses and public hearings, and the one issue committee had not settled, hard-surfacing of parking, was voted on in public (4–2). Three downtown lots were bought at public meetings with their prices ($194,500, $172,900 and $280,000) and recorded votes, Councillor Fehr voting against each. When Councillor Susan Penner moved a $65,000 matching grant for the arts centre building, she noted that such requests would normally wait for “our November study session where we would go through grants requests”; council tabled it 4–3 to the July study session, then approved it unanimously in September after the Arts Council answered questions in public.

At the last meeting of the term, Councillor Siemens put a number on the private side of council’s work:

“We've been through 190 council meetings together, 80 SPC meetings, 16 study sessions, and 8 Christmas gatherings”

Councillor Jac Siemens, farewell remarks for the 2010–2018 council · Oct 16, 2018 · 0:00:23

None of the 2018 minutes mentions an SPC meeting, a study session or a closed session.

Where members differed

This term had the most split votes on the topic. Those on the arts centre, the golf club deal and the budget that paid for it split along one line, with Councillors Zwaagstra, Funk and Susan Penner against the majority; the others were on the arts centre grant, parking surfacing and downtown land, where Councillor Fehr voted alone against each purchase made in public. Before 2018 there are no minutes online, so who voted which way is taken from what members said; the closed-meeting vote on a public presentation is known only from members’ statements.
