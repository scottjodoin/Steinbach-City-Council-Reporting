Behind closed doors · By term

The story by term

How did the share of Steinbach City Council’s work done outside public meetings, and council’s attitude to it, change from one council to the next between December 2015 and November 2026, and what does the new council inherit?

In short

The settings stayed the same across all three councils: a Strategic Priorities Committee of the whole council, budget study sessions in July and November, and closed sessions for land, staff and legal matters. None is recorded, and none has a public agenda or minutes. What changed was the mood. In 2016–2018 three councillors fought the majority over a land deal negotiated in closed meetings. From 2018, with the same seven members for eight years, committee work became routine and public votes short and mostly unanimous, while members spoke often about the value of openness. The council elected in 2026 starts with the same rules.

The settings the public can’t attend

Five kinds of session come up on the record in all three terms. We found no public notice, agenda, minutes or recording for any of them on the city’s website, its meeting calendar, its agenda packages or the Internet Archive.

SessionWhat the record shows
Strategic Priorities Committee (SPC)The whole council sitting as a committee, set up by Organization By-law 2025. It usually meets between regular meetings, though not in summer. Draft by-laws, policies, fee options and outside presentations go here first, and staff take “direction” from it. Councillor Siemens counted 80 SPC meetings in 2010–2018.
Budget study sessionsDay-long or weekend sessions each July and November where the budget and capital list are built and groups’ funding requests are decided before the public hearing on the financial plan. Siemens counted 16 in 2010–2018; members described “a day and a half” in 2022 and two days on flooding in 2025. Procedures By-law 1809 closes every study session.
“Committee meetings”Often another name for SPC. Staff present detailed material here, such as engineering standards, by-law drafts and the event centre’s finances.
Planning sessions and orientationLong-term planning sessions, where traffic was discussed (2020–2022), and private orientation for each new council (a three-hour session in November 2022).
Closed (in-camera) meetingsFor land, staff and legal matters. The golf club deal was negotiated in them over about a year (2016–2017). Others were announced at the end of public meetings (May 2020, October 2021, March and July 2026). No minutes from 2017 to 2026 record a resolution to close, a legal ground or a subject.

2026–2030: the new council

The council elected on October 28, 2026 is the first change in membership since 2018. Michael Zwaagstra, the most persistent voice for open decisions and the member who moved much of the business that came from committee, and Jac Siemens, the member most candid about how much happens there, did not run. Ron Falk, a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years (14 as chair), and Greg Carlos, a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, in his first elected office, are new. Susan Penner, who topped the poll, is Deputy Mayor.

What the new council inherits:

  • The rules themselves. The Municipal Act requires each council to review its procedures and organization by-laws during its term, and By-law 1809 asks for the review within 30 days of the organizational meeting. The review was not on the November 3 inaugural agenda. It is the natural moment to decide whether study sessions stay closed by rule and whether SPC gets public notice and minutes.
  • Its first study session. The outgoing council sent two funding requests (a curling event host fee and a grant request from Agape House) to the new council’s study session, and cancelled the regular meeting set for November 3 so that new members could be oriented before November 17.
  • Open questions. What was decided about the $200,000 provincial safety grant deferred to an August 2026 committee meeting, and whether closed meetings will be minuted as the Act and By-law 1809 require.

At the inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026 the mayor was moving to adjourn after announcing his appointments when staff raised a point of order that council should ratify them by resolution, and council did so. Members received the Code of Conduct package, whose conflict of interest policy says councillors “will endeavour to conduct and convey council business in an open and public manner.”
