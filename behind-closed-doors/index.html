Steinbach City Council · Dec 2015 – Nov 2026 · Topic: Behind closed doors

Behind closed doors? How much of council’s work happens outside public meetings

When Steinbach City Council decides, often unanimously, how much of the discussion has already happened in settings the public can’t attend or read about: study sessions, the Strategic Priorities Committee and closed sessions? What has council said about it, how did each council member act, and how does the practice sit against Manitoba’s open-meeting rules and what other councils do?

Written for residents. Start with the summary, then choose what to read. Last updated Nov 7, 2026. This report replaces our October 2026 page, which covered January 2022 to September 2026; its analysis is kept and updated (see About this report).

Executive summary

Across three councils, Steinbach has held its votes in public and much of its deliberation in private. The pattern is lawful in part, open to question in part, and different from what several Manitoba neighbours now do.

  • Much of the talking happens off the record, in every term. Council works through items first at its Strategic Priorities Committee (the whole council sitting as a committee), at multi-day budget study sessions and in closed sessions. Councillor Jac Siemens counted 80 committee meetings and 16 study sessions beside 190 council meetings in 2010–2018. No notice, agenda, minutes or recording of any of them was found.
  • 2015–2018: the golf club deal. Council bought 40 acres from the golf club for $500,000 in December 2017 after a year of closed meetings. A motion to hear the club in public was defeated 4–3 behind closed doors, known only because members said so. The 2018 budget then passed 4–3, partly over the process: “public business should be done in public,” said one side; “Land deals are always done in camera,” said the other.
  • Since 2018: committee first, a short vote after. Staff routinely introduce items as discussed, “generally agreed to” or given “direction” at committee, and most then pass unanimously with little public debate. Budgets and groups’ funding requests are settled at study sessions, and major agreements sometimes reach council the same day. Public decisions did sometimes depart from the committee: in 2023 council cut the development-fee increase discussed there from 7.5% to 5%, in a public 5–2 vote.
  • Parts of the practice are hard to square with Manitoba’s rules and the city’s own by-law. The Municipal Act says council committees meet in public unless closed for a listed reason, with the closure recorded in the minutes. Steinbach’s Procedures By-law repeats that rule, yet closes every study session as a rule, and no minutes since 2017 record a closure. A $200,000 grant decision sent to a committee meeting in August 2026 has no public resolution. Whether study sessions count as committee meetings under the Act has never been tested; this is our plain reading of the texts, and no court or Ombudsman has ruled on Steinbach’s practice.
  • Council also does things well, and neighbours show what more is possible. Every regular meeting has been on YouTube since 2015 and livestreamed since 2020; land purchases are approved in public with prices; and in 2022–2026 the vote record shows no sign that developers’ files are settled in advance. Selkirk and Morden hold their committee of the whole in public with published minutes, Brandon and Portage la Prairie debate their budgets in public, and Morden and the RM of Ste. Anne minute every closed session with its legal ground.

The story in three councils

Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council split over how the arts centre estimate and the golf club deal were handled. Mayor Earl Funk’s first council, with the same six councillors for eight years, settled into a routine of committee discussion followed by short unanimous votes, and members spoke strongly for openness while doing so. His second council kept the routine, faced late documents and unminuted closed meetings, and was told by a resident that decisions were made in advance, which members firmly denied. The council elected in October 2026 inherits the same rules.

About this report

Quotes are verbatim from automatic transcripts and link to the meeting videos. Council members, the mayor and staff are named; residents are identified by initials. The analysis and plain-language summaries are ours. Nothing here alleges bad faith: this report can only describe what reached the public record, not what was said in sessions that are not recorded.

Methods, coverage and what we couldn’t confirm
  • Transcripts. Meeting quotes come from automatic transcripts of the official videos and are left as transcribed, including repeated words, false starts and misheard names. The one exception is the city’s name, which the transcription often misspells and which quotes show correctly as Steinbach. Ellipses mark trimmed text; no sentence breaks were added and no two speaking turns were joined. Each quote links to the start of the speaker’s turn in the video; check there before quoting elsewhere.
  • Speakers and names. Every quote is from a speaker identified with high confidence: from hand-checked speaker maps for meetings from January 2022, and for earlier meetings from how speakers were addressed and from the minutes. Council members, the mayor and city staff are named in full. Residents, delegations and other members of the public are identified only by initials. Organizations and businesses are named. In 2015–2017 the two Councillors Penner often cannot be told apart in the recordings, and their remarks are not attributed unless certain. Councillor Jake Hiebert’s remarks on development fees (October 2023), quoted on our October page, rest on a medium-confidence identification and are no longer quoted.
  • Coverage. Every council meeting from December 15, 2015 to November 3, 2026 with a recording or minutes was searched: transcripts of 243 meeting videos, and minutes from January 2018 on (plus July 18, 2017; the city’s older minutes are not online). Not covered or covered by minutes only: the first 2015 videos (not yet transcribed); four videos that failed to download (April 4 and October 17, 2017; September 15, 2020; July 6, 2021); the April 5, 2022 budget hearing (no audio); and several meetings with minutes but no recording in this collection, including the November 7, 2023 organizational meeting. Study sessions, committee meetings and closed sessions are not recorded, so what was said in them is known only from what members and staff said later in public.
  • What counts. 82 “cases on the record”: each time a member or staff referred in public to a discussion, agreement or direction from a closed or unrecorded setting; motions about such settings; debates about openness itself; same-day documents on major items; and good practice such as disclosing contacts. Classifying how a case ended (done, partly, no public result, declined) and tagging council’s reasons is our judgment; the reasons are recorded for each case in the data.
  • Votes before 2018. There are no minutes online for 2015–2017. Votes were by voice and counts were rarely announced, so who voted which way is taken from what members said. The December 2016 arts centre vote (4–3) and the December 2017 golf club purchase (4–3) are confirmed by SteinbachOnline’s reports. The closed-meeting vote refusing a public presentation by the golf club (4–3) is known only from Councillors Zwaagstra’s and Funk’s statements on December 5, 2017; its date and the four members who voted against it are not public.
  • The 2022–2026 vote tests. The four tests on the How Steinbach compares page cover only January 2022 to September 2026. They use 527 substantive decisions from the council deliberation report (AI-coded and provisional) and all 1,270 motions in the minutes of 103 regular meetings.
  • Legal reading. The descriptions of the Municipal Act and Steinbach’s by-laws are a plain-language reading of the texts, checked against the consolidated Act current to November 5, 2026. They are not legal advice. No court or Ombudsman has ruled on Steinbach’s practices.
  • Not confirmed. Whether any SPC or study session has ever been given public notice or minuted (none found on the city’s website, meeting calendar, agenda packages or the Internet Archive); whether Councillor Zwaagstra’s 2020 statement that SPC meetings are open to the public reflected practice; whether a meeting was held on August 11, 2026 and what it decided about the $200,000 grant; the subjects of the closed meetings held after adjournment in 2020, 2021 and 2026; who first gave the arts centre estimate to the media in 2016; whether an appraisal of the golf club land existed; and the minutes of October 20 and November 3, 2026, not yet available.
  • Allegations. A resident’s July 2026 suggestion of a “culture of helping out the guy you know” is unproven, was not directed at the project being heard, and was rejected by the mayor and deputy mayor. Councillor Zwaagstra’s 2017 claim that the golf payments were set to avoid Municipal Board review was disputed in the room and is unproven.
  • Changes from our October 2026 page. The record now runs from December 2015 and covers three councils. New peer research found that Morden’s committee of the whole meets in public and publishes minutes (the old page could not confirm Morden), confirmed Portage la Prairie’s open budget deliberations from the meeting video rather than a news report, and found that Selkirk publishes committee minutes and reports out of closed sessions but does not record its meetings. Steinbach’s own Procedures By-law (s. 11.9) requires closures to be minuted, and the search for minuted closures now covers every set of minutes from 2017 to 2026.
  • Data. The data downloads list every case, every council member’s part in it, every split vote, and every quote with its timestamp and link. Quotes in the downloads come only from council members and staff identified with high confidence. Every quote was checked word for word against its transcript.
  • Council change. The council elected in October 2026 held its inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026. Quotes are from members serving at the time, some of whom are no longer on council. Election results are from SteinbachOnline (unofficial counts).
