Steinbach City Council · Jan 2022 – Sept 2026 · Chamber forum topic: Steinbach in 10–20 years

Steinbach in 10 to 20 years: what has council planned?

Where is council steering Steinbach over the next 10 to 20 years, what long-term decisions has it already made, and what's still missing? How do other fast-growing municipalities plan for that horizon?

Quotes link to their moment in the official meeting videos; plans, news and peer sources are linked. Last updated Oct 13, 2026.

Bottom line

  • Steinbach is on track to roughly double in 20 years. The new Official Community Plan projects 34,085 residents by 2041, up from 17,806 in 2021, and the mayor cites 36,936 by 2045.
  • Council has put several long-range pieces in place. They include a new community plan with a 25–30-year horizon (2025), land annexed from the RM of Hanover in 2018, a 7–10-year internal capital look-ahead, an asset management policy (2023), development fees set to 2027, and a climate adaptation plan presented in August 2026.
  • The decision that will shape the next 20 years is wastewater. The lagoon reaches capacity at about 22,000 residents, and the city says a new system is needed by 2032. Its estimated cost grew from $132.5M in the 2025 funding application to $215M by September 2026, with a city share of about $72M. No federal funding has been announced.
  • What's missing is a published plan with targets. Council's 2022–26 priorities are four headings with no measures or public progress reports. There is no published servicing master plan and no joint plan with neighbouring municipalities; council deals with its neighbours mainly through formal objections.
  • Peers show other models. Brandon reviews its strategic plan every quarter with “On Track” and “At Risk” ratings. Winkler built a $73M wastewater plant jointly with the RM of Stanley.

Part 1 · The council record

Vision and planning horizon

“I think we need to be looking 20, 30 years into the future and we need to start looking and setting up the councils that are going to come before us with capital plans, with, with, and, and projects and, and infrastructure repair.”

Mayor Earl Funk, at the first meeting of this council's term · Nov 1, 2022 · 0:03:24

“The OCP provides the long-term vision and goals as to how we want to see the city develop over the next 25 to 30 years.”

Lacey Gaudet, City Planner · Apr 1, 2025 · 0:06:58

“When you look at a city like ours that has unprecedented growth year over year, I think we need to follow a plan like that. We need to have something that guides us through this and a tool for our staff to continue to work with developers and grow our community.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Apr 1, 2025 · 0:53:42

“This is the kind of stuff that really matters, is where we are setting direction for how our city is going to continue to grow in the year— in the years and decades ahead.…This is our, our long-range document. This is the document where we're envisioning, you know, 25, 50 years from now.”

Michael Zwaagstra, Deputy Mayor (chairing) · Dec 3, 2024 · 0:27:40

“This is a generational decision for this council, and what I mean by generational is that these opportunities don't come with every term of council.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, on the Southeast Event Centre · Nov 1, 2022 · 1:36:55
Context

The Official Community Plan (By-law 2244, adopted September 2025) has a vision statement and seven principles: economic development, housing, downtown, growth, active transportation and recreation, environment, and community. It projects population and the residential land needed to 2041, and commits the city to a climate risk assessment and action plan. It contains no measurable targets or monitoring indicators, and no population or land figures were discussed at the public hearing (OCP).

Wastewater: the binding constraint

“to provide at least a basis of additional servicing capacity for approximately 2 square miles of additional land that the city has available and yet unserviced.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager, on the $132.5M federal funding application · Apr 15, 2025 · 0:19:57

“we as a council are looking forward now, even though we've got, you know, another, you know, 5 years of growth left, that we're looking at this now.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Apr 15, 2025 · 0:24:38

“But I know that we're going to have a facility in place before our present lagoon has to tap out, and that's what's exciting.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Apr 15, 2025 · 0:26:02

“The new water treatment facility Steinbach is planning will be the most significant investment the city has ever made. It's exactly the kind of project where costs can escalate quickly based on regulatory requirements. Projected costs of this project have skyrocketed to over $200 million.”

Councillor Susan Penner, opening remarks; she means the wastewater facility · May 5, 2026 · 0:01:32
Context

In September 2026 the mayor called the $215M system, which includes the plant, pipes and lift stations, his top priority for the new term. He said it would let Steinbach grow to about 45,000 people. He expects a three-way split with the federal and provincial governments, leaving the city about $72M, and is open to “creative” longer-term financing, including redirecting the Event Centre's final payment in 2030 (SteinbachOnline). That would be a departure from the city's rule of repaying all debt within five years; see Taxes, spending and priorities. In 2025 council chose its own plant over joining the regional Red-Seine-Rat co-op, which staff estimated would cost 25–30% more and require giving up land.

Land and neighbours

“the friendly annexation of 2,800 acres from the Arm of Hanover in 2018, but it did secure land development for Steinbach for the next 40 to 50 years.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Mar 17, 2026 · 0:02:16

“we have to think in the long term. We have to think about what is in the best interests of our city, and especially as the city is eventually going to continue to grow. Eventually our borders will change and will expand.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, objecting to an RM of Hanover rezoning at the city's edge · Dec 17, 2024 · 0:39:33

“we are going through a time of unprecedented growth, and we don't know when it's going to end or if it's ever going to end. And so we need to make sure that we are saving land for future development. And if one— 'cause once land is developed, it's gone. It can't be developed twice.”

Mayor Earl Funk, objecting to the RM of La Broquerie's new development plan · Sept 15, 2026 · 0:50:41
Context

Council objected to an RM of Hanover rezoning in December 2024 and to the RM of La Broquerie's plan in September 2026, in both cases to protect land it may want to annex later. The 2018 annexation was itself cut back by the Municipal Board from the 7,300 acres Steinbach sought to a 30-year supply. Steinbach has no joint fringe-area plan with either RM, and in January 2026 it also ended talks on a regional library.

Long-range capital and climate

“So my question is, what are the means and measures that the city uses to ensure that we're not falling behind and that our infrastructure remains strong and functional for the long term?”

G.S., resident at the 2025 budget hearing · Mar 4, 2025 · 0:23:52

“While the provincial requirement is that it include a 5-year look ahead, the City of Steinbach has tried to maintain anywhere between a 7 and a 10-year look ahead as far as an official document.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Mar 4, 2025 · 0:24:58

“Beyond that, programs of $17.8 million, $58.3 million, and $45.6 million are currently estimated for the following 3 years.”

Brian Hrehirchuk, Senior Manager of Finance, presenting the 2026 budget · Mar 17, 2026 · 0:29:19

“The climate adaptation and mitigation plan that we'll have, and it'll be done by the spring of 2026, that will give us a roadmap of projects that can be done to alleviate some of our drainage.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Sept 16, 2025 · 0:49:13
Context

Only yearly totals of the capital plan are presented in public; the $58.3M and $45.6M years include the lagoon upgrade but aren't itemized. The asset management policy, strategy and plan were adopted in October 2023, largely as a condition of a federal grant, and haven't been reported on since. The climate plan arrived in August 2026, months late. It sets out phases for 2026–28, 2029–32 and 2033–36, is described as “not a commitment”, and had not been adopted (SteinbachOnline).

Where the long view stops

1Priorities are headings, not a plan

“we have strategic priorities. And for those for 2022 to 2026, for this term, in no apparent order is city relations, enhanced safety, infrastructure renewal and expansion.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Mar 4, 2025 · 0:29:21

The 2022–26 strategic priorities were four headings, set in unrecorded committee sessions. No document with goals, owners, timelines or measures was tabled at a recorded meeting, and the mayor's 2022 call for a 20–30-year plan did not produce one.

2Water supply was assumed to be settled

“…a city like Steinbach, that we have adequate water supply, we have our water treatment plant, it's doing very well, and it's, it's good for the long term…”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on a regional water co-op proposal · Jan 4, 2022 · 0:38:10

Council asked staff in January 2022 for an engineering report on long-term water supply. It never came back to a recorded meeting. Since then, water discussion has been about protecting the aquifer from mining, not about supply for a city of 40,000.

3Growth is hard to predict

“It's going to take I think it's going to take a really unique council, a really strong council to lead a city that's growing this fast in the future.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Sept 15, 2026 · 0:54:08

Key findings

1One project will define the next 20 years

Every growth path runs through wastewater: housing (see the housing report), business land (see the business report) and taxes (see the finance report). The cost has risen from $132.5M to $215M in about 18 months, the federal application's outcome hasn't been reported, and paying for it will likely mean breaking the five-year debt rule. Morden, which ran out of capacity, stopped accepting building-permit applications in 2023.

2Steinbach plans projects well but publishes little

Staff keep a 7–10-year capital look-ahead and plan wastewater years ahead, and council has repeatedly bought time: annexed land, a twinned force main and a doubled lift station. But residents see little of the long view: no itemized long-range capital plan, no servicing master plan, no strategic plan with measures, and a climate plan not yet adopted. A resident asked at the 2025 budget hearing how the city knows it isn't falling behind, and got only a general answer.

3Neighbours are handled by objection, not agreement

Steinbach's interests at its edges are real: once rural land is fragmented by acreages, it can't be annexed for city growth. But objections are decided one by one by the province. Winkler and the RM of Stanley, and the Red-Seine-Rat partners, show that shared plants and joint plans are possible in southern Manitoba.

Decisions that would set up the next 20 years

  • Publish a wastewater funding and servicing plan: the true capacity date, the city's share, the financing approach (including any exception to the five-year rule), and a fallback if federal money doesn't come.
  • Adopt a council strategic plan with measurable targets and public quarterly or annual progress reports, as Brandon does.
  • Adopt one official population forecast and use it across council, the school division and the health authority.
  • Publish an itemized 10-year capital plan and an annual state-of-infrastructure report under the 2023 asset management policy.
  • Adopt the climate plan with funded actions for its first phase (2026–28), starting with drainage.
  • Commission the long-term water supply study requested in 2022, sized for 40,000–45,000 people.
  • Negotiate fringe-area agreements with Hanover and La Broquerie instead of objecting case by case.
