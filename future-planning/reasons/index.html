Planning for the future · Council’s reasons

Council’s reasons, in its own words

What reasons has Steinbach City Council given since 2015 for not planning further ahead, or for planning one project at a time, and how well do those reasons hold up?

In short

Council has given the same eight reasons across three terms. Several are sound: cost and partner money are real limits, and keeping control of land and facilities has served the city. The weaker points are where a reason stood in for a plan that was never written down (“staff have it in hand”), where growth was called unpredictable although the city had forecasts, and where a warning (the lagoon, 2018) waited years for action.

1Growth can’t be predicted

“Without having had a chance to consider it thoughtfully, I won't speculate, but we are entering Somewhat uncharted territory in Manitoba, I think.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager, asked what population would next change how the city works · Feb 6, 2018 · 0:19:58

“we are going through a time of unprecedented growth, and we don't know when it's going to end or if it's ever going to end. And so we need to make sure that we are saving land for future development. And if one— 'cause once land is developed, it's gone. It can't be developed twice.”

Mayor Earl Funk, objecting to La Broquerie’s plan · Sept 15, 2026 · 0:49:15

“It's going to take I think it's going to take a really unique council, a really strong council to lead a city that's growing this fast in the future.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Sept 15, 2026 · 0:54:08
Context

The city has had forecasts. Its own 2014 growth study (MMM Group) projected 102,447 people by 2064 and said vacant land would run out at about 26,977 people, around 2029. In 2018 the City Manager reported growth of just under 4% a year for 20 years and expected it to continue. The 2025 community plan projects 34,085 residents by 2041. But members cite different numbers: 36,936 by 2045 (the mayor) and 44,000 by 2046 (Councillor Siemens), and none of these was used in a public decision except the community plan. In 2022 a planner for neighbouring municipalities told council:

“I'm also not confident about what your forecasting looks in terms of your water supply needs for the next 30 or 40 years.”

D.T., Landmark Planning & Design, delegation · Jan 4, 2022 · 0:38:56

3One step at a time, and study first

“As has been noted, the Official Community Plan sets out the general direction for the city over the next 5 years. It's specifically a 5-year planning document.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jul 17, 2018 · 1:31:25

“In regards to transportation, we note that their comments are definitely something we should be looking into, and within the future, we do want to take on a transportation plan. But that's not something we would be doing immediately.”

Lacey Gaudet, City Planner, after the province urged transportation and transit planning · Jul 17, 2018 · 0:56:06

“And some of these things take time because as development comes, we let the development happen and then we can work on the road system.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Sept 6, 2022 · 0:24:23
Context

Planning in steps has kept costs down, and some steps were taken in time: the second sewer line was twinned before the lagoon route filled. But several steps were put off for years. The province recommended a greenhouse-gas baseline and transit planning in 2018; the first active transportation plan came in 2025, accepted only “in principle,” and there is still no transportation master plan. A north-end roads plan promised in 2020 did not return, and a drainage master plan is listed only as a future program. At the 2022 hearing on a large development, a neighbour asked council to step back:

“Overall, I would ask council to stop looking at the tax revenue, step back and revisit and study your official community plan. Do some serious long-term planning and stick with it.”

R.F., neighbour of the development · Sept 6, 2022 · 1:00:58
