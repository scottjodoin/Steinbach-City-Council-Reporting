Planning for the future · Council member

Jake Hiebert: planning for the next 20 years

What did Jake Hiebert say and do about Steinbach's long-term planning (growth, land, water and wastewater, neighbours, long-range capital and climate) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Jake Hiebert represented the city on the Seine Rat Roseau Watershed District and moved its by-laws. In 2020 he asked whether a regional water co-op would tie in with the watershed's drought and flood planning. He voted with Jac Siemens against First Street infill (2019) and against charging Clearspring Greens most of a pathway's cost, and questioned whether a traffic study from the COVID-19 year was useful for planning.

On industrial land he voted against retail uses in industrial zones and asked council to decide what an area was meant for. In 2023 he voted against 5% development fees, moved the asset management policy, and voted against Holm Street infill and the First Street lane closing. In 2022 he told residents that council sets its spending priorities at a day-and-a-half study session, which is not recorded. On the new council he represents the city on the Eastman Regional Municipal Committee.

“I ask the question, when this area was zoned light industrial, what was the intent at that time? Maybe it's time to rezone this area to something that includes retail.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert · On a request for retail space in an industrial park · Nov 15, 2022 · 0:30:55

Term by term

2018–2022

Moved the watershed district by-law and asked how a regional water co-op fit with watershed planning (2020). Voted against First Street infill (4–3) and the Clearspring Greens levy (5–2), and questioned a COVID-year traffic study. Voted against a school rezoning in an industrial park (2021) and against a retail use there (April 2022).

Took part in 22 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Voted against 5% development fees (5–2; his remarks are only medium-confidence in the speaker maps); moved the asset management policy and the watershed district's expansion; said the event centre would not have been built without this council. Voted against a charity's retail use in an industrial unit and the 6 First Street downtown change at second reading (November 2022), Holm Street infill (4–3) and the First Street lane closing (5–2), and for the three-way stop at Loewen and Hespeler. Called the Manning Canal transfer 'far overdue.'

Took part in 23 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A steady vote whose dissents, like Damian Penner's, were about who pays for growth and what land is for. He returns on the new council.
