Planning for the future · Council member

Susan Penner: planning for the next 20 years

What did Susan Penner say and do about Steinbach's long-term planning (growth, land, water and wastewater, neighbours, long-range capital and climate) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

In short

Susan Penner moved the 2016 motion dropping the multiplex as unaffordable for a city taking in about $13 million a year in taxes. The same year she called for regional cooperation on facilities mostly used by people from outside the city. She moved the objections to Hanover's 2019 plan change and its 2024 rezoning, describing rural development as leapfrogging the city's boundaries.

In the 2022–2026 term she stated the new community plan's 25–30-year horizon, moved its third reading, championed downtown density and moved the Holm Street infill. She opposed higher development fees for affordability. After the 2024 and 2025 storms she was the most urgent voice on drainage and on servicing limits. In 2026 she put the wastewater cost at over $200 million and asked whether provincial environmental rules justify it. On the new council she is Deputy Mayor and the city's member on the Hanover joint action committee.

“It is not feasible for municipalities and smaller cities to be able to afford that magnitude of project. And these projects need to be done because if you can't get them done, your growth literally will stop. You cannot build what you cannot service.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Asking the province to fund more water and sewer projects · Oct 7, 2025 · 0:30:40

Term by term

2014–2018

Led the 2016 task force that surveyed residents on recreation facilities, moved dropping the multiplex, and voted against reaffirming the arts centre (4–3). Asked whether the city could partner with surrounding RMs on regional facilities. Supported the water supply project and moved the Arts Centre repair match after larger projects proved 'too large in scope for our city.' Voted for the 2018 downtown land purchases.

Took part in 10 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Moved the 2019 budget and the objection to Hanover's plan change; called the water project 'planning ahead for the future'; gave a review of a decade of growth in 2020; asked for north-end road capacity to go to the July 2020 study session. Voted for First Street infill (4–3), rebuilt the Clearspring Greens pathway plan, raised the idea of a regional library, and was one of two votes to hear a school rezoning in an industrial park.

Took part in 27 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Stated the community plan's 25–30-year horizon, moved its third reading and the Holm Street infill (4–3), and opposed 7.5% development fees for affordability. Asked why the city needed the Eastman regional committee alongside AMM. Moved the 2024 Hanover objection, the Manning Canal transfer and the aquifer comments. After the storms she said drainage could not wait for 'the 5-year plan' or 'the 10-year plan.' Warned that 'You cannot build what you cannot service' and, in 2026, that the wastewater cost had passed $200 million.

Took part in 27 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistent in tying growth to what the city can service and afford, and more urgent since the storms. As Deputy Mayor and the longest-serving councillor, she is placed to shape the wastewater financing and the next council's priorities.
