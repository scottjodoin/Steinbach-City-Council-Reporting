Planning for the future · Council members

Council members on planning for the future

How did each council member vote and speak on Steinbach’s long-term planning between December 2015 and November 2026?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. The big long-range decisions (water, wastewater, the community plans, objections to neighbours) passed without a recorded dissent, so the differences show up in who moved and explained them, what they questioned, and a few split votes on land use and on who pays for growth.

  • Jac Siemens was the steadiest voice for plan-led land use. He objected to buying or rezoning land without a plan, voted against First Street infill (2019) and the downtown extension (2022–2023), and urged looking 20–30 years ahead.
  • Michael Zwaagstra explained most long-range decisions: five-year debt, local control, keeping industrial land industrial, and the 2025 choice of a city-owned wastewater plant. Both he and Siemens left council in 2026.
  • Damian Penner and Jake Hiebert said growth should pay more, voting against development fees kept at 5% a year (2023). Susan Penner, now Deputy Mayor, has pressed hardest on servicing limits and drainage since the storms.
  • Infill split the council in unexpected ways. Holm Street passed 4–3 in 2024 with Zwaagstra, Damian Penner and Jake Hiebert against; Damian Penner had just argued that sprawl would make the city “fail.”

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

As a councillor he questioned big borrowing and the golf land deal; as mayor he called for a 20–30-year plan, backed the new community plan and the wastewater application, and defended the city's edge against its neighbours.

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Spoke often of long-term strategies (pipes, downtown land, the second water supply) and named the lagoon the 'next major hurdle' in 2018; no wastewater study followed before he left office.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Moved more long-range items than anyone and explained them: five-year debt, local control, land protection at the edges. He called the 2018 plan 'a 5-year planning document' and the 2025 plan the city's long-range document.

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Council's most consistent voice for plan-led land use: he objected to buying land 'when there's no plan in place', urged looking 20–30 years ahead, and cast most of the dissents on infill and the downtown boundary.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

From the motion that dropped the multiplex (2016) to 'You cannot build what you cannot service' (2025), she frames growth around what the city can afford and service; she is now Deputy Mayor and sits on the Hanover joint committee.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Defended long-term land banking as continuity between councils and moved the 2018 objections to neighbours' plans; in 2015–2017 some of her remarks cannot be told apart from Susan Penner's.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

Moved the water supply tender, the 2018 plan's first reading and the golf land purchase as 'an investment for the future', but voted against every 2018 downtown land purchase.

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Frames decisions in long horizons ('25, 30' years; a pipe 'good for 75 to 100 years') and argued growth should pay more, voting against 5% development fees; yet he voted against Holm Street infill with the neighbours.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Mostly a supporting vote on long-range items; he sat on the La Broquerie joint committee, voted against First Street infill, and in 2026 moved the objection to La Broquerie's plan to keep land for annexation.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

The city's watershed member and, with Damian Penner, a vote against 5% development fees; he pressed council to decide what industrial areas are for instead of ruling case by case.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; no record on long-term planning yet. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.
