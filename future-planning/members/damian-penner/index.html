Planning for the future · Council member

Damian Penner: planning for the next 20 years

What did Damian Penner say and do about Steinbach's long-term planning (growth, land, water and wastewater, neighbours, long-range capital and climate) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Damian Penner linked Hanover's 2019 plan change to the city's only sewer route to the lagoon. He said voters had tasked council with looking forward for sustained growth, and called stormwater ponds part of long-term planning for a city as flat as 'a postage stamp.' He repeatedly flagged the shortage of industrial lots, and voted against rezoning industrial land for a school so as not to pass problems on to later councils.

In 2023 he and Jake Hiebert voted against development fees of 5% a year, saying the city was not bringing in enough for future growth. In 2024 he cited a 20-year projection that the population must double and warned that sprawl would make the city fail, but voted against the Holm Street infill with the neighbours. He credited staff's 5-, 10- and 20-year capital plans, which have not been published, and moved the 2026 budget crediting long-term planning and five-year debt.

“We are looking at our official community plan right now in depth, and the 20-year projection is we need to double our population. If we double our population, our population outwards, the city will fail. It's just that simple. We cannot double our population sprawling outwards.”

Councillor Damian Penner · On the Holm Street infill, which he then voted against · Mar 19, 2024 · 1:43:10

Term by term

2018–2022

Linked Hanover's plan change to the only sewer route to the lagoon (2019); voted for First Street infill; called stormwater ponds part of long-term planning; said Clearspring Greens' pathway 'should have been planned for' when the rural zoning was approved. Moved the five-year economic development office under council's strategic priorities, said the community plan 'does need to be looked at' to open land for growth, and voted against rezoning a site in an industrial park for a school (2021).

Took part in 24 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Voted against 5% development fees (5–2); credited staff's 5-, 10- and 20-year capital plans; cited the 20-year doubling but voted against Holm Street infill (4–3). Asked about serviced industrial land, moved the request for provincial money for the lagoon force main design, said developers read the community plan 25 to 30 years ahead, said most of the active transportation plan would be built by later councils, and welcomed a new sewer pipe 'good for 75 to 100 years.' Voted against a charity's retail use in an industrial unit (November 2022) and against the three-way stop at Loewen and Hespeler.

Took part in 25 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Increasingly the member making the cost-of-growth argument from inside the majority. He returns on the new council as one of its most experienced members on servicing and growth.
