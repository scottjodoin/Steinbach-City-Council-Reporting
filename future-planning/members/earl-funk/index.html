Planning for the future · Council member

Earl Funk: planning for the next 20 years

What did Earl Funk say and do about Steinbach's long-term planning (growth, land, water and wastewater, neighbours, long-range capital and climate) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

In short

As a councillor until 2018, Earl Funk voted to drop the multiplex in 2016, calling the principles that replaced it a master plan, and voted against reaffirming the $24.3 million arts centre. He opposed buying 40 acres of the golf club for $500,000, saying the city does not need to own land to control how it is used. In 2018 he moved one of the downtown land purchases as part of what he called a 25-year plan.

As mayor since November 2018 he has chaired almost every planning decision in this record. He opened the 2022 term by calling on council to plan 20 to 30 years ahead. He backed the 2025 community plan as a guide for unprecedented growth and promised a new wastewater facility before the lagoon runs out of room. Several of his promises to plan did not come back to a public meeting: a north-end roads plan (2020), an engineers' report on long-term water supply (2022) and a written strategic plan. He treats growth as hard to predict, and roads and downtown density as following developers.

“I think we need to be looking 20, 30 years into the future and we need to start looking and setting up the councils that are going to come before us with capital plans, with, with, and, and projects and, and infrastructure repair.”

Mayor Earl Funk · At the first meeting of the 2022–2026 term · Nov 1, 2022 · 0:02:41

Term by term

2014–2018

Supported dropping the $71–100 million multiplex in 2016 and voted against reaffirming the $24.3 million arts centre (4–3); warned against the arts centre's borrowing in the 2017 budget. Opposed the golf club land purchase, saying the city designates zoning and does not need to own land to control its use (2017), but moved the July 2018 Hanover Street purchase as part of 'a 25-year plan.' Voted against tabling the Arts Centre repair match (2018).

Took part in 12 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Elected mayor in 2018. Said that without the second water supply the city 'could just have to stop growing,' called the Hanover Road widening 'good planning for the future,' and opposed Hanover's 2019 plan change because it impedes the growth of an urban area. Promised a north-end roads plan (2020) and sent the regional water co-op question to staff for an engineers' report (2022); neither came back to a public meeting. Treated roads as following development. Voted with the majority for First Street infill (2019) and for the event centre.

Took part in 31 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Opened the term calling for a 20–30-year plan; in 2025 he named the term's four strategic priorities, but no written plan with measures followed. Backed the 2025 community plan, downtown density ('developer-driven') and 5% development fees for affordability. Promised a wastewater plant 'before our present lagoon has to tap out' and a climate 'roadmap' by spring 2026 (presented August 2026, not adopted). Objected to Hanover and La Broquerie plans to save land for growth, worried that the new Eastman regional committee would take attention off Steinbach, and ended regional library talks to protect the library's autonomy. Voted against a three-way stop at Loewen and Hespeler, preferring a roundabout in the long-term capital plan.

Took part in 34 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From a councillor who questioned big projects to a mayor whose language is long-range ('20, 30 years', 'unprecedented growth') but whose council still plans mostly project by project. He starts the 2026–2030 term with the wastewater system as his stated top priority.
