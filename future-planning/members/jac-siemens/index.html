Planning for the future · Council member

Jac Siemens: planning for the next 20 years

What did Jac Siemens say and do about Steinbach's long-term planning (growth, land, water and wastewater, neighbours, long-range capital and climate) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Jac Siemens led the city's side of the Steinbach–Hanover joint action committee through the 2018 annexation. More than any other member, he argued that land-use decisions belong in the community plan, not in one application at a time. He objected to buying downtown land without a plan (2016) and voted against First Street infill in 2019, saying council 'had it right when we had the community plan.' In 2020 he warned that council had changed the plan 'one lot at a time.'

In his later terms he pushed for growing up instead of out, a walking and cycling network, a roundabout at Loewen and Hespeler, and orderly serviced growth over the next 20 to 30 years. He was the lone vote against extending the downtown business district to First Street. He represented the city on the Eastman regional committee and the regional library working group, and gave the 44,000-by-2046 population figure. He also called federal climate planning 'downloading' and moved to end the regional library talks. He did not run in 2026.

Term by term

2014–2018

Led the Hanover joint action committee and credited the cooperative annexation. Objected to a 2016 downtown purchase because there was 'no plan in place'; moved a one-time relaxation of servicing standards to attract employers; asked that a Brandt Street redesignation wait for the 2018 plan review so the whole area could be considered; supported the golf land deal; asked a future council to review downtown parking rules.

Took part in 21 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Moved to deny the First Street rezoning and voted against it (4–3), then said council should make looking again at First Street a priority. Defended local planning control against provincial Bills 48 and 37, insisted on serviced, orderly growth when refusing a rural lot split, and acknowledged there was 'not a definitive plan' for traffic at Old Tom Road and Loewen Boulevard. Voted against charging Clearspring Greens most of a pathway's cost, against the Acres Drive levy, and against extending the downtown district to 6 First Street.

Took part in 31 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Lone vote against the First Street downtown extension (5–1); said council had no long-term plan for the Brandt Street corridor; backed Holm Street infill and 'growing up instead of out'; his motion for a three-way stop at Loewen and Hespeler, as a step toward a roundabout, lost 3–4. Moved the community plan's first reading (calling its active transportation part incomplete) and the Active Transportation Plan. Represented the city on regional bodies, warned of threats to the aquifer, called the climate grant federal 'downloading', and moved ending the regional library talks. Did not run in 2026.

Took part in 35 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

The steadiest long-range land-use voice across three councils, often outvoted on individual sites. With him and Michael Zwaagstra gone, the new council loses its two most experienced members on planning.
