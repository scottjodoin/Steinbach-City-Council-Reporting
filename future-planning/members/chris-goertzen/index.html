Planning for the future · Council member

Chris Goertzen: planning for the next 20 years

What did Chris Goertzen say and do about Steinbach's long-term planning (growth, land, water and wastewater, neighbours, long-range capital and climate) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

In short

Chris Goertzen chaired council through the annexation from the RM of Hanover, the second water supply and the 2018 community plan. He described much of the city's work as long-term strategy: renewing underground pipes before they fail, buying downtown land over 15 to 20 years, and waiting for federal and provincial money before building the water supply.

He was the strongest voice for a recreation master plan, voting against the 2016 motion to drop the multiplex. In February 2018 he told council that expanding the lagoon would likely be the city's next major hurdle; no wastewater study or capital line followed before the 2018 election. He set up joint action committees with the RMs of Hanover and La Broquerie, and backed formal objections when either neighbour departed from the transition zone around the city.

“I would suspect that the next major hurdle that we may see is actually the expansion of the lagoon or the waste removal.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · On the City Manager's annual report · Feb 6, 2018 · 0:21:25

Term by term

2014–2018

Chaired every item. Argued to finish the multiplex master plan and voted against dropping it (5–2); asked council to honour the arts centre process; supported the golf club land deal as long-term land banking. Passed the water supply borrowing by-law but waited for senior-government money before building. Formed the La Broquerie joint action committee (2017), backed objections to Hanover's and La Broquerie's plans, and voted against relaxing hard-surfacing rules in the new zoning by-law (4–2). Told council the lagoon was the next major hurdle (2018).

Took part in 34 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

His term set up much of what followed: the annexed land, the second water supply and the downtown land that became the event centre site. The warning he gave about the lagoon was the one not acted on in his term.
