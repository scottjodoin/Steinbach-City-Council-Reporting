Planning for the future · Council member

Bill Hiebert: planning for the next 20 years

What did Bill Hiebert say and do about Steinbach's long-term planning (growth, land, water and wastewater, neighbours, long-range capital and climate) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Bill Hiebert joined council in 2018 and represented the city on the Steinbach–La Broquerie joint action committee. He seconded the 2019 event centre agreement, saying a new recreation centre was a main reason he ran. He voted with Jac Siemens and Jake Hiebert against the 2019 First Street rezoning. In 2021 he told residents that, with the new water treatment plant, the city had no concerns about supply.

In the 2022–2026 term he credited the city for buying land and building reserves ahead of time. He voted for the Holm Street infill and for a three-way stop at Loewen and Hespeler. He cast the lone vote against refusing a variance on annexed land (reason not stated). In September 2026 he moved the objection to La Broquerie's plan, saying it would stop the city from annexing that area to keep growing. He seconded ending the regional library talks, in which he had been the city's alternate. On the new council he represents the city on the watershed district.

“and if we were to annex that area in the future, it would not allow us to annex that area to continue growing Steinbach.”

Councillor Bill Hiebert · Moving the objection to the RM of La Broquerie's plan · Sept 15, 2026 · 0:43:43

Term by term

2018–2022

Seconded the event centre agreement; sat on the La Broquerie joint action committee; voted against the First Street rezoning (4–3) and against a school rezoning in an industrial park. Spoke little on long-range items; in 2021 he said the new water treatment plant left 'no concerns' about supply.

Took part in 13 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Credited land bought and reserves built ahead of time; voted for Holm Street infill (4–3) and for the three-way stop at Loewen and Hespeler (lost 3–4); lone vote against refusing a variance on annexed land (5–1). Moved the 2026 objection to La Broquerie's plan to keep land for annexation, and seconded ending the regional library talks. A February 2025 remark that the sewer system was almost at capacity rests on a medium-confidence speaker identification.

Took part in 20 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A quiet, consistent vote whose clearest long-range positions came at the city's edge: keeping land the city may need to annex.
