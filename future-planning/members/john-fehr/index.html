Planning for the future · Council member

John Fehr: planning for the next 20 years

What did John Fehr say and do about Steinbach's long-term planning (growth, land, water and wastewater, neighbours, long-range capital and climate) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

In short

John Fehr, Deputy Mayor until November 2017, voted against dropping the multiplex (5–2) and moved the December 2016 motion reaffirming the arts centre with a June 30, 2017 deadline. He moved the City Hall addition, which he said had been delayed for years for lack of funds. As the city's conservation district representative, he said holding water upstream was the best answer to runoff onto the golf course.

In 2017–2018 he moved the water supply tender and the first reading of the 2018 community plan. He moved the $500,000 golf club land purchase as an investment for the future of the city. But he voted against all three 2018 Hanover Street purchases, saying the program drove residents and businesses out of downtown and overspent the land budget.

“It's an investment for the future of our city. We purchase land all the time. When we believe we need a piece of property for the future development of our city, the price is reasonable, we buy it.”

Councillor John Fehr · Moving the golf club land purchase · Dec 5, 2017 · 1:01:04

Term by term

2014–2018

Voted against dropping the multiplex; moved the arts centre reaffirmation with a sunset date (4–3); moved the City Hall addition, the water supply tender, the 2018 plan's first reading and the golf land purchase. Voted for relaxing hard-surfacing rules in the zoning by-law (4–2) and for tabling the Arts Centre repair match (4–3). Voted against each 2018 downtown land purchase (5–1, 5–1, 6–1), saying he had heard no support for the downtown development once planned.

Took part in 20 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

Supportive of big projects and land deals he saw as investments, yet late in the term the one member to question the downtown land-assembly strategy.
