Planning for the future · By term

2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk’s first council

What long-term decisions did Steinbach City Council make from November 2018 to October 2022, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • Water was treated as settled. The second water plant was financed with a $5.375 million loan, the largest in city history. Council then set aside a regional water co-op twice (2020, 2022); an engineers’ report on long-term supply was requested in 2022 but never came back to a public meeting.
  • The lagoon came up once. It was raised only as a reason to object to a Hanover plan that would block the city’s sewer route to it (2019). No wastewater study or capital line appeared in public.
  • Local control first. Council objected to La Broquerie’s and Hanover’s plans (2019) and to the province’s Bills 48 and 37, which could create planning regions. Joint action committees met but never reported in public.
  • Growth policy made one site at a time. First Street infill passed 4–3; industrial land and the downtown boundary split council. Jac Siemens warned the community plan was being changed “one lot at a time.” No plan review began.
  • Priorities set out of sight. Four strategic priorities were named only in passing. Promised plans for north-end roads (2020) and water supply (2022) did not return. The term ended with the mayor calling for a 20–30-year plan.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2018–2019: the water plant, and the neighbours

The new council financed the second water supply, which members described as the project that let Steinbach keep growing:

“Within a few years, we could max out our water supply at this point. And so to grow further, we definitely need a second water supply.…you know what could happen is we could just have to stop growing because we don't have the infrastructure”

Mayor Earl Funk · Dec 18, 2018 · 0:28:55

In March and April 2019 council objected to La Broquerie’s new development plan and to a Hanover plan change that would put rural lots on land west of the city. Members tied the Hanover objection to the lagoon:

“What this really does is it blocks off any potential for future land development to the southwest of the city as well, being that that's the only undeveloped lot that would allow us to bring sewer services through to our current existing lagoon.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Apr 2, 2019 · 0:21:25

The 2019 budget showed only yearly capital totals for the next four years. In July and August, council split 4–3 to rezone 44 First Street for an eight-unit building. Councillor Siemens, joined by both Hieberts, said the community plan had kept that side of the street single-family:

“I think we had it right when we had the community plan.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Aug 20, 2019 · 0:10:24

“the city's official community plan is not changing. If the land is designated residential, it will still be designated residential. There is no change to the community plan.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jul 16, 2019 · 1:03:34

In October council signed the event centre agreement, $10 million plus up to half a mill a year, on land past councils had bought over 20–25 years. The mayor named renewing recreation and cultural facilities as one of council’s main priorities; the full list of four priorities was never tabled as a plan.

2020: a co-op set aside, a plan promised

Planners for the Manitoba Water Services Board proposed an East Region Water Co-op for supply and backup. Council received it as information. In March, on north-end traffic, the mayor promised a plan that did not come back to a recorded meeting:

“I think we need to be looking at this and any— in the, in the future, come up with a plan for how we will mitigate these, these, these roads and how we're going to make sure that these roads will handle the traffic, because we are growing.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Mar 3, 2020 · 0:50:45

In June council objected to the province’s Bill 48, which would allow appeals of council’s planning decisions and new regional planning boards. Councillor Siemens supported another First Street rezoning but asked council to look at the street again:

“Now we've changed that as a council one lot at a time at this point.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Jun 16, 2020 · 0:35:20

2021–2022: industrial land, local control and a call for a longer view

Council objected again when the bill returned as Bill 37, applied for a federal asset management grant, and joined six other municipalities in reviewing a silica sand mine’s risk to the shared aquifer. Industrial lots ran short; council refused to rezone an industrial site for a school (5–2) and split on retail in industrial units. It refused to split a rural lot in land held for future growth:

“But part of this is that we need to— council needs to be forward-looking and see where the development will be going over the next 20 to 30 years, and it would be prudent in the long term.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Nov 16, 2021 · 0:20:32

In January 2022 a planning firm again asked whether Steinbach would join a regional water co-op. Councillor Zwaagstra asked why; the mayor sent the question to staff for an engineers’ report on supply, which never came back to a public meeting:

“And so my question is, what is the reason that a city like Steinbach, that we have adequate water supply, we have our water treatment plant, it's doing very well, and it's, it's good for the long term, that's in terms of to put city tax dollars into joining the co-op or even participating in the exploration?”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jan 4, 2022 · 0:38:10

In September council approved a large development at Loewen Boulevard and Old Tom Road over neighbours’ calls for traffic and drainage planning (R.F., a neighbour, asked council to “do some serious long-term planning”):

“And some of these things take time because as development comes, we let the development happen and then we can work on the road system.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Sept 6, 2022 · 0:24:23

“But specifically to Old Tom and Lowen Boulevard, it's been talked about, it's part of our long-term planning strategies. There's not a definitive plan.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Sept 6, 2022 · 1:19:40
