Planning for the future · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

What long-term decisions did Steinbach City Council make from December 2015 to November 2018, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • Land and water for growth. The province approved about 2,800 acres of the city’s annexation request from the RM of Hanover (effective January 1, 2018), less than the city asked for. Council built a second water supply and treatment plant (about $11 million) once federal and provincial money came through.
  • A five-year horizon. The new community plan (2018) was adopted early to cover the annexed land and changed little else. A councillor called it “specifically a 5-year planning document.” Capital plans looked four years ahead, and priorities were set in closed study sessions.
  • The lagoon was flagged, not planned. In February 2018 the mayor called lagoon expansion the “next major hurdle,” and the City Manager said the 2007 expansion gave “a dozen to 20 years.” No study or budget line followed in this term.
  • Recreation megaprojects fell through. Council dropped the multiplex 5–2 (2016) for five principles; the $24 million arts centre died when its funding deadline passed (2017).
  • Land banking split council. Downtown land buying continued for 20–25 years without a decided use, and the 40-acre golf club purchase divided council about 4–3. Neighbours were handled through joint committees and formal objections.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr. This record starts in December 2015, the first meeting with a transcript.

What happened

2015–2016: strategies described, not written down

The mayor described the city’s pipe renewals and downtown land buying as long-term strategies, but no strategic plan, growth study or asset plan was tabled in public. Priorities were set at a summer study session and in the Strategic Priority Committee, which are not recorded.

“this is part of a long-term strategy that we've had and that we've been implementing over the last quite a number of years, and that is to renew our underground infrastructure and making sure that we are ahead of problems rather than reacting to problems.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen, on rebuilding Barkman Avenue · Dec 15, 2015 · 0:09:53

The biggest long-range debate was recreation. Councillor Susan Penner moved to drop the $71–100 million multiplex as unaffordable and adopt five principles instead; the mayor wanted to finish the master plan. The motion carried 5–2, with Mayor Goertzen and Deputy Mayor John Fehr against (count from SteinbachOnline; the video ends before the vote).

“A community our size that takes in only $13 million per year in taxation simply cannot afford the financing or the operating costs of these projects without massive tax increases.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Apr 5, 2016 · 0:46:46

“We have private contributors that are desperate to see just a master plan. Show us that master plan. And we're almost there.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen, voting against · Apr 5, 2016 · 0:56:50

Water supply was the clearest growth project. After three years of aquifer testing, council hired AECOM to design a second supply and plant, a $10 million project that would go ahead only with grants:

“We need to make sure that we have adequate supply, not just for now, but for the medium and long-term future.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jun 21, 2016 · 0:05:19

In December council split 4–3 to reaffirm its $7.5 million arts centre share with a June 30, 2017 deadline, after the estimate reached $24.3 million.

2017: annexation and the water plant

The arts centre ended in July 2017 when its outside funding missed the deadline; the promised consultation on future recreation plans did not appear in public. Council passed the water supply borrowing by-law but waited for federal money:

“This is a secondary water project that will allow us to continue to expand our city and grow.…We continue to wait for further dollars coming through before we can proceed, but we will have this borrowing bylaw in place and we'll be able to proceed quickly if and when those dollars come about.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Jul 4, 2017 · 0:16:55

The Municipal Board and the province approved part of the city’s annexation request, adding just under 3,000 acres from the RM of Hanover from January 1, 2018. Council formed a joint action committee with the RM of La Broquerie after objecting to a rezoning in the transition zone around the city. In December it bought 40 acres of the golf club for $500,000 and leased it back for $1 a year; supporters called it land banking, opponents a bailout.

2018: a new plan, and a warning about the lagoon

The City Manager’s annual report said the city had grown just under 4% a year for 20 years. Asked what came next, the mayor named wastewater:

“I would suspect that the next major hurdle that we may see is actually the expansion of the lagoon or the waste removal.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen, on the City Manager’s annual report · Feb 6, 2018 · 0:21:25

“The last sewage lagoon expansion was completed in 2007. That does provide the city about a dozen to 20 years of capacity, but certainly that, along with other programs and projects, is something that we need to be aware of and work towards.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Feb 6, 2018 · 0:21:43

The second water supply was tendered at about $10.6 million, with $5.6 million from other governments. The new community plan (By-law 2099) and zoning by-law were adopted in October, mostly to cover the annexed land. At the July hearing the province urged a greenhouse-gas baseline and early transit planning; staff said a transportation plan would come “within the future,” and council did not debate it.

“As has been noted, the Official Community Plan sets out the general direction for the city over the next 5 years. It's specifically a 5-year planning document.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jul 17, 2018 · 1:31:25

Downtown land buying continued with three Hanover Street lots. Councillor John Fehr voted against each one; Deputy Mayor Cari Penner defended them as continuity between councils:

“I believe that it's up to council to have long-term vision. And sometimes long-term vision requires several different councils following the vision of the previous councils, and that is a good thing because otherwise there would be no continuity in our city planning.”

Deputy Mayor Cari Penner · Oct 16, 2018 · 0:32:03

Council objected to the RM of Hanover’s new development plan in June and withdrew the objection in October after Hanover changed its policies for the land around the city.
