Planning for the future · By term

The story by term

How did Steinbach City Council’s long-term planning change from one council to the next between December 2015 and November 2026, and what does the new council inherit?

In short

All three councils planned the same way: one project, one parcel or one objection at a time, with priorities set in closed sessions. What changed was the horizon and the pressure. The first council secured land and water. The second treated water as settled and guarded local control. The third rewrote the community plan for 25–30 years and turned to the lagoon only once its limit was in sight. The next council must fund the largest project in the city’s history.

2014–2018

Mayor Goertzen’s council

The Hanover annexation, a second water supply, a “5-year” community plan, recreation megaprojects dropped, and a warning about the lagoon.

39 items · 12 divided votes

2018–2022

Mayor Funk’s first council

The water plant financed, objections to neighbours and provincial planning bills, infill and industrial land decided case by case.

44 items · 13 divided votes

2022–2026

Mayor Funk’s second council

A call to plan 20–30 years ahead, a new community plan, the wastewater application and its rising cost, storms and a climate plan.

2026–2030: the new council

The council elected on October 28, 2026 is the first change in membership since 2018. Michael Zwaagstra, who moved and explained most long-range decisions for a decade, and Jac Siemens, the most consistent voice for plan-led land use, did not run. Ron Falk, a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years (14 as chair), and Greg Carlos, a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, are new. Susan Penner, who topped the poll, is Deputy Mayor.

What the new council inherits:

  • The wastewater project. Funding, design and financing are all open. The mayor has called the system, which he puts at about $215 million, his top priority and has said it must be built by about 2032; he expects a city share of about $72 million and has spoken of longer-term financing (SteinbachOnline, September 2026). That would break the city’s rule of repaying every loan within five years.
  • Priorities for the term. The 2022–2026 priorities were four headings set in closed sessions. The November 3, 2026 inaugural meeting set none in public.
  • The climate plan and drainage. The climate risk assessment and adaptation plan presented in August 2026 has not been adopted. A drainage master plan is listed only as a future program under review.
  • The neighbours. The objection to La Broquerie’s plan and the Hanover rezoning at the Municipal Board have no reported outcome. At the inaugural meeting the mayor named Susan Penner to the Hanover joint action committee, Greg Carlos to the La Broquerie committee and Jake Hiebert to the Eastman Regional Municipal Committee.
  • Reviews coming due. Development fees are due for review in 2027, and the community plan must be reviewed within five years of its 2025 adoption.

The new council’s inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026 dealt only with oaths and appointments.
