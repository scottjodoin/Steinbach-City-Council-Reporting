Who moves council's public-hearing motions?

Between January 2022 and September 2026, which council members moved the motions that decided public-hearing matters (variances, rezonings, conditional uses, subdivisions and the by-laws that follow a hearing)? What did each motion do, and how big was its effect?

Counts come from the official minutes of 77 meetings. Each motion links to the meeting minutes and, where a recording exists, to its moment in the meeting video. Last updated Oct 19, 2026.

Read this first. Moving a motion is how a proposal gets on the floor. In Steinbach, staff prepare the recommendation, the Mayor asks for a mover, and any member may volunteer. Moving a motion does not mean the member wrote it, proposed the project or had any interest in it, and a high or low count is not, by itself, a measure of a member's work or judgment. Almost all of these motions passed unanimously. This page is an independent summary of public records and may contain errors; see About this page.

Bottom line

  • 203 motions on public-hearing matters were moved at 77 council meetings from January 2022 to September 2026. Michael Zwaagstra moved 67 of them (33%, about one in three). Jac Siemens moved 39, Susan Penner 37, Damian Penner 28, Jake Hiebert 27 and Bill Hiebert 5.
  • Seconding evens out the picture. Bill Hiebert seconded 43 of these motions, more than any other member. Counting motions moved or seconded, the range narrows to 48–102 per member, and every member took part in at least 48.
  • The count doesn't measure support or opposition. 171 of the 203 motions carried unanimously. 22 were decided on a recorded vote, and 4 died because no one seconded them.
  • Most were mid-sized decisions. Using this page's rating, 52 were major (city-wide plans, large developments, big capital projects), 85 moderate, 53 minor (single-property variances) and 13 procedural.
  • Data caveats: 6 of the 203 motions were probably counted by mistake, because the minutes parser matched a by-law number. Taking them out gives 64, 39, 36, 27, 26 and 5, the same order. The video confirms the minutes' mover for 173 motions; for the rest the video is unclear or missing, and in no case does it contradict the minutes.

Part 1

What the counts mean

A public hearing is held before council decides certain planning matters: variances, conditional uses, rezonings, Official Community Plan changes, subdivisions, closings of roads or public reserves, and local improvement or special service plans. The City Manager introduces the item, the applicant and any residents may speak, and council then re-opens its meeting to decide. A decision may need several motions: one for each by-law reading, or a tabling first and a decision later.

Under the city's Procedures By-Law 1809, “No motion shall be debated unless the same is moved and seconded” (s. 18.2), and a by-law needs three readings, each with its own vote (s. 18.4). If the Mayor, as chair, wants to present or second a motion, he must first leave the chair (s. 19.1). The by-law does not say how a mover is chosen. In the videos the Mayor asks for a motion and a member volunteers or is recognized, for example:

“Okay, council, so we're looking for a motion to approve third reading. Councillor Susan Penner, seconded by Councillor Damon Penner. Go ahead.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Jun 17, 2025 · 0:35:38 · By-Law 2257, third reading (“Damon” is the transcript's spelling of Damian)

So the chart shows who volunteered or was recognized to move. It does not show who wrote a recommendation (staff prepare those), who supported a project or who had any interest in it. A member who moves a motion may still vote against it, and anyone may oppose it in debate.

2By impact

Each motion is rated for the size of the matter it dealt with. All readings of the same by-law get the same rating. The ratings are this page's judgment, using the rubric below; they are not the city's.

  • Major City-wide or long-term effect, or large spending: the annual financial plan, Official Community Plan changes, new neighbourhoods or large developments, and local improvement or service plans that fund major projects.
  • Moderate One block, neighbourhood or sizeable site: a single rezoning or conditional use, apartment projects, multi-lot subdivisions, road or reserve closings, variances with a noticeable neighbourhood effect.
  • Minor One property with little effect beyond it: small setback, width, area or accessory-building variances and small lot splits.
  • Procedural The motion did not decide the merits: tabling, deferring, rescinding to redo a step, a referral, or a motion that died without a seconder.

About this page

Disclaimer. This is an independent summary of public records, prepared for public information. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the City of Steinbach, is not legal or professional advice, and may contain errors. The official minutes are the authoritative record of every decision; where this page and the minutes differ, the minutes govern. Nothing here suggests that any member acted improperly. Moving a motion is a routine part of council procedure. Corrections are welcome through the site's GitHub issues page.

  • Where the counts come from. A script read the official minutes of every meeting from January 2022 to September 2026 and recorded the mover, seconder and result of each motion. It counts a motion as a “public-hearing motion” when it deals with the subject of a public hearing held at that meeting or an earlier one, or is the first motion after council re-opens from a hearing. That rule gives exactly the 203 motions and the per-member totals in the chart. The mover is the one the minutes record.
  • Known miscounts. 6 motions are flagged on their cards: three readings of By-Law 2233 (development fees, which had no hearing), two agenda adoptions that only removed a by-law item, and a first reading given before that by-law's own hearing. They stay in the chart so it matches the original. Taking them out changes no member's rank.
  • Summaries and impact ratings were drafted with AI assistance from the minutes and meeting transcripts, then checked for consistency across all readings of each by-law. They are plain-language summaries, not the official text. The impact rating is this page's judgment using the rubric in By impact, not a city classification.
  • Video links. 192 motions link to the moment, in the official YouTube recording, where the motion was put on the floor, taken from an automatic transcript. Links may land a few seconds early or late. Five meetings (Apr 5, 2022; Nov 5, 2024; Jun 3 and Jul 15, 2025; May 19, 2026) have no recording in this project's video archive, so their 11 motions have no video link. The automatic transcript often garbles names (for example “Hebert” for Hiebert), and when the chair says only “Councillor Hiebert” or “Councillor Penner”, the video cannot tell the two members with that surname apart. For 173 motions the video is consistent with the minutes' mover; for 19 more the video doesn't settle who moved, and 11 have no video, and it never shows a different mover. In a few motions the seconder heard on video differs from the minutes; the cards note these, and the minutes are used.
  • Results. The minutes print no result for four motions. Three were declared carried on video; the fourth has no recording. Recorded votes list the members voting against as printed in the minutes.
  • Minutes links go to the city's own copy (events.steinbach.ca) where it has one, otherwise to the Internet Archive's copy of the city's former website. Each link opens at the page that records the motion.
  • Names. Council members, the Mayor and city staff are named in full. Residents and applicants who are private individuals appear only by initials. Businesses and organizations are named. The city's name is shown correctly as “Steinbach” wherever the automatic transcript misspells it.
  • Coverage. Regular council meetings from January 4, 2022 to September 15, 2026. Mayor Earl Funk chaired these meetings and moved none of the counted motions.
