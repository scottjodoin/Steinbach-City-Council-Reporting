Residents' election forum: what residents asked, and how four candidates answered

At a forum built around residents' round-table discussions, what did Steinbach residents ask about housing, health, transportation and quality of life, and what did the four council candidates who attended say?

Questions are as presented by the facilitators and published by SteinbachOnline; every answer links to its moment in SteinbachOnline's topic videos. The full transcript is on its own page. For all 10 candidates, see the Chamber of Commerce forum. Election day is Oct 28, 2026. Last updated Oct 16, 2026.

Bottom line

  • Four of the 10 candidates took part: Gary Dyck, Nicholas Goertzen, Mitesh Kumar and Brad Unger. All 10 were invited; none of the four incumbent councillors attended.
  • The questions came from residents. Residents met at four topic tables, and facilitators turned what they heard into questions for the candidates, who had two minutes per topic.
  • All four said yes to public transit, and Gary Dyck and Mitesh Kumar want the city to act on e-scooters now rather than wait for provincial rules.
  • On homelessness, three backed the Inn's shelter plan (Gary Dyck, Nicholas Goertzen, Brad Unger), and Mitesh Kumar said the city is “a little bit late”. Brad Unger and Mitesh Kumar would keep multiplexes and high density out of established neighbourhoods.
  • On health, all four focused on advocacy and community: doctor recruitment, primary care clinics, nurse practitioners, volunteers in the ER, and seniors.

How the forum worked

The forum was hosted by the Southeast Equity Coalition and Strong Towns Steinbach. It began with round-table discussions on four topics; residents joined the table they felt most connected to. Facilitators then summarized the key themes and put residents' questions to the candidates, who had two minutes to respond to each topic's questions. SteinbachOnline published one video per topic.

How to read this page: under each topic, the four candidates are listed in the same alphabetical order. Each has a one-line summary; open Full answer to read everything they said, with key points in bold.

Housing and development

Residents' questions
  1. What is the City's plan to increase low-barrier, transitional, and affordable housing, while addressing the root causes of homelessness and ensuring people experiencing homelessness are treated with dignity?
  2. As our community grows and starter homes are increasingly replaced by multiplexes and higher density developments, what is the city's plan to ensure we have a healthy mix of affordable single-family homes, apartments, and higher-density housing?
  3. How is the City planning for the infrastructure and services needed to support increased density, including sewer capacity, parking, sidewalks, trees, parks, schools, and walkable amenities, and how do we ensure the costs of growth are fairly shared between developers and residents?
