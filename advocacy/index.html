Advocating to Steinbach City Council: what has worked, and what hasn't

When residents and community groups asked Steinbach City Council for something between December 2015 and October 2026, what did they get, and why? How did that change from one council to the next, and how did each council member respond?

Written for residents and advocacy groups. Start with the summary, then choose what to read. Last updated Nov 6, 2026.

Executive summary

Over eleven years and three councils, residents and community groups brought at least 164 requests to Steinbach City Council. The pattern has been remarkably steady.

  • Free asks win; money asks disappear. Letters of support, permission to apply for outside grants and tax receipts won 11 times out of 14 and were never refused. Requests for money mostly went to closed budget meetings: 32 of 57 never got a public answer.
  • Residents rarely win on their own. People writing in by themselves won outright 4 times in 46. The same concerns did better when a council member carried them, when the neighbours affected signed together, or when a group arrived with a plan already worked out with city staff.
  • The engineers’ report usually decides. Every resident request for a stop sign was refused or swapped for something else after a traffic review. Council did overrule its staff, for neighbours who agreed together, for schools, a church and a developer.
  • Follow-up is where requests die. At least 16 requests were sent to staff for a report that never came back in public, and a promised answer to a cancer-support group never came.
  • What changed: more is now decided in public. Each council left fewer requests without a public answer (51%, then 40%, then 35%). Most votes were unanimous; the two members who most often ended up on opposite sides, Jac Siemens and Michael Zwaagstra, both left council in 2026.

The story in three councils

Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council ran a tight, rule-bound process. Mayor Earl Funk’s councils, with the same six councillors for eight years, kept the routine for small requests but said yes to big partnerships and, over time, gave plainer answers in public. The council elected in October 2026 is the first change in membership since 2018.

2014–2018

Mayor Goertzen’s council

Business requests did best; residents’ drainage and sidewalk letters went to staff reports; the biggest request, the golf club’s $500,000, was settled in private.

55 requests · 63% of those decided won or partly won

2018–2022

Mayor Funk’s first council

Big partnerships when groups brought money and a plan; residents learned to use formal objections; COVID-19 closed the door to delegations for months.

57 requests · 74%

2022–2026

Mayor Funk’s second council

Same council, plainer answers: cheap asks passed the same night, traffic asks went by the engineers’ rules, and flooded residents changed the capital budget.

About this report

Quotes are verbatim from automatic transcripts and link to the meeting videos. Council members, the mayor and staff are named; residents are identified by initials. The analysis and plain-language summaries are ours.

Methods, coverage and what we couldn’t confirm
  • Transcripts. Meeting quotes come from automatic transcripts of the official videos and are left as transcribed, including repeated words and false starts. The one exception is the city's name, which the transcription often misspells and which quotes show correctly as Steinbach. Ellipses mark trimmed text; no sentence breaks were added. Each quote links to its moment in the video; check there before quoting elsewhere.
  • Speakers and names. Speakers were matched to names by hand from how they are addressed in each meeting, and every quote here is from a speaker identified with high confidence. Council members, the mayor and city staff are named in full. Residents, delegations and other members of the public, including people presenting for organizations, are identified only by initials. Organizations and businesses are named.
  • Coverage. Every regular council meeting from December 15, 2015 to November 3, 2026 with a recording or minutes was searched: transcripts of 243 meeting videos and minutes from January 2018 on (the city's older minutes are not online). Not covered: the first four 2015 videos (September to December 1, 2015, not yet transcribed); four videos that failed to download (April 4 and October 17, 2017; September 15, 2020; July 6, 2021), where the minutes were used if they exist; and three meetings with no minutes online (July 17, 2018; May 19 and November 17, 2020), where the video was used. Committee and study sessions are closed and unrecorded, so funding outcomes decided there are mostly unknown; where we could not find an outcome, the tables say “No answer.” Classifying a result as won, partly, no answer or lost is our judgment, and the reasons are recorded for each request in the data.
  • What counts as a request. 164 requests from residents, community groups, businesses and institutions such as the school division, including those a council member raised for residents. Not counted: council's own initiatives, requests from other governments and municipal associations, routine agency reports, and public-hearing objections to planning applications (see Public notice). Several routine funding letters from the same year are grouped into one row, so counts are of requests, not letters. Funding letters that were only “received as information” count as “No answer”; other letters filed without action count as “Lost” only where council clearly declined.
  • Speakers before 2022. Meetings from January 2022 have speaker maps checked by hand. For earlier meetings, speakers were identified from how they were addressed and from the minutes; only speakers identified with high confidence are quoted by name. In 2015–2017 the two Councillors Penner often could not be told apart, and their remarks are not attributed unless certain.
  • Data. The data downloads list every request, every council member's part in it, and every quote with its timestamp and link. Quotes in the downloads come only from council members and staff identified with high confidence, and leave out any that name a member of the public. Every quote was checked word for word against its transcript.
  • Not confirmed. Whether the Eastman Raiders, Steinbach Minor Hockey or Southeast Cancer Support Services were funded in a budget; whether the Southwood school-zone by-law amendment has been passed; and which neighbourhood the mayor meant by the delegation that “asked for a path.” The safety survey count (827 adults and 33 youth) is from the published plan; at the March 3, 2026 meeting the mayor recalled it as 872. For 2015–2017, where there are no minutes, decisions come from the recordings and most were made by consensus without a counted vote; the December 5, 2017 golf-club vote is inferred from members' stated positions. Later results not traced: whether the 2021 request for red-light camera authority was granted, and whether the Legion got space in the event centre.
  • Peers and law. Peer practices come from each city's own website and by-laws, checked November 6, 2026. Morden's website blocks automated reading, and some Winkler details (its procedure by-law and petition rules) could not be found online. The 300-person objection threshold is from S.M. 2025, c. 3.
  • Council change. The council elected in October 2026 held its inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026. Quotes are from members serving at the time, some of whom are no longer on council. Council membership by term is from the minutes' attendance lists and the inaugural minutes of 2018 and the 2026 oaths of office; the 2026 election results are from SteinbachOnline (unofficial counts). Process rules may change with a new procedure by-law.
