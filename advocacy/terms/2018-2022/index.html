Advocacy report · By term

2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk’s first council

What happened when residents and groups asked Steinbach City Council for something during the 2018–2022 term, and how did it differ from the terms before and after?

In short

  • Big yeses for groups with money and a plan. The event centre came with a $15 million private pledge and an agreement drafted with staff, and got $10 million that night (2019); the economic development office got matching money for five years (2021).
  • Residents learned to push back formally. Clearspring Greens neighbours used formal objections to cut their share of a pathway’s cost, and it was built in 2021.
  • COVID-19 closed the door. Only four delegations in all of 2020, none from mid-March to September.
  • Volume moved council. When street-racing complaints piled up, the mayor put them on the agenda and council asked for a dedicated traffic officer the same night (2021).
  • Small funding letters were still filed without discussion, and decided, if at all, in closed budget sessions.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

Mayor Funk's first council kept the routine for funding letters (“received as information”) but took on large partnerships when a group brought money and a plan. The Southeast Event Centre group arrived in October 2019 with an agreement already drafted with staff and a $15 million private pledge, and council committed that night:

“I want you to know that tonight's resolution is to commit $10 million plus $500,000 a year.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Oct 1, 2019 · 0:51:37

In 2021 council agreed to match private money for a Chamber of Commerce economic development office, up to $65,000 a year for five years. (That is the grant council ended in 2025 for lack of results.)

Residents learned to use formal objections. The Clearspring Greens pathway, first asked for in 2018, was put off in 2019 (“Our 2019 budget did not allow us to do anything in that area at all”) and then proposed as a local improvement charged mostly to the neighbourhood. After about 20 residents spoke against the levy in January 2020, council moved the $208,000 lighting cost to the city within two weeks:

“I think, um, um, there's nothing wrong with changing your mind after you've heard new information, and that's what we heard as we met with, with the community, the neighborhood.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Jan 21, 2020 · 0:18:21

When 78 owners still objected, Councillor Susan Penner rebuilt the plan as a 50/50 split capped at $1,610 a lot, and it went ahead in 2021:

“While the initial proposal had 78 objections, this new plan has only received 6 objections, which shows how far the city and the neighbourhood have come in working together and coming to an agreement that we both can support.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Feb 16, 2021 · 0:16:15

COVID-19 narrowed the routes in: there were four delegations in all of 2020 and none between mid-March and September. Public hearings stayed in person and became the main place residents spoke. In 2021, when letters about street racing piled up, the mayor put a sample on the agenda and council asked the same night for a dedicated RCMP traffic officer, at about $200,000 a year:

“I have been on some weeks bombarded with emails and phone calls and just visits to my store about the racing issue.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Sept 7, 2021 · 1:10:15
