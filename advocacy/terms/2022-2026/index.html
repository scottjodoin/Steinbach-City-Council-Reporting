Advocacy report · By term

2022–2026: Mayor Earl Funk’s second council

What happened when residents and groups asked Steinbach City Council for something during the 2022–2026 term, and how did it differ from the terms before and after?

In short

  • More decided in public. Only 35% of requests got no public answer, the lowest of the three terms.
  • Cheap asks passed the same night: grant consents, letters of support, tax receipts, patios.
  • Traffic asks went by the engineers’ rules. Every resident stop-sign request was refused or swapped for something else; a councillor’s motion against a consultant’s report lost 3–4.
  • Plainer no’s. Council declined the RCMP Musical Ride, a mobile outreach partnership, the economic development grant’s renewal and a cannabis plebiscite.
  • Residents changed the capital budget. The Georgetown flooding delegation got a sewer reroute within five months, though not the independent study or compensation it also asked for.

Council: The same seven as 2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

The same council, with more decided on the record: 35% of requests got no public answer, against 51% in 2014–2018. Requests that cost nothing passed the same night (the Skimmers' grant consents, the Pistons' arena glass, the golf club's tax receipts, the 55+ Games bid). Traffic requests were judged against the warrant guideline, and a councillor's motion against a consultant's report lost 3–4 (Loewen and Hespeler, 2024). Council also said no more plainly than before: it declined to host the RCMP Musical Ride without a local organizer (2023), declined to apply for federal money for Steinbach Community Outreach's mobile outreach unit after a staff memo said social services are outside the city's role (2024), ended the economic development grant (2025) and refused a cannabis-retail plebiscite 6–1 (2025). Requests to the province ended in letters (crosswalk lights, disaster aid).

The term's biggest change came from residents: the Georgetown sewer delegation (see the timeline). It ended with a $400,000 operating loan to the event centre group, an endorsement of The Bridge on Third supportive housing, and a mayor's task force on seniors placed outside Steinbach while they wait for a care-home bed, an issue Councillor Susan Penner raised after hearing from residents:

“dozens and dozens of people have shared their stories with me in our community, either through emails, through phone calls, through direct messages.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Oct 6, 2026 · 1:42:33

What changes in 2026–2030

Context: 2026–2030, what changes for advocates

The council elected on October 28, 2026 was sworn in on November 3. Mayor Earl Funk was acclaimed for a third term; Susan Penner (now Deputy Mayor), Jake Hiebert, Damian Penner and Bill Hiebert return. Michael Zwaagstra and Jac Siemens did not run. Apart from the mayors, they took part in more of the requests in this record than anyone else, and in the recorded split votes they were usually on opposite sides. The new councillors, Ron Falk (a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years) and Greg Carlos (a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association), have no record on requests yet. The routes, deadlines and budget calendar stay the same unless the new council changes the procedure by-law.
