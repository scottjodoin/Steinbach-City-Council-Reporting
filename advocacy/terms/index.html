Advocacy report · By term

The story by term

How did council’s response to requests from residents and groups change from one council to the next, and from year to year?

In short

Three councils, one steady pattern: cheap asks pass, money asks go behind closed doors, and staff reports decide traffic and drainage. What changed was mostly how much was decided in public. Under Mayor Goertzen (2014–2018) half of all requests have no public answer on the record; under Mayor Funk’s councils that fell to 40% and then 35%, and council started saying a plain no more often. In between, COVID-19 shut delegations out for most of 2020.

The record covers parts of three councils. Mayor Chris Goertzen's council (2014–2018) is covered from December 2015: the city's videos begin in September 2015, but the first few have not been transcribed yet. Mayor Earl Funk's first council (2018–2022) and his second (2022–2026) had the same six councillors: all were re-elected in 2022, so the October 2026 election brought the first change in membership in eight years.

2026–2030: the new council

Context: 2026–2030, what changes for advocates

The council elected on October 28, 2026 was sworn in on November 3. Mayor Earl Funk was acclaimed for a third term; Susan Penner (now Deputy Mayor), Jake Hiebert, Damian Penner and Bill Hiebert return. Michael Zwaagstra and Jac Siemens did not run. Apart from the mayors, they took part in more of the requests in this record than anyone else, and in the recorded split votes they were usually on opposite sides. The new councillors, Ron Falk (a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years) and Greg Carlos (a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association), have no record on requests yet. The routes, deadlines and budget calendar stay the same unless the new council changes the procedure by-law.

  • 2015–2016. The Chamber of Commerce's persistence on sign rules pays off; a developer's drainage exception passes over staff's objection; residents' drainage and sidewalk letters go to staff reports. (2015 is one meeting, December 15.)
  • 2017. The golf club's refused $500,000 grant returns as a land purchase. Home Street North residents bring a petition; ten months later the fix is put at $500,000 or more and nothing is done beyond extra maintenance. Small traffic fixes with staff support pass within weeks.
  • 2018. The Arts Council match is tabled, then passed; Sunday shopping plebiscite defeated 2–5; Calvary Chapel gets 52 public parking stalls against the city manager's advice. New council sworn in on November 6.
  • 2019. The event centre partnership is approved the night it is presented; a developer's $10,000-a-lot demolition grant is refused unanimously; Clearspring Greens residents fill the gallery and are told the budget did not allow it.
  • 2020. COVID-19: four delegations all year. The Clearspring Greens levy fight moves the lighting cost to the city; a charity's request to be spared the Acres Drive levy is refused 5–1.
  • 2021. Clearspring Greens pathway goes ahead after 78 objections force a redesign; residents' street-racing letters lead to a request for a traffic officer; the economic development office is funded; council backs one affordable-housing proposal for provincial money and not two others.
  • 2022. Cottonwood neighbours' petition beats staff's advice; the golf club's tax receipts are approved; a school police officer is approved only if the school division pays all of it.
  • 2023. Councillor Jac Siemens relays residents' complaints about highway signal timing and the province fixes them by May; the RCMP Musical Ride is declined; the Chapel Drive parking plan passes the night it arrives.
  • 2024. Loewen and Hespeler stop defeated 3–4; council takes no action on Steinbach Community Outreach's mobile outreach request; Southeast Cancer Support Services is promised an answer that never appears.
  • 2025. The Georgetown delegation changes the 2026 capital budget; the economic development grant ends; the cannabis plebiscite is refused 6–1.
  • 2026 (to November 3). Event centre loan; The Bridge on Third endorsed; seniors' task force created; Parkhill School's traffic request is referred to staff with no report by the end of the term.
