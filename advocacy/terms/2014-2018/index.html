Advocacy report · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

What happened when residents and groups asked Steinbach City Council for something during the 2014–2018 term, and how did it differ from the terms before and after?

In short

  • Business asks did best. The Chamber of Commerce rewrote the sign rules through steady lobbying, and a developer got an exception from drainage standards over staff’s objection.
  • Residents got staff reports. Letters about drainage, sidewalks and speeding usually went to staff for a report; many of those reports never came back in public.
  • A narrow grants policy. Requests for operating money, like the Pat Porter seniors’ centre’s, were turned down; most funding letters were simply filed.
  • The biggest decision was made in private. The golf club’s $500,000 grant request was refused in a closed meeting and came back as a land purchase for the same amount.
  • Half the requests have no public answer on the record (28 of 55), partly because there are no minutes online before 2018.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr. Chris Goertzen’s record.

What happened

Business requests did best. The Chamber of Commerce rewrote the mobile-sign rules with a delegation, written submissions and drawings of real storefronts (2016), and a commercial developer won an exception from storm-sewer standards despite the city manager's warning about precedent (2016). Residents' drainage and sidewalk letters mostly ended in a staff report or “the policy stands”: Home Street North, Albert Street, Old Tom Road. Grants followed a “simplified and focused” policy, and the Pat Porter Active Living Centre's request for a grant in lieu of taxes was left out of the 2018 budget:

“the reality is that there hasn't been a change in the policy of the grants of the city, and so that will not be included in this budget.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen, at the 2018 financial plan hearing · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:16:26

The largest request of the term was decided out of public view. The Steinbach Fly-In Golf Club asked for a $500,000 grant in a closed meeting; it was refused, and came back as a purchase of 40 acres of the club's land for the same amount, leased back to the club for $1 a year. The first public discussion was the night of the vote:

“That's defeated 4-3, and so here we are. … It is a bailout that is dressed up as a land deal.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on his closed-meeting motion to have the club present in public · Dec 5, 2017 · 1:09:41

“The reality is, is that we have a win-win scenario here.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Dec 5, 2017 · 1:21:30

Council did change its mind when a group came back better prepared. The Arts Council's $65,000 matching grant for repairs to the city-owned arts building was tabled 4–3 in June 2018, with one councillor calling it “reactionary,” and passed unanimously in September after the Arts Council presented a costed plan. A resident's request for a plebiscite on Sunday shopping was defeated 2–5.
