Advocacy report · Council member

Michael Zwaagstra: what they said and did when people asked council for something

What did Michael Zwaagstra say and do when residents and groups brought requests to Steinbach City Council between December 2015 and October 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Michael Zwaagstra took part in more requests than any member except the mayors, and his approach held for eleven years. He asked what a request would cost, who else would pay and what the experts said, and he spelled out the tests council applied: “neighbourhood consensus” for parking changes, the engineers’ warrant for stop signs. Requests that passed those tests often had him as the mover.

He was not simply a no. He moved many same-night approvals, pushed the Southwood school zone further than staff recommended, turned residents’ street-racing letters into a motion for a traffic officer (2021) and fought to keep a public walkway open. But he also led the case against several asks: the golf-club land deal, a developer’s grant, shifting the Clearspring Greens cost onto all taxpayers and the Loewen–Hespeler stop.

“And frankly, we're in the business of building sidewalks. We want more sidewalks, and I would have concerns about removing a pathway that is an access point for people, not just in the neighborhood but even beyond.”

On neighbours’ request to close the Cottonwood Bend walkway · Jul 17, 2018 · 1:54:08

Term by term

2014–2018

Pro-business on sign rules but against a developer’s drainage exception as a precedent (2016). He led the opposition to buying the golf club’s land, calling it “a bailout that is dressed up as a land deal,” after his motion to hear the club in public was defeated in a closed meeting (2017). He wanted the Arts Council’s request tabled, then supported it once his questions were answered (2018).

Took part in 27 of the requests in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

The most active member on requests. He moved the event centre agreement (2019), refused a developer’s demolition grant as a cost taxpayers should not carry, sent a resident’s pool-accessibility letter to staff, argued against shifting the Clearspring Greens cost onto everyone and then moved the lighting compromise (2020), moved the enVision charity’s levy (2020), and turned street-racing complaints into a request for an RCMP traffic officer (2021).

Took part in 30 of the requests in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He set the bar for neighbourhood requests (“pretty clear there’s a neighbourhood consensus,” 2023) and defended engineering reports (“the reason that we hire experts,” 2024), voting against the Loewen–Hespeler stop. He moved most of the cheap same-night approvals (Chapel Drive, the crosswalk letter, Harv’s Air), the Southwood school zone beyond staff’s advice, and the refusals of the RCMP Musical Ride and the cannabis plebiscite. In 2026 he was the strongest voice for pressing the province on disaster aid for flooded residents.

Took part in 22 of the requests in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Remarkably steady across three terms. His departure in 2026 means council loses the member who most often explained, on the record, why a request did or didn’t meet the test.
