Advocacy report · Council member

Susan Penner: what they said and did when people asked council for something

What did Susan Penner say and do when residents and groups brought requests to Steinbach City Council between December 2015 and October 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

In short

Susan Penner is the member most often found carrying a request from start to finish. She moved the Arts Council’s matching grant twice until it passed (2018), gave Calvary Chapel the use of the city’s parking stalls (2018), rebuilt the Clearspring Greens pathway plan after talking with the residents who had objected (2021), and in 2026 raised the issue of seniors sent out of town while they wait for a care-home bed, after “dozens and dozens of people” contacted her.

She also holds to rules and evidence when she thinks they apply. She seconded the enVision levy (“We also have to do what our bylaws call us to do,” 2020) and voted against the Loewen–Hespeler stop, warning against deciding on “one-off personal experiences” (2024). Yet she was the only vote for a stop sign at Wyndham Estate Drive because it was by a school (2023).

“I've talked to many Clear Spring Greens residents that objected to the current plan, and they say they would be in support of this revised plan.”

Moving the revised Clearspring Greens plan · Jan 5, 2021 · 0:35:38

Term by term

2014–2018

Several of her remarks in 2015–2017 can’t be told apart from Cari Penner’s in the recordings. In 2018 she was the main champion of the Arts Council’s request and moved the parking stalls for Calvary Chapel; she voted against the Sunday-shopping plebiscite.

Took part in 13 of the requests in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

During the Clearspring Greens levy fight she asked practical questions (2020), then designed the compromise that got the pathway built: a 50/50 split capped at $1,610 a lot. As she put it, the first plan had 78 objections and the new one “only received 6” (2021). She also moved the Chicken Chef patio and backed Steinbach Community Outreach’s housing project (2021).

Took part in 16 of the requests in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

She moved many same-night approvals (a patio, the Arts Council resolution, the 55+ Games bid, the farmers’ market) and the endorsement of The Bridge on Third. Her own home flooded in 2025; she opened the September 2025 meeting with it and moved the first resolution to the province on disaster aid (2026). She was the lone vote for the Wyndham stop sign (2023), voted against the Loewen–Hespeler stop (2024), and moved the seniors’ task force, which she will chair (2026).

Took part in 14 of the requests in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Her role grew over the three terms, from supporting groups’ requests to designing the compromises that settled them. She is Deputy Mayor for 2026–2030.
