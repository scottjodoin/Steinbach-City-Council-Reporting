Advocacy report · Council member

Jac Siemens: what they said and did when people asked council for something

What did Jac Siemens say and do when residents and groups brought requests to Steinbach City Council between December 2015 and October 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

When council divided on a request, Jac Siemens was more often than anyone on the side of the people asking: for a Sunday-shopping plebiscite (2018), for a cheaper Clearspring Greens pathway (2020), against charging a charity the Acres Drive levy (2020), for the Loewen–Hespeler stop he moved for residents (2024) and against refusing a cannabis plebiscite (2025). He also brought residents’ complaints in and kept asking until something happened, as with traffic-light timing on Highway 12 (2023).

He was not an automatic yes. He voted to table the Arts Council’s request (2018), opposed dropping Calvary Chapel’s conditions (2018), voted against the city paying for the Clearspring Greens lighting (2020, with no reason given on the record), asked “Do we have a history?” of a seniors’ group’s request (2024) and argued for ending the economic development grant (2025).

“But this is the first time that we're asking residences from any area to pay the majority of the cost of a pathway to connect them to our community.”

On the Clearspring Greens levy · Jan 7, 2020 · 1:39:10

Term by term

2014–2018

He moved a developer’s drainage exception as a “one-off” (2016), opposed year-long mobile-sign permits, asked for a report on a resident’s Albert Street sidewalk request (2016), seconded and defended the golf-club land purchase (2017) and moved the Sunday-shopping plebiscite (2018), which lost 2–5.

Took part in 23 of the requests in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

He took the residents’ side in both levy fights of 2020: he proposed a cheaper Clearspring Greens pathway and cast the only vote against charging the enVision charity. He got STARS’s request onto the fall budget session (2020) and called the Clearspring Greens pathway “a safety issue” when it finally went ahead (2021).

Took part in 17 of the requests in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He moved the Cottonwood parking ban against staff’s advice (2022), relayed residents’ complaints about highway signal timing until the province fixed them (2023), moved the Loewen–Hespeler stop (lost 3–4, 2024), rescued a Baseball Hall of Fame request from being filed (2023) and pressed for the library’s security needs when it was left off a grant list (2026). He also argued for ending the economic development grant for lack of results (2025) and questioned how far the city should go on social issues (2024).

Took part in 17 of the requests in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

The pattern held across all three terms: willing to break with the majority for a resident’s request, especially on cost-sharing and plebiscites, while cautious about grants and precedent.
