Advocacy report · What works

What works, and what doesn’t

Which routes into council, which kinds of evidence and which timing moved decisions, and why did council say no?

In short

The same lessons hold across all three councils:

  • Ask for something that costs the city little, in words council can approve that night: a letter, a consent, a tax receipt.
  • Show that the people affected agree, and work it out with city staff before it reaches council.
  • Bring evidence that fits the test council will apply: the traffic warrant, the policy, the budget calendar.
  • Follow up. Referrals and promised answers often go nowhere unless someone asks again.

Most examples below are from 2022–2026, where we could identify speakers most reliably; the same patterns appear in earlier terms (see By term).

The routes into council, and what each one usually gets

Council meets on the first and third Tuesday of the month at 7:30 p.m. Its 2006 procedure by-law (By-law 1809) sets the formal routes in. Items for the agenda must reach the City Secretary “at least 120 hours prior” to the meeting (s. 8.2), which means by the Thursday before. Delegations are recommended to register 120 hours ahead and say what they will cover (s. 14.2), and the chair “may require the delegation to appoint a spokesperson” (s. 14.1). In practice delegations are scheduled for 7:45 p.m., and the mayor described the usual time limit:

“Well, normally something like this, we give— we allow 10 minutes for a delegation like this.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Jan 4, 2022 · 0:18:09

Turning up without booking does not work. When flooded residents filled the gallery in September 2025, the mayor explained the route:

“I'm very sorry for the short notice tonight. We can't have you speak, but it's great you're here… We've heard you. And we do welcome you to set a date and come as delegation. And if you could pick one person to do all the speaking at that delegation, it would be great.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Sept 16, 2025 · 0:38:25
Context

By-law 1809 does allow more than this. A member of the public may ask to speak on a subject not on the agenda for “generally 5 minutes,” at the chair's discretion, and the chair may then place it on a future agenda or refer it to the City Manager (s. 8.4(5)). The “Council member question period” is described as “Intended to be an open session, allowing questions to the Mayor or any member of Council” (s. 8.4(9)). In the recordings neither is offered to the public. We found no delegation request form or booking page on the city's website; the City Clerk's office is the contact.

What worked

1Ask for something that costs the city little or nothing

Letters of support, resolutions that let a group apply for provincial money, and arrangements that use the city's charitable status passed with almost no debate. Council members said why:

“…looking at getting grant funding for reinvestment into our facilities, I think that's a great opportunity to, to partner with that club.”

Councillor Damian Penner, on the Steinbach Skimmers' request · Oct 4, 2022 · 0:41:10

“We're not asking for a grant. We're not asking for any money out of pocket.”

D.B., Steinbach Fly-In Golf Club board · Mar 15, 2022 · 0:22:13

“I love it that they've come and asked this for this publicly”

Mayor Earl Funk, on the golf club's 2026 bridge request · Jul 7, 2026 · 0:56:41

A useful sequence: ask for the free thing first and the money later. The Pat Porter Active Living Centre asked only for a letter of support for its 55+ Games bid and said it would return about costs. It got a unanimous resolution, with the City Manager suggesting a formal resolution on top of the letter:

“So today I'm here for 2 reasons. First of all, to ask for a letter of support to include with our application… If we are selected though, we will come back at a later time and have that conversation”

A.H., Executive Director, Pat Porter Active Living Centre · Feb 20, 2024 · 0:14:20
Context

The second step did not succeed. When Serving Seniors later wrote asking for $10,000 toward facility rentals, Councillor Jac Siemens asked “Do we have a history?” and the City Manager answered that at the last event “there was no additional funding awarded by council” (Nov 19, 2024). The letter was received as information.

2Show that the people affected agree

For street rules, council asks whether a request comes from one person or the neighbourhood. When a resident wrote about parking on Henry Street in 2023:

“…normally with that sort of thing, we would only really consider that if there was pretty clear there's a neighbourhood consensus that this is what they want.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · May 16, 2023 · 0:27:33

On Cottonwood Drive in 2022, residents wrote twice and then sent a petition signed by 12 neighbours, the people who would lose weekday parking. The RCMP advisory group backed them. Council approved the restriction unanimously, against administration's advice:

“And I think by listening to the residents who live there 24 hours a day, that number one, it shows that city council listens to their concerns, and they do have valid concerns, I believe.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, who noted that administration recommended against it · Aug 2, 2022 · 0:44:44

“…significant number of neighbors have signed this request, and so they are aware that by restricting parking from 8 to 5, that means you can't park on that street.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Aug 2, 2022 · 0:47:29
Context

A petition worked here because it came from the people who would bear the cost of the change, on a matter council controls. Petitions against planning applications have done less well: the city's hearing notice says petitions “will not be considered a formal objection,” and a “quite lengthy petition” against 48 units on McKenzie Avenue did not stop an application that needed no variances (Apr 7, 2026). See Public notice.

3Work it out with staff, and the RCMP, before it reaches council

Requests that arrived already designed with the people who would carry them out were approved on the night. Hanover School Division's Chapel Drive parking plan in 2023:

“I appreciate the due diligence that HSD did to, to come up with this plan and the consultation with the city as well as RCMP to come up with the request.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Oct 3, 2023 · 0:29:16

Southwood School's principal sent a detailed letter that offered options, including “another safe crossing treatment as recommended by the city.” Council went further than staff and created a reduced-speed school zone:

“They've obviously spent a lot of time examining safety around their school.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Apr 15, 2025 · 0:44:35

“I think when we factor in that we are receiving an official request from the school We know that the student population is increasing, it's not decreasing…”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving the school zone · Aug 5, 2025 · 0:17:00

The opposite also happened. When the same school division announced a cost-shared school police officer in the press before talking to the city:

“I am disappointed that this letter was out in the press before we had an opportunity to sit down with the Hanover School Division to speak to this issue, whether through our police advisory or through City Council.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Jul 5, 2022 · 0:29:46

4Use council's own priorities, and its calendar

Council sets priorities for each term and builds its budget in closed study sessions in July and November. Groups that quoted the priorities back, or arrived just before a budget session, were received best:

“But what I like about this is how it fits in with our priorities that we've set for this term.”

Mayor Earl Funk, on the Steinbach Pistons' arena-glass donation · May 2, 2023 · 0:46:09

“It's very timely that you've come now before our study session in November.”

Mayor Earl Funk, to STARS · Oct 21, 2025 · 0:11:27

“Thank you for doing your homework and having all these numbers for us. That's very good. And I think what we'll do is we will refer this to our study session for July. That's where we build our budget for 2026, and then it'll be finalized in November.”

Mayor Earl Funk, to the Eastman Raiders · May 6, 2025 · 0:37:53

STARS shows the model for recurring funding: a formula (a dollar per resident), a visit every year with Steinbach-specific numbers, thanks for past support, and no surprise asks.

“…maybe we don't have many questions because you've been here a few times now, and I think we've got a great working relationship with you and an understanding of what you do.”

Mayor Earl Funk, to STARS · Sept 3, 2024 · 0:15:49

5A focused delegation, with a specific explanation, can move the capital budget

After being turned away in September 2025, flooded residents of the Georgetown and Golfview area returned on Oct 21 with one spokesperson, M.R. She brought a door-to-door survey, a history of the sewer connections added upstream, and a specific theory: that a low-pressure line from Clear Spring Greens was overloading their sewer. It was two weeks before the November budget session. Five months later the City Manager explained where the rerouting of that line in the 2026 budget came from:

“…that was an initiative that was entirely council-initiated. It wasn't due to any particular study or analysis other than council in its deliberations, as far as what was communicated to administration, heard and understood the concerns of the ratepayers of the neighborhood.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager, on the Clear Spring Greens line reroute · Mar 17, 2026 · 0:34:25

“…but in government terms, this is lightning speed as far as how quickly this operates”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Mar 17, 2026 · 0:44:52
Context

The residents also had an ally at the table: Councillor Susan Penner, whose own home flooded, opened the September 2025 meeting with her account. The delegation's other requests (compensation and an independent study) were not met. See the timeline and Sewer backups.

6Persistence and relationships

Repeat, polite contact paid off. B.B. emailed the province and the mayor about flashing crosswalk lights for three years before the mayor invited him to present, and council sent his request to the province unanimously. A councillor recalled how the city's library began:

“Over the next 2 years, they made several presentations to the town council and they built some support.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, on the Friends of the Library (1968–71) · Feb 18, 2025 · 0:00:10

“…a community came to us and asked, I asked for a delegation and asked for a path, and then we worked on it. That worked in that situation, but in this situation, I think it's going to work way better to be proactive instead of reactive.”

Mayor Earl Funk, on how a regional pathway was built · May 6, 2025 · 1:06:52

What didn't

1Funding letters on the agenda

Letters asking for a grant were almost never approved at a public meeting. The exceptions, over eleven years, were small or long negotiated: $300 for KidSport left to the mayor's discretion (2019), the Chamber of Commerce's economic development office after about 18 months of talks with the mayor and a councillor (2021), and an advertisement in a Baseball Hall of Fame banquet program (2023). The chair's script was nearly always the same:

“…This is also grant funding request. It's on page 54. Council, how would you like to proceed? Also take his information?”

Mayor Earl Funk, on Steinbach & Area Animal Rescue's request · Nov 15, 2022 · 0:41:43

“…put it on our study session so that it can be discussed better, like at length with our budget in front of us.”

Mayor Earl Funk, on Steinbach Minor Hockey's request · Oct 3, 2023 · 0:26:11
Context

“Received as information” is not always a no. Some groups were funded in the budget, which council adopts in one vote after a public hearing each spring; the city gives about $1.1 million a year in grants (see Taxes and spending). But a group that writes in can't tell from the minutes whether it was considered, or why it was refused.

2A request without a defined ask or cost

“Like, what would be the compelling reason for us to choose to do that?”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, to Landmark Planning on a regional water co-op that came with no costs · Jan 4, 2022 · 0:38:10

“…what, what I'll need to do is meet with you and maybe you can write a letter explaining what that's supposed to look like.”

Mayor Earl Funk, to KodaGo's founder, who asked for a “partnership” · Aug 4, 2026 · 1:13:43

Council's questions to the Eastman Raiders were about whether the number would hold:

“Yeah, because the challenge is once we do set budget, our budget is fixed.”

Mayor Earl Funk · May 6, 2025 · 0:34:55

3Stories against an engineering report

Traffic devices are judged against the city's warrant guideline, and council majorities defer to it:

“The reason that we hire experts to do this kind of analysis using standards and in evaluating the overall situation is so that we don't end up with the law of unintended consequences.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on the Loewen–Hespeler three-way stop · Apr 16, 2024 · 0:34:51

“We can't base it on our one-off personal experiences because then we're going to make very poor decisions.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Apr 16, 2024 · 0:38:38

“Well, the numbers don't warrant it, but at the same time, it would make sense to have one 'cause it's by a school.”

Councillor Susan Penner, the one vote for a stop at Wyndham Estate Drive · Jun 20, 2023 · 0:32:00

Drainage letters met the same test. Residents of Chrysler Gate wrote about standing water; the staff report found the swales were built to the approved plans:

“So in effect, they were built according to what was spec'd.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Jul 16, 2024 · 0:09:06
Context

The lesson for advocates is not that stories fail, but that they need to be paired with evidence that speaks to the test being applied: counts at the times that matter, speed readings, photos with dates, or an argument that the standard itself is out of date. Where the problem is speeding rather than the intersection, council's usual answer is RCMP enforcement, which residents can also request directly through the RCMP advisory group.

4The right request in the wrong forum

Public hearings are limited to the application in front of council. M.R. raised her request for an independent sewer study at a hearing on a nearby six-storey building:

“I want a third-party study. I have said that over and over and I will not stop until we get one.”

M.R., resident · Jul 21, 2026 · 0:05:38

“…studies are a valid point. We are doing things and, um, we are moving forward and we are working on, on as much as we can. But today we're talking about a variance For height, variance for parking.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Jul 21, 2026 · 0:40:38

A city-wide request like a study is more likely to be acted on as its own delegation or budget request, with a scope and an estimated cost, than as an objection to one building.

5No one tracked the promised answer

Southeast Cancer Support Services made one of the strongest pitches in the record: peer programs in Winkler, Morden and Portage, a matching pledge with a deadline, and visits to neighbouring municipalities. The mayor promised a decision:

“…we will take your grant request, we will take it back, we'll give it to administration, they'll get onto an agenda, and we will discuss it, and, and we will have an answer.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Jun 18, 2024 · 1:32:15

No agenda item, motion or later mention appears in the 2024–2026 record. Landmark Planning's water co-op (2022) was also sent for a staff report that never came back. When province-bound requests were answered with a non-answer, council filed it:

“So I guess we'll wait and see what comes of this study.”

Mayor Earl Funk, on the province's reply about flashing crosswalk lights · Oct 15, 2024 · 0:26:01

6Work done where no one can see it

The South East Equity Coalition presented its transit survey and business plan to council in February 2024 and May 2025 at sessions outside the recorded regular meetings. Council gave verbal support, but there is no motion, vote or video, so residents can't see what council said or hold it to it. See Transit. A recorded delegation at a regular meeting, followed by a written request for a motion, leaves a public trail.

Context: why the residents' survey carried little weight, and how to do better

No council member criticized the survey's method on the record; this is our assessment of why it was easy to set aside. It was run by two affected residents, with no stated way of choosing homes, no count of homes that didn't answer, and no map. “Experienced sewer backup” was self-reported and not separated from overland flooding or seepage. The figures were a few sentences inside a long presentation that ended in questions rather than one request. And the provincial disaster-aid decision council then pursued turned on a different test, regional insurability (Adam Thiessen, Senior Manager of Corporate Services, Apr 21, 2026), which the survey didn't measure. The coalition's transit survey had a similar problem: most of its 751 respondents came from its own client groups, so it showed need among those groups but not city-wide demand.

A resident survey is more likely to count if it: covers every address in a defined area, with a map and a list of non-responses; uses neutral questions, ideally shown to staff first; measures what the decision turns on (dates, type of damage, dollar losses, insurance denials, backwater-valve status); comes with a short written summary of the method; and ends in a single request. Even then, expect council to treat it as a reason to collect its own data, and ask in writing for that data to be published.

Council's reasons for saying no

1Money is decided at budget time, against everyone else's requests

“Thanks, Council. We'll deal with it in November.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, chairing, on Summer in the City's request for 2027 · Jul 21, 2026 · 1:38:24
Context

Weighing all requests together is reasonable, and the mayor said it is “more fair” to both the group and the city. The cost is that the weighing happens in closed sessions with no published list of requests or results, so groups can't learn why they were refused or how to do better. Brandon and Winkler run grant programs with published criteria and deadlines (see Part 2).

2The experts' report says it isn't warranted

“But the reason we commission reports like this is so that way we can have the evidence before us”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, accepting the report against a Harmony Lane stop sign · Feb 18, 2025 · 0:52:00
Context

Consistent standards protect against favouritism. But residents rarely see the warrant guideline or the counts before the decision, and the reports measure the device asked for, not necessarily the problem (speeding, children crossing). Council has overridden staff when residents showed consensus (Cottonwood) or an institution made a careful case (Southwood).

3One person isn't the neighbourhood

“…normally with that sort of thing, we would only really consider that if there was pretty clear there's a neighbourhood consensus that this is what they want.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · May 16, 2023 · 0:27:33
Context

In the Henry Street case, staff went door to door themselves, a fair way to check. But the bar is not written down anywhere, so residents don't know whether they need 5 signatures or 50.

4It's the province's (or Ottawa's) decision

“Obviously, this is not directly within city jurisdiction because this is a provincial highway, but I think that by the city adding its voice to the request and letting the province know about it, and also the fact that this will also get into the news, I think these are all good, good ways of adding profile to the request.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on B.B.'s crosswalk lights · Feb 20, 2024 · 0:26:33
Context

Jurisdiction rarely produced a flat no: council usually agreed to “add its voice” with a letter, and sometimes the province needs that resolution before it will act (engine-brake signs, 2022). But council did not follow up when the province didn't act. Groups should send the province their own letter citing council's resolution, and ask council to raise it through the Association of Manitoba Municipalities.

5No precedent, or no results

“The, the reason I think we need to stop the funding is that we haven't seen the development that we would have liked to have seen”

Councillor Jac Siemens, on ending the economic development grant · Aug 5, 2025 · 0:24:52
Context

Precedent cuts both ways: once something is approved (the golf club's tax receipts, STARS' per-resident grant) it becomes the template for next time. Groups asking for something new should point to peer councils that fund it, and groups with ongoing funding should report measurable results every year.
