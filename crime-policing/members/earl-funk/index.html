Crime and policing · Council member

Earl Funk: crime, policing and community safety

What did Earl Funk say and do about crime, policing and community safety in Steinbach (the RCMP contract and its cost, policing priorities, safety officers and private security) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

In short

As a councillor on Mayor Chris Goertzen's council, Earl Funk was the member most active on policing. He sat on the RCMP Advisory Group (chairing it in 2018), moved the resolutions that confirmed council's four policing priorities in 2016, 2017 and 2018, and led a 2016 meeting with the RCMP's Manitoba headquarters about coverage for Steinbach, whose outcome was never reported. When the province offered its community safety officer program in 2017, he pointed out that two province-paid constables already did similar work and offered to take the letter to the advisory group; no report back was found. He voted against the 2017 and 2018 budgets, but over the arts centre and the golf course land deal, not policing.

As mayor since November 2018 he has chaired every policing item and sits on the closed advisory group. He supported the 17th officer (2020) and championed the traffic officer in 2021 after, he said, complaints about street racing reached his store. He insisted the school division pay 100% of a school officer because an added officer is permanent. He sent residents' safety complaints to the advisory group and most outside requests, including every Crime Stoppers letter, to 'received as information.' From 2025 he backed private overnight security as 'tiered policing' and pressed for bail reform through the cities' caucus of the Association of Manitoba Municipalities.

Term by term

2014–2018

Council's most active member on policing. He moved the 2016, 2017 and 2018 priorities resolutions (police visibility, drugs and organized crime, property crime, traffic), asking the RCMP to be more visible and later arguing the priorities were 'working' because incidents were falling. He led the 2016 meeting with RCMP D Division on coverage (no outcome reported), took the 2017 community safety officer offer to the advisory group (no report back found), asked the RCMP about meth (2018) and, as advisory chair, moved council's support for a federal study of rural crime and RCMP staffing (2018). He called the 2018 tax increase a golf course 'bailout' rather than an RCMP cost, and voted against that budget.

Took part in 11 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

As the new mayor he made Michael Zwaagstra chair of the RCMP Advisory Group. He cited crime prevention through design to keep the Cottonwood walkway open (2019), backed the 17th officer as the best way to support the police (2020), and put residents' street-racing letters on the agenda in 2021, saying the downtown racing outweighed the $200,000 cost of a traffic officer. He told a resident reporting thefts to call the RCMP when it happens and sent her concern to the advisory group (2022). He supported the condition that the school division pay all of a school officer's cost (2022) and praised Citizens on Patrol without proposing support when it reported shrinking numbers.

Took part in 16 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He welcomed the provincial grant increase in 2023, backed the January 2025 letter to Ottawa ('we're happy to pay' but want a say), and sent Harmony Lane and First Street speeding complaints to the advisory group. He said in June 2025 that the city had not received the 18 officers it pays for since 2022–23 and backed private security as 'boots on the ground'; in October 2026 he called it 'tiered policing' and 'a safety net.' He presented the Community Safety and Well-Being Plan as mostly a summary of existing work, took Crime Stoppers' and Adult & Teen Challenge's requests as information, and arranged the August 2026 meeting on the $200,000 safety grant, whose outcome was not made public.

Took part in 26 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Steady on the same approach for a decade: support the RCMP, keep the same priorities, and add help when a specific problem is loud enough (street racing, construction-site theft). As mayor he has preferred measures that cost little or come from outside money (the traffic officer was the exception) and has handled residents' complaints through the closed advisory group rather than in open council.
