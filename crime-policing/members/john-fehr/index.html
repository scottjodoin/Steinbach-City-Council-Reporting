Crime and policing · Council member

John Fehr: crime, policing and community safety

What did John Fehr say and do about crime, policing and community safety in Steinbach (the RCMP contract and its cost, policing priorities, safety officers and private security) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

In short

John Fehr seconded the 2017 financial plan while saying council would have liked the population to stay under 15,000 so policing costs could be contained, since passing that mark raised the city's share of RCMP costs from 70% to 90%. At a 2016 development hearing he told neighbours that speeding on their street was a police issue, not a matter for the hearing.

He praised the RCMP's stepped-up traffic enforcement and police visibility as deterrents (2017–2018) and asked whether the RCMP would order council's priorities the same way. On the Cottonwood walkway (2018) he first suggested an RCMP review, then wanted 'a little bit more permanent step', perhaps a gate. As acting chair in 2017 he encouraged members to attend a crime prevention network event.

“we would have liked to seen our population remain at under 15,000 so that we could have contained our policing costs for another couple years.”

Councillor John Fehr · On the 2017 budget and the higher RCMP cost share · Feb 21, 2017 · 0:15:19

Term by term

2014–2018

Treated speeding as a police matter (2016), seconded the 2017 budget while regretting the cost of passing 15,000 people, praised the link between more traffic tickets and fewer collisions (2017), voted for the 2018 budget, and pushed for a firmer step than reporting and patrols on the Cottonwood walkway (2018).

Took part in 8 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

Supportive of enforcement and conscious of cost; one of the members most willing to look for a physical fix to a neighbourhood safety complaint.
