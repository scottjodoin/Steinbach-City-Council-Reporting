Crime and policing · Council member

Chris Goertzen: crime, policing and community safety

What did Chris Goertzen say and do about crime, policing and community safety in Steinbach (the RCMP contract and its cost, policing priorities, safety officers and private security) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

In short

Mayor Chris Goertzen chaired council's yearly RCMP reports and the priorities resolutions that followed them. He linked falling thefts in 2015 to council's priorities and drug enforcement, and repeatedly credited council's funding of the two-officer General Investigation Section, the detachment's drug and organized-crime unit. When the staff sergeant said in 2017 that the unit had sat empty for months because of staffing shortages, he asked how the work would hold up while short of officers.

He handled requests from partners as information: Manitoba Crime Stoppers (2016 and 2017) and the Headway youth program, to which he promised an answer under the city's grant policy. He preferred province-paid community constables to the province's community safety officer program (2017), backed a federal study of rural crime and RCMP resources (2018), and three weeks before the 2018 election declined an invitation to meet RCMP leaders because the city already had a good relationship with its detachment. He voted for the 2017 and 2018 budgets, whose tax increases were tied to the higher RCMP cost share.

“I think we'll take it as information. We have a very good relationship with our RCMP. We have very good communication, so we'll leave it at that.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Declining an invitation to meet RCMP leaders at the AMM convention · Oct 16, 2018 · 1:05:55

Term by term

2014–2018

Chaired the RCMP reports of 2016, 2017 and 2018 and the priorities that followed, crediting council's GIS unit and the 'marked success' in falling thefts. He took Crime Stoppers' and Headway's funding requests as information, preferred province-paid constables to the community safety officer option, supported the 2018 rural crime study, and declined an AMM meeting with RCMP leaders. He summed up the Cottonwood walkway complaints (2018) as a consensus to call the RCMP, write to the school division and involve Citizens on Patrol.

Took part in 14 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

The trajectory

A supportive chair rather than a driver of change: he praised the RCMP and council's drug unit, kept the same priorities, and left funding requests and new programs to policy and the advisory group.
