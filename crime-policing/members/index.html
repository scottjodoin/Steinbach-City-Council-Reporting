Council members on crime and policing

How did each council member speak and vote on crime, policing and community safety in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. Not one policing decision was divided, so the differences between members show up in who led, what they asked, and the reasons they gave.

  • Two members led. Earl Funk moved the priorities as a councillor (2016–2018); Michael Zwaagstra, advisory group chair from 2018, moved almost every policing motion after that, including both new officers, the school-officer condition and private security. Both said RCMP costs are out of the city’s control.
  • The questioners. Jac Siemens’ questions revealed the empty drug unit (2017) and a vacancy (2024); Jake Hiebert, a former police officer, asked how “visibility” is measured and why mischief rose; Susan Penner questioned the 2025 RCMP increase and whether private security worked.
  • The only split votes on the topic were the 2017 and 2018 budgets, and those who voted against objected to other spending, not to policing.
  • Little on the record: Bill Hiebert and, before 2025, Damian Penner spoke rarely on policing. In 2015–2017 the two Councillors Penner often cannot be told apart in the recordings.

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

As a councillor he moved the yearly policing priorities and pushed for RCMP coverage; as mayor he backed both new officers, then private security as 'boots on the ground.'

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Chaired the yearly RCMP reports, credited council's drug-investigation unit, and took partners' funding requests as information.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Council's lead voice on policing for a decade: advisory group chair from 2018, mover of both new officers, the school-officer condition and private security.

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Asked the questions that exposed staffing gaps, seconded the traffic officer, and questioned how far the city should go into social issues.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

Rarely spoke on policing until 2025; then questioned the RCMP cost increase, backed private security, slowed the camera grant over privacy and moved the 2027 security contract.

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Said the 16 officers answered residents' safety concerns (2019) and that most crime is 'small-scale and petty' (2025); otherwise seconded routinely.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Little on the record: praised the local police in 2020 and voted with the unanimous majority.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

A former police officer: wanted a full-time traffic officer for years, asked how 'visibility' is measured, and blamed 'catch and release' for crime.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

Regretted the cost of passing 15,000 people, treated speeding as a police matter, and wanted a firmer fix for the Cottonwood walkway.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Seconded the 2018 budget; named by the RCMP as helping start Headway; most 'Councillor Penner' remarks can't be attributed with certainty.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; no record on this topic yet. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.
