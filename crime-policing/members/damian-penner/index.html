Crime and policing · Council member

Damian Penner: crime, policing and community safety

What did Damian Penner say and do about crime, policing and community safety in Steinbach (the RCMP contract and its cost, policing priorities, safety officers and private security) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Damian Penner spoke on policing mostly at budget time. In 2019 he said residents had asked council about safety and that the 16 officers in the budget were the answer. He seconded the 2021, 2023 and 2024 priorities, the RCMP's multi-year financial plan and the Community Safety and Well-Being plan, mostly without comment, saying in 2023 that officers need 'the best resources available to them.'

In 2025 he argued that police and fire are not candidates for budget cuts, and backed private security, relaying Manitoba Justice's view that RCMP shortages are national and saying that much of Steinbach's crime is 'small-scale and petty.' In 2024 he moved to deny a barbed-wire security fence on an unused lot, saying there was no security need.

“although I'm not going to say that Steinbach doesn't have serious crime— every community does have instances of it— a lot of the crime that we are seeing is, is small-scale and petty.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Backing private security in June 2025 · Jun 17, 2025 · 0:12:42

Term by term

2018–2022

He said in the 2019 budget debate that residents asked council for safety and the 16 officers were the answer. He seconded the 2021 priorities and moved the yearly grants list and the 2022 advisory group appointments (routine), with no other recorded statements on policing.

Took part in 5 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He seconded the 2023 and 2024 priorities, the RCMP multi-year plan and joining the Community Safety and Well-Being plan, said police and fire were not on the table for cuts (2025), and supported private security, citing Manitoba Justice on national shortages and calling most local crime petty. He moved to deny a barbed-wire variance where no security need was shown (2024).

Took part in 9 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A reliable supporter of the policing budget who said little in debate until 2025, when he argued that shortages were national and most local crime minor, and backed the private patrol.
