Crime and policing · Council member

Jac Siemens: crime, policing and community safety

What did Jac Siemens say and do about crime, policing and community safety in Steinbach (the RCMP contract and its cost, policing priorities, safety officers and private security) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Jac Siemens often asked the question that showed a gap. In 2017 he asked why drug enforcement was down, which revealed that council's drug unit had been empty for three or four months; in 2020 he asked what a new officer would cost; in 2024 he asked whether the detachment actually had its 18 officers. He explained the RCMP cost-share increase when he moved the 2018 budget, seconded the 2021 traffic officer request and the 2022 priorities, and supported private security in 2025, pointing to the city's night patrols of parks.

He agreed the city should not pay for a school officer, noting that many high school students live outside Steinbach. On the 2024 Main Street encampment he said council's protective role was already met by the RCMP and a by-law inspector and asked how far tax dollars should go into social issues. In 2026 he pressed staff to include the library, which had raised security concerns, in the cameras proposed for the $200,000 provincial grant.

“I think there's 18 officers and Do you have your full complement of officers that you can use?”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Asking the RCMP commander whether the detachment had its officers · Apr 2, 2024 · 0:45:54

Term by term

2014–2018

He asked how the Headway youth program connected with other youth agencies (2016) and pressed on falling drug enforcement, which revealed the GIS vacancy (2017). He called the 2017 RCMP increase 'a 30% increase' and supported the 0.7-mill rise to cover it, and in 2018 moved the budget, explaining that council had agreed new tax dollars would pay for the higher policing share. He moved for a report on the Cottonwood walkway and proposed Citizens on Patrol and RCMP monitoring instead of removing it.

Took part in 7 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

He asked the cost of a 17th officer before supporting it (2020), seconded the 2021 traffic officer request, noting its $200,000 cost, and seconded the 2022 priorities, crediting council's work with the RCMP. On the school officer he said 'there is a need' but the division should pay. He moved the Cottonwood parking ban against staff advice, saying the real issue was students gathering, and asked Citizens on Patrol about its numbers and funding.

Took part in 10 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He asked in April 2024 whether the detachment had its full 18 officers. On the encampment (2024) he questioned the city's role in social issues. He supported private security in 2025, citing the city's park patrols, seconded the 2027 security contract, and in 2026 asked about the plain-clothes officer council had requested years earlier and pressed for library cameras from the provincial grant.

Took part in 10 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A consistent questioner and a supporter of the RCMP, cautious about the city taking on costs that belong to others (school officers, social services) but ready to fund practical measures such as patrols and cameras.
