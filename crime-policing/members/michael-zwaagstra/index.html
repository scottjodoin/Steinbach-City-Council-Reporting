Crime and policing · Council member

Michael Zwaagstra: crime, policing and community safety

What did Michael Zwaagstra say and do about crime, policing and community safety in Steinbach (the RCMP contract and its cost, policing priorities, safety officers and private security) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Michael Zwaagstra sat on the RCMP Advisory Group by 2016 and chaired it from November 2018 until he left council in 2026, so most policing motions came from him. He seconded the 2016–2018 priorities and moved them every year from 2019 to 2025, explaining each one and stressing property crime: 'it matters a whole lot to the person whose home got broken into.' He moved the request for a 17th officer in December 2020, saying Steinbach had far fewer officers per resident than comparable communities, and the 2021 request for an 18th officer dedicated to traffic, with a request for red light camera authority. He also moved the condition that the school division pay 100% of a school officer.

His reasons for not doing more were consistent: RCMP costs are set nationally and 'not controlled by the city' (2017, 2023, 2025), and in 2025 adding positions was pointless because the RCMP was not filling the 18 already paid for. Instead he moved the 2025 private security motions, noting the $145,000 cost was 'less than what we spend on one RCMP officer.' He urged residents to report crimes, told the RCMP to 'show no mercy' to street racers, and supported the Community Safety and Well-Being plan because it used 'no city tax dollars.'

“I should note that compared to other communities, we still have far less, far fewer officers on a per capita basis than, than virtually any other community, uh, that's of any kind of comparable size.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Moving the request for a 17th RCMP officer · Dec 1, 2020 · 0:10:57

Term by term

2014–2018

RCMP Advisory Group member (reappointed in 2016 and 2017). He seconded the 2016 and 2017 priorities and gave the fullest case for them, especially property crime and the GIS drug unit. He called the 2017 RCMP cost increase 'out of our control' but voted against that budget over the arts centre, and against the 2018 budget, saying the 2017 increase had already covered the RCMP cost. He argued against closing the Cottonwood walkway (a closed path would be more secluded) and urged neighbours to report every crime.

Took part in 11 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Advisory group chair from November 2018. He moved the 2019–2022 priorities, the 17th-officer request (2020), the $250,000 transfer to a policing reserve (2020), the traffic officer and red light camera request (2021, proposing ticket, complaint and collision measures that were never reported back) and the 100% condition on the school officer (2022). He tied the 2022 tax increase to the traffic officer, relayed the advisory group's support for the Cottonwood parking ban, and suggested the cannabis home-grow referral to staff.

Took part in 18 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

He moved the 2023, 2024 and 2025 priorities, the RCMP's multi-year financial plan ('set at the national level'), joining the Community Safety and Well-Being plan, and the January 2025 letter to Ottawa on RCMP bargaining (a '23.7% retroactive pay increase'). In 2025 he said the detachment was '15 to 25% short' and moved private overnight security from the unspent RCMP money, then its extension. He raised the 2024 Main Street encampment as unsafe and, in September 2026, welcomed full strength while warning it had not been that way for years.

Took part in 24 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

The architect of council's policing approach: a fixed set of priorities, officers added for specific problems, costs blamed on Ottawa, and, when the RCMP could not fill its positions, a private patrol. He leaves council with the detachment full for the first time in years and no successor named as advisory chair in the public record.
