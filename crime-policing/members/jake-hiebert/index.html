Crime and policing · Council member

Jake Hiebert: crime, policing and community safety

What did Jake Hiebert say and do about crime, policing and community safety in Steinbach (the RCMP contract and its cost, policing priorities, safety officers and private security) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Jake Hiebert brought decades of policing to council: he described 39 years in policing, including the RCMP and Winnipeg police, and six years on the Parole Board of Canada. He seconded the 2019 priorities and the 2020 request for a 17th officer, and said he had wanted a full-time traffic officer for many years; when one arrived in 2022 he welcomed it and hoped Manitoba Public Insurance would one day pay for it, and for a second one the RCMP suggested in 2024. He asked more than once how 'police visibility', one of council's four priorities, could be measured.

He asked the RCMP why mischief to property had risen from 66 to 106 (2023); no answer came back in public. He agreed with the RCMP commander about the 'revolving door' of release but said it is the law police must work under, and used opening remarks to argue for long-term addiction and mental-health treatment and to blame ten years of federal 'catch and release' for making the public less safe. In 2026 he asked who pays overtime for replacement officers.

“These past 10 years of catch and release have definitely made it more difficult for the police, made more work for prosecutors and judges, and foremost, causing the general public to be less safe and more at risk for property damage and serious violent crime.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert · Opening remarks after the federal election · May 6, 2025 · 0:00:13

Term by term

2018–2022

He seconded the 2019 priorities and the 17th-officer request (2020), supported the 2021 traffic officer as a long-held priority and suggested MPI might fund it, said 'visibility' is hard to measure except through public feedback (2022), and told Citizens on Patrol that its patrols prevent crime.

2022–2026

He asked about the rise in mischief to property (2023), agreed with the RCMP on the 'revolving door' but called it the law (2024), hoped MPI would sponsor a second traffic member rather than the city (2024), spoke on homelessness, parole and addiction in opening remarks (2023–2024), blamed 'catch and release' (2025), and asked about overtime billing for replacement officers (2026).

The trajectory

The council member with the most policing experience, who framed crime as driven by addiction and the justice system and looked to other payers (MPI, other governments) for more enforcement.
