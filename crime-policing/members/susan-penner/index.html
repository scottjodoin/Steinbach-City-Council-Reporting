Susan Penner: crime, policing and community safety

What did Susan Penner say and do about crime, policing and community safety in Steinbach (the RCMP contract and its cost, policing priorities, safety officers and private security) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

In short

For most of the decade Susan Penner said little on policing in recorded meetings. The RCMP staff sergeant credited her (with Cari Penner) in 2016 with helping start the Headway youth program through the RCMP Advisory Committee. Several questions to the RCMP in 2016–2017 came from 'Councillor Penner', but the recordings do not show which of the two Penners spoke, so they are not attributed to her. In 2022 she asked whether the RCMP thought a parking ban would solve loitering on Cottonwood before voting for it.

From 2025 she became more active. She asked why the RCMP line rose by $400,000 in the 2025 budget, seconded the letter to Ottawa on RCMP bargaining, the 2025 priorities and both private security motions, and asked the RCMP commander in 2026 whether private security had helped. She raised privacy concerns about the cameras proposed for the $200,000 provincial grant and wanted committee discussion, and in October 2026 moved the new overnight security contract, saying the campaign had shown that property theft and community safety are residents' priority.

“Okay, that $400,000 for the RCMP you mentioned, why would there be such a significant increase in that line?”

Councillor Susan Penner · Questioning the RCMP increase in the 2025 budget · Mar 4, 2025 · 0:17:14

Term by term

2014–2018

Named by the staff sergeant as helping start Headway through the RCMP Advisory Committee (2016). Other 'Councillor Penner' questions to the RCMP and the 2016 report on the Mental Health Act change may be hers but cannot be attributed with certainty. She voted against the 2018 budget over the golf course grant process, not policing.

Took part in 5 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Few recorded statements on policing. She moved routine ratification of the 2018 advisory group appointments and, in 2022, asked whether the RCMP had said a parking ban would resolve the Cottonwood loitering complaints.

Took part in 2 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

She questioned the $400,000 RCMP increase (2025), seconded the letter to Ottawa ('it's really easy to spend other people's money'), the 2025 priorities and both private security motions, and noted the patrol was paid from money already budgeted for the RCMP. She asked the RCMP whether private security helped (2026), slowed the camera grant over privacy, and moved the 2027 security contract.

Took part in 14 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From a background voice to one of council's active members on safety in its last two years, framing private security as flexible and paid from existing RCMP money. As Deputy Mayor from November 2026 she is the senior returning member on the topic.
