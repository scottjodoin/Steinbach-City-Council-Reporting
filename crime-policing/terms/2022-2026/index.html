Crime and policing · By term

2022–2026: Mayor Earl Funk’s second council

What did Steinbach City Council do about crime and policing from November 2022 to October 2026, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • Eighteen positions, rising costs. Every budget kept 18 RCMP officers. Police and fire together rose from $4.6 million (2023) to $5.9 million (2026), and the RCMP line alone rose about $400,000 in 2025 with no new officers. Council’s answer was that Ottawa sets RCMP pay; it wrote to the federal minister in January 2025 and got no commitment.
  • Paid for, not delivered. From 2022 to 2025 the detachment ran 15 to 25% short of its 18 positions. Instead of adding positions, council hired private overnight security from the unspent RCMP money (June 2025), extended it, and in October 2026 contracted it to the end of 2027. Feedback was positive but anecdotal; a promised report with RCMP statistics never came to open council.
  • Priorities by habit. The same priorities were approved in 2023, 2024 and 2025. In 2026 the new commander set them with the closed advisory board, and no council resolution was found. Questions raised at council (why mischief rose from 66 to 106; a second traffic officer) got no public follow-up.
  • Plans and partners at no city cost. Council joined the provincial Community Safety and Well-Being plan because it used “no city tax dollars,” and received the finished plan as information. Crime Stoppers, Adult & Teen Challenge and an addiction outreach partnership got no funding. A second community safety officer was added, and a $200,000 provincial grant was decided in a closed meeting.
  • No disagreement on the record. Every policing vote was unanimous or by consensus.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2023: a new commander and a national cost plan

The 2023 budget kept 18 officers in a $4.6 million police and fire line. In May a new detachment commander, three weeks in the job, brought the usual priorities and described tickets and impoundments for street racers:

“I want to affirm what you're saying about this being a priority. This has been an issue for some time. … By all means, keep on catching these racers, give them the maximum. This is not a time for warnings.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, advisory group chair · May 2, 2023 · 0:09:19

Councillor Jake Hiebert asked why mischief to property had risen from 66 to 106; the commander said he would find out, and no answer was given in a later public meeting. In June an Association of Manitoba Municipalities resolution on the “fair allocation of the costs of policing” was taken as information without discussion. In July council accepted the RCMP’s 2024–2029 multi-year financial plan, with no figures read out:

“this is a plan that is set at the national level. … Obviously, most of this are things that the city does not have any direct control over, but it does provide us with valuable information about the kind of services that we are receiving”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jul 4, 2023 · 0:14:00

In August the mayor welcomed a 28% increase in the province’s policing grant; council did not discuss using it for more officers. Crime Stoppers’ request for 25 cents per resident (November 2023, and again in 2024) was taken as information.

2024: addiction-driven crime, a safety plan, and an encampment

In February council joined the province’s Community Safety and Well-Being planning process, with staff time limited to about two hours a week:

“And so there's no city tax dollars that are being allocated here. The request is simply that we provide a limited amount of staff time so that way we can facilitate this.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Feb 6, 2024 · 0:12:01

In April the commander reported a significant rise in break-ins and mischief:

“most of what we're seeing with break-and-enters and some of the thefts are addiction-driven. So the people that are committing them are, for the most part, the same people. They're doing the same ones over and over again.”

Staff Sgt. G.L., RCMP detachment commander · Apr 2, 2024 · 0:36:34

“For the most part. I'm down one, as we call, a hard vacancy, and right now I'm working with staffing to fill that.”

Staff Sgt. G.L., asked by Councillor Siemens whether he had all 18 officers · Apr 2, 2024 · 0:46:03

He also said a second traffic officer would be more effective; Councillor Jake Hiebert hoped Manitoba Public Insurance would pay for one, and no motion followed. The Headway program, based at the detachment, reported a drop of about 90% in its young clients’ involvement with the justice system (its own figure). Council approved the priorities:

“And we have certainly told our staff sergeant at our RCMP advisory meetings, and as council as a whole, show no mercy, give them every ticket you possibly can, take their vehicles away.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, on street racers · Apr 2, 2024 · 1:03:06

In June, $550,000 was drawn from reserves for “Policing” in 2023 without explanation. In September councillors described a Main Street encampment as unsafe, and council asked staff to study other cities’ approaches (see the homelessness report). In November council resolved to take no action on Steinbach Community Outreach’s proposal that the city apply to a federal fund for a mobile addiction outreach unit, and took Adult & Teen Challenge’s urgent request and an RCMP body-camera billing notice as information.

2025: a letter to Ottawa, a shortage, and private security

“in the last round of collective bargaining, the federal government negotiated a 23.7% retroactive pay increase, which obviously has a significant impact on municipal budgets. … we should be getting a lot more say than what we're getting now.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving the letter to the federal Public Safety Minister · Jan 7, 2025 · 0:20:23

The minister replied that municipalities are consulted through an RCMP contract committee, with no commitment on costs. At the 2025 budget hearing Councillor Susan Penner asked why the RCMP line rose about $400,000:

“RCMP costs are not controlled by the city. All that we're able to do is we're able to say the number of officers that we wish to have”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Mar 4, 2025 · 0:31:04

In May council let the Ste. Anne police do traffic enforcement in Steinbach paid by Manitoba Public Insurance, and adopted the priorities again. In June the mayor said the city had not received the 18 officers it pays for since 2022–23, and council voted to hire private security with the unspent money (R25-118):

“The solution is not simply adding, you know, RCMP officers because we're not getting the number that we're paying for now, that we're requesting now. So just saying we're going to go from 18 to 19 officers, we wouldn't.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jun 17, 2025 · 0:07:37

Steinbach Security Services began patrolling construction sites and neighbourhoods from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m., seven nights a week, on July 2. In November staff reported positive feedback from the RCMP and developers, and council extended it:

“the amount that this would cost to continue the program in 2026 is $145,000. That is less than what we spend on one RCMP officer”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Nov 4, 2025 · 0:09:55

Crime Stoppers’ 2026 appeal was taken as information. The provincial policing grant was $2.61 million for 2025–26.

2026: a safety plan, a grant, and a full detachment

In March the mayor presented the city’s Community Safety and Well-Being Plan, built on a survey of more than 800 residents that found a strong perception of too little RCMP presence. Council received it as information:

“this plan really just summarizes what's already happening by wonderful organizations in our community.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Mar 3, 2026 · 0:20:43

Two community safety officers were named that month, after decades with one. In April the province sent an unexpected $200,000 safety grant. In July staff proposed a $65,000 truck for the safety officers and $135,000 of cameras at city buildings and parks; Councillor Siemens asked why the library was left out, and Councillor Susan Penner raised privacy:

“I understand cameras are important for security, but also privacy in public locations too is something that is valuable.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Jul 21, 2026 · 1:29:21

The decision went to a closed meeting on August 11; no allocation appeared in the public minutes. In September the new commander reported all 18 positions filled from September 2026, break-ins up from 78 to 91 and Mental Health Act calls up from 251 to 311 so far that year, and said he had set the 2026–27 priorities with the Police Advisory Board. Council passed no resolution on them. Asked whether private security had helped:

“This is a very anecdotal answer, but yes, I do think that. A lot of deterrence comes from presence.”

Staff Sgt. M.M., RCMP detachment commander, asked by Councillor Susan Penner · Sept 15, 2026 · 0:31:05

When the patrol firm announced it would close, council in October awarded the overnight patrol to Next Level Servicing Corp. to the end of 2027 ($148,409 for 2027):

“They're finding that due to high cost of policing or just the lack of RCMP, that tiered policing is something that is, is, is positive.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Oct 20, 2026 · 0:25:36

Where members differed

No policing vote in this term was divided. Members differed in emphasis: Susan Penner questioned the RCMP increase and slowed the camera grant over privacy; Jac Siemens asked whether the detachment had its officers and questioned how far the city should go into social issues; Jake Hiebert blamed “catch and release” and looked to MPI to pay for more traffic enforcement; Damian Penner called most local crime “small-scale and petty.”

No recorded split votes on this topic in this period.
