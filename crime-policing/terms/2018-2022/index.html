Crime and policing · By term

2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk’s first council

What did Steinbach City Council do about crime and policing from November 2018 to October 2022, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • Two officers added, one problem at a time. The city kept 16 officers until it asked for a 17th in December 2020, the first increase since 2015, saying Steinbach had far fewer officers per resident than comparable communities. After residents’ letters about street racing it asked for an 18th, dedicated to traffic, in September 2021.
  • A tax increase for traffic enforcement. The traffic officer (about $200,000 a year) was the main reason for the 0.4-mill increase in 2022 and started in May 2022, four months after the date requested. The measures proposed for it (tickets, complaints, collisions) were not reported back. The request for red light camera authority drew no recorded reply.
  • Priorities renewed without targets. The same four priorities were confirmed in 2019, 2020 (without an RCMP presentation, because of COVID-19), 2021 and 2022. Jake Hiebert asked how “visibility” could be measured.
  • Others should pay for extras. Council offered to request a school officer only if the Hanover School Division paid 100% of the cost (2022).
  • Complaints went to a closed group. Residents’ concerns (the Cottonwood walkway, thefts behind Whitby Crescent) were sent to the RCMP Advisory Group, chaired by Michael Zwaagstra, which meets in private. Citizens on Patrol’s warning that it was down to 8 volunteers, and Crime Stoppers’ requests, drew thanks but no action.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2019–2020: sixteen officers for a growing city

At the November 2018 inaugural meeting the new mayor made Councillor Zwaagstra chair of the RCMP Advisory Group, a role he kept until 2026. The 2019 and 2020 budgets held police and fire at $4.2 million and kept 16 officers:

“We were tasked by our citizens about safety, which we are doing with the 16 officers, as well as investment into our fire truck.”

Councillor Damian Penner, at the 2019 budget hearing · Mar 19, 2019 · 0:28:56

In April 2019 the staff sergeant reported break-ins down 8.3% and mischief down 15.7%, explained that provincially paid traffic officers and constables cost the city nothing, and asked council to advocate for more provincial officers. No such advocacy was found in the record.

“I've had an increase of 2. I haven't been here the whole time, but since 2001, increase of 2, one in 2006 and one in 2015. Meanwhile, the population has grown exponentially.”

Staff Sgt. H.L., RCMP detachment commander, on the city’s officers · Apr 16, 2019 · 0:26:21

When the school division asked again to close the Cottonwood walkway, the advisory group advised against it and council agreed:

“The more you close it off, the more likely you are to get offenses.”

Mayor Earl Funk, on crime prevention through design · Sept 3, 2019 · 0:08:17

Citizens on Patrol told council in May 2019 that its 9 volunteers act as the RCMP’s “eyes and ears” and that the city reimburses its fuel. The province’s Urban Policing Grant was $1.78 million in 2019, received as information. In 2020 there was no RCMP presentation because of COVID-19; council renewed the priorities from the consultation form, and set aside $250,000 of the 2019 surplus for future policing costs. In December it asked Ottawa for a 17th officer:

“I should note that compared to other communities, we still have far less, far fewer officers on a per capita basis than, than virtually any other community, uh, that's of any kind of comparable size.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving the request · Dec 1, 2020 · 0:10:57

“Generally, once all related administrative costs for that officer are factored in, I believe it's in the range of $160,000 to $180,000 per year.”

City Manager Troy Warkentin, asked by Councillor Siemens · Dec 1, 2020 · 0:13:56

2021: street racing and an 18th officer

In June council confirmed the priorities again; break-ins had roughly halved, which Zwaagstra put down to people being home during COVID-19. Asked how visibility could be measured, he replied:

“over the years we've heard from residents that they want to see a visible police presence. And so we put it in there recognizing, yes, this one is tough to measure, but we want it there as a constant reminder that this is important to people to, to have that presence there.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jun 1, 2021 · 0:53:26

In September the mayor put three residents’ letters about street racing on Main Street on the agenda, out of many more emails and calls. Council unanimously asked for an officer dedicated to traffic from January 1, 2022, and for provincial authority to use red light cameras (R21-195):

“An additional RCMP officer would be about $200,000 a year. It's not an insignificant cost. It would impact our budget. But again, I think this is a problem, a significant problem, and we have to invest in order to address these types of problems.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving the request · Sept 7, 2021 · 1:00:47

“$200,000 a year, yes, that will make a difference and it will impact our budget. But right now, the downtown racing is impacting our community.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Sept 7, 2021 · 1:10:15

Members said they disliked red light cameras but wanted the option; no reply from the province was found. In November an Association of Manitoba Municipalities letter on the cost of the RCMP’s first union contract was taken as information without discussion, as were Crime Stoppers’ and Steinbach Alternative Justice’s requests in December.

2022: the traffic officer arrives; a school officer at the division’s cost

At a March hearing a resident described thefts near a proposed apartment:

“Just thinking of things like, I mean, we've already had theft there in the past. I know I have in my home personally more than once.”

N.P., Whitby Crescent resident · Mar 1, 2022 · 0:42:27

The mayor said theft and speeding were matters for the RCMP, told her to call when it happens, and asked Councillor Zwaagstra to raise it at the advisory group; no report back was found. The 2022 budget raised the mill rate from 13.7 to 14.1:

“There is a small tax increase of 0.4 mills, and that is mainly because of the extra RCMP officer that we're putting in place for this year in order to deal with traffic issues in our city.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · May 3, 2022 · 0:04:56

“As a council, in consultation with our staff sergeant and with our administration, we always determine our priority areas that we want police to focus on. And for the last few years, we've had the same priority areas because they are areas that we have deemed to be important.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving the 2022 priorities · May 17, 2022 · 0:17:14

At the same meeting he reported the traffic officer had just started full time, bringing the complement to 18, and council sent an RCMP letter about home cannabis grows to staff for a zoning review (no report back found). In August, against staff advice and with the advisory group’s support, council banned weekday daytime parking on Cottonwood Bend and Cottonwood Drive to curb racing and loitering; a neighbourhood representative later said it had “greatly improved things.” In July the school division asked the city to request a school officer on a cost-shared formula:

“because they're making the request, this is their initiative, they should be responsible for 100% of the cost. We've already added an extra officer. We're not at this point planning to add another and impact our budget in that way.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jul 5, 2022 · 0:26:09

“So when you purchase an officer like this, you own them forever.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Jul 5, 2022 · 0:32:20

In August Citizens on Patrol said it was down to 8 volunteers from about 12 to 14 and that its funding “used to be MPI, now it’s kind of in limbo.” Members praised the group; no motion followed.

Where members differed

Every policing decision in this term was unanimous or by consensus. Differences showed in questions, not votes: Jac Siemens asked what a new officer would cost and said a school officer was needed but should be paid by the division; Jake Hiebert pressed on measuring “visibility” and hoped MPI would fund traffic enforcement; Susan Penner asked whether the RCMP thought the Cottonwood parking ban would work before voting for it.

No recorded split votes on this topic in this period.
