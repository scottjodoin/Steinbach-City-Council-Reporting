Crime and policing · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

What did Steinbach City Council do about crime and policing from December 2015 to October 2018, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • The same 16 officers throughout. Council paid for 16 RCMP officers, including a two-officer drug and organized-crime unit (the General Investigation Section, or GIS) that members called their point of pride. No member proposed more officers.
  • The cost jumped anyway. When Steinbach passed 15,000 people, its share of RCMP costs rose from 70% to 90% from April 1, 2017. All of the 0.7-mill tax increase in 2017 went to policing, and police and fire together rose from about $3.1 million (2016) to about $4.1 million (2018).
  • Same priorities, every year. Council confirmed police visibility, organized crime and drugs, property crime and traffic in 2016, 2017 and 2018, each time moved by Earl Funk and seconded by Michael Zwaagstra, with no numeric targets.
  • The drug unit sat empty. In 2017 the staff sergeant said the GIS unit had been vacant for three or four months because the RCMP was short of officers; he asked council for nothing more.
  • Partners heard, not funded on the spot. Crime Stoppers (2016, 2017), the Headway youth program (2016) and the province’s community safety officer offer (2017) were taken as information or referred to the closed RCMP Advisory Group.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr.

What happened

2016: falling thefts, a meth problem and a youth program

The 2016 budget funded 16 officers and one community service officer for by-laws, with no policing debate:

“Police and fire services includes 16 RCMP officers, a community service officer, fire protection services, which includes 40 firefighters and 8 trucks. The total cost of these protective services will be approximately $3.1 million in 2016.”

City Treasurer Brian Hrehirchuk, at the 2016 budget hearing · Feb 16, 2016 · 0:01:21

In April the staff sergeant reported that thefts and vehicle thefts had fallen by more than half from 2014 to 2015, and credited the GIS unit council funds. He also warned of a methamphetamine problem, and said RCMP traffic services in Steinbach had been cut back by RCMP repositioning:

“there is a meth issue in Steinbach right now. I think you're seeing that some of the vandalism, or what I call the minor thefts and mischief, have gone up because of that.”

Staff Sgt. H.L., RCMP detachment commander · Apr 5, 2016 · 0:20:08

“Creating the GIS unit, that's one of those areas that it wasn't hard to get unanimous agreement on council and support in community amongst organizations.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, seconding the 2016 priorities · Apr 5, 2016 · 0:22:11

At the same meeting Headway, a new program for youth at risk run with the RCMP, the school division and health agencies, asked the city to help close a $20,000 gap in its roughly $70,000 budget. The mayor promised an answer under the grant policy; the follow-up letters (June 2016, November 2017) were taken as information. In July a Councillor Penner reported a Mental Health Act change, which came from the RCMP Advisory Group, that lets someone other than a police officer wait with a person at the hospital. In November the mayor reappointed Councillors Funk and Zwaagstra to the advisory group and said Funk was leading a meeting with the RCMP’s Manitoba headquarters about coverage; its outcome was never reported.

2017: the 15,000 threshold, and an empty drug unit

The 2017 budget raised the mill rate 0.7, to 13.93, with the same 16 officers:

“All of the additional revenue generated by the mill rate increase will be used to fund the additional policing costs as a result of the increased funding requirement now that we have reached or surpassed a population of 15,000.”

City Treasurer Brian Hrehirchuk, at the 2017 budget hearing · Feb 21, 2017 · 0:00:49

“In terms of the RCMP aspect, we recognize that that is out of our control in terms of the amount that we have to pay. We have the same number of officers and we're having to pay more.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Feb 21, 2017 · 0:16:03

“we would have liked to seen our population remain at under 15,000 so that we could have contained our policing costs for another couple years.”

Councillor John Fehr, seconding the budget · Feb 21, 2017 · 0:15:19

In March the province offered its community safety officer program. The city manager said no review had been done for Steinbach; Councillor Funk noted the detachment already had two province-paid community constables and offered to take the letter to the advisory group. No report back was found.

“We have the 2 community constables, and they're under staff sergeants' detail, and from what I understand, they're from the province, they don't cost us anything.”

Councillor Earl Funk · Mar 7, 2017 · 0:30:08

At the September RCMP report, Councillor Siemens asked why drug enforcement was down:

“Unfortunately, it has been vacant for the last probably 3 or 4 months, and that's just because of manpower situations.”

Staff Sgt. H.L., RCMP, on the GIS unit · Sept 19, 2017 · 0:03:20

Asked by a Councillor Penner what more he needed, the staff sergeant said the city had provided the resources. Council confirmed the same four priorities:

“An individual property crime may not be as big as a major drug takedown, but it matters a whole lot to the person whose home got broken into, and we do need to make that a priority.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Sept 19, 2017 · 0:14:40

In December a second Crime Stoppers request was taken as information.

2018: a disputed increase, a walkway, and the same priorities

Staff explained the cost-share change at the 2018 budget hearing:

“When the city reached a population of 15,000, our share of policing costs increased from 70% to 90%. The majority of this increase, or approximately $630,000, affected the 2017 budget year as it was effective April 1st, 2017.”

Director of Finance Brian Hrehirchuk · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:00:58

Councillor Siemens, moving the budget, said policing had risen from about $2.6 million to $3.4 million and the 0.16-mill increase covered the last of it. Three members voted against, saying the real cause was a $500,000 golf course land deal (see below). In April council backed a federal study of rural crime and RCMP staffing, moved by Councillor Funk as advisory group chair. In May the staff sergeant reported most property crime and impaired driving down, and youth stealing to buy cheap meth; council kept its priorities:

“As you can see with the incident report, because of these priorities, many of these incidents are going down. So these priorities are working for us”

Councillor Earl Funk, moving the 2018 priorities · May 15, 2018 · 0:13:15

Neighbours of the Cottonwood walkway near the high school asked for it to be removed, citing vandalism and threats. Staff said a storm sewer under it meant it had to stay; council kept it open and agreed by consensus to urge residents to call the RCMP, write to the school division and ask Citizens on Patrol to patrol there:

“I would encourage anyone who lives in that area, anytime a crime happens, it should be reported.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jul 17, 2018 · 1:54:08

Three weeks before the election, the mayor declined an Association of Manitoba Municipalities invitation to meet RCMP leaders:

“I think we'll take it as information. We have a very good relationship with our RCMP. We have very good communication, so we'll leave it at that.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Oct 16, 2018 · 1:05:55

Where members differed

No policing decision in this term was divided. The two split votes on the topic were budgets whose tax increases were tied to RCMP costs; in both, the members against objected to other spending (the arts centre in 2017, the golf course land deal in 2018), not to policing.

“Even though other councilors are placing the blame on other expenses such as our increase to our RCMP contract, I think we should call this what it is. It's a bailout”

Councillor Earl Funk, voting against the 2018 budget · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:24:42
