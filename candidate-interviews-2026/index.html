SteinbachOnline candidate questions · Oct 7, 9 and 15, 2026

Candidates in writing: growth and housing, flooding, and public safety

SteinbachOnline asked all 10 candidates for Steinbach City Council the same questions on three issues. What did each say about the housing the city should build, how to stop basement flooding, and the most pressing public safety problem? And do the incumbents' answers match their record?

Each candidate has our one-line summary and a few short quotes; SteinbachOnline's articles, linked under each question, have every answer in full. Election day is Oct 28, 2026. Last updated Oct 16, 2026.

Bottom line

  • On housing, most candidates want a mix, but split on where density goes. Brad Unger and Mitesh Kumar would keep multi-family or high-density projects out of established neighbourhoods, and Damian Penner says growth shouldn't “leapfrog” into them; Jake Hiebert would expand outward instead; Susan Penner says market demand should decide.
  • On flooding, incumbents point to work already underway (lift station upgrades, the Park Road West force main, doubled pumping to the lagoon), while newcomers ask for more: a public “white paper” (Mitesh Kumar), sewer sensors and sump-pump enforcement (Brad Unger), a risk matrix (Greg Carlos), and on-site stormwater rules (Nicholas Goertzen).
  • On public safety, property crime and homelessness lead. Susan Penner and Greg Carlos name property crime; Gary Dyck and Ron Falk focus on homelessness. Damian Penner and Greg Carlos want more community safety officers; Brad Unger wants five more RCMP officers.
  • Incumbent check: answers match the record, with one repeat tension. Jake Hiebert again favours outward growth over density, despite making the case for infill in December 2025.

Growth and housing

SteinbachOnline askedWhat types of housing and development should the city prioritize, and how, as a councillor, would you address concerns about traffic, parking, and the character of existing neighbourhoods?

Published Oct 7, 2026: read every answer in full on SteinbachOnline.

Greg Carlos

A full mix including starter homes; traffic, pedestrian and parking assessments before approval; developers pay their fair share.

Key quotes · full answer 124 words

“Before approving a development, I want a traffic and pedestrian safety assessment, realistic parking, sidewalks, safe crossings and emergency vehicle access.”

“Having developers as partners to pay their fair share for infrastructure is a good path”

Greg Carlos, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 7, 2026

Gary Dyck

The city hasn't been proactive enough and has left too much to developers; plan with builders; keep mature street trees; plan medium density strategically.

Key quotes · full answer 165 words

“I don't think the city has been proactive enough in city planning and development. Too much has been left in the hands of the developers”

“Medium density housing areas should be strategically planned for the long-term future of the city”

Gary Dyck, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 7, 2026

Ron Falk

All types of housing; parking on site; tall buildings where they already exist; recruit manufacturers that pay a living wage.

Key quotes · full answer 136 words

“Adequate parking needs to be accounted for on-site, not on the street.”

“Buildings of substantive height should be placed in areas where those already exist.”

Ron Falk, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 7, 2026

Nicholas Goertzen

Affordable housing first; every application should show its effect on streets and services; plan water, sewer and roads with zoning.

Key quotes · full answer 131 words

“I'd prioritize what residents need right now, which is affordable housing options.”

“Growth should also be matched with capacity planning for water, sewer, and roads at the same table as the zoning decision.”

Nicholas Goertzen, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 7, 2026

Bill HiebertIncumbent

A mix of entry-level and higher-end homes; steer high density downtown and to areas zoned for it; parking rules work; traffic is “quite tolerable”.

Key quotes · full answer 116 words

“I feel that we need to focus on higher density to specific areas, such as the downtown”

“Overall, I feel that traffic is quite tolerable in the residential areas of our city.”

Bill Hiebert, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 7, 2026

Jake HiebertIncumbent

Prioritize affordable low- and medium-density homes for first-time buyers; expand the city outward rather than build high density.

Key quotes · full answer 157 words

“I believe the city should be prioritizing low and medium-density housing and keeping them affordable, especially to first-home buyers.”

“I think more priority should be given to expanding our city versus high density.”

Jake Hiebert, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 7, 2026
Record check

The same position he gave at the Chamber forum. In December 2025 he argued that higher-density infill “promotes housing choice” and moved to approve a 31-unit project, and in July 2026 he voted for a six-storey building (details).

Mitesh Kumar

Smart growth that creates jobs, but no high density in existing neighbourhoods; flexible on seniors' housing and granny suites.

Key quotes · full answer 97 words

“I would not support high-density development in existing neighbourhoods”

“a similar relaxed process for secondary 'granny' suites in more areas”

Mitesh Kumar, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 7, 2026

Damian PennerIncumbent

All housing types; vacancy rates show the right mix is being built; yearly traffic studies; growth shouldn't “leapfrog” into established neighbourhoods.

Key quotes · full answer 160 words

“there is a clear indication that the correct variety of housing is being built.”

“Growth needs to progress into a neighbourhood and not leapfrog into the middle of it, changing the character of the entire community.”

Damian Penner, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 7, 2026
Record check

Consistent: in March 2024 he voted against the Home Street rezoning inside an established single-family area, while also saying “We cannot double our population sprawling outwards” (housing report).

Susan PennerIncumbent

Market demand decides what gets built; the city's job is policy and infrastructure; public hearings let residents raise concerns.

Key quotes · full answer 69 words

“Market demand will largely determine what types of housing are built in Steinbach.”

“public hearings allow residents an opportunity to voice their questions and concerns.”

Susan Penner, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 7, 2026
Record check

Consistent with December 2025, when she told council that “developers generally know best where the needs are in terms of housing our community” (housing report).

Brad Unger

“We need as many homes as possible”, but not multi-family housing in well-established neighbourhoods; enforce parking near schools.

Key quotes · full answer 139 words

“Quite frankly we need as many homes as possible.”

“I would be less inclined though to put multi-family housing in well-established neighbourhoods.”

“Enforced parking restrictions will be needed.”

Brad Unger, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 7, 2026

Flooding and drainage

SteinbachOnline askedWhat specific steps should the city take to reduce flooding and drainage problems, and how would you decide which projects should come first?

Published Oct 9, 2026: read every answer in full on SteinbachOnline.

Greg Carlos

Treat flooding like a safety risk: an updated stormwater master plan and a risk matrix to rank projects; seek provincial and federal money.

Key quotes · full answer 98 words

“an updated storm water master plan mapping problem areas and system capacity.”

“I would rank projects using a risk matrix”

Greg Carlos, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 9, 2026

Gary Dyck

More resilient infrastructure and higher standards; don't overlook older neighbourhoods; set priorities with experts and those affected.

Key quotes · full answer 165 words

“Standards for water management and development need to be enhanced”

“there should be no anxiety when the rain starts to pour down.”

Gary Dyck, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 9, 2026

Ron Falk

Check sewer and lift-station capacity before permits for large infill; affirms the lagoon expansion.

Key quotes · full answer 149 words

“These types of questions have to be addressed prior to building permits being issued.”

“Expanding our lagoon, I believe, is already in the long-term plan”

Ron Falk, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 9, 2026

Nicholas Goertzen

Rank by risk and impact; keep the Park Road West upgrade on schedule; on-site stormwater for new builds; fund the wastewater plant without burdening residents.

Key quotes · full answer 142 words

“We need to look at projects based on where failure risk and resident impact are highest, not whichever is cheapest or most visible.”

“New developments should be required to manage their own storm water onsite wherever possible.”

Nicholas Goertzen, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 9, 2026

Bill HiebertIncumbent

Unprecedented rain on flat land; the city diverted wastewater and doubled pumping to the lagoon; keep engineering the system.

Key quotes · full answer 84 words

“we doubled the capacity of wastewater being pumped to our lagoon.”

“We will continue to work on engineering our city's drainage systems to meet demands.”

Bill Hiebert, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 9, 2026

Jake HiebertIncumbent

Lists work already done (lift stations, larger pipes); next, a mechanical plant and bigger lagoon, or reconsider joining the Red-Seine-Rat co-op.

Key quotes · full answer 141 words

“I think the better question is what has the city, in the past couple of years, already done to mitigate future flooding.”

“Our future plan is to either build a new mechanical wastewater plant and expand our lagoon or reconsider joining the Red-Seine-Rat Wastewater Cooperative near Niverville.”

Jake Hiebert, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 9, 2026
Context

In 2025 council chose its own plant over joining the Red-Seine-Rat co-op, which staff estimated would cost 25–30% more; reconsidering would reopen that decision (future planning report).

Mitesh Kumar

Re-engage residents; a public drainage “white paper” by an expert; sump-pump audits; fix the worst areas first and report progress.

Key quotes · full answer 117 words

“a clear public drainage 'white paper' prepared by a trusted expert”

“prioritize the worst-affected areas”

Mitesh Kumar, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 9, 2026

Damian PennerIncumbent

Flat land is the challenge; detention ponds in new areas work, older areas still struggle; council invested over $30M and took over provincial drains.

Key quotes · full answer 166 words

“The new standard in Steinbach works and works well, however, there are still challenges in the existing older neighbourhoods.”

“Last year, this council invested over 30 million into sewer and infrastructure projects”

Damian Penner, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 9, 2026
Context

The city's 2025 capital program was $36.6M across all infrastructure, equipment and facilities, and the 2026 budget put over $20M into wastewater (taxes report; sewer backups report).

Susan PennerIncumbent

Keep drainage a priority; lists work underway (Lift Station #1, Park Road West, a catchment master plan); rank by risk and repeat flooding.

Key quotes · full answer 94 words

“Drainage and flood prevention need to remain an infrastructure priority.”

“I would prioritize projects based on risk and impact, particularly in areas experiencing repeated flooding”

Susan Penner, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 9, 2026

Brad Unger

“This cannot happen again”: fast-track sewer sensors, enforce the sump-pump by-law, inspect sewers near construction.

Key quotes · full answer 146 words

“This cannot happen again.”

“I would like to see the proposal for a sensor system for the sewers fast-tracked.”

“the by-law for sump-pumps is quite clear … yet it is not as enforced as it should be.”

Brad Unger, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 9, 2026

Policing and public safety

SteinbachOnline askedWhat is the most pressing public safety issue in Steinbach, and what action would you support Council take to address it?

Published Oct 15, 2026: read every answer in full on SteinbachOnline.

Greg Carlos

Property crime tied to addictions and mental health; more community safety officers; outreach partnerships; lighting; neighbourhood watch.

Key quotes · full answer 92 words

“the most pressing issue is property crime, linked to addictions and mental health.”

“I would like to expand our community safety officers in the city.”

Greg Carlos, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 15, 2026

Gary Dyck

“Safe caring community”: homelessness is the top safety issue; strengthen policing, security and front-line groups, including a winter shelter.

Key quotes · full answer 99 words

“Homelessness and the problems that result from it are Steinbach's most pressing safety issue.”

“including some basics like winter shelter.”

Gary Dyck, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 15, 2026

Ron Falk

A visible police presence; no homeless encampments in town; council can help the agencies supporting people.

Key quotes · full answer 138 words

“Simply having a visible police presence within the city is a big deal.”

“Not allowing the establishment of encampments of the homeless in town is an important message”

Ron Falk, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 15, 2026

Nicholas Goertzen

Street racing and visible homelessness; traffic calming and speed cameras; data on RCMP response times; support housing-first outreach.

Key quotes · full answer 137 words

“I don't think either issue gets solved by enforcement alone.”

“Traffic calming, speed cameras, or physical design changes at key stretches would do more in the long term”

Nicholas Goertzen, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 15, 2026

Bill HiebertIncumbent

The RCMP now has its full 18 officers; street racing easing with more presence; extra security for construction sites.

Key quotes · full answer 53 words

“we finally have full complement of 18 police officers for the city.”

“the extra police presence seems to have an effect.”

Bill Hiebert, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 15, 2026

Jake HiebertIncumbent

Provincial e-bike and e-scooter law, then city by-laws; on homelessness, tackle root causes; backs the new Third Street facility.

Key quotes · full answer 187 words

“I wish our present provincial government would enact legislation regarding e-bikes and electric scooters.”

“social organizations and the city must jointly focus on the deep-rooted problems and not on the symptoms.”

Jake Hiebert, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 15, 2026

Mitesh Kumar

Visible RCMP and CSO coordination, mobile cameras, lighting, youth recreation and public reporting; keep a city police service open for discussion.

Key quotes · full answer 113 words

“temporary/mobile safety cameras in troubled areas”

“keeping the longer-term question of whether Steinbach needs its own police service open for evidence-based discussion”

Mitesh Kumar, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 15, 2026

Damian PennerIncumbent

Most crime is opportunistic, so “drastically increase” community safety officers patrolling in marked vehicles.

Key quotes · full answer 158 words

“The majority of the crime that occurs in Steinbach is crime of opportunity”

“What I propose is to drastically increase the amount of Community Safety Officers (CSOs) in marked vehicles patrolling Steinbach”

Damian Penner, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 15, 2026
Record check

Consistent with his Chamber forum answer and his 2025 view that most crime here is “small-scale and petty” (details).

Susan PennerIncumbent

Property crime; points to overnight security patrols, a second CSO and a fully staffed RCMP; continue the “layered approach”.

Key quotes · full answer 102 words

“The most pressing public safety concern is property crime.”

“I would continue this layered approach: a strong RCMP presence, Community Safety Officers, and targeted security where it is effective.”

Susan Penner, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 15, 2026
Record check

Matches the record: council started an overnight security patrol in July 2025, extended it, and added a second community safety officer by early 2026 (crime and policing report).

Brad Unger

Five more frontline RCMP officers; prioritize theft and drug trafficking; stop Main Street racing.

Key quotes · full answer 106 words

“I would like to see five more positions created and filled.”

“Theft and drug trafficking are directly related and should be prioritized.”

Brad Unger, written answer · SteinbachOnline, Oct 15, 2026
