Steinbach City Council · Dec 2015 – Nov 2026 · Topic: Quality of life

Quality of life: noise, belonging, parks, recreation and arts

How has Steinbach City Council handled the quality-of-life issues residents raise (noise, newcomers and inclusion, flags and proclamations, green space and trees, recreation, and arts and culture) since December 2015? What has it done, what hasn’t it, what reasons has it given, how did each council member act, and how do other Manitoba councils compare?

Written for residents. Start with the summary, then choose what to read. Last updated Nov 9, 2026.

Executive summary

Across three councils, Steinbach has been practical and steady on parks, trees and facilities, cautious on new plans and new spending, and divided in its members’ words, though rarely in its votes, on symbolic gestures.

  • Parks, trees and facilities: steady and mostly unanimous. Council kept the Dutch elm program when the province shifted its cost (2016), renewed playgrounds with provincial grants, opened a first dog park (2021), renovated the Aquatic Centre during the 2020 pool closure, and won federal tree-planting grants (2025, 2026). It also closed or realigned park reserve land many times, always unanimously, usually saying green space still exceeded the city’s requirements.
  • Plans arrived late and are partial. The first Active Transportation Plan (2025, about $11 million of unfunded projects), park and accessibility policies in the new Official Community Plan (2025), and an Accessibility Plan for 2025–2027. There is no parks, urban-forest, recreation or culture master plan.
  • Arts: one big project ended, steady support since. A $24.3 million performing arts centre ended in 2017 when outside funding missed council’s deadline. Since then the city has kept funding the library, the Arts Council and the Mennonite Heritage Village, and raised some grants, but has no plan for a performance venue, and the Arts Council’s operating top-up was approved for one year.
  • Flags and proclamations: decided case by case, with no written policy. Council recognized the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation (2021) and passed a resolution supporting Ukraine (2022), both unanimously. Requests to fly the Ukrainian flag and for proclamations were received as information. Councillors Michael Zwaagstra and Damian Penner spoke against flags, proclamations and diversity criteria; other members spoke about welcoming newcomers, belonging and, once in 2020, racism.
  • Noise and newcomers are handled mostly by others. Noise complaints went to RCMP traffic enforcement and to requests to the province; the 2003 noise by-law, with no fine schedule, was not reviewed. Newcomer services are run by the Chamber of Commerce with federal money; in 2025 council chose not to renew its grant to the economic development office that runs a federal immigration pilot.

The story in three councils

Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council spent its arts energy on a performing arts centre that never got its outside funding, and kept parks and trees funded without dissent. Mayor Earl Funk’s first council built practical facilities (a pool renovation, a dog park, a pathway, the event centre partnership) and acted on recognition only when senior governments led. His second council adopted the city’s first planning documents on parks and pathways, while its members spoke more openly, for and against, about symbolic gestures and belonging.

About this report

Quotes are verbatim from automatic transcripts and link to the meeting videos. Council members, the mayor and staff are named; residents and delegations are identified by initials; organizations are named. The analysis and plain-language summaries are ours. On contested questions such as flags, Pride and diversity criteria, this report records what was requested, said and decided, and who said it; it does not rate anyone’s views.

Methods, coverage and what we couldn’t confirm
  • Transcripts. Meeting quotes come from automatic transcripts of the official videos and are left as transcribed, including repeated words and false starts. The one exception is the city’s name, which the transcription often misspells and which quotes show correctly as Steinbach. Ellipses mark trimmed text; no sentence breaks were added. Each quote links to the start of the speaker’s turn in the video, so the quoted words may come a little later; check there before quoting elsewhere.
  • Speakers and names. Every quote is from a speaker identified with high confidence. Speakers were matched to names with AI assistance, from how they were addressed in each meeting and from the minutes. Council members, the mayor and city staff are named in full. Residents, delegations and other members of the public are identified only by initials. Organizations and businesses are named. In 2015–2017 the two Councillors Penner often cannot be told apart in the recordings, and their remarks and votes are not attributed unless certain.
  • Coverage. Every council meeting from December 15, 2015 to November 3, 2026 with a recording or minutes was searched: transcripts of 243 meeting videos and minutes from January 2018 on (the city’s older minutes are not online). Not covered: the first 2015 videos (not yet transcribed) and four videos that failed to download (April 4 and October 17, 2017; September 15, 2020; July 6, 2021), where the minutes were used if they exist. Budget study sessions and committees are closed and unrecorded, so decisions made there (such as grant amounts) appear only when reported later.
  • Statements that something never came to council (for example, no agenda item on racism, Pride or land acknowledgements) are based on searching every transcript and the minutes for those words. Events outside council meetings, such as the 2016 Pride march, the 2022 flag-raising by residents, and the 2025 and 2026 Pride marches, are reported from news coverage and linked.
  • What counts. 79 items: noise, newcomers and inclusion, flags and proclamations, Indigenous recognition, accessibility, parks, trees, trails, recreation, the library, arts, museum and festivals. The event centre’s cost, homelessness and policing are covered in their own reports and appear here only briefly. Classifying how an item ended (done, partly, no public result, declined) and tagging council’s reasons is our judgment; the reasons are recorded for each item in the data.
  • Votes before 2018. There are no minutes online for 2015–2017. Votes were by voice and counts were rarely announced, so who voted which way is taken from what members said. In the December 2016 arts-centre vote, one Councillor Penner voted each way and we could not tell which.
  • Figures. Grants and budget figures are from the minutes, staff statements at meetings and the 2026 Financial Plan presentation. Green space per resident is our calculation from the city’s 260 acres and the 2021 census; the city does not publish one. Immigration figures are from the 2021 Census.
  • Not confirmed. Whether Steinbach has a written flag or proclamation policy (none found; the city’s online policy library did not list its documents); whether a separate ticketing by-law sets a noise fine; the amount of the economic development grant council chose not to renew, and its reasons, which the August 2025 resolution does not state; the outcomes of the 2019 pool-lift referral, the 2021 red-light camera request, the 2022 engine-brake signs request, the outdoor pool grant applications and the library’s security cameras; whether the Arts Council’s top-up becomes permanent (expected November 2026); and the date of the city’s Boulevard Tree Policy.
  • Transcription errors. The transcription sometimes renders “Steinbach” as other words, including “Islamic”; no meeting item concerned Islam. Quotes with such errors were trimmed or not used.
  • Data. The data downloads list every item, every council member’s part in it, every split vote, and every quote with its timestamp and link. Quotes in the downloads come only from council members and staff identified with high confidence. Every quote was checked word for word against its transcript.
  • Council change. The council elected in October 2026 held its inaugural meeting on November 3, 2026. Quotes are from members serving at the time, some of whom are no longer on council. Election results are from SteinbachOnline (unofficial counts).
