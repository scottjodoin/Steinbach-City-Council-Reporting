Quality of life · By term

2018–2022: Mayor Earl Funk’s first council

How did Steinbach City Council handle parks, trees, recreation, the library, recognition requests, newcomers and noise from November 2018 to October 2022, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • Practical facilities. Council signed the event centre partnership (2019), moved the Aquatic Centre renovation up to 2020 while COVID-19 closed the pool, opened the city’s first dog park (2021), and built the Clearspring Greens pathway after cutting the neighbourhood’s share of its cost.
  • Trees divided council. Twice a majority refused to waive the rule that new buildings plant trees and shrubs along the street (5–2 in 2019, 4–3 in 2020). Councillors Jac Siemens and Susan Penner voted for the waivers both times.
  • Recognition when senior governments led. Council recognized the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation (2021) and passed a resolution supporting Ukraine (2022), both unanimously. Proclamation requests, a resident’s city-flag design and a request to fly the Ukrainian flag were received as information.
  • Racism and newcomers came up in words, not motions. Councillor Bill Hiebert spoke on racism after George Floyd’s death (2020); the Filipino community presented Filipino Heritage Month. Newcomer settlement ran through the Chamber’s Eastman Immigrant Services.
  • Noise went to the police and the province. After complaints about Main Street racing, council asked for a dedicated RCMP traffic officer (started May 2022) and red-light cameras; in 2022 it asked the province for engine-brake signs. No reply on the cameras or the signs is on the record.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

2019: trees, a pool lift, and the event centre

In January a builder asked to skip the street-edge trees and shrubs the zoning by-law requires. Council refused, 5–2, and did so again in 2020, 4–3:

“We have a city policy, and the city policy is one that requires planting of trees and shrubs and curbing that accommodates that, and it's a good policy.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving to deny · Jan 15, 2019 · 0:29:46

“Now that the asphalt has been down and it is in place, it would take quite an effort to plant trees that are going to survive that area.”

Councillor Jac Siemens, for the waiver · Jan 15, 2019 · 0:28:31

A resident (C.K.B.) wrote that the outdoor pool and hot tub had no lift, citing her daughter’s needs. Council agreed by consensus to have staff report back; no report on a lift was found, and the 2020 renovation covered lighting, acoustics and painting. Council gave the library a one-time $7,527 technology grant, and in October signed the event centre partnership:

“So renewing recreation and cultural facilities is one of council's main priorities for the city.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Oct 1, 2019 · 0:29:08

Proclamation requests from Agape House and Canadian Blood Services, and a resident’s design for a city flag, were received as information without debate.

2020: a pathway, the pool, and racism

Clearspring Greens residents had long asked for a safe path into the city. In January council approved a lit asphalt pathway with most of the cost levied on the 146 lots, 5–2. Councillors Siemens and Jake Hiebert, who wanted a cheaper limestone path, voted against:

“But this is the first time that we're asking residences from any area to pay the majority of the cost”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Jan 7, 2020 · 1:39:10

Lighting was moved to a city reserve two weeks later. After 78 owners objected, Councillor Susan Penner had the plan redone in January 2021 as a 50/50 split capped at $1,610 a lot, and the path was built that year. When COVID-19 closed the pool, council had the Aquatic Centre’s renovation (a $1.48 million budget, from the recreation reserve) done in summer 2020.

In June, K.O., a past president of the South Eastman Filipino association, presented Manitoba’s Filipino Heritage Month resolution and spoke of “racial tensions.” Two weeks later, after the killing of George Floyd, Councillor Bill Hiebert gave the only council remarks focused on racism in this record:

“Bringing me to the topic of racism, which we need to be aware of and address in our own thinking. … So for that, I want to apologize publicly and say sorry. I want to encourage all of us to accept all people as equals, no matter what color.”

Councillor Bill Hiebert, opening remarks · Jun 16, 2020 · 0:00:15

No motion, policy or program on racism followed.

2021: a dog park, Truth and Reconciliation, and racing

The first city dog park, asked for since 2016, was approved at L.A. Barkman Park and built that year. In September, after the federal and provincial governments acted, council recognized September 30 as the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation, closing city offices and lowering flags:

“I think we recognize and appreciate the important role of Indigenous people in Canada. They are the original peoples in, in our country, and there have been a lot of mistakes made by previous governments very serious mistakes”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving the resolution · Sept 21, 2021 · 0:46:42

Earlier that month, after letters about racing on Main Street, council unanimously asked for an 18th RCMP officer dedicated to traffic and for provincial permission for red-light cameras:

“I have been on some weeks bombarded with emails and phone calls and just visits to my store about the racing issue.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Sept 7, 2021 · 1:10:15

2022: Ukraine, engine brakes and the arts

After Russia’s invasion, council passed a resolution supporting the people of Ukraine and Steinbach’s sister city, Zaporizhzhia. Two weeks later a local business asked the city to fly the Ukrainian flag. The mayor preferred practical help, working with Eastman Immigrant Services, the MP’s office and churches to host Ukrainian families, and the request was received as information:

“I get it. I see the desire to have the Ukrainian flag flying, but we're Canadians. This is our Canadian flag. But I want to work hard to save and keep as many people safe as possible.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Mar 15, 2022 · 0:41:14

In July council asked the province for no-engine-brake signs on all four highways into the city:

“I've had a couple of citizens south of Steinbach complaining about engine brake noise. Early mornings, late nights, and just wondering if there's any way we can address that.”

Councillor Bill Hiebert, moving the request · Jul 19, 2022 · 0:18:26

In August, outside council, residents put up their own flagpoles at City Hall to fly Pride flags after, organizers said, the city refused to fly them; the city did not comment to CBC (CBC). No Pride flag request appears in the council record. In October council backed the Arts Centre’s provincial grant application, and the Chamber of Commerce reported that its Eastman Immigrant Services was busy and that the mayor and Councillor Siemens had joined bus tours for Ukrainian newcomers.

Where members differed

The split votes were about trees and the Clearspring Greens pathway. Every recognition, library and park reserve decision was unanimous.
