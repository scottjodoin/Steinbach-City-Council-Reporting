Quality of life · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

How did Steinbach City Council handle parks, trees, recreation, arts, accessibility, noise and newcomers from December 2015 to October 2018, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • The performing arts centre rose and ended. In April 2016 council dropped the multiplex and backed a downtown performing arts centre, but only with outside funding. When the estimate reached $24.3 million, a divided council kept the city’s $7.5 million share with a June 30, 2017 deadline. The money didn’t come, and the project ended in July 2017.
  • Parks and trees were funded without dissent. Council kept the Dutch elm program when the province shifted its cost to the city, renewed neighbourhood playgrounds with provincial grants, built long-awaited washrooms at the soccer park, and won the provincial beautification award again.
  • The grants policy was the test for groups. The Pat Porter seniors’ centre’s request was not funded; a $65,000 match for Arts Centre repairs was tabled 4–3 in June 2018 over precedent, then approved unanimously in September.
  • Accessibility and noise came up in small ways. The accessible-parking fine doubled to $100 (2017); a study of Main Street accessible parking was requested but no report was found. Noise came up only at a 2016 zoning hearing, where council pointed neighbours to the complaint process.
  • Newcomers, Pride and flags were not on the agenda. Members spoke of welcoming newcomers in their openings. The one related motion asked Ottawa to remove the Canada Summer Jobs attestation (2018, unanimous).

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr.

What happened

2016: elms, pesticides, and the arts centre

In January the province shifted the cost of removing diseased elms to cities. Council chose to keep the Dutch elm program, about $20,000 a year more, and wrote to the minister about the change:

“It's unfortunate, but we as council have to decide whether we want to maintain our elm tree population in our city or whether we want to let things go.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Jan 19, 2016 · 0:18:57

Council also built washrooms and change rooms at the soccer park (about $370,000) before the Manitoba Summer Games, and in July reaffirmed its opposition to the provincial cosmetic pesticide ban on cost grounds:

“If we were to use the organic products that are recommended, that would increase our cost over $200,000 a year for lawn care from $18,000.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Jul 19, 2016 · 1:14:52

The year’s biggest debate was recreation. After a task force of Councillors Susan Penner, Cari Penner and Michael Zwaagstra reported strong survey support for arts and culture, a motion in April set new principles: no multiplex, and a downtown performing arts centre only with other governments’ money and 10% private fundraising. The mayor objected that council was abandoning its master plan:

“This does not stop recreation. It does not stop moving forward.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, seconding the new principles · Apr 5, 2016 · 0:53:58

“… see the finish line just ahead of our planning process, and we're abandoning it.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Apr 5, 2016 · 0:56:50

By December the estimate had reached $24.3 million. A motion to keep the city’s $7.5 million share with a June 30, 2017 deadline carried over the objections of Councillors Zwaagstra, Funk and one Councillor Penner:

“I have no problem saying that I'm opposing the motion because I don't support that number.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Dec 20, 2016 · 0:54:50

That summer Steinbach held its first Pride march. According to Global News, council declined to officially endorse it, while a councillors’ statement said the LGBT community is welcome in the city; the matter does not appear in any recorded council meeting. In a July opening, Councillor John Fehr urged residents to look for ways to be “a friendlier and more welcoming city every day.”

2017: the arts centre ends, accessibility steps

The 2017 budget carried $24 million for the arts centre over two years; Councillors Zwaagstra and Funk voted against it. In July the mayor announced the result:

“Well, we know that we hit June 30th and we did not receive all the funding, and so the project will not move forward in its present form.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Jul 4, 2017 · 0:21:01

Council renamed its handicapped-parking by-law the Accessible Parking By-law and doubled the fine to $100. In December Councillor John Fehr asked for a study of accessible stalls on Main Street; Councillor Zwaagstra voted against, expecting a report that it was not feasible, and no report came back in the record. The mayor raised the emerald ash borer threat; staff said the city had stopped planting ash about five years earlier. Playground renewals continued with Community Places grants.

2018: the grants policy, a Summer Jobs letter, and the Arts Centre

The Pat Porter Active Living Centre asked for a yearly grant in lieu of property taxes. The mayor cited the focused grants policy, and the 2018 budget, passed 4–3 for other reasons, made no change. Councillor Funk contrasted the seniors with the golf club land purchase:

“If we compare this to the same request made by the Pat Porter Centre, on one hand we had an organization, our seniors, that followed the right process, made public requests, and to date have merely received a pat on the back.”

Councillor Earl Funk, voting against the 2018 budget · Mar 20, 2018 · 0:24:42

In March council unanimously asked Ottawa to remove the 2018 Canada Summer Jobs attestation, which asked applicants to affirm respect for rights including reproductive rights and freedom from discrimination on grounds such as sexual orientation and gender identity. Councillor Funk, who moved it, called it “an unfair values test”; council framed the letter as protecting faith-based and community groups that could not sign it.

In June Councillor Susan Penner moved a $65,000 match for repairs to the city-owned Arts Centre, from the recreation and culture reserve. A majority tabled it, 4–3:

“Part of the challenge has been that the Centre's need for improvements has been overshadowed by the Performing Arts Centre debate”

Councillor Susan Penner · Jun 19, 2018 · 0:51:53

“let's actually redo those sections of the policy and do this on a proper permanent basis and look at some of those things because we're setting a precedent here.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, moving to table · Jun 19, 2018 · 1:08:21

After the Arts Council presented its plan in September, the match passed unanimously. In July, at the hearing on the new Official Community Plan, a resident (T.B.) pointed to a lack of park designation south of Loewen Boulevard; the plan went ahead without a change on that point. Councillor Funk, in a September opening, urged residents to “welcome all the newcomers to our area” after a Filipino newcomers’ barbecue.

Where members differed
