2022–2026: Mayor Earl Funk’s second council

How did Steinbach City Council handle flags and belonging, newcomers, green space, recreation, arts, the library and noise from November 2022 to October 2026, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • Symbolic gestures were debated in words, not votes. No flag, proclamation, Pride or anti-racism item came to a council vote. In opening remarks, Councillor Michael Zwaagstra (2023, 2024, 2026) and Councillor Damian Penner (2024) spoke against flags, proclamations and diversity criteria; other members spoke about newcomer communities, belonging and loneliness.
  • Newcomer services stayed with others. The Chamber of Commerce’s Eastman Immigrant Services, federally funded, grew. Council questioned the economic development office’s federal immigration pilot and in August 2025 chose not to renew the city’s grant to that office after 2025.
  • Park reserves gave way to development. Council closed or realigned park reserve land at least six times, for a business, a road, a developer’s extra lot and new subdivision phases, all unanimously, and created one new reserve for a future creek-bed path. It approved seniors’ housing on Friesen Avenue land neighbours used as green space.
  • The first plans. A new Official Community Plan (2025) and zoning by-law (2026) added park and accessibility policies; the first Active Transportation Plan (2025) mapped about $11 million of unfunded pathways. Federal grants paid for new trees.
  • Recreation, arts and the library grew, bigger asks waited. The event centre opened in 2025 and library support rose to $515,000 (2026). The outdoor pool waits on provincial grants, a football club’s floodlight request got no public answer, and regional library talks ended in 2026. Main Street racing was handled through the RCMP; the noise by-law was not reviewed.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert.

What happened

Flags, proclamations and belonging

Members used their rotating opening remarks to say where they stood. Two councillors spoke against symbolic gestures and diversity criteria:

“People won't care how many climate emergency resolutions you pass, how many proclamations you announce, or how many flags you fly at City Hall if they can't turn on their taps and know that clean water is going to come out.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, opening remarks · Nov 19, 2024 · 0:00:11

“often general debate and discussion is readily labeled as uncompassionate, homophobic, racist, and even hate speech at seemingly a moment's blush.”

Councillor Damian Penner, opening remarks · Oct 1, 2024 · 0:00:13

Other members spoke about newcomer communities and connection:

“And while loneliness is not a municipal responsibility, the fact is the health and well-being of, of our community is important to us. That's why spaces like our new Southeast Event Centre matter.”

Councillor Susan Penner, opening remarks · Jun 17, 2025 · 0:00:47

“I learned that there are over 500 Sikh families in Steinbach, and many were in attendance later in the celebration.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert, on Steinbach’s first Sikh celebration, which he attended at the mayor’s request · Jul 7, 2026 · 0:00:09

In 2026 Councillor Damian Penner called on residents to “accept each other's differences” while celebrating what makes “the whole mosaic,” and the mayor welcomed a privately funded Town Square statue (2023) honouring the Russlaender Mennonites and “all the families that have come here.” The city’s Community Safety and Well-Being Plan, announced in March 2026, names belonging and inclusion among its priorities; council has not discussed its actions on the record.

Outside council, the 2025 Pride march was postponed after threats; then-Deputy Mayor Zwaagstra said he opposed “any form of violence and any form of threats” (SteinbachOnline). All council members were invited to the September 2026 march, and none attended, according to SteinbachOnline.

Newcomers

About 21% of residents were immigrants at the 2021 Census. The Chamber of Commerce runs Eastman Immigrant Services with federal money (about $2.6 million for 2025–2028). In March 2025 Steinbach Economic Development said it would run the federal Rural Community Immigration Pilot; councillors said they had not been consulted, and the mayor was wary:

“Okay, so usually that's how they call it, pilot project, and then all of a sudden, it's great for the community, you get involved, and before you know it, you're funding it 100%.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Mar 18, 2025 · 1:22:05

In August 2025 council resolved not to renew its five-year matching grant to the economic development office when it ended that December (R25-158, unanimous). A year later the mayor cited immigration as an economic strength:

“About 28% of our workforce is immigrant-based. Strong immigration helps solve a common rural problem throughout our area, and that is labor shortage.”

Mayor Earl Funk, State of the City · Apr 21, 2026 · 0:04:50

Green space, parks and plans

Council closed public reserve land for a neighbouring business (2023), a road extension (2024), a developer’s extra lot (2024) and two subdivision realignments (2024, 2026), all unanimously. At the 2024 hearing a neighbour (D.M.) objected that closing part of a reserve for one more lot would benefit the developer, not residents. The chair replied:

“I will note that in terms of the amount of green space that's required, there is still more green space than what is actually required by city bylaws, even with this becoming the additional lot.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, Deputy Mayor, chairing · Jan 16, 2024 · 0:36:16

In June 2026 council approved a Steinbach Housing seniors’ building at 185 Friesen Avenue, where neighbours said they had been drawn by green space set aside in 1989:

“It is difficult, and I like green spaces, especially in areas that don't have, but The overriding principle for me here is what's good for Steinbach and our senior housing list.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Jun 2, 2026 · 0:47:40

Council also created a new reserve near Main Street for a possible path along the old creek bed (2023), named a Hampton Village park after former Mayor Chris Goertzen, and won federal grants of $46,170 and $67,160 for trees at the event centre and the soccer park. In 2025 it accepted the city’s first Active Transportation Plan as a guide:

“The price tags are very high, and so no one should look at this and assume that, okay, all of this is going to happen in the near term.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · May 6, 2025 · 1:03:48

The new Official Community Plan and zoning by-law added park policies, including universal accessibility. At the August 2026 hearing for the 153-lot Williams Creek subdivision, a resident (G.W.) asked where playgrounds would go; the first phase pays a fee instead of setting aside a park.

Recreation, arts and the library

The event centre opened in 2025. In budget debates members described recreation as part of quality of life and refused to cut it, while saying infrastructure comes first. The outdoor pool’s renewal has been on the capital list for years; council applied for provincial grants in 2025 ($3.6 million) and 2026 ($3.85 million), with no decision on the record. The Eastman Raiders asked for about $70,000 toward floodlights at A.D. Penner Park, and the mayor said the budget was set; no decision followed in public. The Arts Council’s grant was increased in budget sessions, to $185,400 in 2026, with the operating top-up approved for one year.

Council explored a regional library with neighbouring municipalities, then ended its part in January 2026 after most of them withdrew:

“One of the biggest things I never wanted our library to do is to lose our autonomy in this.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Jan 20, 2026 · 0:15:31

Noise

Racing and loud vehicles on Main Street came up at every yearly RCMP report. The mayor credited the traffic officer with less noise in 2024; when complaints rose again, council agreed to joint enforcement with the Ste. Anne police (2025) and welcomed a Main Street blitz (2026):

“We're often 2, 3, or 4 officers short of the amount that we have requested, and of course that affects the ability to do proper enforcement.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · May 6, 2025 · 1:13:46

In January 2024 council unanimously denied a truck yard next to Creekside homes after neighbours cited noise, lights and children’s safety. The 2003 noise by-law did not come to council.

Only two votes on the topic split, both on the Friesen Avenue seniors’ building, and the June split was about parking, not green space.
