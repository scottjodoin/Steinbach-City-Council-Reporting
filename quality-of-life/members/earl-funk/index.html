Quality of life · Council member

Earl Funk: quality of life

What did Earl Funk say and do about quality-of-life issues in Steinbach (noise, newcomers and inclusion, flags and proclamations, green space, arts and culture) between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

In short

As a councillor, Earl Funk voted against the performing arts centre's growing cost and the 2017 budget that carried it, but supported the Arts Council's $65,000 repair match both times it came up in 2018. He argued that the Pat Porter seniors' centre had followed the rules and got 'a pat on the back' while the golf club got a land deal, moved playground renewals, and moved council's 2018 letter asking Ottawa to remove the Canada Summer Jobs attestation, which he called 'an unfair values test.'

As mayor since November 2018 he has made recreation and cultural facilities a stated priority: the event centre, the pool renovation, the first dog park and the Clearspring Greens pathway. He supported Truth and Reconciliation Day and the 2022 Ukraine resolution, but steered council away from flying the Ukrainian flag, saying 'This is our Canadian flag' and pointing to work with churches and immigrant services to host families. He has welcomed newcomer communities and public art, cited immigrants as about 28% of the workforce, been wary of unfunded immigration pilots, and ended regional library talks to keep the library's autonomy.

“So renewing recreation and cultural facilities is one of council's main priorities for the city.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Introducing the event centre partnership · Oct 1, 2019 · 0:29:08

Term by term

2014–2018

Opposed the performing arts centre once its estimate reached $24.3 million and voted against the 2017 budget over its borrowing. Supported the Arts Council's repair match in June (against tabling) and September 2018, and argued the grants policy treated the seniors' centre unfairly compared with the golf club deal. Seconded the 2016 request for dog park options, saying it would be well used; moved playground grant applications; praised parks staff for the beautification award; urged residents to welcome all newcomers (2018 opening); and moved the 2018 Canada Summer Jobs attestation letter.

Took part in 9 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Elected mayor. Framed renewing recreation and cultural facilities as a main priority and signed the event centre partnership; highlighted the first dog park and praised 'the council that got it done' on the Clearspring Greens pathway. Voted to enforce the street-edge tree rule both times. Relayed residents' calls to reopen the library in 2020 and their complaints about Main Street racing in 2021. Spoke for Truth and Reconciliation Day and proposed the Ukraine resolution, but recommended against flying the Ukrainian flag, preferring practical resettlement help; he joined the Chamber's bus tours for Ukrainian newcomers.

Took part in 12 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Credited the traffic officer with less racing noise and backed joint enforcement when complaints rose again. Welcomed the Russlaender statue as honouring all newcomer families, reported on Filipino community events and asked Councillor Jake Hiebert to attend the first Sikh celebration. Warned that federal immigration pilots end up funded by cities, then in 2026 cited immigrants as about 28% of the workforce. Defended conditional-use hearings as fair, ended regional library talks to protect the library's autonomy, told a football club the budget was fixed, and announced the city's Community Safety and Well-Being Plan.

Took part in 12 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From critic of a big arts project to champion of big recreation projects: as a councillor he opposed the performing arts centre on cost and process; as mayor he has led the event centre and other facilities. On recognition and newcomers his consistent line has been practical help over symbols.
