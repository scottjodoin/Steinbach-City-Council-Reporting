Quality of life · Council member

Susan Penner: quality of life

What did Susan Penner say and do about quality-of-life issues in Steinbach (noise, newcomers and inclusion, flags and proclamations, green space, arts and culture) between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

In short

Susan Penner sat on the 2016 task force that surveyed residents on recreation and the arts, and in 2018 moved the $65,000 match for repairs to the city-owned Arts Centre twice, arguing its needs had been overshadowed by the performing arts centre debate. She voted against the 2018 budget, citing how groups' requests were treated. In 2019 and 2020 she voted to waive the street-edge tree rule, and in 2021 she rebuilt the Clearspring Greens pathway plan as a 50/50 cost split after most owners objected.

She seconded Truth and Reconciliation Day, speaking of residential schools' generational trauma, and the Ukraine resolution. In 2022–2026 she moved support for the Arts Centre's grant application, the outdoor pool grant, a park naming and an accessible ramp on Main Street, and spoke of loneliness and why public spaces matter. She has also said infrastructure is what council should be doing first.

“And while loneliness is not a municipal responsibility, the fact is the health and well-being of, of our community is important to us. That's why spaces like our new Southeast Event Centre matter.”

Councillor Susan Penner · Opening remarks on community connection · Jun 17, 2025 · 0:00:47

Term by term

2014–2018

Served on the 2016 recreation and arts task force. Moved the Arts Centre repair match in June 2018 (voted against tabling it) and again in September, when it passed. Voted against the 2018 budget, contrasting the golf club deal with small requests from youth and seniors' groups. Seconded the 2018 Canada Summer Jobs attestation letter. In 2016 her votes on the arts centre cannot always be told apart from Cari Penner's in the recordings.

Took part in 5 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Voted to waive the street-edge tree rule in 2019 (moving the motion) and 2020. Brought the January 2021 plan that cut Clearspring Greens residents' share to 50/50 with a $1,610 cap. Seconded Truth and Reconciliation Day and the Ukraine resolution, asked whether Steinbach could have a regional library, and moved support for the Arts Centre's provincial grant application.

Took part in 10 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Defended the event centre as a facility the city paid only about a third of. Spoke on loneliness and the value of spaces like the event centre. Moved the outdoor pool grant application, the naming of Chris Goertzen Park, the Sports Heritage Committee grant and the accessible ramp. Said the regional library study was not a waste, wanted more discussion of library cameras and privacy, called the Friesen Avenue seniors' building a difficult file and moved its variance, and said infrastructure is what council should be doing.

Took part in 14 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A steady supporter of community facilities and groups who looks for compromise (the pathway, the trees), while holding that core infrastructure comes first.
