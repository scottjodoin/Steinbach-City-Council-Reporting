Quality of life · Council member

Michael Zwaagstra: quality of life

What did Michael Zwaagstra say and do about quality-of-life issues in Steinbach (noise, newcomers and inclusion, flags and proclamations, green space, arts and culture) between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Michael Zwaagstra was council's most frequent mover and speaker on quality of life. In 2016–2018 he argued from cost: he opposed the $24.3 million performing arts centre, reaffirmed opposition to the provincial pesticide ban, voted against a Main Street accessible-parking study, and moved to table the Arts Centre match over precedent before supporting it. In 2018–2022 he moved the Truth and Reconciliation Day and Ukraine resolutions, the request for a traffic officer, the pool renovation and the event centre partnership, called for a lift at the outdoor pool, and defended the street-edge tree rule.

From 2023 he used his opening remarks to praise council for not issuing proclamations, raising multiple flags, making climate declarations or imposing diversity, equity and inclusion criteria. He also condemned threats against the 2025 Pride march (in the news, not at council). As Deputy Mayor and RCMP advisory member he pressed for traffic enforcement, moved the denial of a truck yard over noise, defended park reserve closings as leaving more green space than required, and cautioned that the Active Transportation Plan's projects were costly.

“I'm proud that we don't spend our time making symbolic climate emergency declarations or issue a different proclamation for a new cause every week or raise multiple flags in front of City Hall.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · His final opening remarks · Sept 1, 2026 · 0:00:11

Term by term

2014–2018

Seconded the 2016 principles that dropped the multiplex; opposed reaffirming the arts centre at $24.3 million, its federal grant application and the 2017 budget that carried it. Moved the Canada 150 windmill mural and the motion reaffirming opposition to the pesticide ban. Voted against a Main Street accessible-parking study as not feasible. Moved to table the Arts Centre repair match in June 2018 over precedent, then supported it in September. Argued against removing the Cottonwood pathway.

Took part in 10 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

The most active mover: the event centre partnership, the pool renovation tender, the traffic officer and red-light camera request, Truth and Reconciliation Day (citing the role of Indigenous peoples and past governments' mistakes) and the Ukraine resolution. Proposed the referral of a resident's request for a pool lift, pressed for the library to reopen in 2020, and moved to deny the 2019 tree waiver. Seconded the 2020 Clearspring Greens levy and opposed raising other taxpayers' share. Agreed with the mayor not to fly the Ukrainian flag.

Took part in 15 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Spoke against flags, proclamations, climate declarations and diversity criteria in openings in 2023, 2024 and 2026. Raised racing complaints and the RCMP's staffing shortfall, moved the truck yard denial, defended closing park reserves as leaving surplus green space, warned that the Active Transportation Plan's price tags were high, blamed the provincial funding formula for ending regional library talks, asked about library security cameras, and supported the outdoor pool grant. Voted against the parking variance for the Friesen Avenue seniors' building.

Took part in 24 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistent throughout: cost-first on new projects, firm on rules once made, supportive of recognition when other governments led, and from 2023 the clearest voice against City Hall taking up causes through flags and proclamations.
