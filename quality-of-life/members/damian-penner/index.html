Quality of life · Council member

Damian Penner: quality of life

What did Damian Penner say and do about quality-of-life issues in Steinbach (noise, newcomers and inclusion, flags and proclamations, green space, arts and culture) between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

As council's library board representative in 2018–2022, Damian Penner brought the library's one-time technology grant and reported on its COVID-19 reopening and non-resident users. He moved the 2020 Clearspring Greens pathway plan with residents paying most of the cost, and voted to enforce the street-edge tree rule both times.

In 2022–2026 he praised volunteer-run festivals, said recreation and other services that improve quality of life were not on the table for cuts, and highlighted universally accessible parks in the new community plan. In a 2024 opening he criticised hiring under diversity, equity and inclusion principles and how readily debate is labelled homophobic or racist; in 2026 he urged residents to accept differences and unite around what they share as a 'mosaic.' He seconded the truck yard denial.

“Let's do the Canadian thing and accept each other's differences, but more importantly, focus and celebrate all the things we have in common that creates the whole mosaic and unite behind the maple leaf as a nation.”

Councillor Damian Penner · Opening remarks on national unity · Feb 17, 2026 · 0:00:12

Term by term

2018–2022

Library board representative: brought and moved the $7,527 technology grant, reported on phased reopening in 2020, and explained that about one in six library members live outside the city. Moved the 2020 Clearspring Greens plan with residents paying most. Voted to deny both street-edge tree waivers and moved the 2020 denial ('A tree planted today is only going to be more beautiful 25 years from now').

Took part in 9 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Praised festival volunteers, supported policing priorities on racing, seconded the truck yard denial, highlighted universal accessibility in parks, and said he would not cut recreation. Spoke against DEI hiring and quick labels (2024) and for a shared Canadian 'mosaic' (2026). Asked how conditional-use hearings protect against discrimination (speaker identified with medium confidence). Asked a football club about provincial sport grants.

Took part in 15 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From hands-on library liaison to a member who used his openings to speak about national unity and open debate, while consistently backing recreation and festivals.
