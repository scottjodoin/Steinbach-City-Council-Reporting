Quality of life · Council member

Jac Siemens: quality of life

What did Jac Siemens say and do about quality-of-life issues in Steinbach (noise, newcomers and inclusion, flags and proclamations, green space, arts and culture) between December 2015 and November 2026?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Jac Siemens spoke most often for pathways, trees and green space. He moved the soccer-park washrooms and Dutch elm renewals, said in 2018 that he wanted more pathways and bike paths, argued for protecting the downtown elms, and moved the first dog park. He voted twice to waive the street-edge tree rule in specific cases and voted against charging Clearspring Greens most of its pathway's cost.

In 2022–2026 he called recreation and green space quality of life and asked for more of both, but put the seniors' housing wait list ahead of neighbourhood green space on Friesen Avenue. As library liaison he led the regional library study and moved to end it, explained higher non-resident fees, and pressed for library security cameras. He moved the motion not to renew the city's grant to the economic development office.

“We need more active transportation routes, we need more green space, we need more opportunities for people to get out and enjoy our community.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · On the 2024 budget · Apr 2, 2024 · 0:23:20

Term by term

2014–2018

Moved the soccer-park washrooms ('long overdue') and the 2017 Dutch elm renewal. At a 2016 noise complaint hearing he said neighbours could use the complaint process under existing by-laws. Supported the arts centre process and its grant applications. Voted to table the Arts Centre match in June 2018, then supported it. Said the 2018 budget was missing grants for seniors and youth and that he wanted more pathways.

Took part in 8 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Moved the provincial Community Forest grant to protect the downtown elms and the first dog park. Voted to waive the street-edge tree rule in 2019 and 2020. Moved a cheaper limestone, unlit Clearspring Greens path and voted against the 2020 levy and the move of lighting to a reserve, then seconded the 50/50 plan. Gave 2021 remarks on the meaning of City Hall's Canadian flag; joined the Chamber's bus tours for Ukrainian newcomers; widened the engine-brake sign request to all four highways.

Took part in 9 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Called recreation and 260 acres of green space 'a quality of life piece' and asked for more active transportation routes and green space; moved the Active Transportation Plan, the tree grant application and a new reserve for a creek-bed path. Supported the Friesen Avenue seniors' building over neighbours' green-space objections. As library liaison, led and then moved to end regional talks and pressed for security cameras. Moved not to renew the economic development grant.

Took part in 16 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A consistent advocate for paths, trees and green space who weighed them against other needs case by case, and who favoured flexibility on rules where others favoured enforcement.
