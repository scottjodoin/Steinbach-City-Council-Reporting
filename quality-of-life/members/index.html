Quality of life · Council members

Council members on quality of life

How did each council member vote and speak on noise, newcomers and inclusion, flags and proclamations, parks and trees, recreation, and arts and culture between December 2015 and November 2026?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. Most quality-of-life decisions were unanimous, so the differences between members show up in a few split votes, in who brought items forward, and in what members chose to say in their opening remarks.

  • 2016–2018 split over money for the arts. Michael Zwaagstra and Earl Funk opposed the $24.3 million performing arts centre; Mayor Chris Goertzen worked to deliver it. In 2018 a majority tabled the Arts Centre repair match before approving it unanimously.
  • 2019–2020 split over trees and a pathway. Jac Siemens and Susan Penner voted twice to waive the street-edge tree rule; the majority enforced it. Siemens and Jake Hiebert voted against charging Clearspring Greens most of a pathway’s cost.
  • On symbolic gestures, members differed in words. Zwaagstra and Damian Penner spoke against flags, proclamations and diversity criteria; Bill Hiebert spoke on racism (2020); Funk, Susan Penner, Jake Hiebert and Damian Penner spoke about newcomers and belonging. Every recognition motion that came to a vote passed unanimously.

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

As a councillor he opposed the performing arts centre and backed the Arts Centre and seniors; as mayor he has championed facilities and practical help for newcomers rather than flags.

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Chaired the rise and end of the performing arts centre, which he had not voted for, and backed parks, trees and accessibility studies.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

The member most active on this topic: he moved recognition and recreation motions, enforced the tree rule, and spoke most often against flags, proclamations and diversity criteria.

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Council's steadiest voice for pathways, green space and elms, who also put seniors' housing ahead of green space and led the library's regional talks to their end.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

Moved the Arts Centre's repair match and rebuilt the Clearspring Greens pathway plan; spoke on reconciliation, loneliness and public spaces, while saying infrastructure comes first.

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

The library's voice on council in his first term; later spoke against diversity hiring and quick labels, and for a shared 'mosaic,' accessible parks and festivals.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Gave council's only remarks focused on racism (2020) and brought residents' engine-brake complaints to the province.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Voted against charging one neighbourhood most of a pathway's cost, enforced the tree rule, and reported on Steinbach's first Sikh celebration.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

Urged a friendlier, more welcoming city and asked for accessible parking on Main Street; moved to keep the arts centre alive with a deadline.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Seconded the Arts Centre repair match both times, saying she had championed its move into the building, and moved the Accessible Parking By-law.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; no record on this topic yet. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.
