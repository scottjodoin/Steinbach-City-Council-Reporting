Traffic and congestion · Council member

Susan Penner: traffic, congestion and road safety

What did Susan Penner say and do about traffic congestion and road safety in Steinbach (Main Street and the highways, city streets, stop signs and signals, speed and enforcement) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

In short

Before 2018 Susan Penner's traffic contributions are hard to separate from Cari Penner's in the recordings; the one certain one is her 2017 question about when the Industrial Road signals would come. From 2018 she mostly moved or seconded routine items, such as readings of the Loewen widening, the speed-limit by-law and the Loewen debt by-law.

Her distinct positions came on neighbourhood requests. She asked that the Wyndham Estate stop-sign request be studied once school and the new playground were in use, and in 2023 cast the only vote against refusing it, arguing that warrants miss the school context. Yet in 2024 she voted against the Loewen–Hespeler three-way stop, saying 'there's rush hour times in every community' and that council should not decide on one-off experiences. In 2025 she brought the Harmony Lane request forward and argued the issue was speed. She is Deputy Mayor from November 2026.

“I'm not sure that the issue here is the volume of traffic for the stop sign. I think the issue here is speed.”

Councillor Susan Penner · On residents' stop-sign request at Harmony Lane after a speeding driver killed their dogs · Jan 21, 2025 · 0:34:17

Term by term

2014–2018

In August 2017 asked staff when the Industrial Road and PTH 52 signals would come; the province had not replied. Several requests moved by 'Councillor Penner' in this term (the PTH 52 West speed review, PTH 12 crosswalks, Park Road West, Henry Street) cannot be attributed to her rather than Cari Penner.

Took part in 3 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Mostly routine roles: third reading of the Loewen widening by-law and second reading of the speed-limit by-law. Asked that edge-of-city road traffic go to a study session (2020; identification medium-confidence). Voted with the majority on the Clearspring Greens pathway. Asked that the Wyndham Estate stop-sign request be studied once school and the playground were in use, and whether the RCMP had weighed in on Cottonwood parking.

Took part in 12 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

The only vote against refusing the Wyndham stop sign (6–1, 2023): 'the numbers don't warrant it, but at the same time, it would make sense'. Voted against the Loewen–Hespeler three-way stop (2024). Asked whether PTH 12 signals could be switched off at quiet times and when the province would repair the highway. Brought the Harmony Lane request forward and argued it was about speed (2025). Welcomed borrowing $3 million at 3.83% for Loewen rather than the $3.5 million at up to 7% budgeted.

Took part in 15 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Generally aligned with the majority that follows engineering warrants, with an exception for children near schools and parks. As Deputy Mayor she is now the longest-serving councillor on a council that has lost its two most active traffic voices.
