Michael Zwaagstra: traffic, congestion and road safety

What did Michael Zwaagstra say and do about traffic congestion and road safety in Steinbach (Main Street and the highways, city streets, stop signs and signals, speed and enforcement) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

In short

Michael Zwaagstra moved more traffic decisions than any other member: most of the yearly traffic counts and their requests to the province, most steps of the Loewen Boulevard and PTH 12 rebuild, the speed-limit by-law, and the request for signals at Giesbrecht Street. He explained why the city pays for its own counts: to have evidence when lobbying the province. As chair of the RCMP advisory committee he moved the 2021 request for a dedicated traffic officer and red-light cameras, and in 2025 the joint patrols with the Ste. Anne police.

On city streets he consistently deferred to staff. He argued against the 2024 three-way stop at Loewen and Hespeler ('the law of unintended consequences'), backed refusing stop signs at Wyndham and Harmony Lane on warrant grounds, and told residents at rezoning hearings that a few more units add few cars and that through traffic is the real load. He went beyond staff advice once, for a full 30 km/h school zone at Southwood School (2025), and warned that the Active Transportation Plan must not get in the way of driving. He did not run in 2026.

Term by term

2014–2018

The most frequent mover on traffic items: Main Street East parking, the full Old Tom Road paving, the Industrial Road/PTH 52 signal request (identification medium-confidence), the Industrial Road stop sign and the province-funded Penfeld overrun, which he noted cut the city's share to a third. Generally followed staff advice and asked what evidence the city had before going to the Highway Traffic Board. Said traffic must stay an RCMP priority as trips across town take longer.

Took part in 17 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

Moved the 2020 request that the province make Loewen and PTH 12 a priority, citing a 9.5-minute wait to turn left, and then the design contract and $3.5 million borrowing. Moved the speed-limit by-law, the McKenzie–Giesbrecht study, the Giesbrecht signal request, and the September 2021 motion for a traffic officer and red-light cameras ('I'm not a fan of red light cameras in principle'). Told residents to report stop-sign runners to police, argued infill adds few cars, and opposed raising other taxpayers' share of the Clearspring Greens path.

Took part in 20 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Explained the yearly count as the city's lobbying evidence, moved the crosswalk-light letter (2024), and urged no warnings for Main Street racers. Led the argument against the Loewen–Hespeler three-way stop (defeated 3–4) and backed the Harmony Lane refusal. Moved a full school zone at Southwood School beyond staff advice. Arranged MPI-funded joint patrols with the Ste. Anne police, noting the detachment was often short of officers. Said the $11 million Active Transportation Plan 'won't be happening anytime soon'.

Took part in 32 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Steady across three terms: evidence and lobbying on the highways, police enforcement for speeding and racing, and deference to engineering standards on city streets. His departure removes the member who explained and moved most of council's traffic decisions.
