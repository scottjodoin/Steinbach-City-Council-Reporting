Traffic and congestion · Council member

Jake Hiebert: traffic, congestion and road safety

What did Jake Hiebert say and do about traffic congestion and road safety in Steinbach (Main Street and the highways, city streets, stop signs and signals, speed and enforcement) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Jake Hiebert told council he had asked for a full-time traffic officer years earlier, when he was the RCMP staff sergeant in charge in Steinbach, and welcomed the 2021 decision to fund one; in 2024 he suggested MPI should pay for a second. He questioned the value of the pandemic-year traffic count.

He cast most of council's lone dissents on traffic grounds: against a zero setback at 5 Loewen Boulevard over drivers' sight lines (6–1, 2021), against more First Street units until a traffic study was done (6–1, 2021; his motion to deny had no seconder), against the 6 First Street apartments by Giesbrecht (5–2, 2022), and against a 12-unit building on Henry Street, a street used by ambulances and fire trucks (6–1, 2026). He seconded the Loewen–Hespeler three-way stop and rejected more study, though he followed the warrant to refuse the Wyndham stop sign. In November 2026 he was named to the regional committee that lobbies ministers on highways.

“I don't agree with that we need further study, further analysis. I think there's been enough complaints and I think enough observations made of this intersection that it would definitely benefit.”

Councillor Jake Hiebert · Seconding the three-way stop at Loewen Boulevard and Hespeler Street · Apr 16, 2024 · 0:33:37

Term by term

2018–2022

Moved the Old Tom and Park Road streetlights and first reading of Traffic By-law 2164. Sided with Siemens against charging Clearspring Greens most of a pathway's cost. Questioned the COVID-year count. Lone vote against the 5 Loewen variance over sight lines, and against the 2021 First Street rezoning pending a traffic study. Welcomed the dedicated traffic officer he had sought as staff sergeant. Voted against 6 First Street.

Took part in 15 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Seconded the Loewen–Hespeler three-way stop and said enough complaints had been heard without further study (defeated 3–4). Agreed to refuse the Wyndham stop sign on the administration's recommendation. Suggested MPI fund a second traffic officer. Cited another driveway onto the four-lane highway in refusing a Brandt Street lot split. In 2026 was the only member to ask for a traffic study before an infill project (288 Henry Street) and voted against it.

Took part in 17 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

The most consistent advocate on council for studying traffic before adding density, and for police enforcement, drawing on his policing career. He takes over the regional highway-lobbying seat from Jac Siemens.
