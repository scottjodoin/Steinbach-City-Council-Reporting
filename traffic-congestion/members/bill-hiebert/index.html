Traffic and congestion · Council member

Bill Hiebert: traffic, congestion and road safety

What did Bill Hiebert say and do about traffic congestion and road safety in Steinbach (Main Street and the highways, city streets, stop signs and signals, speed and enforcement) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Bill Hiebert's traffic record is mostly seconding: the Old Tom and Park Road streetlights, third reading of the speed-limit by-law, the 2024 crosswalk-light letter, and routine readings of the traffic and debt by-laws. In 2021 he said he drove First Street at the evening peak and 'it actually wasn't bad', and in 2024 that apartments near his home added little traffic.

He took his own position twice. In 2022 he raised residents' complaints about engine-brake noise and moved the request that the province post 'no engine brake' signs on the highways into the city. In 2024 he voted with Siemens and Jake Hiebert for the three-way stop at Loewen and Hespeler, describing a five-minute wait to turn left; it lost 3–4. Several of his remarks are on speaker labels identified with only medium confidence and are not quoted.

Term by term

2018–2022

Seconded the streetlights at Old Tom and Park Road and third reading of the speed-limit by-law; voted with the majority on the Clearspring Greens pathway. Said First Street at the evening peak 'wasn't bad' before voting for more units (2021). Raised engine-brake noise south of the city and moved the request for signs on the highways (2022); no installation was reported.

Took part in 9 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Voted for the Loewen–Hespeler three-way stop (defeated 3–4) and seconded the letter asking the province for flashing crosswalk lights on Main and Brandt. Said apartments add little traffic. Otherwise routine roles: second reading of Traffic By-law 2254 and of the Loewen debt by-law, the Williams Creek subdivision and the McKenzie Avenue third reading.

Took part in 15 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

A low-profile member on traffic who sided with residents on the one intersection vote that split council, and otherwise voted with the majority.
