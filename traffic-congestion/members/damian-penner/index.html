Traffic and congestion · Council member

Damian Penner: traffic, congestion and road safety

What did Damian Penner say and do about traffic congestion and road safety in Steinbach (Main Street and the highways, city streets, stop signs and signals, speed and enforcement) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

In short

Damian Penner seconded the 2022 request for signals at Giesbrecht Street, saying it was 'time to get signals on there', and called Loewen and PTH 12 the intersection flagged 'year over year'. He led the 2021 refusal of a church school in the industrial park, arguing that heavy truck traffic and no paths made it unsafe for children.

On neighbourhood traffic he usually sided with staff and with development. He called construction traffic 'growing pains', said three or four more cars across from a school of 1,800 would not matter, approved the 6 First Street apartments on the expectation that the Giesbrecht lights would come first (they came about four years later), voted against the Loewen–Hespeler three-way stop, declined a sidewalk condition on a McKenzie Avenue rezoning, and said 155 new lots would not cause significant congestion. In November 2026 he became council's liaison to the RCMP advisory committee.

“we've already requested lights. And so at this time, I feel we can move forward on this project. And then once the lights are up, which will be up well in advance of this being in completion, we can start analyzing the traffic”

Councillor Damian Penner · Approving apartments beside PTH 52 and Giesbrecht Street; the signals were operating by October 2026 · Nov 1, 2022 · 0:52:46

Term by term

2018–2022

Moved the Clearspring Greens pathway local improvement, saying the path mainly served the homes paying for it. Led the 5–2 refusal of a school in the industrial park over truck traffic and the lack of paths. Called construction traffic in Hampton Village 'growing pains'. Seconded the Loewen by-law and the Giesbrecht signal request, citing growth in Mitchell and on First Street. Backed the 6 First Street apartments on the expectation that signals would come first.

Took part in 14 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Argued a fourplex across from the regional secondary school would add only a few cars (2022). Asked that the Loewen–Hespeler count be done on a school day, then voted against the three-way stop, deferring to the engineer. Credited reserves for the Park Road West extension. Said much of the Active Transportation Plan would be built by later councils. Moved the 48-unit McKenzie Avenue rezoning and declined Siemens' sidewalk amendment, and said the Williams Creek subdivision would not cause significant congestion.

Took part in 19 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

Consistent across two terms: support for the highway asks and for density near major roads, and deference to engineers on intersections. As the new RCMP liaison he inherits council's main enforcement channel.
