Traffic and congestion · Council member

Earl Funk: traffic, congestion and road safety

What did Earl Funk say and do about traffic congestion and road safety in Steinbach (Main Street and the highways, city streets, stop signs and signals, speed and enforcement) between December 2015 and November 2026, and how did that change over time?

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

In short

As a councillor, Earl Funk moved council's yearly RCMP priorities, which always included traffic, and defended the higher Main Street East speed limit with a speed study showing most drivers already went 55–60 km/h. He asked for traffic counts before deciding on a resident's stop-sign request at Reimer Avenue and Elmdale Street.

As mayor since November 2018 he has chaired every traffic decision. He told the infrastructure minister in 2019 that Loewen Boulevard and PTH 12 was the city's top priority, backed the 2021 request for a traffic officer after being 'bombarded' with complaints about racing, and in 2026 said the Giesbrecht signals had been waited on for five years. On city streets he usually sided with staff and the RCMP: speeding was 'an RCMP issue', roads follow development, and he voted against the 2024 three-way stop at Loewen and Hespeler, preferring a roundabout that has not been budgeted. In 2025 he ruled out designated bike lanes, calling Steinbach 'the automobile city'.

“the signalization there, that was first, I think, recognized in 2021. And now we're 5 years in, 2026, and we still don't have signal lights there.…it's a problem area and there's a lot of vehicles coming in from the west, coming in every morning to work, and it really causes congestion in that area.”

Mayor Earl Funk · On the PTH 52 and Giesbrecht Street signals, requested in 2022 · Feb 17, 2026 · 0:20:30

Term by term

2014–2018

Moved the RCMP priorities that kept traffic on the list (2016, 2017, 2018) and seconded the request to the Highway Traffic Board to review the PTH 52 West speed zone. Defended the 60 km/h limit on Main Street East with the speed study and voted against Siemens' stricter no-parking amendment. In 2018 asked for counts or a study before deciding on stop signs at Reimer Avenue and Elmdale Street; no result was reported.

Took part in 12 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2014–2018 story.

2018–2022

As the new mayor he welcomed the province's handover of speed limits on city streets (2019), said the 2019 budget had no room for the Clearspring Greens pathway, and in 2020 promised a plan for edge-of-city roads such as Bush Farm Road that never came to a public meeting. He backed a push-button crossing over a four-way stop at McKenzie and Giesbrecht because it 'keeps traffic flowing', named Loewen and PTH 12 the city's priority to the minister, and supported the traffic officer and the request for red-light cameras. He called Giesbrecht and PTH 52 'our priority intersection' (2022), treated neighbourhood speeding as an RCMP matter, and told residents at the Old Tom–Loewen hearing that roads follow development.

Took part in 18 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2018–2022 story.

2022–2026

Voted with Siemens against the McKenzie Avenue fourplex (2022), though he said traffic was not his reason. Brought a resident's crosswalk-light request to council (2024) and kept pressing the province on PTH 12 signal timing and the Giesbrecht lights. Voted against the 2024 three-way stop at Loewen and Hespeler, saying the answer was a roundabout in the long-term capital plan; none was budgeted. Accepted the Harmony Lane report as long as the RCMP enforced speeds, ruled out designated bike lanes, did not support a resident's idea of a one-way street, and in November 2026 named Damian Penner to the RCMP advisory committee and Jake Hiebert to the regional committee that lobbies on highways.

Took part in 27 of the items in this report during this term. Read the 2022–2026 story.

The trajectory

From councillor to mayor his approach stayed the same: lobby the province hard on its highways, fund police enforcement, and on city streets follow staff warrants and let roads follow development. What grew was his frustration with provincial delays, which by 2026 he voiced openly.
