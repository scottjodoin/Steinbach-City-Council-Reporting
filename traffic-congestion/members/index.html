Traffic and congestion · Council members

Council members on traffic and road safety

How did each council member vote and speak on traffic congestion and road safety in Steinbach between December 2015 and November 2026?

In short

Ten people served on council in these years; two more joined in November 2026. Every request to the province, every traffic by-law and the traffic officer passed unanimously, so the differences show up in who raised problems, who moved the decisions, and how members voted on residents’ requests and on rezonings where traffic was at issue.

  • Michael Zwaagstra moved most of it. The yearly counts and requests to the province, most steps of the Loewen rebuild, the traffic officer and the joint patrols. He also led the case for following engineering warrants rather than residents’ requests.
  • Jac Siemens raised most of it. Signal timing, Loewen and Hespeler, the Giesbrecht lights and highway lobbying. He moved the two stop signs council considered against advice: one passed (Barkman and Fourth, 2018), one lost 3–4 (Loewen and Hespeler, 2024).
  • The 2024 vote showed the two camps. Siemens, Jake Hiebert and Bill Hiebert voted for the Loewen–Hespeler three-way stop; Zwaagstra, Damian Penner, Susan Penner and Mayor Earl Funk against.
  • Jake Hiebert cast most lone dissents on traffic. A former Steinbach RCMP staff sergeant, he cast three lone votes against development approvals, over drivers’ sight lines, a missing traffic study and emergency access.
  • The mayor pressed the province and backed police but sided with staff on city streets. Zwaagstra and Siemens did not run in 2026.

Choose a member

Earl Funk

Councillor 2010–2018 · Mayor since November 2018 (re-elected by acclamation in 2026)

Pressed the province on Loewen Boulevard and the Giesbrecht lights and championed the traffic officer, but on city streets backed staff, the RCMP and 'the automobile city' over new stop signs, studies or bike lanes.

Chris Goertzen

Mayor until October 2018 (this record covers December 2015 on)

Chaired the last years of the cost-shared PTH 12 intersection program and pushed the province on Industrial Road; reminded council that lights and crosswalks only work with careful drivers and enforcement.

Michael Zwaagstra

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

Council's main voice on traffic for a decade: moved the yearly counts, the Loewen rebuild and the traffic officer, and led the case for following engineering warrants rather than residents' requests.

Jac Siemens

Councillor in all three terms covered · did not run in 2026

The member who most often brought residents' traffic complaints to the table, pushed for a roundabout at Loewen and Hespeler, and called the city's traffic 'horrendous'; he also dismissed a bypass and lights at Lund Road.

Susan Penner

Councillor in all three terms covered · Deputy Mayor from November 2026 (topped the 2026 poll)

Mostly a routine voice on traffic, but the lone vote for a stop sign by a school and park in 2023, and the member who said Harmony Lane's problem was speed, not volume.

Damian Penner

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Backed the big highway asks and refused a school in the industrial park on traffic-safety grounds, but most often argued that new homes add little traffic and that council should 'let the plumbers plumb'.

Bill Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

Mostly routine roles, but raised the engine-brake complaints and voted for the Loewen–Hespeler three-way stop from his own experience waiting there.

Jake Hiebert

Councillor since November 2018 · re-elected 2026

A former Steinbach RCMP staff sergeant who had long wanted a traffic officer, and the member most willing to stop or slow development until traffic was studied.

John Fehr

Councillor 2010–2018 · Deputy Mayor until November 2017

Moved the 2016 PTH 12 intersection contract as part of making the city safer and the Loewen Boulevard widening by-law for truck traffic.

Cari Penner

Councillor 2010–2018 (and an earlier term before 2010) · Deputy Mayor from November 2017

Moved the 2018 request to share with the province the cost of new pedestrian crossing bands on Main Street; the province said no money was available.

Ron Falk and Greg Carlos

Councillors since November 2026

Elected in October 2026; no record on this topic yet. Ron Falk was a Hanover School Division trustee for 34 years, 14 as chair; Greg Carlos is a past president of the South Eastman Filipino Association, and council is his first elected office.

Most decisions were unanimous, and councillors take turns moving routine motions, so moving a motion is not the same as championing it. Split votes are the clearest evidence of where members differed. Several below are on a pathway levy or on rezonings, where traffic was one of the reasons given.
