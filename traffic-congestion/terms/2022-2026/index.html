Traffic and congestion · By term

2022–2026: Mayor Earl Funk’s second council

What did Steinbach City Council do about traffic and road safety from November 2022 to October 2026, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • The highway asks were finally delivered. The province retimed PTH 12 signals four months after council pressed (2023), Loewen Boulevard and PTH 12 was rebuilt in 2024–25 (about $11.2 million, about $6.1 million of it the city’s), and the Giesbrecht signals, put off from 2023 to 2024 and then again, were operating by October 2026.
  • Residents’ stop-sign requests divided council. Michael Zwaagstra, Damian Penner, Susan Penner and the mayor followed the engineer and defeated a three-way stop at Loewen and Hespeler 3–4 (2024); Jac Siemens, Jake Hiebert and Bill Hiebert wanted it. Stop signs at Wyndham (2023) and Harmony Lane (2025) were refused on warrant grounds.
  • One exception, for a school. Council made Kroeker Avenue at Southwood School a full 30 km/h school zone in 2025, where staff had suggested advisory signs only.
  • Enforcement grew; planning didn’t. Council credited the traffic officer with less Main Street racing, added joint patrols with the Ste. Anne police (2025), and combined its speed and school-zone rules in Traffic By-law 2254. There was no budget for a traffic-calming study, no transportation master plan, and the Active Transportation Plan was accepted without funding.

Council: Mayor Earl Funk; Councillors Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Damian Penner, Bill Hiebert and Jake Hiebert (the same seven as 2018–2022).

What happened

2023: a quick fix on PTH 12, a slow one at Giesbrecht

At the first meeting of 2023, councillors relayed complaints about the PTH 12 signals:

“Some of the complaints I've heard are due to the amount of time it takes to drive through Steinbach on any given day, especially during peak hours.…The left-hand turn at Superstore Co-op intersection allows only 6 cars through when there's often 10 or more cars stacked up in the turning lane.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · Jan 3, 2023 · 0:19:46

Council sent staff to meet the province. By May the problem was found and fixed:

“there were several intersections that were found to have programming for the existing signals that, for whatever reason, had become locked into certain cycles inappropriately.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager, reporting the province’s fix · May 2, 2023 · 0:28:00

Other requests ran into the province’s budget or rules. Overnight flashing signals are against provincial policy on highways. And the Giesbrecht signals council had asked to have built in 2023 were put off:

“The latest update that administration has received from that office is that budget funding was not available to see that project be completed in 2023. However, it has been scheduled for 2024.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Mar 21, 2023 · 0:31:26

Council paved the Park Road West extension to Industrial Road ($1.97 million from reserves) as a second way north during the coming Loewen rebuild. In June it refused a Hampton Village resident’s request for a stop sign at Wyndham Estate Drive and Ashford Lane: a staff count found low volumes, normal speeds and waits under 15 seconds. The vote was 6–1:

“I see this, it's a bit of a similar situation with SCS, Steinbach Christian School, asking for a crosswalk on the number 12 every time the study's done. Well, the numbers don't warrant it, but at the same time, it would make sense to have one 'cause it's by a school.”

Councillor Susan Penner, the vote against · Jun 20, 2023 · 0:32:00

2024: Loewen and Hespeler, and Loewen and PTH 12

A resident, B.B., asked council to back a request made to the province three years earlier for flashing crosswalk lights on Main Street and Brandt Street. Council sent a letter (R24-040); the province acknowledged it and wrote to the resident about a study, and no lights were reported. In April a new count at Loewen Boulevard and Hespeler Street found the Hespeler approach at level of service E, acceptable by the city’s standard. The engineer advised more data. Councillor Siemens moved a three-way stop anyway, and it lost 3–4:

“The reason that we hire experts to do this kind of analysis using standards and in evaluating the overall situation is so that we don't end up with the law of unintended consequences.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra, against · Apr 16, 2024 · 0:34:51

“I think the best plan is probably to keep working and get the roundabout in our long-term capital plan and get that project done.”

Mayor Earl Funk, against · Apr 16, 2024 · 0:41:21

No roundabout has appeared in a published budget. In May council awarded the $9.98 million Loewen and PTH 12 contract, three years after the cost-sharing deal:

“we had to postpone and postpone and postpone. And now we are finally doing it.”

Mayor Earl Funk · May 21, 2024 · 0:25:53

The province said the Giesbrecht signals would be built in 2024; they were not.

2025: rules, refusals and a school zone

In January council adopted Traffic By-law 2254, combining the speed-limit, school-zone and traffic by-laws, adding a zone for the new Park Hill school, banning engine retarder brakes and raising fines. The same night, residents whose two dogs had been killed by a speeding driver asked for a stop sign at Harmony Lane and Cutlass Drive. Councillors asked about other calming measures:

“traditionally the city would seek the services of an outside consultant to evaluate other measures. That would come with a cost.…there is no budget funding approved for that type of initiative.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Jan 21, 2025 · 0:33:51

The engineer found the existing stop sign appropriate, and council accepted “no changes” on the condition that the RCMP watch for speeders. In April, adopting the new community plan, the mayor ruled out bike lanes:

“But we also realize that we are the automobile city and we do have to make sure that our vehicles still have a place on the road.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Apr 1, 2025 · 0:46:15

In May council accepted the city’s first Active Transportation Plan, which prices multi-use paths at about $11.8 million (asphalt) and sidewalks at about $1.6 million in 2025 dollars, with no funding attached, and backed MPI-paid joint patrols with the Ste. Anne police after racing complaints rose while the detachment was short of officers. In August it went beyond staff advice for Southwood School:

“Reducing speed to 30 kilometers an hour certainly does affect traffic flow.…I think it makes more sense to err on the side of safety.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Aug 5, 2025 · 0:17:00

Loewen Boulevard and PTH 12 reopened fully on November 24, 2025. The city borrowed $3 million over five years at 3.83%, less than the $3.5 million budgeted.

2026: the lights at Giesbrecht

The 2025 count found nearly 3,500 vehicles an hour at the evening peak at Brandt and Main, and recommended retiming three PTH 12 intersections. Staff called its advice a matter for “general consideration” and “perhaps budget funding”; the signals are the province’s. Members turned their frustration on Giesbrecht:

“the signalization there, that was first, I think, recognized in 2021. And now we're 5 years in, 2026, and we still don't have signal lights there.”

Mayor Earl Funk · Feb 17, 2026 · 0:20:30

SteinbachOnline reported the Giesbrecht signals operating on October 13, 2026. Through 2026 residents at rezoning hearings (Friesen Avenue, Stonebridge Crossing, Giesbrecht Street, McKenzie Avenue, Henry Street) asked whether traffic studies had been done; none was required, because the city asks for them only near the highways. Jake Hiebert voted alone against a Henry Street building near the hospital over traffic and emergency access. The Park Hill School parents’ request for safety measures went to staff in April and had no public answer by October. In September the RCMP reported its traffic officer back on full duty and a targeted blitz against Main Street racing.

Where members differed

Every request to the province and every traffic by-law passed unanimously. The splits were over residents’ stop-sign requests and over rezonings where some members cited traffic: Jac Siemens and Jake Hiebert cast most of the dissents.
