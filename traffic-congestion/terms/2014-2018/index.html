Traffic and congestion · By term

2014–2018: Mayor Chris Goertzen’s council

What did Steinbach City Council do about traffic and road safety from December 2015 to October 2018, and where did its members disagree?

In short

  • A shared highway program finished. The last of three years of PTH 12 intersection work, cost-shared with the province, put signals and turn lanes at Penfeld/Acres Drive in 2016. Extra work the province paid for cut the city’s share to about a third.
  • The count drove every highway ask, and the province took its time. Staff waited for three straight years of met warrants before council asked for signals at Industrial Road and PTH 52 (January 2017). The province approved them in March 2018. Council asked for 50 km/h on PTH 52 West and got 70.
  • Loewen Boulevard became the worst corner. The counts named Loewen and PTH 12 the signalized intersection most in need from January 2016. Council secured corner land for future turn lanes in 2018, but asked the province for nothing more in this term.
  • City streets followed staff, with one exception. Council approved staff’s parking and stop-sign changes, set aside costly requests (an Albert Street sidewalk, a wider Henry Street), and in January 2018 put a four-way stop at Barkman and Fourth against staff advice.
  • No transportation plan. The city planner said in July 2018 the city wanted one “within the future” but not immediately.

Council: Mayor Chris Goertzen; Councillors Earl Funk, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Susan Penner, Cari Penner and John Fehr.

What happened

2016: the end of the PTH 12 program

Since 2014 the city and the province had split the cost of rebuilding four PTH 12 (Brandt Street) intersections under the province’s Urban Highway Fund: Park Road, Stonebridge/Millwork, Madison and, last, Penfeld/Acres Drive. Council awarded the final $2.23 million contract in April 2016:

“Number 12 is a safer street and a more efficient street because of this program. And there'll be a little bit more pain this year before the gain”

Mayor Chris Goertzen, awarding the Penfeld/Acres contract · Apr 5, 2016 · 0:06:30

The intersection was open by November 2016. In December 2017 the province paid all of $571,150 in extra work, bringing the project to just over $3.6 million:

“So instead of paying 50% of the total, the city is contributing 1/3, and so that's certainly a better arrangement than the 50/50.”

Councillor Michael Zwaagstra · Dec 19, 2017 · 0:06:03

Council also paved Old Tom Road in full despite a $200,000 overrun; by 2017 a councillor described Old Tom and Industrial Road as “unofficial bypasses” around the city. On Main Street East, where the province had raised the limit to 60 km/h, council adopted staff’s peak-hour no-parking rule and defeated Councillor Siemens’ stricter version, the term’s only split vote on traffic:

“This increased restriction is only going to be valuable, though, if there's actually enforcement.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Jul 5, 2016 · 0:15:42

2017: asking the province for Industrial Road

The count presented in January 2017 showed Industrial Road and PTH 52 West over capacity for a third year in a row. Staff had advised waiting for a pattern:

“And I think with the 3rd year in a row having shown that the warrant is likely there, that may be a good opportunity for council to act.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager · Jan 17, 2017 · 0:10:23

Council made its first formal request for signals there and, by amendment, for crosswalks on PTH 12 near the Christian school and near seniors’ housing at Friesen/Woodhaven. The mayor also suggested a faster step, asking the Highway Traffic Board to move the PTH 52 West speed zone:

“Right now, it will take quite some time to get signalization there as a request going forward, and for us to make this request to the Highway Traffic Board, which is more brisk in their responses, Not always what we like, but at least then we're taking some action to hopefully make things a little bit safer.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Jan 17, 2017 · 0:36:52

Seven months later there was still no answer:

“At this time, I have not had or seen a response from Manitoba Infrastructure on that request. It is still under consideration.”

Troy Warkentin, City Manager, answering Councillor Susan Penner · Aug 1, 2017 · 0:24:04

Congestion on city streets came up too. Councillor Siemens asked for a count at Loewen Boulevard and Hespeler Street, outside the yearly highway count; council agreed to an informal staff count, and no result was reported:

“I was backed up past Lyndhurst. I couldn't move from the stop sign because of the traffic flow that was coming into town.”

Councillor Jac Siemens · May 2, 2017 · 0:28:07
Context

Residents raised traffic mostly at land-use hearings. At a 2017 rezoning on Loewen Boulevard, Albert Street neighbours said traffic had grown “tenfold” and children walked a busy road with no sidewalk; staff said no traffic study had been required of the developer and there were no immediate sidewalk plans. At a December 2017 hearing on Brandt Street, neighbours (K.L., A.P., B.B.) described frequent collisions at Brandt and Woodhaven, heavy trucks and seniors crossing PTH 12 without a controlled crossing. MPI counted 2,205 collisions in the city in 2015, 2,196 in 2016 and 2,268 in 2017; the fire department’s 2017 list of collision calls was led by PTH 12 at PTH 52, Industrial Road at PTH 52 and Brandt at Reimer, nearly all on the provincial highways (SteinbachOnline, Jan 2018).

2018: the province answers, partly

In March 2018 the province approved $350,000 for signals at Industrial Road, after four straight years of met warrants. They were operating by early 2020:

“That has been a place that we have been concerned about for a long time, Industrial Road and 52, and we've had numerous accidents and motor vehicle incidents there, so we're very happy that The Minister of Infrastructure has given us the go-ahead for that.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen · Mar 6, 2018 · 1:11:36

Other answers were smaller or no. The Highway Traffic Board lowered PTH 52 West from 80 to 70 km/h, not the 50 council asked for. The province said it was “still looking into” the PTH 12 crossings, and in August declined to share the cost of new pedestrian crossing bands on Main Street. In January, on a request from the Hanover School Division, council made Barkman Avenue and Fourth Street a four-way stop although staff had recommended keeping the two-way stop; it did not add the lower school-zone speed the division also wanted.

At Loewen Boulevard, now the worst corner in the count (level of service F, no turn lanes), council used a variance and a road-widening by-law to secure land for future turn lanes:

“This eventually will improve that scenario, and obviously this is in regards to our long-term planning initiative when it comes to traffic management.”

Mayor Chris Goertzen, on the Loewen Boulevard widening · Aug 7, 2018 · 0:19:01

That long-term planning did not include a city transportation plan. Answering the province’s comments on the new community plan:

“In regards to transportation, we note that their comments are definitely something we should be looking into, and within the future, we do want to take on a transportation plan. But that's not something we would be doing immediately.”

Lacey Gaudet, City Planner · Jul 17, 2018 · 0:56:06

The highway access plan for PTH 12 and 52 prepared with the province would wait, she said, until the province adopted it. No transportation master plan has been adopted since.
